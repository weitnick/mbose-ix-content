## 1. Complete Lesson

### Growing up: growth and development

From the day you were born you have been changing. You have become taller and heavier, you have learnt to walk, talk, read and reason, and you have learnt to make friends and control your feelings. Two words describe these changes:

- **Growth** is the increase in the **size** of the body: height, weight and the size of its parts. It can be measured in centimetres and kilograms.
- **Development** is the change in **abilities, skills and behaviour** as a person grows: learning to think, to speak, to control emotions and to live with others.

**Human development** includes both. It covers the **physical, intellectual (mental), emotional and social** sides of a person, and it moves step by step towards **maturity**.

**Knowledge of our body.** Knowledge of the structure of our body and how it works is essential for maintaining **good health**. When you understand the changes taking place in you, you are less worried by them and can look after yourself better.

**What controls growth?** Growth is controlled by many factors:

1. **Genes** — the characters passed on from parents. They set the limits, for example how tall a person can grow.
2. **Endocrine secretions** — hormones from the endocrine glands (the pituitary, thyroid and sex glands) regulate growth and the changes of adolescence.
3. **Food** — a balanced diet supplies the materials for building the body.
4. **Exercise** — strengthens muscles and bones and helps normal growth.
5. **Rest, relaxation and sleep** — the body repairs and builds its tissues during rest.

**All parts of the body grow at different rates.** The head of a newborn baby is large compared with its body; the legs and arms grow fast later, and during adolescence the hands and feet often grow before the rest of the body catches up.

### Stages of growth

![Fig. 1.1 Stages of growth](figures/hpe_ch01_fig01_stages.png)

| Stage | Approximate age | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Infancy | birth to about 2 years | very fast growth; the baby depends completely on others |
| Childhood | about 2 to 10–12 years | steady growth; learning language, skills and habits |
| Adolescence | about 10–12 to 19 years | **striking changes in the body**; growth spurt; puberty |
| Adulthood | from about 19–20 years | growth is complete; full responsibilities |

**Adolescence** is the period of **transition from childhood to adulthood**. It is the phase in which you move from **childhood dependencies to sharing adult responsibilities**. The World Health Organization treats the ages **10 to 19 years** as adolescence.

**Puberty** is the stage, early in adolescence, when the reproductive organs mature and a boy or girl becomes **capable of sexual reproduction**. It usually begins earlier in girls than in boys.

**Changes during adolescence.** Adolescence brings major changes in the body:

- a sudden increase in height and weight, called the **growth spurt**;
- growth of hair in the armpits and the pubic region;
- more active sweat and oil glands, which can cause **pimples (acne)** and body odour;
- in boys: the **voice cracks** and deepens, a moustache and beard appear, and the shoulders broaden and the muscles develop;
- in girls: the breasts develop, the hips broaden and **menstruation** begins.

These changes are normal. They come at different times for different people, so there is no need to compare yourself with friends.

### What is maturity?

**Maturity** is indicative of one's **readiness to share adult experiences, privileges and responsibilities**. A mature person can look after himself or herself, take sensible decisions and live in harmony with others.

Maturity has several **aspects**. A person may be mature in one aspect and immature in another: a tall fifteen-year-old who sulks whenever she does not get her way is physically mature but emotionally immature.

![Fig. 1.2 Aspects of maturity](figures/hpe_ch01_fig02_aspects.png)

The aspects of maturity are:

1. Chronological maturity
2. Physical maturity
3. Intellectual maturity
4. Emotional maturity
5. Social maturity
6. Philosophical maturity

When all of these develop together, a person has **all-round maturity**.

### Chronological maturity

**Chronological maturity** is your age counted in years: the number of birthdays you have celebrated. **Chronological age cannot change; you cannot hurry it up or slow it down.**

**Why is chronological maturity important?** The law and society use age to decide when a person may take on adult rights and duties. Chronological maturity is important for:

- getting the **right to vote** (18 years in India);
- **inheriting property** and managing it oneself;
- obtaining a **driving licence**;
- **getting married** (the legal minimum is 18 for a woman and 21 for a man);
- opening a **savings account** in a post office or bank in one's own name.

Age alone does not make a person mature: a twenty-year-old can still behave like a child.

### Physical maturity

One gains **physical maturity** when one has obtained one's **full height, weight and strength**. Proper nutrition and physical exercise can help, but they cannot altogether change the pattern of physical growth, because that is set largely by the genes. Physical maturity is the **easiest aspect to measure**: height, weight and strength can be measured with simple instruments.

### Intellectual maturity

**Intellectual maturity** is maturity of the mind: the ability to think, understand, judge and decide. It is **difficult to measure**, but it can be judged in the following manner:

1. You can **handle and understand the language of words, figures and signs or symbols** in accordance with your culture.
2. You **take your own decisions** and do not wait for advice or prompting from others. The more independently you take decisions, the more intellectually mature you are.
3. You can look at your problems **impartially, objectively and from the right perspective**.
4. If you make a mistake and **accept it**, you are more mature than those who shift the responsibility on to others.
5. You **avoid hasty judgements**. A person who judges in haste is like a child who makes quick moves without weighing the pros and cons; **hasty judgements are always risky**. You are intellectually mature if you can **reserve judgement until you have considered the pros and cons** of a problem.

![Fig. 1.3 How intellectual maturity can be judged](figures/hpe_ch01_fig03_intellectual.png)

**Intellectual growth varies from person to person** because each individual is different, and it also depends on the situations in which one lives. It **continues as long as one wants to grow and keeps alert**.

### Emotional maturity

You often lose your temper and feel extremely annoyed when things do not happen the way you want. This may be due to a **lack of emotional maturity**. **Emotional maturity** concerns your **feelings and how you express them**.

- Children who do not get easily disturbed and upset are able to take rational decisions and tend to grow emotionally mature.
- An emotionally mature person **restrains himself** and is not easily carried away by an immediate impulse.
- There are some feelings you accept and keep to yourself, and some you express and share with others. Such control over one's emotions is **not learnt in a day or two**; you slowly learn more and more self-control.
- **Emotional maturity helps you to have an attractive and charming personality. It makes you a socially acceptable person.**

### Social maturity

**Social maturity** is the **ability to get along with people**. As you grow from a baby to a child, then to an adolescent and eventually to an adult, your **spheres of social relationship and activity keep on enlarging**: from your family to your neighbourhood, school, community and, later, your workplace.

- Social maturity is **gradually learned** as you learn to live in a world full of people.
- It is a **very difficult area of growth**. It is **neither predictable nor regular**, and it is **not the same all the time**.
- Your **success, popularity and happiness** depend to a great extent on how you get along with the people around you.

**Why must you develop social maturity as you grow up?** Because the spheres of relationship and activity keep enlarging, you need more and more people of various kinds in your life, and you need different things from them.

**Adaptability** — the ability to **adjust oneself to new people, places and situations** — is a mark of a socially and emotionally mature person. A student who joins a new school and soon settles in with new classmates shows adaptability.

### Philosophical maturity

Every human being has a **philosophy of life**. It includes **long-term values, goals worth striving for, making true friends, and having a dedication and a mission in life**. You cannot be quite mature until you have a good, workable philosophy of life, and having one can help towards a successful career or marriage.

Your philosophy of life depends on the **customs and values of the people in your family, religion and community**. The teachings of your religion set goals before you; great thoughts, noble ideas and the lives of great men and women have moulded the philosophy of many people; and the people you love and admire also help to build it.

### All-round maturity

**All-round maturity** is the balanced development of all the aspects together. It is the maturity that **helps in maintaining an equilibrium in the present as well as in future life**. A person with all-round maturity is healthy, thinks clearly, controls his or her feelings, gets on well with others and has worthwhile goals.

### Summary of the chapter

- Growth is increase in size; development is change in abilities. Human development covers the physical, intellectual, emotional and social sides.
- Growth is controlled by genes, endocrine secretions, food, exercise, and rest, relaxation and sleep. Body parts grow at different rates.
- The stages are infancy, childhood, adolescence and adulthood. Striking changes take place in adolescence, the transition from childhood to adulthood; puberty is when one becomes capable of reproduction.
- Maturity is readiness to share adult experiences, privileges and responsibilities.
- Chronological maturity is age in birthdays and cannot be hurried; it matters for voting, inheritance, a driving licence, marriage and a bank account.
- Physical maturity means full height, weight and strength; it is the easiest aspect to measure.
- Intellectual maturity is the most difficult to measure: understanding words, figures and symbols; independent decisions; objectivity; accepting mistakes; no hasty judgements.
- Emotional maturity is control over feelings; it gives an attractive, charming personality and makes one socially acceptable.
- Social maturity is the ability to get along with people; it is a very difficult area of growth, neither predictable nor regular.
- Philosophical maturity covers values, goals, true friends and a mission in life. All-round maturity keeps an equilibrium in present and future life.

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — Growth, development and adolescence

Growth is measurable increase in size; development is change in function and ability. Genes set the limits, endocrine secretions regulate the pace, and food, exercise and rest decide whether a person reaches the limit. Adolescence is the bridge from childhood to adulthood and the time of the most striking bodily change, beginning with puberty.

**Exam focus:** "Growth is controlled by many factors. Name them" (sample Q32) and "Striking changes take place in the body during …" (sample Q3).

### Section 2 — Meaning of maturity; chronological and physical maturity

Maturity is readiness for adult experiences, privileges and responsibilities. Chronological maturity is fixed by the calendar and is used by the law; physical maturity is reached with full height, weight and strength and is the easiest aspect to measure.

**Exam focus:** "Why is chronological maturity important?" (sample Q40; SSLC 2023, Q3(c)).

### Section 3 — Intellectual and emotional maturity

Intellectual maturity is shown by independent, objective, unhurried judgement; it is the hardest aspect to measure. Emotional maturity is self-control over feelings, learnt slowly; it gives a charming personality and social acceptance.

**Exam focus:** "How is intellectual maturity measured? (any five points)" — sample Q49, and a 10-mark essay in 2020 and 2022.

### Section 4 — Social, philosophical and all-round maturity

Social maturity is getting along with people as one's social world widens; it is a very difficult, irregular area of growth. Philosophical maturity is a workable philosophy of life. All-round maturity balances them all and keeps one's life in equilibrium.

**Exam focus:** "What is social maturity?" (SSLC 2020, 2023) and "Why do you need to develop social maturity?" (SSLC 2023, Q2(c)).

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas

- **Maturity has many sides.** Being tall or turning 18 does not by itself make a person mature.
- **Self-control and judgement grow slowly.** They are learnt through experience, not in a day.
- **Getting along with people decides much of life's success.** Social maturity widens with your world.
- **A philosophy of life gives direction.** Values and goals keep a person steady.

### 7.2 Real-life connections

- **Board-exam stress:** a student who stays calm, plans and asks for help when needed shows emotional and intellectual maturity.
- **Choosing a stream after Class X:** weighing interests, marks and career options yourself, instead of simply copying friends, is intellectual maturity.
- **Joining a hostel in Shillong or Tura:** adjusting to roommates from different districts shows adaptability and social maturity.
- **Turning 18:** you get your voter card and may apply for a driving licence — chronological maturity brings legal rights and duties.

### 8.3 More practice in the board's style

**Q1.** What is growth?

**Q1.** What do you understand by human development?

**Q2.** Growth is controlled by many factors. Name them.

**Q9.** What is puberty?

**Q10.** What is social maturity?
**Answer:** The ability to get along with people; it is learnt gradually as one lives among people.

**Q16.** Mention four changes seen in the body during adolescence.

### Short answers (3 marks each)

**Q17.** Why is chronological maturity important?
**Answer:** It is important for getting the right to vote, inheriting property, obtaining a driving licence, getting married, and opening a savings account in a post office or bank. The law grants these only at a fixed age.

**Q18.** Why do you need to develop social maturity as you grow up?
**Answer:** As we grow, our spheres of relationship and activity keep enlarging. We need more and more people of various kinds in life and we need different things from them, so we must learn to get along with all of them.

**Q19.** In which way does intellectual growth vary from person to person?
**Answer:** Every individual is different, so intellectual growth differs. It also depends on the situations in which one lives, and it continues as long as one wants to grow and keeps alert.

**Q20.** What is physical maturity? How do nutrition and exercise affect it?
**Answer:** Physical maturity is gained on obtaining full height, weight and strength. Proper nutrition and exercise help a person reach it, but they cannot altogether change the pattern of growth set by the genes.

**Q21.** What is emotional maturity? Why is it important?
**Answer:** Emotional maturity is control over one's feelings and how they are expressed. It prevents rash behaviour, gives an attractive and charming personality, and makes one socially acceptable.

**Q22.** Why is social maturity called a very difficult area of growth?
**Answer:** It is learnt only gradually by living among people; it is neither predictable nor regular; and it is not the same all the time, as our relationships keep changing and widening.

**Q23.** What helps to build a person's philosophy of life?
**Answer:** The customs and values of family, religion and community; religious teachings; great thoughts, noble ideas and the lives of great people; and the people one loves and admires.

**Q24.** What is all-round maturity? Why should it be the aim of growing up?
**Answer:** All-round maturity is balanced maturity in all aspects. It should be the aim because it keeps life in equilibrium now and in the future: a person who is mature in one aspect only can still fail in others.

**Q25.** Mention three changes that take place in boys and three in girls during adolescence.

**Q27.** How do genes and endocrine secretions control growth?
**Answer:** Genes, inherited from parents, set the limits of growth such as height. Endocrine glands (pituitary, thyroid, sex glands) release hormones that regulate the rate of growth and the changes of adolescence.

**Q28.** Suggest three ways in which a teenager can grow intellectually mature.
**Answer:** Take small decisions independently after weighing the pros and cons; look at problems objectively; accept mistakes and learn from them instead of blaming others.

## 11. Long-Answer Questions (5 marks each)

### Long answers (5 marks each)

**Q1.** How is intellectual maturity measured? (Any five points)
**Answer:** Intellectual maturity is difficult to measure, but it can be judged as follows:
1. You can handle and understand the language of words, figures and signs or symbols in accordance with your culture.
2. You take your own decisions without waiting for advice or prompting; the more independently you decide, the more mature you are.
3. You look at your problems impartially, objectively and from the right perspective.
4. You accept your mistakes instead of shifting the responsibility on to others.
5. You avoid hasty judgements and weigh the pros and cons first, since hasty judgements are always risky.

**Q2.** Explain what you understand by philosophical maturity.
**Answer:** Every person has a philosophy of life. It includes long-term values, goals worth striving for, true friends, and a dedication and mission in life.
One cannot be quite mature without a workable philosophy of life, and it aids a successful career and marriage.
It depends on the customs and values of one's family, religion and community.
Religious teachings, great thoughts, noble ideas and the lives of great people mould it.
The people one loves and admires also help to build it.

**Q3.** Write a note on emotional maturity.
**Answer:** Emotional maturity is control over one's feelings and how they are expressed.
Losing one's temper when things go wrong shows a lack of it.
Children who are not easily upset take rational decisions and grow emotionally mature.
A mature person restrains himself, is not carried away by impulses, and keeps some feelings to himself while sharing others.
This control is learnt slowly. It gives an attractive, charming personality and makes one socially acceptable.

**Q4.** Explain what you understand by social maturity.
**Answer:** Social maturity is the ability to get along with people.
As we grow from baby to child, adolescent and adult, our spheres of relationship and activity keep enlarging.
It is learnt gradually as we learn to live in a world full of people.
It is a very difficult area of growth: neither predictable nor regular, and not the same all the time.
Our success, popularity and happiness depend largely on how we get along with others.

**Q5.** What is maturity? Describe physical maturity and social maturity.
**Answer:** Maturity is one's readiness to share adult experiences, privileges and responsibilities.
Physical maturity is gained on obtaining full height, weight and strength. It is the easiest aspect to measure; nutrition and exercise help but cannot altogether change the pattern of growth.
Social maturity is the ability to get along with people. It grows as our circle widens, is learnt gradually, and is a very difficult, irregular area of growth.
Our success and happiness depend largely on it.

**Q6.** Describe briefly the different aspects of maturity.
**Answer:** Chronological: age in birthdays; it cannot be hurried and decides legal rights.
Physical: full height, weight and strength.
Intellectual: independent, objective, unhurried thinking and judgement.
Emotional: control over feelings, giving a charming personality.
Social: the ability to get along with people.
Philosophical: values, goals, true friends and a mission in life.
Together these make all-round maturity.

**Q7.** What is growth? Explain the factors that control growth.
**Answer:** Growth is increase in the size of the body: height, weight and the size of its parts.
Genes, inherited from parents, set its limits.
Endocrine secretions (hormones from the pituitary, thyroid and sex glands) regulate its rate.
Food supplies the materials for building the body.
Exercise strengthens muscles and bones.
Rest, relaxation and sleep let the body repair and build tissues.

**Q8.** Why is adolescence called a period of transition? Describe the main changes that take place in it.
**Answer:** Adolescence (about 10–19 years) is the transition from childhood to adulthood: one moves from childhood dependence to sharing adult responsibilities.
Striking changes take place, beginning with puberty, when one becomes capable of reproduction.
There is a growth spurt in height and weight, hair grows in the armpits and pubic region, and pimples may appear.
Boys' voices crack and deepen, beards appear and shoulders broaden.
Girls' breasts develop, hips broaden and menstruation begins.

**Model answer:** Physical maturity is the easiest to measure; intellectual maturity is the most difficult. Emotional control is learnt slowly, not in a day or two.

## 13. Assertion–Reason Questions

**Directions:** Read the Assertion (A) and the Reason (R). Choose the correct option:
- (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- (b) Both A and R are true, but R is **not** the correct explanation of A.
- (c) A is true, but R is false.
- (d) A is false, but R is true.

**Q1.**
**A:** Chronological maturity cannot be hurried up or slowed down.
**R:** Chronological age is the number of birthdays one has celebrated.
**Answer:** (a)
**Explanation:** Age in years moves only with the calendar.

**Q2.**
**A:** Intellectual maturity is the easiest aspect of maturity to measure.
**R:** Intellectual maturity concerns thinking, judging and deciding.
**Answer:** (d)
**Explanation:** It is the most difficult to measure; R is true.

**Q3.**
**A:** Emotional maturity makes a person socially acceptable.
**R:** An emotionally mature person controls his feelings and is not carried away by impulses.
**Answer:** (a)
**Explanation:** Self-control makes a person pleasant to live and work with.

**Q4.**
**A:** Social maturity is a very difficult area of growth.
**R:** Social maturity is neither predictable nor regular.
**Answer:** (a)
**Explanation:** Guidebook Section D Q4.

**Q5.**
**A:** Proper nutrition can completely change the pattern of a person's physical growth.
**R:** Genes set the limits of physical growth.
**Answer:** (d)
**Explanation:** Nutrition helps but cannot altogether change the pattern; R is true.

**Q6.**
**A:** Striking changes take place in the body during adolescence.
**R:** Puberty, when one becomes capable of reproduction, occurs in adolescence.
**Answer:** (a)
**Explanation:** The changes of puberty are the striking changes of adolescence.

**Q7.**
**A:** A person who accepts his mistakes is intellectually more mature than one who blames others.

## 14. Case-Based Questions

*Read each passage and answer the four questions, 1 mark each (4 × 1 = 4).*

### Case 1 — Two classmates

> Ibapynshai is 15. She has grown as tall as her mother over the last two years, but she sulks and shouts whenever things do not go her way. Her classmate Daniel is shorter, but he stays calm, thinks before he speaks, and admits his mistakes when he is wrong.

**(i)** In which aspect is Ibapynshai mature?
**Answer:** Physical maturity.

**(ii)** Which aspect of maturity does she lack?
**Answer:** Emotional maturity.

**(iii)** Daniel admits his mistakes. Which aspect of maturity does this show?
**Answer:** Intellectual maturity.

**(iv)** What does the case show about maturity?
**Answer:** A person can be mature in one aspect and immature in another.

### Case 2 — Turning eighteen

> On her eighteenth birthday, Wanda registered as a voter and applied for a learner's licence. Her grandmother reminded her that she was now old enough to take charge of the land she had inherited, but also that "being eighteen is not the same as being grown up".

**(i)** Which aspect of maturity allowed Wanda to register as a voter?
**Answer:** Chronological maturity.

**(ii)** Name one more right linked to this aspect.
**Answer:** Getting married (or opening a bank account, inheriting property).

**(iii)** Can chronological maturity be hurried up?
**Answer:** No; it cannot be hurried up or slowed down.

**(iv)** What did the grandmother mean?
**Answer:** True maturity also needs intellectual, emotional, social and philosophical maturity, not just age.

### Case 3 — Growing up

> Between the ages of 12 and 15, Ricky grew 20 cm taller, his voice cracked, and pimples appeared on his face. The doctor said this was normal and depended on his genes, his hormones, a good diet, exercise and enough sleep.

**(i)** Name the stage Ricky is in.
**Answer:** Adolescence.

**(ii)** What is the sudden increase in his height called?
**Answer:** The growth spurt.

**(iii)** Which factor controlling growth comes from the parents?
**Answer:** Genes.

**(iv)** Name two other factors the doctor mentioned.
**Answer:** Endocrine secretions (hormones) and food (also exercise, rest and sleep).

### Case 4 — A new school

> When Pynskhem's family moved to Tura, she joined a new school where she knew no one. In a month she had made friends, joined the debate club and learnt a little Garo. Her teacher said, "Your success and happiness will depend on how you get along with people."

**(i)** What quality did Pynskhem show by settling in quickly?
**Answer:** Adaptability.

**(ii)** Which aspect of maturity is the ability to get along with people?
**Answer:** Social maturity.

**(iii)** How do our spheres of relationship change as we grow?
**Answer:** They keep on enlarging.

**(iv)** Why is social maturity called a difficult area of growth?
**Answer:** It is neither predictable nor regular and is not the same all the time.

## 15. Revision Notes

### Chapter at a glance
- Growth = increase in size; development = change in abilities. Human development: physical, intellectual, emotional, social.
- Growth controlled by genes, endocrine secretions, food, exercise, rest, relaxation and sleep; body parts grow at different rates; knowing the body helps keep good health.
- Stages: infancy → childhood → adolescence (10–19; striking changes; growth spurt; puberty) → adulthood.
- Maturity = readiness to share adult experiences, privileges and responsibilities. Six aspects + all-round.
- Chronological: birthdays; cannot be hurried; vote, property, driving licence, marriage, bank account.
- Physical: full height, weight, strength; easiest to measure; nutrition and exercise help but cannot altogether change the pattern.
- Intellectual: hardest to measure — words, figures, symbols; independent decisions; objectivity; accepting mistakes; no hasty judgements; reserve judgement; varies, continues while one keeps alert.
- Emotional: feelings and their expression; restraint; learnt slowly; attractive, charming personality; socially acceptable.
- Social: getting along with people; spheres enlarge; learnt gradually; very difficult area of growth; neither predictable nor regular; success, popularity and happiness depend on it. Adaptability.
- Philosophical: values, goals, true friends, mission; shaped by family, religion, community, great lives. All-round: equilibrium in present and future life.

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct idea |
|---|---|
| Physical maturity gives a charming personality | **Emotional** maturity does (sample Q1). |
| Physical maturity is hardest to measure | Physical is easiest; **intellectual** is hardest. |
| Social maturity is learnt living alone | It is learnt living among people. |
| Nutrition can completely change growth | It helps but cannot altogether change the pattern. |
| Equilibrium in life = philosophical maturity | It is **all-round** maturity. |
| Being 18 means being mature | That is only chronological maturity. |
