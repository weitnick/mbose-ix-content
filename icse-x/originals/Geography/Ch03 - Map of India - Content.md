## 1. Complete Lesson

### How Question 2 is set and marked

Question 2 carries **ten marks**, one for each item. Each mark is lost for a **wrong place**, a **missing label**, an **over-large shading** or the **wrong symbol**.

![Fig. 3.1 Marking conventions](figures/g_ch03_fig01_marking_conventions.png)

**Golden rules from the examiners' reports (2018–2025)**
1. **Follow the verb** — "shade and label" needs both; shading without a name loses the mark.
2. **Keep shading inside India** — the Gangetic plain must not spill into Nepal or Bangladesh; the Thar must not spread into Punjab or Uttar Pradesh.
3. **Use the right symbol** — triangle for a **peak**, a line of Vs for a **range**, >< for a **pass**, a dotted line or arrow for a **strait**.
4. **Rivers** — trace from **source to mouth** (tributaries up to the point where they join); write the name **on the river**, or point at it with an arrow; never write it between two rivers.
5. **Cities and ports** — a **small dot on the land side**; never extend it into the sea.
6. **Winds** — arrows drawn **over the sea**, pointing **towards the land** they bring rain to; the arrow head must face the right way.
7. **Use an atlas**, not only textbook sketches, and practise on blank outline maps.

### The frame: latitudes and longitudes

- **Mainland India** lies between about **8°4′N and 37°6′N** and **68°7′E and 97°25′E**.
- The **Tropic of Cancer (23½°N)** runs across the middle of the country through **eight states** — **Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura and Mizoram**. It is the **most important latitude** passing through India.
- The **Standard Meridian of India (82½°E or 82°30′E)** passes near **Mirzapur (Uttar Pradesh)**; Indian Standard Time is reckoned from it. It crosses **five states** — **Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Odisha and Andhra Pradesh**.
- **80°30′E** is a meridian **2° west of the Standard Meridian**; it meets the east coast in Andhra Pradesh, north of Chennai.

Draw a latitude or longitude **right across the map** and write its value at the end. The examiners warn against marking the **Tropic of Cancer** when a **longitude** is asked.

### Mountains, peaks and passes

| Feature | Where it is | How to mark |
|---|---|---|
| **Himalayas** | The great arc across the north, from Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh to Arunachal Pradesh | Line of Vs along the northern mountain belt; label |
| **Karakoram Range** | The **northernmost** range, in **Ladakh**, north of the Himalayas | Line of Vs (not a triangle — that is for a peak) |
| **Karakoram Pass** (≈ 35½°N, 78°E) | In the Karakoram, on Ladakh's border with China, **north of Leh** | >< ; do not confuse with Nathu La or draw it to the west |
| **Nathu La** (≈ 27½°N, 89°E) | On **Sikkim's** border with Tibet (China), **east of Gangtok** | >< ; not "north of Sikkim" or between Nepal and Sikkim |
| **Kanchenjunga** (≈ 27¾°N, 88°E; 8,586 m) | In **north-west Sikkim**, on the Nepal border — the **highest peak of the Himalayas in India** | ▲ ; not in Nepal, not at Nathu La |
| **Aravalli Range** | The **oldest fold mountains** of India, running **south-west to north-east across Rajasthan**, from Gujarat towards Delhi | Line of Vs |
| **Satpura Range** | Between the **Narmada (north)** and the **Tapi (south)**, across Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra | Draw **parallel** to the rivers without touching them |
| **Western Ghats (Sahyadri)** | A continuous wall **close to the west coast**, from the Tapi valley to the far south | Line of Vs **near the Arabian Sea** |
| **Eastern Ghats** | Broken hills **inland from the east coast**, from Odisha through Andhra Pradesh to Tamil Nadu | Line of Vs **well away from the Bay of Bengal** |
| **Nilgiris (Blue Mountains)** (≈ 11½°N, 76¾°E) | In **Tamil Nadu**, where the **Eastern and Western Ghats meet** | ▲ or a short line of Vs; label |
| **Garo Hills** | **Western Meghalaya**, south of the Brahmaputra valley | Short line of Vs |

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2024, Q2(ix))* Mark and name the highest peak of Himalayas in India.
**Solution:** **Kanchenjunga** — a small triangle in **north-west Sikkim** on the Nepal border, with its name. (The examiners noted that many candidates marked **K2** instead.)

### Plateaus, plains and the desert

| Feature | Where it is | How to mark |
|---|---|---|
| **Deccan Plateau** | The triangular plateau **south of the Satpura–Vindhya**, between the **Western and Eastern Ghats** | Lightly mark the Satpura and both Ghats, then shade between them |
| **Chota Nagpur Plateau** | The **north-east** of the peninsula, mainly **Jharkhand** (with edges in Chhattisgarh, Odisha, West Bengal), south of the Ganga plain | Shade and label |
| **Gangetic Plain** | From Haryana and Delhi **east through Uttar Pradesh and Bihar to West Bengal**, between the Himalayan foothills and the plateau | Shade **only inside India** |
| **Thar Desert** | **Western Rajasthan**, west of the Aravallis, up to the Pakistan border | Shade a limited area; do not spread into Punjab, Haryana or Gujarat |

### Rivers

| River | Course | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Jhelum** | Rises at **Verinag** in the Kashmir valley; flows north-west through **Srinagar** and the **Wular Lake**, then west | An Indus tributary — do not mix it up with the Chenab or Ravi |
| **Sutlej** | Enters India from Tibet at **Shipki La** (Himachal Pradesh); flows south-west through **Bhakra** into **Punjab**, joins the **Beas** at Harike | Not the Beas, not the Indus |
| **Ravi** | Rises in Himachal Pradesh; flows west and south-west through Punjab (Pathankot–Amritsar side), between the Beas and Chenab | "River B" in the 2027 specimen |
| **Chambal** | Rises in the **Malwa plateau** (Madhya Pradesh); flows north-east through **Kota** (Rajasthan) to join the **Yamuna** | Point an arrow at the river |
| **Kosi** — "Sorrow of Bihar" | Enters **north Bihar** from Nepal and joins the Ganga | Floods and shifting course |
| **Damodar** — "Sorrow of Bengal" | Rises in the **Chota Nagpur Plateau** (Jharkhand); flows east into **West Bengal** to join the **Hooghly** | Jharia coalfield lies in its valley |
| **Brahmaputra** | Enters **Arunachal Pradesh** from Tibet; flows west through the **Assam valley**; turns south into Bangladesh | Trace from Arunachal to the Bangladesh border |
| **Narmada** | Rises at **Amarkantak**; flows **west** through a rift valley between the **Vindhya** and **Satpura** into the **Gulf of Khambhat** | West-flowing |
| **Godavari** — "Ganga of the south" | Rises at **Trimbakeshwar** near **Nashik**; flows south-east across Maharashtra, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh to the **Bay of Bengal** | Longest peninsular river |
| **Krishna** | Rises near **Mahabaleshwar** (Maharashtra); flows east through Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh to the Bay of Bengal | Do not mark the Godavari or Tungabhadra |
| **Tungabhadra** | In **Karnataka**; joins the Krishna | A tributary of an important peninsular river |
| **Kaveri (Cauvery)** | Rises at **Talakaveri** in Kodagu (Karnataka); flows south-east through Karnataka and **Tamil Nadu** to the Bay of Bengal | Delta in Tamil Nadu |
| **Mahanadi** | Rises in **Chhattisgarh**; flows east through **Odisha** (Hirakud, Cuttack) to the Bay of Bengal | Delta north of Chilika Lake |

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2027 specimen, Q2(ii))* Identify on the outline map: (a) the river marked A, known as the Ganga of the south (b) the river marked B (c) the coalfield marked C (d) the riverine port city marked D.
**Solution:** (a) **Godavari**. (b) **Ravi**. (c) **Jharia**. (d) **Kolkata**. *(From the 2027 draft marking scheme's map.)*

### Water bodies

| Feature | Where it is | How to mark |
|---|---|---|
| **Wular Lake** | Jammu & Kashmir, **north-west of Srinagar**, on the **Jhelum** — India's largest **freshwater** lake (an **inland freshwater lake**) | Small shaded oval touching the northern bend of the Jhelum |
| **Chilika (Chilka) Lake** | **Odisha coast**, south-west of the Mahanadi delta — a **brackish lagoon** | Shade and label |
| **Gulf of Kutch** | **Gujarat**, the **northern** gulf, between **Kutch** and the **Kathiawar** peninsula | Shade the gulf — not the Gulf of Khambhat |
| **Gulf of Khambhat (Cambay)** | Gujarat, **south-east of Kathiawar**; the Narmada and Tapi flow into it | Shade and label |
| **Palk Strait** | Between **Tamil Nadu** and **northern Sri Lanka**, north of the Gulf of Mannar | A **dotted line or arrow**, not shading; do not extend into the Gulf of Mannar |
| **Andaman Sea** | **East of the Andaman and Nicobar Islands** | Shade the sea east of the islands — not the Bay of Bengal or the islands |
| **Arabian Sea / Bay of Bengal** | West / east of the peninsula | Label in the open sea |

| Coast | Extent | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Konkan** | **Daman to Goa** (Maharashtra, Goa) | Mumbai lies on it; examiners found many marked it too far south |

**Example 3.** *(ICSE 2017, Q2(g))* Shade and name the coastal plain that receives rainfall in October–November.
**Solution:** The **Coromandel coast** — shade the narrow plain along the **Tamil Nadu coast**, from the Krishna delta southwards, and name it.

### Winds

| Wind | Season | How to mark |
|---|---|---|
| **South-west monsoon — Arabian Sea branch** | June–September; rain on the **west coast** (Mumbai in July) | Arrows from the south-west over the Arabian Sea, pointing **north-east onto the west coast** |
| **South-west monsoon — Bay of Bengal branch** | June–September; rain in the north-east and the Ganga plain | Arrows over the Bay of Bengal pointing north / north-east towards Bengal and the north-east |
| **North-east (retreating) monsoon** | October–December; **winter rain in Tamil Nadu (Chennai)** | Arrows from the north-east over the **Bay of Bengal**, pointing **south-west onto the Coromandel coast** |
| **Western disturbances** | Winter; **rain in Punjab**, Haryana and snow in the hills | Arrows entering **from the west / north-west** over Jammu & Kashmir and Punjab |

![Fig. 3.6 The two branches of the south-west monsoon, the north-east (retreating) monsoon and the western disturbances](figures/g_ch03_fig06_winds_map.png)

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2025 specimen, Q2(v))* Mark with arrows and name the wind bringing rainfall to Punjab in winter.
**Solution:** **Western disturbances** — arrows entering India **from the west / north-west** over Jammu & Kashmir and Punjab, labelled.

### Summary of the chapter

- **Rules** — follow the verb; symbols (▲ peak, ^^^ range, >< pass, • city, arrows for winds); shade lightly inside India; label every item.
- **Lines** — Tropic of Cancer 23½°N (8 states); Standard Meridian 82½°E near Mirzapur (5 states); 80°30′E two degrees west of it.
- **Relief** — Himalayas, Karakoram (pass north of Leh), Nathu La (Sikkim–Tibet), Kanchenjunga (NW Sikkim), Aravalli, Satpura (between Narmada and Tapi), Western Ghats (near the coast), Eastern Ghats (inland), Nilgiris (Ghats meet), Garo Hills; Deccan, Chota Nagpur, Gangetic plain, Thar.
- **Rivers** — Jhelum, Sutlej, Ravi, Chambal, Kosi, Damodar, Brahmaputra, Narmada (west), Godavari, Krishna, Tungabhadra, Kaveri, Mahanadi.
- **Water** — Wular (freshwater), Chilika (lagoon), Gulf of Kutch (north) and Khambhat, Palk Strait, Andaman Sea.
- **Coasts** — Konkan, Kanara, Malabar; Northern Circars, Coromandel (Oct–Nov rain).
- **Winds** — SW monsoon branches, NE monsoon onto Tamil Nadu, western disturbances into Punjab.
- **Soils, population, places** — alluvial (plains, Assam), black (Deccan), red, laterite; dense (Ganga plain, Kerala), sparse (Thar, Ladakh, Arunachal); Delhi, Chandigarh, Srinagar, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Kochi, Visakhapatnam; Mumbai High, Digboi, Jharia, Singhbhum.

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — One mark, three ways to lose it
Wrong place, missing label, sloppy symbol. Most lost marks in Question 2 are not about knowledge but about **precision**.

### Section 2 — Use anchors
Fix every location against something already on the outline map — the coastline, the Tropic of Cancer, a river mouth, a neighbouring country. "Nathu La: on Sikkim's eastern edge"; "Satpura: between the two west-flowing rivers".

### Section 3 — Pairs that trap students
Kutch vs Khambhat; Konkan vs Kanara; Nathu La vs Karakoram Pass; Kanchenjunga vs K2; Krishna vs Godavari vs Tungabhadra; Sutlej vs Beas; Western vs Eastern Ghats; Tropic of Cancer vs a longitude.

### Section 4 — Practise, don't just read
Map work is a skill. Mark a blank outline map every week and check it against an atlas and against Figs. 3.2–3.9.

## 4. Vocabulary (Key Terms)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Outline map | A map showing only the boundary and coastline, for marking. |
| Peak | The highest point of a mountain. |
| Pass | A gap through a mountain range used as a route. |
| Range | A chain of mountains or hills. |
| Plateau | An elevated area of fairly level land. |
| Gulf | A large inlet of the sea into the land. |
| Strait | A narrow sea passage joining two larger seas. |
| Lagoon | A shallow coastal water body cut off by a sand bar. |
| Coastal plain | A low, flat strip of land along the sea. |
| Tributary | A river that joins a larger river. |
| Delta | The fan of deposits at a river's mouth. |
| Riverine port | A port on a river rather than on the open sea. |
| Offshore oil field | An oil field under the sea bed. |
| Western disturbances | Winter weather systems from the west that bring rain to north-west India. |
| Retreating (north-east) monsoon | Winds of October–December that bring rain to Tamil Nadu. |
| Tropic of Cancer | The latitude 23½°N. |
| Standard Meridian | 82½°E, used for Indian Standard Time. |
| Regur | Black cotton soil of the Deccan. |

### 5.2 Memory aids

- **West coast, north to south: "Kon–Kan–Mal"** — Konkan, Kanara, Malabar.
- **East coast: "Circars then Coromandel"** — Northern Circars (north), Coromandel (south).
- **"Kutch is on top"** — the Gulf of Kutch is north of Kathiawar; Khambhat is south-east of it.
- **"Satpura sits between N and T"** — Narmada and Tapi.
- **"Delhi on the Right"** — the right bank of the Yamuna.

### 5.3 Beyond the text (general knowledge, not needed for exam answers)

- Indian Standard Time is 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of Greenwich Mean Time, because 82½°E ÷ 15° per hour = 5½ hours.
- The sun rises in Arunachal Pradesh about two hours earlier than in western Gujarat — about 29° of longitude apart.
- The Survey of India publishes the official political map of India; the outline map in your exam follows it.

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 James Rennell (1742–1830)
Surveyor-General of Bengal who produced the first reasonably accurate map of the Indian subcontinent (1782), sometimes called the "father of Indian geography".

### 6.2 George Everest (1790–1866)
Surveyor-General of India (1830–1843) who completed the Great Arc of the Great Trigonometrical Survey, measuring the meridian from the far south to the Himalayan foothills; the world's highest peak was later named after him.

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Location explains geography** — rain on the Coromandel in winter, black soil on the lava plateau, coal in the Damodar valley.
- **Maps are a language of symbols** — triangles, arrows, dots and shading all have fixed meanings.
- **Precision matters** — a peak a few millimetres off on a map is many kilometres off on the ground.

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **Meghalaya:** the Garo Hills lie in the west of the state, the Khasi Hills (Shillong) in the centre and the Jaintia Hills in the east; the plateau's laterite soils and heavy rain belong together.
- **Travel:** Nathu La is a trade and pilgrimage route between Sikkim and Tibet.
- **News:** floods on the Kosi in Bihar and cyclones on the Coromandel and Odisha coasts make sense once you can place them on the map.

## 8. Exam-Oriented Questions

> **How this chapter is examined (ICSE pattern):** The ICSE Geography paper (H.C.G. Paper 2) is of **2 hours, 80 marks**. **Part I** (30 marks) is compulsory: **Question 2** (10 marks) is on the **outline map of India** — ten items of **1 mark**. Questions 4–12 of Part II may also mention places you must know (Mumbai High, Jharia, Digboi, the Coromandel coast).

### 8.1 Graded exercises — solutions

#### Exercise 3A — Relief, rivers and water

**Q1.** Between which two rivers would you mark the Satpura?
**Answer:** Between the Narmada (north) and the Tapi (south).

**Q2.** How should a pass be marked? Name a pass in Sikkim.
**Answer:** With >< across the range; Nathu La.

**Q3.** Which gulf lies north of the Kathiawar peninsula?
**Answer:** The Gulf of Kutch.

**Q4.** Name the river known as the Ganga of the south and give its source.
**Answer:** The Godavari; it rises at Trimbakeshwar near Nashik.

#### Exercise 3B — Climate, soils, people and places

**Q5.** Which way should the arrows of the north-east monsoon point?
**Answer:** From the north-east over the Bay of Bengal, south-west onto the Tamil Nadu (Coromandel) coast.

**Q6.** Where would you shade an area of black soil?
**Answer:** On the Deccan lava plateau — Maharashtra (also Gujarat and western Madhya Pradesh).

**Q7.** Name two sparsely populated areas north of the Tropic of Cancer.
**Answer:** Western Rajasthan (Thar) and Ladakh (also Arunachal Pradesh).

**Q8.** In which state is Singhbhum, and what is it known for?
**Answer:** Jharkhand; iron ore and copper.

## 12. Competency / Application Questions

**Q1. (The cyclone warning)** A cyclone moves from the Bay of Bengal towards the Odisha coast near the Chilika Lake. Which coastal plain and which river delta are at risk?
**Model answer:** The northern part of the east coast (Northern Circars and Odisha coast) and the **Mahanadi delta**, just north-east of the Chilika Lake.

**Q2. (Planning a trip)** A family drives from Delhi to Chandigarh and on to Srinagar. Name the river beside Delhi and the river at Srinagar.
**Model answer:** The **Yamuna** at Delhi (on its right bank) and the **Jhelum** at Srinagar.

**Q3. (Rain calendar)** A student in Chennai and a student in Mumbai compare their wettest months. Explain the difference with winds.
**Model answer:** Mumbai is wettest in **June–September** (SW monsoon, Arabian Sea branch); Chennai in **October–December** (north-east / retreating monsoon over the Bay of Bengal).

**Q4. (Where's the coal?)** A steel company wants coking coal and iron ore close together. Which two places in Jharkhand would you mark?
**Model answer:** **Jharia** (coal, Damodar valley) and **Singhbhum** (iron ore), both in Jharkhand.

**Q5. (Spot the error)** A map shows the Satpura north of the Narmada and the Gulf of Khambhat labelled "Gulf of Kutch". Correct both.
**Model answer:** The Satpura lies **south** of the Narmada, between it and the Tapi; the Gulf of Kutch is the gulf **north** of Kathiawar.

## 15. Revision Notes

### Chapter at a glance
- Rules — follow the verb; ▲ peak, ^^^ range, >< pass, • city, arrows for winds, dotted line for a strait; shade lightly inside India; label everything.
- Lines — Tropic of Cancer 23½°N (8 states); Standard Meridian 82½°E near Mirzapur (5 states); 80°30′E.
- Relief — Karakoram Range and Pass (Ladakh), Nathu La (Sikkim–Tibet), Kanchenjunga (NW Sikkim), Himalayas, Aravalli (Rajasthan), Satpura (Narmada–Tapi), Western Ghats (near coast), Eastern Ghats (inland), Nilgiris (Ghats meet), Garo Hills (Meghalaya); Deccan, Chota Nagpur, Gangetic plain, Thar.
- Rivers — Jhelum (Srinagar, Wular), Sutlej (Shipki La), Ravi, Chambal (to Yamuna), Kosi (Bihar), Damodar (Bengal), Brahmaputra, Narmada (west), Godavari (Nashik), Krishna (Mahabaleshwar), Tungabhadra, Kaveri, Mahanadi.
- Water and coasts — Wular, Chilika; Gulf of Kutch (north), Khambhat (south-east); Palk Strait; Andaman Sea; Konkan, Kanara, Malabar; Northern Circars, Coromandel.
- Winds — SW monsoon (Arabian Sea → Mumbai; Bay branch), NE monsoon (→ Chennai), western disturbances (→ Punjab).
- Soils and people — alluvial (plains, Assam), black (Maharashtra, Gujarat), red (Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Odisha), laterite (Meghalaya, Ghats); dense (Ganga plain, Kerala, West Bengal), sparse (Thar, Ladakh, Arunachal).
- Places — Delhi (right bank of Yamuna), Chandigarh, Srinagar, Mumbai, Kolkata (river port), Chennai, Kochi, Visakhapatnam; Mumbai High, Digboi, Jharia, Singhbhum (Jharkhand).

|---|---|

| Singhbhum in Odisha | It is in Jharkhand. |
