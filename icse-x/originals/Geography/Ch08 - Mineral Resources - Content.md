## 1. Complete Lesson

### What are minerals?

- A **mineral** is a **naturally occurring, inorganic substance** with a **definite chemical composition** and **physical properties** (colour, hardness, lustre, crystal form).
- **Rocks** are made of one or more minerals.
- An **ore** is a **rock or mineral from which a metal can be extracted profitably** — for example **haematite** is an ore of iron, **bauxite** is the ore of aluminium.
- Minerals are the **raw materials of industry** — iron and steel, aluminium, copper, cement, chemicals and electrical goods all depend on them. A country's **industrial development** depends greatly on its mineral wealth.

**Characteristics of mineral resources**
1. **Unevenly distributed** — some regions are rich, others poor.
2. **Exhaustible and non-renewable** — they took **millions of years** to form and **cannot be replaced** once used.
3. **Quality and quantity are inversely related** — high-grade ores are found in **smaller quantities** than low-grade ones.
4. They are found in **particular rock types** — metallic minerals mostly in **igneous and metamorphic rocks**; non-metallic and fuel minerals mostly in **sedimentary rocks**.

**How do minerals occur?**
- In **veins and lodes** — cracks in igneous and metamorphic rocks (copper, zinc, lead).
- In **beds or layers** — in sedimentary rocks (coal, limestone, gypsum).
- As **residual deposits** — left behind when other substances are washed away by weathering (**bauxite**).
- As **placer deposits** — in the sands of valleys and beaches (gold, tin).
- In **ocean water and the ocean floor** (salt, manganese nodules).

### Classification of minerals

| Class | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Metallic — ferrous** | Contain **iron** (or are used mainly with iron) | **Iron ore, manganese**, nickel, cobalt |
| **Metallic — non-ferrous** | Metals **without iron** | **Copper, bauxite (aluminium)**, lead, zinc, gold |
| **Non-metallic** | Do not yield metals | Limestone, mica, gypsum, dolomite |
| **Energy (fuel) minerals** | Give energy | Coal, petroleum, natural gas, uranium, thorium (Chapter 9) |

**Ferrous minerals** account for about three-fourths of the value of India's metallic mineral production and give a strong base to the **iron and steel** and **metallurgical** industries.

### India's mineral belts

Most of India's metallic minerals are found in the **old crystalline rocks of the peninsular plateau**; the Northern Plains of alluvium have hardly any.

| Belt | Area | Minerals |
|---|---|---|
| **North-eastern plateau (Chhota Nagpur)** | Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal | **Iron ore, coal, manganese, copper, bauxite, mica, limestone** — the "**mineral heartland**" of India |
| **South-western plateau** | Karnataka, Goa, Kerala | **Iron ore**, manganese, bauxite, monazite sands |
| **North-western region** | Rajasthan, Gujarat | **Copper**, zinc, lead, building stones, petroleum (Gujarat) |
| **Himalayan belt / others** | Scattered | Small deposits |

**Why are industries concentrated in the Chhota Nagpur plateau?** **Coal and iron ore** — both **bulky** and both needed for making **steel** — are found **close together**, along with manganese and limestone. So **iron and steel plants** grew up there: **Jamshedpur, Bokaro, Durgapur, Rourkela, Burnpur**.

### 1. Iron ore

**Importance** — Iron ore is the **basic mineral** and the **backbone of modern industry**. **Steel** made from it is used for almost everything — **from a needle to a spacecraft** — so iron ore is called a **versatile mineral**. A country's industrial strength is often measured by its **steel production**.

**Types of iron ore**

| Ore | Iron content | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Magnetite** | **60–72 %** (the highest) | **Black**, **magnetic**; the **best quality** ore |
| **Haematite** | **60–70 %** | **Reddish-brown** (red in colour); the **most important** industrial ore — most of India's iron ore is haematite |
| **Limonite** | 40–60 % | **Yellowish or brown**; contains water (hydrated) |
| **Siderite** | 40–48 % | **Brownish**; iron carbonate; many impurities, so seldom used |

![Fig. 8.2 The four iron ores and their iron content](figures/g_ch08_fig02_iron_ores.png)

**Distribution (leading states and mining areas)**

| State | Prominent mining areas |
|---|---|
| **Odisha** — the **largest producer** | **Mayurbhanj** district (**Gurumahishani, Badampahar, Sulaipat**); **Keonjhar** district (**Barbil, Joda, Banspani**); **Sundargarh** district (**Bonai**) |
| **Chhattisgarh** — second | **Bailadila** (Dantewada district — very high-grade haematite); **Dalli–Rajhara** (Balod district — supplies the **Bhilai** steel plant); Rowghat |
| **Karnataka** | **Ballari–Hospet–Sandur** area; **Chikkamagaluru** (Bababudan hills, **Kudremukh**) |
| **Jharkhand** | **West Singhbhum** — **Noamundi, Gua, Kiriburu–Meghahatuburu** |
| **Goa** | North Goa — **Pale, Sanquelim, Sirigao**; ore is exported through **Marmagao** port |

The **Odisha–Jharkhand belt** (Mayurbhanj, Keonjhar, Sundargarh and Singhbhum) and the **Durg–Bastar–Chandrapur belt** (Chhattisgarh–Maharashtra) are the two great iron-ore belts of India.

**Uses of iron ore**
1. To make **pig iron, cast iron and steel**.
2. **Steel** is used for **machinery, tools, railway lines, bridges, ships, motor vehicles, buildings** and **defence equipment**.
3. **Household goods** — utensils, furniture, cycles, almirahs.
4. **Exports** — India exports iron ore, mainly to **China, Japan and South Korea**, through **Paradip, Visakhapatnam, Marmagao** and New Mangalore.

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2024, Q8(ii))* (a) Name the state that is the largest producer of iron ore in India. (b) Name any one mining area of iron ore from the state mentioned by you.
**Solution:** (a) **Odisha**. (b) Any one: **Gurumahishani, Badampahar, Sulaipat** in Mayurbhanj district; **Bonai** in Sundargarh; **Keonjhar**.

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2025, Q8(iii)(a))* Give a reason: iron ore is called a versatile mineral.
**Solution:** It is used for making **steel**, which in turn is used for **varied purposes** — **from a needle to a spacecraft** everything is made with its help.

### 2. Manganese

**Properties** — a **hard, brittle, greyish metal**, usually found as **manganese ore** (pyrolusite, psilomelane) in **metamorphic rocks**.

**Uses**
1. **Iron and steel industry** — about **10 kg of manganese** is needed to make **one tonne of steel**. It is added as **ferro-manganese** to make steel **hard, tough and rust-proof (resistant to corrosion)** and to remove impurities (oxygen, sulphur).
2. **Dry-cell batteries**.
3. **Bleaching powder**, **insecticides**, **paints** and **chemicals** (potassium permanganate).
4. **Glass** (removes colour) and **pottery / ceramics**.

**Distribution (leading states and mining areas)**

| State | Prominent mining areas |
|---|---|
| **Madhya Pradesh** — the **largest producer** | **Balaghat** district (**Bharweli, Ukwa**); **Chhindwara** |
| **Maharashtra** | **Nagpur** district (**Kandri, Mansar, Ramtek**); **Bhandara** |
| **Odisha** | **Keonjhar, Sundargarh (Bonai)**, Koraput |
| **Karnataka** | **Shivamogga**, Ballari, Tumakuru |
| Andhra Pradesh | Srikakulam, Vizianagaram |

The **Balaghat–Nagpur–Bhandara belt** (Madhya Pradesh–Maharashtra) is the chief manganese belt of India.

**Example 3.** *(ICSE 2023, Q8(i))* (a) Name the state of India that leads in the production of manganese. (b) What is the importance of manganese for the iron and steel industry?
**Solution:** (a) **Madhya Pradesh** is the largest producer of manganese in the country. (b) It makes the steel **tough and rust-proof** / **increases the strength** of steel.

### 3. Copper

**Properties** — copper is a **reddish-brown, non-ferrous metal**; it is **malleable** (can be beaten into sheets), **ductile** (can be drawn into wires), **rust-proof** and an **excellent conductor of heat and electricity**.

**Uses**
1. **Electrical industry** — being a **good conductor of electricity**, it is used for **electric wires, cables, motors, generators, transformers**, telephone and electronic goods.
2. **Household utensils** — being malleable, ductile and rust-proof.
3. **Alloys** — copper is a vital base for alloys:
   - **Brass = copper + zinc**
   - **Bronze = copper + tin**
   - It adds **strength** to the alloy; also used in **German silver** and **coins**.
4. **Chemicals**, roofing, **plumbing pipes**, ornaments.

**Distribution (leading states and mining areas)**

| State | Prominent mining areas |
|---|---|
| **Madhya Pradesh** — the **largest producer** | **Malanjkhand** in **Balaghat** district — India's largest copper mine (open-cast) |
| **Rajasthan** | **Khetri–Singhana** belt in **Jhunjhunu** district; **Kho-Dariba** (Alwar); Udaipur |
| **Jharkhand** | **Singhbhum** — **Mosabani, Rakha, Ghatsila**, Surda |

India's copper production is **far less than its needs**; much copper is **imported**. Copper smelting is done at **Khetri** (Rajasthan), **Ghatsila** (Jharkhand) and private smelters such as **Dahej** (Gujarat).

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2026 specimen, Q8(iii))* (a) Why is copper an important household mineral? (b) Name two Indian states where copper is found. (c) How is the distribution of mineral deposits responsible for the concentration of industries in the Chhota Nagpur plateau?
**Solution:** (a) Being **malleable, ductile and rust-proof**, it is used to make **utensils**; being a **good conductor of electricity**, **copper wires** are commonly used. (b) **Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan**. (c) **Coal and iron ore**, both of which are **bulky** and useful for making **steel**, are found **in close vicinity**.

### 4. Bauxite

**What is bauxite?** **Bauxite** is the **ore of aluminium** — a **clay-like** rock, rich in **hydrated aluminium oxide**. It is a **residual deposit**, formed by the **weathering of aluminium-silicate rocks** (such as basalt) in a **hot, humid climate with alternate wet and dry seasons** — the same conditions that form **laterite**. So bauxite is found as caps on **plateaus and hill tops**.

**Aluminium** is extracted in two stages: bauxite is refined into **alumina**, and alumina is **smelted using huge amounts of electricity** into aluminium. So aluminium plants need **cheap, plentiful power**.

**Uses of aluminium** (the metal from bauxite)
- Aluminium is **light, strong, rust-resistant**, a **good conductor of heat and electricity** and **malleable**. So it is used for:
1. **Aircraft**, **ships**, **automobiles**, **railway coaches** (light but strong).
2. **Utensils** and **cooking ware**.
3. **Electric cables** — especially **overhead power lines**.
4. **Packaging** — foil, cans; **window frames**, doors, paints.
- Bauxite itself is also used in **cement**, **abrasives** and **refractory bricks**.

**Distribution (leading states and mining areas)**

| State | Prominent mining areas |
|---|---|
| **Odisha** — by far the **largest producer** | **Koraput** district — **Panchpatmali hills** (one of the largest deposits in the world, mined by NALCO); **Kalahandi, Rayagada**, Sundargarh, Sambalpur, Bolangir |
| **Gujarat** | **Jamnagar, Kutch, Junagadh, Bhavnagar**, Devbhumi Dwarka |
| **Jharkhand** | **Lohardaga, Gumla**, Palamu |
| **Chhattisgarh** | **Mainpat** plateau, **Amarkantak** plateau, Bastar |
| **Maharashtra** | **Kolhapur, Ratnagiri**, Raigad |
| Madhya Pradesh | **Katni, Jabalpur**, Amarkantak |

**Aluminium plants** — **Angul** (NALCO, Odisha), **Renukoot** (Hindalco, Uttar Pradesh — near the Rihand hydel power), **Korba** (BALCO, Chhattisgarh), **Jharsuguda** (Odisha).

**Example 5.** *(ICSE 2018, Q7(d))* (i) Name the metal obtained from bauxite. Give any one use of the metal mentioned by you. (ii) Which multi-purpose project provides power to both Punjab and Himachal Pradesh?
**Solution:** (i) **Aluminium** — it is used to make **utensils, aircraft**, parts of **automobiles** and **wires**. (ii) **Bhakra Nangal**.

### Limestone — a mineral of the older syllabus

**Limestone** is a **non-metallic, sedimentary** mineral (calcium carbonate). It is **not named in the 2027 syllabus**, but it appeared in older papers and is worth knowing because it links to two industries. **Rajasthan** has long been the **largest producer**, followed by **Madhya Pradesh** and **Andhra Pradesh**. Uses: the **main raw material of cement**; a **flux** in iron and steel blast furnaces (removes impurities); lime for **chemicals, paper, sugar refining and fertilisers**; building stone.

### Mining and the environment

Mining brings minerals, jobs and income, but it also causes problems:
1. **Deforestation** and loss of wildlife habitat.
2. **Land degradation** — pits, heaps of waste rock, abandoned mines.
3. **Air pollution** — dust from blasting and transport; **lung diseases** among miners.
4. **Water pollution** — mine waste and chemicals reach streams.
5. **Displacement** of tribal and village communities.

### Conservation of minerals

Since minerals are **non-renewable** and **exhaustible**, they must be used **wisely**:
1. **Recycling** of **scrap metals** — iron, aluminium and copper can be melted and reused many times.
2. **Using substitutes** — plastics, glass and other materials in place of scarce metals.
3. **Improved technology** to use **low-grade ores** and reduce wastage in mining and processing.
4. **Exporting finished goods** rather than raw ore, so that more value stays in the country.
5. **Planned, scientific mining**, with **restoration (reclamation) of mined land** and tree planting.
6. **Strategic stockpiling** of scarce minerals for the future.

### Summary of the chapter

- **Minerals** — natural inorganic substances with definite composition; **ores** yield metals profitably; minerals are **exhaustible, unevenly distributed**, quality and quantity inversely related.
- **Classes** — metallic ferrous (**iron ore, manganese**), metallic non-ferrous (**copper, bauxite**), non-metallic (limestone, mica), energy (coal, oil, gas, uranium).
- **Chhota Nagpur plateau** — "mineral heartland"; coal + iron ore close together → steel plants.
- **Iron ore** — backbone of industry, versatile; **magnetite** (best, 60–72 %, black, magnetic), **haematite** (red, most used), limonite, siderite; **Odisha** (Mayurbhanj — Gurumahishani, Badampahar, Sulaipat; Keonjhar; Bonai), **Chhattisgarh** (Bailadila, Dalli–Rajhara), Karnataka (Ballari, Kudremukh), Jharkhand (Noamundi), Goa.
- **Manganese** — 10 kg per tonne of steel; makes steel **tough, rust-proof**; batteries, bleaching powder, paints; **Madhya Pradesh** (Balaghat), **Maharashtra** (Nagpur, Bhandara), Odisha, Karnataka.
- **Copper** — conductor; malleable, ductile, rust-proof; wires, utensils; **brass (Cu + Zn), bronze (Cu + Sn)**; **Madhya Pradesh** (Malanjkhand), **Rajasthan** (Khetri), Jharkhand (Singhbhum); imported.
- **Bauxite** — ore of **aluminium**; residual deposit on plateaus; light metal for aircraft, utensils, cables, foil; **Odisha** (Koraput — Panchpatmali), **Gujarat** (Jamnagar, Kutch), Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra.
- **Conservation** — recycling, substitutes, better technology, value addition, reclamation.

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — Why steel needs a family of minerals
Steel needs **iron ore** for iron, **coking coal** to heat and reduce it, **limestone** as a flux and **manganese** to make it tough and rust-proof. Where these lie close together — the **Chhota Nagpur plateau** — steel plants grew.

### Section 2 — Ferrous and non-ferrous
**Ferrous** minerals (iron ore, manganese) go into **steel**. **Non-ferrous** metals (copper, aluminium) are valued for **special properties** — copper for **conducting electricity**, aluminium for **lightness**.

### Section 3 — Learning distribution the smart way
Pair each mineral with **one state and one district**: iron ore — Odisha, Mayurbhanj; manganese — Madhya Pradesh, Balaghat; copper — Madhya Pradesh, Malanjkhand (also in Balaghat); bauxite — Odisha, Koraput. Notice that **Balaghat** gives both manganese and copper.

### Section 4 — A resource that does not grow back
Minerals took millions of years to form and are being used up in decades. **Recycling** an aluminium can saves most of the energy needed to make new aluminium from bauxite.

## 4. Vocabulary (Key Terms)

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 Pramatha Nath Bose (1855–1934)
A geologist of the Geological Survey of India, P. N. Bose discovered the rich iron-ore deposits of **Gorumahisani** in Mayurbhanj (Odisha). He brought them to the attention of the Tatas, which helped in the choice of **Jamshedpur** for the Tata Iron and Steel Company (1907).

### 6.2 Jamsetji Nusserwanji Tata (1839–1904)
The industrialist who dreamt of an Indian steel plant. After his death, his sons set up TISCO at Sakchi (Jamshedpur), close to the iron ore of Mayurbhanj and the coal of Jharia — a perfect example of industry following minerals.

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Minerals build nations** — steel, aluminium and copper are the frame, skin and nerves of modern industry.
- **Location follows raw materials** — heavy industries grow where bulky minerals lie together.
- **Use with care** — minerals are exhaustible; recycling is mining without digging.

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **Your home:** steel in the almirah and gas cylinder, copper in the wires, aluminium in the pressure cooker, brass in the door handle.
- **Meghalaya:** limestone of the Khasi and Jaintia hills feeds cement plants; unscientific mining has also caused land and water damage — a lesson in planned mining.
- **Recycling drive:** collecting old cans and wires for recycling saves bauxite and copper ore.

### 8.1 Graded exercises — solutions

#### Exercise 8A — Minerals and ores

**Q1.** What is an ore?
**Answer:** A rock or mineral from which a metal can be extracted profitably, e.g. haematite (iron), bauxite (aluminium).

**Q2.** Distinguish between ferrous and non-ferrous minerals with one example each.
**Answer:** Ferrous minerals contain iron or are used with iron (iron ore, manganese); non-ferrous minerals contain no iron (copper, bauxite).

**Q3.** Why must minerals be conserved?
**Answer:** They are non-renewable and exhaustible — they took millions of years to form and cannot be replaced.

#### Exercise 8B — Iron ore and manganese

**Q4.** Name the four types of iron ore. Which has the highest iron content?
**Answer:** Magnetite, haematite, limonite, siderite; magnetite.

**Q5.** Name the largest iron-ore producing state and one mining area in it.
**Answer:** Odisha; Gurumahishani (Mayurbhanj) — also Badampahar, Sulaipat, Bonai, Keonjhar.

**Q6.** Why is manganese important to the iron and steel industry?
**Answer:** It makes steel hard, tough and rust-proof.

#### Exercise 8C — Copper and bauxite

**Q7.** Name two alloys of copper and their other metals.
**Answer:** Brass (copper + zinc); bronze (copper + tin).

**Q8.** Name the largest copper mine of India and its state.
**Answer:** Malanjkhand, Madhya Pradesh (Balaghat district).

### 8.3 More practice in the ICSE style

**Q1.** (2 marks) Give one difference between magnetite and haematite.
**Answer:** Magnetite is black and magnetic with the highest iron content (up to 72 %); haematite is reddish-brown, with 60–70 % iron, and is the most important ore mined in India.

**Q2.** (3 marks) Name the mineral associated with each mining area: (a) Bailadila (b) Malanjkhand (c) Panchpatmali.
**Answer:** (a) Iron ore. (b) Copper. (c) Bauxite.

**Q3.** (3 marks) Name the mineral associated with each: (a) Khetri (b) Bharweli in Balaghat (c) Noamundi.
**Answer:** (a) Copper. (b) Manganese. (c) Iron ore.

**Q4.** (2 marks) Why are aluminium plants located near sources of cheap electricity?
**Answer:** Smelting alumina into aluminium needs huge amounts of electricity; cheap power keeps costs low.

**Q5.** (3 marks) Give reasons: (a) bauxite is found on plateau tops (b) copper is used for electric wires (c) aluminium is used for aircraft.
**Answer:** (a) It is a residual deposit formed by weathering under alternate wet and dry seasons, like laterite. (b) It is an excellent conductor of electricity and ductile. (c) It is light, strong and rust-resistant.

**Q6.** (2 marks) Name two steel plants that use the iron ore of the Odisha–Jharkhand belt.
**Answer:** Jamshedpur and Rourkela (also Bokaro).

**Q7.** (3 marks) Name the two leading states of: (a) iron ore (b) manganese (c) bauxite.
**Answer:** (a) Odisha, Chhattisgarh. (b) Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra. (c) Odisha, Gujarat.

**Q8.** (2 marks) State two ways of conserving minerals.
**Answer:** Recycling scrap metals; using substitutes (also better technology, value addition).

**Q9.** (3 marks) Give one use each of: (a) haematite (b) bronze (c) bauxite other than aluminium.
**Answer:** (a) Making iron and steel. (b) Statues, medals, bells. (c) Cement / abrasives / refractory bricks.

**Q10.** (2 marks) Name the port through which Goa exports iron ore, and one buyer country.
**Answer:** Marmagao; Japan (also China, South Korea).

## 15. Revision Notes

### Chapter at a glance
- Mineral — natural inorganic substance; ore — yields metal profitably; exhaustible, uneven, quality ∝ 1/quantity.
- Ferrous: iron ore, manganese. Non-ferrous: copper, bauxite. Non-metallic: limestone, mica. Energy: coal, oil, gas, uranium.
- Chhota Nagpur — mineral heartland; coal + iron ore together → Jamshedpur, Bokaro, Durgapur, Rourkela.
- Iron ore — backbone, versatile; magnetite (60–72 %, black, magnetic), haematite (60–70 %, red, most used), limonite, siderite. Odisha (Mayurbhanj — Gurumahishani, Badampahar, Sulaipat; Keonjhar; Bonai), Chhattisgarh (Bailadila, Dalli–Rajhara), Karnataka (Ballari, Kudremukh), Jharkhand (Noamundi, Gua), Goa (Marmagao exports).
- Manganese — 10 kg/tonne steel; tough, rust-proof steel; batteries, bleaching powder. MP (Balaghat), Maharashtra (Nagpur, Bhandara), Odisha, Karnataka.
- Copper — conductor, malleable, ductile, rust-proof; wires, utensils; brass (Cu + Zn), bronze (Cu + Sn). MP (Malanjkhand), Rajasthan (Khetri), Jharkhand (Singhbhum). Imported.
- Bauxite — aluminium ore; residual, plateau tops; aircraft, utensils, cables, foil. Odisha (Koraput — Panchpatmali), Gujarat (Jamnagar, Kutch), Jharkhand (Lohardaga), Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra.
- Conservation — recycling, substitutes, technology, value addition, land reclamation.

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct idea |
|---|---|
| Listing coal types for "types of iron ore" | Iron ores: magnetite, haematite, limonite, siderite. |
| General uses of manganese when asked its importance for steel | Say it makes steel tough and rust-proof. |
| "Jharkhand" as the largest iron-ore state | Odisha is the largest. |
| Brass = copper + tin | Brass = copper + zinc; bronze = copper + tin. |
| Bauxite is a metal | Bauxite is the ore; aluminium is the metal. |
| Naming a district without the state | Give both — e.g. Malanjkhand, Madhya Pradesh. |
| "Magnetite has the highest calorific value" | Calorific value is for coal (anthracite); magnetite has the highest iron content. |
