## 1. Complete Lesson

### What is soil?

- **Soil** is the **thin upper layer of the earth's surface**, made of **loose, weathered rock particles** mixed with **humus** (decayed plant and animal matter), **air, water and living organisms**, in which plants grow.
- It takes **hundreds to thousands of years** to form a few centimetres of soil — so soil is a **precious, slowly renewable resource**.

**Components of soil**
1. **Mineral matter** — sand, silt and clay from weathered rock.
2. **Humus** — **organic matter** formed by the **decay of dead plants and animals**; it gives soil a **dark colour**, adds **nutrients** (especially nitrogen) and helps it **hold water**.
3. **Water** and **air** in the pore spaces.
4. **Organisms** — bacteria, fungi, earthworms, insects.

**Factors of soil formation**
1. **Parent rock** — decides colour, texture and minerals (e.g. basalt → black soil; granite → red soil).
2. **Climate** — temperature and rainfall decide the kind and speed of weathering (e.g. heavy rain → leaching → laterite).
3. **Relief** — soil is thin on steep slopes and thick on plains.
4. **Vegetation and organisms** — supply humus.
5. **Time** — mature soils need a long time to form.

**Soil profile** — a vertical section of soil shows layers called **horizons**: the **topsoil** (rich in humus), the **subsoil**, **weathered parent rock**, and the **bedrock**.

![Fig. 5.1 A soil profile, from the humus-rich topsoil down to the bedrock](figures/g_ch05_fig01_soil_profile.png)

**Some useful terms**
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **In-situ (residual) soil** | Soil formed **in the same place** as its parent rock, lying over it — e.g. **black, red and laterite** soils. |
| **Ex-situ (transported) soil** | Soil **carried away** from its parent rock by rivers, wind or glaciers and **deposited elsewhere** — e.g. **alluvial soil**. |
| **Texture** | The **feel and consistency** of soil — sandy (coarse), loamy (medium) or clayey (fine). |
| **Soil fertility** | The **ability of a soil to supply nutrients** for plant growth. |
| **Leaching** | The **washing down of soluble minerals** from the topsoil by heavy rain. |

### The major soils of India

#### 1. Alluvial soil

**Formation** — alluvial soil is a **transported (ex-situ) soil**, formed by the **deposition of silt** brought down by rivers — the **Indus, the Ganga and the Brahmaputra** and their tributaries in the north, and the peninsular rivers in their **deltas**.

**Distribution** — it is the **most widespread soil of India** (about **40 per cent** of the land):
- the **Northern Plains** — **Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Assam**;
- the **deltas** of the **Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri**, and the **coastal plains** (Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Gujarat).

**Khadar and Bhangar**
| Khadar (new alluvium) | Bhangar (old alluvium) |
|---|---|
| Found on the **floodplains** near the rivers | Found on **higher ground (river terraces)** away from the rivers |
| **Renewed every year** by floods | **Not flooded**, not renewed |
| **Fine-grained**, light in colour, **more fertile** | **Coarse**, darker, contains **lime nodules (kankar)**, **less fertile** |

![Fig. 5.2 Khadar on the floodplain and Bhangar on the higher terraces of a river valley](figures/g_ch05_fig02_khadar_bhangar.png)

**Characteristics**
- **Colour:** light grey to ash-grey (darker in older deposits).
- **Texture:** **varies** — **coarse and porous** in the upper courses and near the foothills, **finer and more compact** in the lower courses and **deltas** (the river drops coarse material first and fine silt last).
- **Minerals:** rich in **potash** and **lime**; poor in **nitrogen, phosphorus** and humus (except the khadar and deltas).
- **Properties:** **deep, friable (easily ploughed), moisture-retentive**, responds well to **fertilisers** and **irrigation** — the **most fertile** soil.

**Crops** — **rice, wheat, sugarcane, jute, pulses, oilseeds, cotton, tobacco, maize**. That is why the Ganga plain is called the **"food bowl of India"**.

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2025, Q5(i))* The Ganga Plain is known for its fertile soil and is often called the food bowl of India. (a) Name the soil found in large parts of the Ganga plain. (b) Give two properties of this soil which help it to produce a variety of crops.
**Solution:** (a) **Alluvial soil** (khadar / bhangar, new / old alluvium). (b) Any two: it is rich in **potash, lime, humus, iron, magnesium, calcium, aluminium**; it is **renewed / replenished** by fresh deposition; it is **friable / easily cultivable**; it is **moisture-retentive**; it is **fine-textured / loamy**; it **responds to fertilisers**; it is **fertile and deep**.

#### 2. Black soil (Regur / Black cotton soil)

**Formation** — black soil is formed by the **weathering of basaltic lava rocks** of the **Deccan Trap** (basic igneous rocks from ancient volcanic eruptions). It develops **at its place of origin, over the parent rock** — so it is an **in-situ (residual)** soil. Its dark parent rock gives it its name.

**Distribution** — the **Deccan lava plateau**: **Maharashtra**, **Gujarat** (Saurashtra), **western Madhya Pradesh**, **Telangana**, northern **Karnataka**, parts of **Andhra Pradesh** and **Tamil Nadu**.

**Characteristics**
- **Colour:** **black**, varying to **chestnut brown** (due to titanium-bearing iron minerals and humus).
- **Texture:** **clayey and fine-grained**; **sticky when wet**, **cracks when dry**.
- **Self-ploughing:** in the dry season the soil **shrinks and develops deep cracks**; air enters, and loose soil falls into the cracks, so the soil **turns over and is aerated by itself**.
- **Moisture-retentive:** being **clayey and fine**, it **holds water for a long time** — crops can grow without much irrigation.
- **Not leached:** being clayey and **impermeable**, its minerals are **not washed down**.
- **Minerals:** rich in **LIMCAP** — **lime, iron, magnesium, calcium (carbonates), alumina and potash**; poor in **nitrogen, phosphorus and organic matter**.

**Crops** — **cotton** (the best soil for cotton — hence "**black cotton soil**"), **sugarcane, jowar, wheat, groundnut, soyabean, tobacco, citrus fruits**.

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2024, Q5(i))* Why is black soil found suitable for the production of cotton? (Any two points)
**Solution:** Any two: it is **moisture-retentive**; it has a **self-ploughing capacity** (it is **aerated**); it is **rich in minerals** because of its **volcanic origin** — **carbonates of calcium and magnesium**, **LIMCAP**.

#### 3. Red soil

**Formation** — red soil is formed by the **weathering of ancient crystalline igneous and metamorphic rocks** such as **granite and gneiss**, in areas of **low to moderate rainfall**. It is an **in-situ** soil.

**Distribution** — the **eastern and southern Deccan**: **Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh**, eastern **Madhya Pradesh**, and the hills of **Meghalaya, Manipur, Nagaland and Mizoram**.

**Characteristics**
- **Colour:** **red** because of the **iron oxide** spread through it (yellowish where the iron is hydrated).
- **Texture:** **coarse, sandy to loamy, porous** and **loose** — it **cannot retain moisture**.
- **Minerals:** rich in **iron** and **potash**; poor in **nitrogen, phosphorus, lime and humus**.
- **Fertility:** generally **less fertile**, but it **becomes productive with irrigation and fertilisers**.
- **Dry farming:** because it is porous and does not hold water, it suits **dry farming** — crops that need little water.

**Crops** — **millets (ragi, bajra), pulses, groundnut, tobacco, potato**; with **irrigation**: **rice, cotton, sugarcane, wheat**.

**Example 3.** *(ICSE 2018, Q4(c))* Give one geographical reason for each of the following: (i) Red soil requires irrigation. (ii) Afforestation prevents soil from getting eroded. (iii) Laterite soil is red in colour.
**Solution:** (i) Red soil needs irrigation because it **does not retain moisture**, being **highly porous**. (ii) The **roots of the trees hold the soil together**. (iii) It is **rich in iron oxide**.

#### 4. Laterite soil

**Formation** — the word comes from the Latin *later*, "**brick**". Laterite soil forms in areas of **high temperature and heavy rainfall** with **alternate wet and dry seasons**. Heavy rain causes **intense leaching**: **silica (desilication)** and soluble minerals such as lime, potash and nitrogen are **washed down**, leaving the **oxides of iron and aluminium** in the topsoil. It is an **in-situ** soil.

**Distribution** — the **summits of the Western and Eastern Ghats**, the **Rajmahal hills**, and parts of **Kerala, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra (Konkan), Goa, Odisha, Jharkhand, West Bengal**, and the hills of **Assam and Meghalaya**.

**Characteristics**
- **Colour:** **red** (iron oxide), varying to brown and yellow.
- **Texture:** **coarse, porous**; it **hardens like brick when exposed** to the air and **dries** — so it is cut into **building bricks**.
- **Chemical nature:** **acidic**; **poor in nitrogen, lime, phosphorus, potash and organic matter**.
- **Fertility:** **infertile** because of **leaching**; it can be made productive by adding **manure, fertilisers and lime** and by **irrigation**.

**Crops** — **tea, coffee, rubber, cashew, coconut, tapioca** (plantation crops on the hill slopes).

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2024, Q5(iii))* With reference to Laterite soil: (a) How is it formed? (b) How can it be made productive? (c) Name one region where it is found.
**Solution:** (a) It is formed by **leaching** of essential minerals down the soil profile, leaving the **topsoil infertile** — under **high temperature, heavy rain and alternate wet and dry seasons** (weathering of lateritic rocks, **desilication**). (b) By using **fertilisers and irrigation**, and adding **lime**. (c) The **summits of the Western and Eastern Ghats** / the **Rajmahal hills** / parts of Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Maharashtra, West Bengal, Odisha, Jharkhand, Kerala, Assam, Meghalaya, Goa.

### The four soils compared

| Point | Alluvial | Black | Red | Laterite |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Formation | Deposited by rivers | Weathering of basalt (lava) | Weathering of granite and gneiss | Intense leaching under heavy rain and heat |
| In-situ / ex-situ | **Ex-situ** (transported) | In-situ | In-situ | In-situ |
| Main areas | Northern Plains, deltas, coasts | Deccan lava plateau — Maharashtra, Gujarat | Eastern and southern Deccan — TN, Karnataka, Odisha | Summits of the Ghats, Kerala, Meghalaya hills |
| Colour | Light grey | Black / chestnut | Red (iron oxide) | Red (iron oxide) |
| Texture | Varies — coarse to fine | Clayey, sticky, cracks | Coarse, porous | Coarse, porous, hardens |
| Rich in | Potash, lime | **LIMCAP** | Iron, potash | Iron, aluminium |
| Poor in | Nitrogen, phosphorus | Nitrogen, phosphorus, humus | Nitrogen, phosphorus, humus, lime | Nitrogen, lime, potash, phosphorus (leached) |
| Special | Most fertile, most widespread | Self-ploughing, moisture-retentive | Good for dry farming | Acidic, infertile, used as bricks |
| Crops | Rice, wheat, sugarcane, jute | **Cotton**, sugarcane, jowar | Millets, pulses, groundnut | Tea, **coffee**, rubber, cashew |

### Soil erosion

**Meaning** — **soil erosion** is the **removal or destruction of the fertile topsoil** by **running water, wind** or the **activities of human beings**.

**Agents of soil erosion**
1. **Running water** — the most important agent in India:
   - **Sheet erosion** — a **thin layer** of topsoil is removed over a **large area** by water flowing as a sheet over gentle slopes (common on the gentle slopes of the Deccan plateau and the Shiwalik foothills).
   - **Rill erosion** — small channels (rills) are cut into the slope.
   - **Gully erosion** — rills deepen into **gullies and ravines**, making land unfit for farming — e.g. the **ravines of the Chambal valley** (Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh).
2. **Wind** — in **arid and semi-arid areas** (Rajasthan, Gujarat), where there is **no vegetation** to hold the loose soil, strong winds blow it away.
3. **Sea waves** along the coasts, and **glaciers** in the mountains.

**Causes (human activities)**
1. **Deforestation** — removal of trees leaves the soil bare.
2. **Overgrazing** by cattle, sheep and goats.
3. **Faulty farming practices** — **shifting cultivation**, ploughing **up and down the slope**, leaving fields bare after harvest, excessive use of chemicals.
4. **Mining, quarrying, construction, urbanisation and industrialisation**.
5. Natural causes — **heavy and torrential rain**, **floods**, steep slopes.

**Effects** — loss of fertile topsoil and **lower crop yields**; **silting** of rivers and reservoirs, causing **floods**; spread of **ravines and deserts**.

**Example 5.** *(ICSE 2025, Q5(ii))* (a) What is meant by soil erosion? (b) Name any two agents of soil erosion.
**Solution:** (a) It is the **removal and/or destruction of the topsoil** due to **running water, wind or the activities of man** (removal of the fertile top layer / its detachment and transportation). (b) Any two: **running water**; **wind**; **man**; **sea waves**.

### Soil conservation

**Meaning** — **soil conservation** means **protecting the soil from erosion** and **maintaining its fertility**, so that it remains productive.

**Importance**
1. It **preserves the fertile topsoil**, so that the **productivity** of the land is retained and the **growing population can be fed**; if soil is not conserved, there may be **scarcity of food**.
2. Soil **takes a very long time to form**, so it must be used carefully.
3. Soil conservation also helps to conserve **forests** (soil is the medium of plant growth) and **groundwater** (vegetated soil lets rainwater soak in and recharge the water table).

**Methods of soil conservation**
| Method | How it works | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Afforestation** | Tree roots **hold the soil**; leaves break the force of rain | Everywhere, especially catchments and hill slopes |
| **Terrace farming** | **Steps (terraces)** cut on hill slopes **slow down running water** | **Hilly areas** — Himalayas, NE hills, Western Ghats |
| **Contour ploughing** | Ploughing **along the contours** (across the slope); the furrows **trap water** | Gentle slopes |
| **Contour bunding** | Small embankments along the contours | Slopes in the Deccan |
| **Strip cropping** | **Strips of grass** between strips of crops **check wind and water** | Arid and semi-arid areas |
| **Shelter belts / wind-breaks** | **Rows of trees at right angles to the wind** reduce its speed | **Arid areas** — Rajasthan, Gujarat |
| **Controlled grazing** | Prevents **overgrazing** | Grasslands |
| **Crop rotation, cover crops, mulching** | Keep the soil **covered** and fertile | Farmland |
| **Plugging gullies, check dams** | Stop gullies from growing | Ravines — Chambal |
| **Dams, embankments, deepening river beds** | Control **floods** that erode soil | **Flood-prone river valleys** |

![Fig. 5.3 Four methods of soil conservation](figures/g_ch05_fig03_conservation_methods.png)

**Example 6.** *(ICSE 2023, Q5(iv))* Give a geographical reason: (a) Terrace farming is an ideal soil conservation method for hilly areas. (b) Khadar soils are preferred over Bhangar soils. (c) The foothills of the Himalayas have been affected by soil erosion.
**Solution:** (a) Terrace farming prevents soil erosion by **slowing the speed of running water**, and thus contributes to soil conservation. (b) **Khadar** is **more fertile** than Bhangar — it is **new alluvium**, **fine-grained**, **replenished** every year and has **no calcium (kankar) nodules**. (c) Due to **large-scale deforestation** in the past and **heavy, torrential rain**.

### Summary of the chapter

- **Soil** — top layer of weathered rock + humus + air + water + organisms; forms very slowly. **Humus** = decayed plants and animals — adds fertility.
- **In-situ** (black, red, laterite) vs **ex-situ** (alluvial). **Texture** = feel/consistency. **Leaching** = washing down of minerals.
- **Alluvial** — river deposits; most widespread; khadar (new, fine, fertile, floodplain) vs bhangar (old, coarse, kankar); texture varies; potash, lime; rice, wheat, sugarcane, jute.
- **Black (regur)** — basalt of the Deccan Trap; Maharashtra, Gujarat; clayey, sticky, cracks — **self-ploughing**, moisture-retentive; **LIMCAP**; **cotton**.
- **Red** — granite/gneiss; TN, Karnataka, Odisha; **iron oxide** gives red; porous, coarse; **dry farming**; millets, pulses, groundnut; fertile with irrigation and fertilisers.
- **Laterite** — heavy rain + heat → **leaching / desilication**; summits of the Ghats; red, acidic, infertile, hardens (bricks); tea, **coffee**, rubber, cashew.
- **Erosion** — removal of topsoil; agents: running water (sheet, rill, gully — Chambal ravines), wind (arid areas); causes: deforestation, overgrazing, faulty farming, mining.
- **Conservation** — afforestation; **terrace farming** (hills); **contour ploughing**; **strip cropping, shelter belts** (arid); controlled grazing; check dams; dams and embankments (flood valleys).

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — One rock, one soil
Each in-situ soil inherits its character from its parent rock: basalt → black, clayey; granite → red, sandy. Alluvium is different — rivers bring it from far away.

### Section 2 — Why red?
**Red** and **laterite** soils are both red because of **iron oxide**. The difference: red soil forms in **low rainfall**, laterite in **heavy rainfall** with leaching.

### Section 3 — "Name the soil"
Self-ploughing, cotton, LIMCAP → **black**. Varies in texture, khadar, most widespread → **alluvial**. Dry farming, porous, iron oxide → **red**. Leaching, acidic, bricks, summits of the Ghats → **laterite**.

### Section 4 — Erosion and conservation
Match the method to the place: hills → terraces and contour ploughing; deserts → shelter belts and strip cropping; river valleys → dams, embankments, afforestation.

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Soil links rocks, climate and food** — the parent rock and the rain decide what a farmer can grow.
- **Soil is slow to make and quick to lose** — centuries of formation can be washed away in a season.
- **Right method for the right place** — terraces on hills, wind-breaks in deserts.

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **North-East India:** terrace farming and the bamboo-based farming of the hills reduce erosion on steep, rain-soaked slopes; jhum (shifting cultivation) with short cycles can increase erosion.
- **Kitchen gardens:** adding compost (humus) makes garden soil darker, richer and better at holding water.

### 8.1 Graded exercises — solutions

#### Exercise 5A — Soil basics

**Q1.** What is humus?
**Answer:** Dark organic matter formed by the decay of dead plants and animals; it makes soil fertile.

**Q2.** Distinguish between in-situ and ex-situ soil.
**Answer:** In-situ soil forms over its parent rock; ex-situ soil is transported and deposited elsewhere.

**Q3.** What is leaching?
**Answer:** The washing down of soluble minerals from the topsoil by heavy rain.

#### Exercise 5B — Soil types

**Q4.** Why is black soil called self-ploughing?
**Answer:** It develops deep cracks when dry, which let in air and turn the soil over naturally.

**Q5.** Why are red and laterite soils red?
**Answer:** Because of iron oxide.

**Q6.** Name the soil that varies in texture, and why.
**Answer:** Alluvial soil — rivers deposit coarse material upstream and fine silt downstream.

#### Exercise 5C — Erosion and conservation

**Q7.** Name two causes of soil erosion.
**Answer:** Deforestation; overgrazing (also faulty farming, mining).

**Q8.** Name a conservation method for arid areas.
**Answer:** Shelter belts (also strip cropping).

**Q9.** Why are terraces made on hill slopes?
**Answer:** To slow down running water and prevent the soil from being washed away.

**Answer:** Sheet — a thin layer removed over a wide area; rill — small channels cut into slopes; gully — deep channels and ravines, e.g. the Chambal valley.

**Q4.** (2 marks) How does overgrazing cause soil erosion?
**Answer:** Animals eat away the grass cover and their hooves loosen the soil, leaving it bare to wind and water.

**Q5.** (3 marks) Suggest one conservation method each for (a) Meghalaya hill slopes (b) western Rajasthan (c) the Chambal ravines.
**Answer:** (a) Terrace farming / contour ploughing. (b) Shelter belts / strip cropping. (c) Plugging gullies with check dams and afforestation.

## 12. Competency / Application Questions

**Q1. (Two farmers)** Farmer A's field is beside the Ganga and floods every few years; Farmer B's is on higher ground with kankar. Whose soil is more fertile, and why?
**Model answer:** Farmer A's — it is khadar, renewed with fine silt by floods; B has bhangar, older and coarser.

**Q2. (Choosing crops)** A farmer in Maharashtra's black-soil area wants a cash crop. Suggest one and explain.
**Model answer:** Cotton — black soil holds moisture and is rich in lime and potash, which cotton needs.

**Q3. (A hill village)** A village on steep slopes in Mizoram loses topsoil every monsoon. Suggest two methods.
**Model answer:** Terrace farming and planting trees/bamboo on the slopes to bind the soil.

**Q4. (A desert village)** Sand blows onto fields in western Rajasthan. What should the village plant, and how?
**Model answer:** Shelter belts — rows of trees at right angles to the wind.

**Q5. (School garden)** Your school garden soil is pale and dries fast. What would you add and why?
**Model answer:** Compost or manure (humus) — it adds nutrients and helps the soil hold water.

## 13. Assertion–Reason Questions

**Directions:** Read the Assertion (A) and the Reason (R). Choose the correct option:
- (a) A is true, but R is false.
- (b) A is false, but R is true.
- (c) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- (d) Both A and R are true, but R is **not** the correct explanation of A.

**Q1.**
**A:** Black soil is called black cotton soil.
**R:** It is the most suitable soil for growing cotton.
**Answer:** (c)
**Explanation:** Moisture and minerals suit cotton.

**Q2.**
**A:** Alluvial soil is an in-situ soil.
**R:** Alluvial soil is deposited by rivers.
**Answer:** (b)
**Explanation:** It is ex-situ; R is true.

**Q3.**
**A:** Red soil is suitable for dry farming.
**R:** Red soil is porous and does not retain moisture.
**Answer:** (c)
**Explanation:** It suits crops needing little water.

**Q4.**
**A:** Laterite soil is used for making bricks.
**R:** Laterite soil is found on the summits of the Ghats.
**Answer:** (d)
**Explanation:** Both true; R does not explain A.

**Q5.**
**A:** Shelter belts reduce soil erosion in deserts.
**R:** Trees reduce the speed of the wind.
**Answer:** (c)
**Explanation:** Less wind, less erosion.

**Q6.**
**A:** Bhangar is more fertile than khadar.
**R:** Bhangar contains kankar nodules.
**Answer:** (b)
**Explanation:** Khadar is more fertile; R is true.

**Q7.**
**A:** Laterite soil is acidic.
**R:** Lime and other bases are leached away.
**Answer:** (c)
**Explanation:** Loss of bases makes it acidic.

**Q8.**
**A:** Overgrazing causes soil erosion.
**R:** Grass cover protects soil from wind and water.
**Answer:** (c)
**Explanation:** Removing grass exposes soil.

## 14. Case-Based Questions

*Read each situation and answer the four questions, 1 mark each (4 × 1 = 4).*

### Case 1 — The Deccan cotton belt

> A farmer in Maharashtra notices that her dark, sticky soil develops deep cracks in summer and holds water well after the rains.

**(i)** Name the soil.
**Answer:** Black soil (regur).

**(ii)** From which rock did it form?
**Answer:** Basaltic lava (Deccan Trap).

**(iii)** What property do the cracks give it?
**Answer:** Self-ploughing (natural aeration).

**(iv)** Name its most important crop.
**Answer:** Cotton.

### Case 2 — A hill-top soil

> On the summit of the Western Ghats, a road-builder cuts a reddish soil into blocks that harden in the sun. Farmers there grow cashew and rubber.

**(i)** Name the soil.
**Answer:** Laterite.

**(ii)** Which process formed it?
**Answer:** Intense leaching under heavy rain and heat.

**(iii)** Why is it red?
**Answer:** Iron oxide.

**(iv)** How can its fertility be improved?
**Answer:** Adding manure, fertilisers and lime, with irrigation.

### Case 3 — Ravines

> A survey of the Chambal valley shows deep channels cutting through farmland after heavy rains.

**(i)** Name this type of erosion.
**Answer:** Gully erosion.

**(ii)** Name the agent.
**Answer:** Running water.

**(iii)** Name one human cause that worsens it.
**Answer:** Deforestation (also overgrazing).

**(iv)** Suggest one remedy.
**Answer:** Plugging gullies with check dams and afforestation.

## 15. Revision Notes

### Chapter at a glance
- Soil = weathered rock + humus + air + water + organisms; forms slowly. Humus — decayed matter, fertility.
- In-situ: black, red, laterite; ex-situ: alluvial. Texture = feel; leaching = washing down.
- Alluvial: rivers; most widespread; Northern Plains, deltas; khadar (new, fine, fertile) vs bhangar (old, coarse, kankar); texture varies; potash, lime; rice, wheat, sugarcane, jute.
- Black: basalt (Deccan Trap); Maharashtra, Gujarat, MP; clayey, cracks, self-ploughing, moisture-retentive, not leached; LIMCAP; cotton.
- Red: granite, gneiss; TN, Karnataka, Odisha, Jharkhand, NE hills; iron oxide; porous; dry farming; millets, pulses, groundnut; fertile with irrigation + fertilisers.
- Laterite: heat + heavy rain → leaching, desilication; Ghat summits, Rajmahal, Kerala, Meghalaya; red, acidic, infertile, bricks; tea, coffee, rubber, cashew.
- Erosion: removal of topsoil; running water (sheet, rill, gully — Chambal), wind (arid); deforestation, overgrazing, faulty farming, mining.
- Conservation: afforestation; terraces and contour ploughing (hills); shelter belts and strip cropping (arid); controlled grazing; check dams; dams and embankments (floods).

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct idea |
|---|---|
| Black soil is transported | It is in-situ, over basalt. |
| Laterite is fertile because it is red | It is infertile because of leaching. |
| Khadar is old alluvium | Khadar is new; bhangar is old. |
| Red soil holds water well | Red soil is porous and holds little water. |
| Shelter belts parallel to the wind | They are planted at right angles to the wind. |
| Writing only "LIMCAP" | Expand at least two minerals (lime, iron). |
