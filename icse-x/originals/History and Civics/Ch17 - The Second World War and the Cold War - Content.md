## 1. Complete Lesson

### Introduction

- The **Second World War** (**1939–1945**) was the most destructive war in history. About **5 crore (50 million)** people — soldiers and civilians — were killed; cities were flattened; millions became refugees.
- It was fought between the **Axis Powers** — **Germany, Italy and Japan** (the **Berlin–Rome–Tokyo Axis**) — and the **Allied Powers** — **Britain, France, the USSR** (from 1941), the **USA** (from December 1941), **China** and many others.

### Causes of the Second World War

#### 1. Dissatisfaction with the Treaty of Versailles

- The Treaty of Versailles (1919) **humiliated Germany**: it was declared guilty of the war, burdened with a **war indemnity of 33 billion dollars** that could never be paid, made to cede large territories to **France, Belgium, Poland and Denmark**, lost all its **colonies**, and was **divided into two parts** to give Poland a corridor to the sea; **Danzig** was internationalised. Germany was **crippled**.
- This humiliation created a **spirit of revenge**; Germany looked for an opportunity to **do away with the Treaty**, which was not possible without **armaments and aggression**. War became inevitable.
- **Italy** felt it had **won the war but lost the peace** — the Allies deserted it and it received no valuable territory.

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2020, Q10(a))* Explain briefly three reasons for the dissatisfaction with the Treaty of Versailles.
**Solution:** Any three: it demanded the annexation of German territories and the creation of many states; the **Germans felt humiliated and helpless**; it **sowed the seeds of bitterness** and conflict; all **German colonies were forcibly taken away** and Germany was **divided into two parts** for the benefit of Poland; Germany was burdened with a **huge war indemnity** (33 billion dollars) which could never be paid; this humiliation gave rise to the **spirit of revenge**, and Germany looked for an opportunity to do away with the Treaty; it had to **cede large territories** to France, Belgium, Poland and Denmark; **Italians felt they won the war but lost the peace**; Germany was **crippled**; **Danzig was internationalised**.

#### 2. The rise of Fascism and Nazism

- The dictators believed in **aggressive nationalism**, **imperialism** and **war as an instrument** of policy (Chapter 16).
- **Italy** conquered **Abyssinia** (1935) and **Albania** (1939).
- **Germany** under Hitler **rearmed** (1935), **remilitarised the Rhineland** (1936), **annexed Austria** (the *Anschluss*, March 1938), took the **Sudetenland** (September 1938) and then the **rest of Czechoslovakia** (March 1939).
- The dictators drew together: the **Rome–Berlin Axis** (1936), the **Anti-Comintern Pact** (Germany and Japan, 1936; Italy joined 1937) and finally the **Berlin–Rome–Tokyo Axis** (1940).

#### 3. The Policy of Appeasement

- **Appeasement** meant **accepting the hostile demands of an aggressive nation** in order to **gain peace**.
- **Britain and France** (under **Neville Chamberlain** and Édouard Daladier) followed this policy towards **Germany and Italy** because:
  1. they felt the dictators had a **real grievance** due to the **humiliating terms of Versailles**, and that if their grievances were removed they would not disturb world peace;
  2. they wanted to **check the rise of communism** and limit the influence of **Russia** — a strong Germany would be a barrier against the USSR;
  3. they were **unprepared for war**, and their people did not want another war.
- So they **allowed Germany to rearm**, to **remilitarise the Rhineland** and to capture **Austria** and **Czechoslovakia**. At the **Munich Conference** (September 1938) Britain and France agreed to give the **Sudetenland** to Hitler; Chamberlain claimed he had won "peace for our time".
- Appeasement **encouraged the dictators** — each success made them bolder — and so it **led to war**.

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2027 specimen, Q9(ii))* Explain the Policy of Appeasement which led to the Second World War.
**Solution:** Appeasement meant **accepting the hostile demands of an aggressive nation to gain peace**. **Britain and France** followed this policy towards dictatorial countries like **Germany and Italy** because they felt that the dictators had a **real cause of grievance** due to the humiliating terms of the **Treaty of Versailles**, and that if their grievances were removed they would not disturb world peace. They also wanted to **check the rise of communism** and limit the influence of **Russia**. Therefore they **allowed Germany to rearm**, **remilitarise the Rhineland** and **capture Austria and Czechoslovakia**. This policy led to the war as it **encouraged dictatorship**. *(Any three points.)*

#### 4. The Japanese invasion of China

- **Japan**, a rapidly industrialising and militaristic power, needed **raw materials** (coal, iron), **markets** and **land for its growing population**, and wanted to **dominate the Far East**.
- In **September 1931** Japan **invaded Manchuria** (north-east China), **occupied it** and set up a puppet government (**Manchukuo**, 1932) — an **undeclared war** against China.
- China appealed to the **League of Nations**; a commission under **Lord Lytton** found Japan guilty, but **Britain and France paid no real attention** — no action was taken. **Japan left the League** (1933).
- In **1937** Japan began a full-scale war against China and started **occupying British and American properties** there. It joined **Germany and Italy** to form the **Berlin–Rome–Tokyo Axis**.
- The League's failure in Manchuria showed every aggressor that it could act **without punishment**.

**Example 3.** *(ICSE 2025, Q9(i))* [A passage on Japan's invasion of Manchuria and the League's Lytton Commission.] How did the incident being described above become a cause for the Second World War?
**Solution:** Any three: **Japan was determined to dominate the Far East**; it **invaded Manchuria**, **occupied it** and **set up a government there**, starting an **undeclared war against China**; **China appealed to the League**, but **Britain and France paid no attention**; **Japan left the League** and started **occupying British and American properties** in China; Japan **joined Germany and Italy** to form the **Berlin–Rome–Tokyo Axis** and **continued its policy of expansion and conquest**; France and Britain followed the **policy of appeasement** — they wanted Japan to be used to weaken China.

#### 5. The failure of the League of Nations

1. The **USA did not join** the League; **Germany** was at first not allowed to be a member and later left; **Russia** was outside it for many years. The great powers who joined were **not interested in collective security**.
2. It had **no army of its own** to enforce its decisions.
3. **Economic sanctions** against aggressors were useless, because member states would not apply sanctions that hurt their own economies.
4. In every crisis the aggressors **defied or ignored** the League — **Japan seized Manchuria**; **Italy attacked Abyssinia (Ethiopia)**; **Hitler annexed Austria** and **attacked Czechoslovakia**.
5. It was unable to **stop the rise of dictatorship** and **rearmament**.
6. Countries **lost faith** in the League and went back to **mutual military alliances**; Britain and France chose **appeasement**.
7. Finally, it **failed to maintain peace** and could not prevent the Second World War.

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2025, Q9(iii))* Mention any four reasons for the failure of the League of Nations.
**Solution:** Any four: the **USA did not join** the League, and the countries which joined were **not interested in collective security**; it was **unable to stop the rise of dictatorship**; it was helpless when **Japan seized Manchuria**, **Italy attacked Ethiopia** and **Hitler attacked Czechoslovakia and annexed Austria**; in every crisis the countries **either defied or ignored** the League; it **did not have an army of its own**; **economic sanctions** were of no use, as member states would not apply sanctions that affected their own economy; countries **lost faith** in the League and entered into **mutual political and military alliances**; the **policy of appeasement**; **Germany was not allowed** to be a member; **Russia was not part** of the League; it **failed to maintain peace** and could not stop the World War.

#### 6. The immediate cause — Hitler's invasion of Poland (1 September 1939)

- After the Treaty of Versailles, **Danzig** — a port inhabited mainly by **Germans** — had become a **free port**, and the **Polish Corridor** gave Poland a land route to the sea, **dividing Germany into two** (East Prussia was cut off).
- Hitler demanded **Danzig** and a road and railway through the Corridor. Poland, backed by a British–French guarantee, **refused**.
- On **23 August 1939** Germany signed a **Non-Aggression Pact** with the **USSR**, so that it would not have to fight on two fronts.
- On **1 September 1939** **Germany invaded Poland**. **Britain and France declared war on Germany on 3 September 1939** — the **Second World War had begun**.

**Reasons Hitler gave (and had) for attacking Poland**
1. To **recapture the port of Danzig**, which Germany had lost and which was inhabited mainly by Germans.
2. To **regain the territories** Germany had lost under the Treaty of Versailles.
3. Germany had been **divided into two** to give Poland a route to the sea; by occupying the **Danzig Corridor**, Germany could **connect with East Prussia**.
4. Poland was **accused of committing atrocities against the Germans** living there.
5. **Polish independence** after the First World War had always irked Germany.
6. The **Non-Aggression Pact with Russia** made the attack safe.

**Example 5.** *(ICSE 2024, Q1(xvi))* [A cartoon by Milton Rawson Halladay showing Hitler about to hurl a giant bomb marked "DANZIG".] Which incident depicted in the above cartoon led to the beginning of the Second World War? (a) Hitler's policy of Imperialism (b) Hitler's attack on Poland (c) Hitler's annexation of Austria (d) Hitler's attack on Czechoslovakia
**Solution:** **(b) Hitler's attack on Poland.**

### The course of the war (brief)

| Year | Main events |
|---|---|
| 1939 | Germany invades Poland (1 Sept); Britain and France declare war (3 Sept) |
| 1940 | Germany conquers Denmark, Norway, the Netherlands, Belgium and **France**; Italy joins Germany; Battle of Britain; the Axis treaty (Germany, Italy, Japan) |
| 1941 | Germany invades the **USSR** (June); **Japan attacks Pearl Harbor** (7 December); the **USA** enters the war |
| 1942–43 | Allied victories — Stalingrad (USSR), North Africa; **Italy surrenders** (1943) |
| 1944 | Allied landing in Normandy (**D-Day**, 6 June); France liberated |
| 1945 | **Germany surrenders** (May); **atom bombs on Hiroshima (6 August) and Nagasaki (9 August)**; **Japan surrenders** (August–September) |

#### The attack on Pearl Harbor (7 December 1941)
- **Pearl Harbor** in **Hawaii** was the main base of the **US Pacific Fleet**. On **7 December 1941**, without declaring war, **Japanese** aircraft made a surprise attack, sinking or damaging many battleships and killing over 2,000 Americans.
- The next day the **USA declared war on Japan**; Germany and Italy declared war on the USA. The war became truly **worldwide**, and America's huge resources tipped the balance in favour of the Allies.

#### The bombing of Hiroshima and Nagasaki (August 1945)
- To force **Japan** to surrender quickly, the **USA** dropped **atom bombs** on **Hiroshima** (**6 August 1945**) and **Nagasaki** (**9 August 1945**).
- The two cities were destroyed; over **two lakh** people died, many later from burns and radiation sickness.
- **Japan surrendered** (announced 15 August; signed 2 September 1945), ending the war. The world entered the **nuclear age**.

### The two rival blocs

| Axis Powers | Allied Powers |
|---|---|
| **Germany** | **Britain** |
| **Italy** | **France** |
| **Japan** | **USSR** (from 1941) |
| (with Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria) | **USA** (from December 1941), **China** and others |

**Example 6.** *(ICSE 2018, Q2(h))* Name the countries that formed the Axis Bloc during World War II.
**Solution:** **Germany, Italy and Japan** (the Berlin–Rome–Tokyo Axis).

### Consequences of the Second World War

1. **Defeat of the Axis Powers** — **Germany** was defeated and **divided into four zones** (occupied by the USA, Britain, France and the USSR); **Nazism came to an end**; **Italy** had surrendered; **Japan** lost all the territories it had seized and its **Emperor was reduced to a constitutional head**; **dictatorship fell**.
2. **Formation of the United Nations** (24 October 1945) — to maintain international peace and prevent another war (Chapter 18).
3. **The Cold War** — the world was divided into two **power blocs**: the **Democratic (Capitalist) bloc led by the USA** and the **Communist bloc led by the USSR**; the **USA and the USSR** became **superpowers**.
4. **Destruction of life and property** on an unprecedented scale.
5. **Weapons of mass destruction** — new weapons, especially **atomic bombs**, were invented and used.
6. **End of imperialism / decolonisation** — weakened Britain, France and the Netherlands gave up colonies; **India** (1947) and many Asian and African countries became free.
7. **Rise of communism** in **Eastern Europe** and in **China** (1949).

**Example 7.** *(ICSE 2023, Q9(ii))* State the three consequences of the Second World War.
**Solution:** The **Cold War**; the **defeat of the Axis Powers** (or the **division of Germany**); the **establishment of the UNO**.

### The Cold War

**Meaning**
- The **Cold War** was a state of **tension, hostility and rivalry** between the **USA-led Western (capitalist, democratic) bloc** and the **USSR-led Eastern (communist) bloc** from about **1945–47 to 1991**.
- It is called "cold" because the two superpowers **did not fight a direct war** with each other. Instead it was an **uneasy peace** marked by **propaganda**, an **arms race** (including nuclear weapons), **military alliances**, spying and support for opposite sides in local wars.
- It **ended in 1991** with the break-up of the Soviet Union.

**NATO** — the **North Atlantic Treaty Organisation** (1949) — a **military alliance** of the **USA, Canada and the countries of Western Europe** (Britain, France and others), under which an **attack on one member is treated as an attack on all**.

**The Warsaw Pact** (1955) — a **military alliance** of the **USSR and the communist countries of Eastern Europe** (Poland, East Germany, Czechoslovakia, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria, Albania), formed in reply to NATO. It was dissolved in 1991.

> India, newly independent, chose to join **neither bloc** — the policy of **non-alignment** (Chapter 19).

**Example 8.** *(ICSE 2025 Improvement, Q1(x))* From the given descriptions of the Cold War, choose the odd one out. (a) It was a consequence of the Second World War. (b) Uneasy peace between the two countries. (c) Division of the world into two power blocs. (d) Two countries joined hands to fight Britain and France.
**Solution:** **(d) Two countries joined hands to fight Britain and France.** The Cold War was a rivalry between the USA and the USSR, not an alliance against Britain and France.

### Summary of the chapter

- **War:** 1939–45; **Axis** — Germany, Italy, Japan; **Allies** — Britain, France, USSR, USA, China.
- **Causes:** Versailles (humiliation, revenge; Italy "lost the peace"); Fascism and Nazism (Abyssinia, Rhineland, Austria, Czechoslovakia; Axis); **Appeasement** (accepting aggressors' demands for peace; grievance, anti-communism, unpreparedness; Munich 1938); **Japan in China** (Manchuria 1931, Manchukuo; Lytton Commission; left League 1933; war 1937); **League's failure** (USA absent, no army, useless sanctions, ignored); **Poland** (Danzig, Corridor, East Prussia, alleged atrocities; Nazi–Soviet Pact; invasion **1 Sept 1939**; Britain and France declare war **3 Sept**).
- **Pearl Harbor** — Japan, **7 Dec 1941**; USA enters. **Hiroshima (6 Aug) and Nagasaki (9 Aug 1945)** — atom bombs by the USA; Japan surrenders.
- **Consequences:** defeat of the Axis (Germany in four zones; Nazism ended; Japan's emperor constitutional); **UN** (1945); **Cold War** (two blocs; USA and USSR superpowers); destruction; weapons of mass destruction; decolonisation; communism in Eastern Europe.
- **Cold War** — rivalry without direct war; **NATO** (1949, USA + Western Europe) vs **Warsaw Pact** (1955, USSR + Eastern Europe).

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — From Versailles to Munich
Versailles created grievance; the dictators exploited it; appeasement rewarded them. Learn the chain: humiliation → dictators → aggression → appeasement → more aggression.

### Section 2 — Asia
Japan's attack on Manchuria (1931) was the first big test of the League; its failure encouraged Italy and Germany.

### Section 3 — Poland
Danzig and the Corridor explain the immediate cause. Note the difference between Hitler's aim (Danzig and lost lands) and his excuses (alleged atrocities).

### Section 4 — Results
Three consequences in the syllabus: Axis defeat, UN, Cold War. Add destruction, atom bombs and decolonisation for extra points.

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 Neville Chamberlain (1869–1940)
British Prime Minister (1937–40) who led the policy of appeasement and signed the Munich Pact (1938).

### 6.2 Winston Churchill (1874–1965)
British Prime Minister from 1940, who led Britain through the war and opposed appeasement.

### 6.3 Franklin D. Roosevelt (1882–1945)
President of the USA, who led the country into the war after Pearl Harbor.

### 6.4 Joseph Stalin (1878–1953)
Leader of the USSR, who signed the Non-Aggression Pact with Hitler and then led the Soviet war against Germany.

### 6.5 Harry S. Truman (1884–1972)
President of the USA from April 1945, who ordered the atomic bombing of Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Appeasing aggression invites more** — every concession made the dictators bolder.
- **Collective security needs commitment** — the League failed because powerful members would not act.
- **The nuclear age** — Hiroshima and Nagasaki showed that a future world war could destroy humanity.

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **Hiroshima Day (6 August)** is observed worldwide for peace and nuclear disarmament.
- **The UN Security Council** — the five permanent members are the main Allied powers of 1945.

### 8.3 More practice in the ICSE style

**Q1.** (3 marks) Describe the attack on Pearl Harbor and its effect on the war.
**Answer:** On 7 December 1941 Japan made a surprise air attack on the US naval base at Pearl Harbor, Hawaii, sinking many ships; the USA declared war on Japan the next day, and Germany and Italy declared war on the USA; the war became worldwide and US power helped the Allies win.

**Q2.** (3 marks) Why did the USA drop atom bombs on Japan, and with what result?
**Answer:** To force Japan to surrender quickly without a costly invasion; Hiroshima (6 August) and Nagasaki (9 August 1945) were destroyed and over two lakh people died; Japan surrendered and the war ended.

**Q3.** (2 marks) What is the Warsaw Pact?
**Answer:** A military alliance (1955) of the USSR and the communist countries of Eastern Europe, formed in reply to NATO.

**Q4.** (4 marks) Explain how Germany was punished after the Second World War.
**Answer:** It was defeated and occupied; divided into four zones under the USA, Britain, France and the USSR; the Nazi Party was abolished and Nazi leaders were tried for war crimes; it lost territory in the east.

**Q5.** (3 marks) Why did the Munich Pact (1938) fail to keep peace?
**Answer:** It gave Hitler the Sudetenland without a fight; it convinced him that Britain and France would not resist; within six months he seized the rest of Czechoslovakia and then attacked Poland.

**Q5.** Why did Japan invade China?
**Answer:** It needed raw materials, markets and land.
It wanted to dominate the Far East.

**Q6.** Give two reasons why Hitler wanted Danzig.
**Answer:** It was inhabited mainly by Germans and had been lost at Versailles.
The Corridor divided Germany from East Prussia.

**Q7.** What is the Cold War?
**Answer:** Rivalry and tension between the USA-led and USSR-led blocs.
Without a direct war between them — an uneasy peace.

**Q8.** What is NATO?
**Answer:** The North Atlantic Treaty Organisation (1949).
A military alliance of the USA, Canada and Western European countries.

**Q9.** What is the Warsaw Pact?
**Answer:** A military alliance formed in 1955.
Of the USSR and the communist countries of Eastern Europe.

**Q10.** What happened at Pearl Harbor?
**Answer:** Japan made a surprise attack on the US naval base on 7 December 1941.
The USA entered the war.

**Q11.** State two reasons for the failure of the League of Nations.
**Answer:** The USA did not join it.
It had no army of its own (also: sanctions useless; aggressors ignored it).

**Q12.** State two consequences of the Second World War.
**Answer:** Defeat of the Axis Powers.
Formation of the United Nations (also: Cold War).

### Part II parts (3 marks each)

**Q13.** Explain the policy of appeasement.
**Answer:** Accepting an aggressor's demands to keep peace.
Britain and France felt Germany had grievances and wanted to check communism.
They allowed rearmament, the Rhineland, Austria and Czechoslovakia — encouraging war.

**Q14.** How did the Japanese invasion of China become a cause of the war?
**Answer:** Japan seized Manchuria (1931) and set up a puppet state.
The League failed to act; Japan left the League.
Japan joined the Axis and continued its conquests.

**Q15.** Mention three reasons for Hitler's attack on Poland.
**Answer:** To recapture Danzig.
To regain territories lost at Versailles; reconnect East Prussia.
Alleged atrocities against Germans; Non-Aggression Pact made it safe.

**Q16.** Explain three reasons for dissatisfaction with the Treaty of Versailles.
**Answer:** Germans felt humiliated by war guilt and huge reparations.
Loss of colonies and territories; Germany divided by the Corridor.
Spirit of revenge; Italy "lost the peace".

**Q17.** State three consequences of the Second World War.
**Answer:** Defeat of the Axis Powers; division of Germany.
Formation of the UNO.
Cold War between two power blocs.

**Q18.** Which country bombed Pearl Harbor? Mention two consequences of the war.
**Answer:** Japan.

Cold War (also: defeat of the Axis).

**Answer:** Axis — Germany, Italy, Japan.
Allies — Britain, France, the USSR.
USA joined the Allies later.

**Q20.** Explain the consequences of the war with reference to the Cold War.

Arms race and rival alliances — NATO and the Warsaw Pact.

**Q21.** Briefly mention the bombing of Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

**Q22.** State three reasons for the failure of the League of Nations.

Aggressors (Japan, Italy, Germany) ignored it.

## 14. Case-Based Questions

### Case 1 — Munich, September 1938

> Two prime ministers flew to meet a dictator and agreed to give him a German-speaking region of a neighbouring country, hoping this would satisfy him. Six months later he seized the rest of that country.

**Answer:** Appeasement.

**(ii)** Name the British prime minister.
**Answer:** Neville Chamberlain.

**(iii)** Name the region handed over.
**Answer:** The Sudetenland (Czechoslovakia).

**(iv)** Why did this policy lead to war?
**Answer:** It encouraged the dictator to make more demands and attack Poland.

> A dictator's army crossed into a neighbouring country to seize a port city with a German population and a strip of land that divided his country in two.

**(i)** Name the country invaded.
**Answer:** Poland.

**(ii)** Name the port city.
**Answer:** Danzig.

**(iii)** Which part of Germany had been cut off?
**Answer:** East Prussia.

**(iv)** Which two countries declared war two days later?
**Answer:** Britain and France.

> In August, two cities were destroyed by a new weapon. In October, a new world organisation was set up. Soon the world divided into two rival camps.

**(i)** Name the two cities.
**Answer:** Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

**(ii)** Which country dropped the weapon?
**Answer:** The USA.

**(iii)** Name the new world organisation.
**Answer:** The United Nations.

**(iv)** What is the rivalry between the two camps called?
**Answer:** The Cold War.

## 15. Revision Notes

### Chapter at a glance
- War 1939–45; Axis (Germany, Italy, Japan) vs Allies (Britain, France, USSR, USA, China).
- Causes: Versailles (humiliation; $33 billion; colonies lost; Corridor; revenge; Italy lost the peace); Fascism and Nazism (Abyssinia, Rhineland, Austria, Czechoslovakia; Axis); Appeasement (accept demands for peace; grievance; anti-communism; Munich 1938); Japan in China (Manchuria 1931; Manchukuo; Lytton; left League 1933; war 1937; Axis); League's failure (USA absent, no army, sanctions useless, ignored, appeasement); Poland (Danzig, Corridor, East Prussia, alleged atrocities; Nazi–Soviet Pact 23 Aug; invaded 1 Sept 1939; Britain and France 3 Sept).
- Pearl Harbor — Japan, 7 Dec 1941 → USA enters. Hiroshima 6 Aug, Nagasaki 9 Aug 1945 — USA's atom bombs → Japan surrenders.
- Consequences: Axis defeated (Germany four zones; Nazism ended; Japan's emperor constitutional); UN (24 Oct 1945); Cold War (USA vs USSR; two blocs); destruction; WMD; decolonisation; communism in East Europe.
- Cold War: rivalry without direct war; arms race; NATO (1949) vs Warsaw Pact (1955); ended 1991.

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct idea |
|---|---|
| Pearl Harbor was the immediate cause | The invasion of Poland (1939) was; Pearl Harbor (1941) brought the USA in. |
| Germany bombed Hiroshima | The USA did. |
| Austria was an Axis power | Axis = Germany, Italy, Japan. |
| League of Nations formed after WW II | It was formed after WW I; the UN after WW II. |
| Lord Lytton (Manchuria) = Lytton the Viceroy | A later Lord Lytton, a diplomat. |
| Cold War = a war fought in cold places | A rivalry without direct war. |
