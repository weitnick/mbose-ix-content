## 1. Complete Lesson

### The President — the head of the State

> - **Article 52:** *"There shall be a President of India."* **Article 53:** the **executive power of the Union** is vested in the President.
> - **Article 74:** there shall be a **Council of Ministers** with the **Prime Minister** at its head to **aid and advise** the President, who **shall act in accordance with such advice**.
> - So the President is the **nominal (constitutional) head** of the State; the **real executive** is the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers. All government orders are issued **in the President's name**, and the President is called the **first citizen** of India.

### Qualifications

A person is eligible for election as President if he or she:
1. is a **citizen of India**;
2. has completed **35 years** of age;
3. is **qualified for election as a member of the Lok Sabha**;
4. does **not hold an office of profit** under the Union or a State government (the offices of Vice-President, Governor and minister do not count).

> A candidate's nomination must be **proposed by 50** and **seconded by 50** members of the Electoral College, with a security deposit.

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2026 specimen, Q1(iii))* W, 34, is a professor in a government college; X, 35, has taken up citizenship of the USA; Y, 27, is a reputed sportsperson; Z, 36, is a scientist. Who fulfils the eligibility criteria to become President?
**Solution:** **Z.** W is under 35 and holds an office of profit; X is no longer an Indian citizen; Y is under 35.

### Election of the President

**The Electoral College** (Article 54) consists of:
- the **elected members of both Houses of Parliament**; and
- the **elected members of the Legislative Assemblies** of the States and of the Union Territories of **Delhi** and **Puducherry**.

> **Nominated members** of Parliament and of the Assemblies, and members of State **Legislative Councils**, do **not** vote.

**Method**

- The election is **indirect**, by the system of **proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote**, by **secret ballot**.
- The votes have different **values**, so that the States are represented in proportion to their population, and the MPs' votes as a whole equal the MLAs' votes as a whole (**uniformity and parity**).
- Disputes about the election are decided by the **Supreme Court**.

**Why is the President elected indirectly?**

1. A **directly elected** President would have a mandate from the whole people and could become a **rival centre of power** to the Prime Minister — against the spirit of the **parliamentary system**, in which the President is only a nominal head.
2. Election by MPs **and MLAs** makes the President the representative of the **whole nation, including the States**.
3. A nationwide direct election would cost huge **time, energy and money**, for an office with limited real power.
4. An indirect election keeps it a **quiet and dignified** affair, and the President less of a party nominee.

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2020, Q4(a))* How is the President of India elected? State the composition of the Electoral College that elects the President.
**Solution:** The President is elected **indirectly** by the members of an **Electoral College**, by **proportional representation** through the **single transferable vote**. The Electoral College consists of the **elected members of both Houses of Parliament** and the **elected members of the Legislative Assemblies** of the States, including the National Capital Territory of Delhi and the Union Territory of Puducherry.

### Term, vacancy and privileges

- The term is **five years** from the date the President enters office; a President may be **re-elected**.
- The President may **resign** by writing to the **Vice-President**.
- If the office falls vacant, the **Vice-President acts as President**, and a new election must be held **within six months**.
- The President's salary is charged on the **Consolidated Fund of India** and cannot be reduced during the term.
- The President is **not answerable to any court** for official acts, and no criminal case can be started against the President during the term.

### Impeachment — the removal of the President

The President can be removed **only by impeachment**, for **violation of the Constitution** (Article 61).

1. The charge may be brought in **either House** of Parliament.
2. A **written notice** of the resolution, signed by at least **one-quarter of the total members** of that House, must be given **14 days** in advance.
3. The resolution must be passed by a majority of **not less than two-thirds of the total membership** of that House.
4. The **other House investigates** the charge. The **President has the right to appear** and to be represented.
5. If the other House also passes the resolution by **two-thirds of its total membership**, the President is **removed** from the date it is passed. If either House does not pass it, the charge fails; there is **no joint sitting**.

> Both elected and **nominated** members of Parliament take part in impeachment, though nominated members cannot vote in the election. No President of India has ever been impeached.

**Example 3.** *(ICSE 2025, Q4(i))* Explain the impeachment process through which Parliament can remove the President.
**Solution:** The motion may be introduced in **either House**, with **14 days' written notice** signed by **one-fourth** of its members. It must be passed by a **two-thirds majority** of that House, which frames the charges. The **other House investigates**; the President or his representative can be present. If that House also passes it by **two-thirds**, the President is removed. If either House disagrees, the motion is dropped — there is no joint session.

### Powers of the President

#### 1. Executive powers

- All executive action of the Union is taken **in the President's name**; the President is the **head of the Union administration**.
- **Appoints the Prime Minister** and, on the PM's advice, the **other ministers**, and allocates portfolios.
- **Appoints** high officials: the **Chief Justice and judges** of the Supreme Court and the High Courts, the **Governors** of States, the **Attorney-General**, the **Comptroller and Auditor-General**, the **Chief Election Commissioner** and Election Commissioners, the **Chairman and members of the UPSC**, the **Finance Commission**, and **ambassadors**.
- **Administers the Union Territories** through administrators or Lieutenant-Governors.
- Is the **Supreme Commander** of the **Defence Forces**; declares **war and peace**, subject to Parliament.
- **Diplomatic powers:** treaties are made in the President's name; the President sends and receives **ambassadors**.
- Has the right to be **informed** by the Prime Minister of all decisions of the Council of Ministers (Article 78).

#### 2. Legislative powers

- **Summons** and **prorogues** the Houses; **dissolves** the Lok Sabha.
- **Addresses** Parliament at the **first session after each general election** and at the **beginning of the first session each year**, and may **send messages** to the Houses.
- **Nominates 12 members** to the Rajya Sabha.
- **Summons a joint sitting** in case of a deadlock.
- **Assent to Bills:** no Bill becomes law without it. The President may **give assent**, **withhold assent**, or **return** a Bill (other than a Money Bill) **once** for reconsideration.
- **Prior recommendation** is needed for introducing **Money Bills** and Bills to form new States or alter boundaries.
- Considers **State Bills reserved** by a Governor.
- **Promulgates Ordinances** (Article 123).
- Lays the reports of the **CAG**, the **UPSC** and the **Finance Commission** before Parliament.

**Ordinances**

> - An **Ordinance** is a **temporary law** issued by the **President** when **Parliament is not in session** and immediate action is needed.
> - It has the **same force as an Act** of Parliament.
> - It must be **laid before Parliament** when it reassembles, and it **ceases to operate six weeks after Parliament reassembles** unless approved earlier; it can also be withdrawn at any time. (So its longest life is about six months and six weeks.)
> - It is issued on the **advice of the Council of Ministers**, which prepares it.

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2024, Q2(vii))* "An Ordinance shall have the same force and effect as an Act of Parliament. But the government is required to bring an Ordinance before Parliament for ratification — and failure to do so will lead to its lapsing at the expiration of six weeks from the reassembly of Parliament." (The Indian Express, 25 May 2023.) Mention any two points about an Ordinance that can be deduced from this news.
**Solution:** An Ordinance has the **force of an Act**; it must be **presented in Parliament** and **ratified**; it **lapses** if not approved **within six weeks** of the reassembly of Parliament.

#### 3. Financial powers

- A **Money Bill** can be introduced only on the **President's recommendation**.
- The **Annual Financial Statement (Budget)** is laid before Parliament **on the President's behalf**; no demand for a grant can be made except on the President's recommendation.
- The **Contingency Fund of India** is at the President's disposal — advances can be made from it for **unforeseen expenditure**.
- The President appoints a **Finance Commission** every five years to recommend how taxes are shared between the Union and the States.

#### 4. Judicial powers

- **Appoints** the Chief Justice and the judges of the Supreme Court and the High Courts.
- **Power of pardon** (Article 72): can **pardon**, **reprieve** (postpone), **respite** (reduce because of special circumstances), **remit** (reduce the period) or **commute** (change to a lighter kind) the sentence of any person convicted of an offence — including a **death sentence** and a sentence by a **court-martial**.
- Can seek the **advice of the Supreme Court** on a question of law or fact of public importance (Article 143).
- Enjoys **immunity** from the courts for official acts.

#### 5. Emergency powers

| Emergency | Article | Grounds | Approval | Two effects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **National (General) Emergency** | 352 | **War, external aggression or armed rebellion** threatening the security of India or a part of it | Both Houses within **one month**, by a special majority; renewed every six months | Parliament can **make laws on State subjects** and the Union can **give directions** to the States; the **Lok Sabha's term** can be extended; Article 19 freedoms are **suspended** (in war or external aggression) and the right to approach courts for other rights may be suspended |
| **President's Rule (Emergency due to breakdown of constitutional machinery in a State)** | 356 | The government of a State **cannot be carried on** according to the Constitution — usually on the **Governor's report** | Parliament within **two months**; six months at a time, up to three years | The **State government is dismissed** and the **Assembly suspended or dissolved**; **Parliament makes laws** for the State and the **Governor** runs it for the President |
| **Financial Emergency** | 360 | A threat to the **financial stability or credit** of India | Parliament within **two months** | Salaries of all government servants, **including judges**, can be **reduced**; **Money Bills** of the States can be **reserved** for the President |

> National Emergencies were proclaimed in **1962** (war with China), **1971** (war with Pakistan) and **1975** ("internal disturbance"). After 1975, the **44th Amendment (1978)** replaced "internal disturbance" with **armed rebellion** and required the **written advice of the Cabinet**. A Financial Emergency has **never** been declared.

#### 6. Discretionary powers

The President normally acts on the advice of the Council of Ministers, but in some situations must use personal **judgement**:
1. **Appointing the Prime Minister** when **no party has a clear majority** (a "hung" Lok Sabha), or when the Prime Minister **dies suddenly** and there is no agreed successor.
2. **Dismissing** a Council of Ministers that has **lost the confidence** of the Lok Sabha but refuses to resign.
3. **Dissolving the Lok Sabha** when the government has lost its majority and no alternative government is possible — or exploring the possibility of an alternative government first.
4. **Returning a Bill** for reconsideration once; and, as no time limit is laid down, **delaying assent** to an Ordinary Bill (the "pocket veto").
5. **Seeking information** from the Prime Minister and asking the Council of Ministers to **reconsider** its advice once.

**Example 5.** *(ICSE 2024, Q1(vi))* During a hung assembly, when no party gets the majority, the President appoints the Prime Minister. What power is the President exercising?
**Solution:** **Discretionary** power.

### Position of the President

- The President is the **constitutional head**, like the British monarch: **"reigns but does not rule"**. Since the 42nd and 44th Amendments, the President **must act on the advice** of the Council of Ministers, though he or she may send it back **once** for reconsideration.
- Yet the President is **not a rubber stamp**: the office has **discretionary powers**, gives **advice and warning** to the government, is the **guardian of the Constitution** (by the oath to "preserve, protect and defend" it) and is the symbol of the nation's unity.

### The Vice-President

**Qualifications** (Article 66): a **citizen of India**; at least **35 years** of age; **qualified for election as a member of the Rajya Sabha**; holds **no office of profit**.

**Election:** by an **Electoral College** of **all members of both Houses of Parliament** — **elected and nominated** — by **proportional representation** through the **single transferable vote**, by secret ballot. (State MLAs do not vote.)

**Term:** **five years**; may be re-elected; may resign by writing to the **President**.

**Removal:** by a resolution of the **Rajya Sabha** passed by a **majority of all its then members** and **agreed to by the Lok Sabha**, after **14 days' notice**. No impeachment is needed, and no ground has to be given.

**Functions and powers**

1. **Ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** — presides over its meetings; **maintains order**; can **suspend** an unruly member and **adjourn** the House in grave disorder; decides the **admissibility** of questions and resolutions and the **order of speeches**; **expunges** unparliamentary words; **authenticates** Bills passed by the House; has a **casting vote** in a tie; decides **disqualification for defection** of its members; decides questions of **contempt** of the House.
2. **Acts as President** when the office falls vacant through the President's **death, resignation or removal**, until a new President is elected (within six months), and **discharges the President's functions** when the President is **absent, ill or otherwise unable** to act.
3. While acting as President, the Vice-President **does not act as Chairman** of the Rajya Sabha and receives the President's salary.

> If the offices of both the President and the Vice-President are vacant, the **Chief Justice of India** acts as President (as happened briefly in 1969).

**Example 6.** *(ICSE 2018, Q4(a))* State any three qualifications required for a candidate to be elected as the Vice-President of India.
**Solution:** The candidate must be a **citizen of India**; must be **not less than 35 years** of age; must be **qualified for election as a member of the Rajya Sabha**; must **not hold an office of profit** under the Union or a State government.

### Summary of the chapter

- **President:** nominal head; executive power vested in him/her; acts on the advice of the Council of Ministers (Art. 74).
- **Qualifications:** citizen, 35, qualified for the Lok Sabha, no office of profit.
- **Election:** indirect — elected MPs + elected MLAs (incl. Delhi, Puducherry); PR by single transferable vote. Indirect to avoid a rival power centre, to represent the States, to save cost.
- **Term** 5 years; **impeachment** for violation of the Constitution — 1/4 notice, 14 days, 2/3 of total membership in each House.
- **Powers:** executive (appointments), legislative (summon, dissolve, assent, Ordinances), financial (Money Bills, Budget, Contingency Fund, Finance Commission), judicial (pardon), emergency (Arts. 352, 356, 360), discretionary (PM in a hung House, dismissal, dissolution, return of Bills).
- **Vice-President:** citizen, 35, qualified for the Rajya Sabha; elected by all MPs; 5 years; removed by the Rajya Sabha with the Lok Sabha's agreement; Chairman of the Rajya Sabha; acts as President.

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — Election and removal
The President is chosen by the representatives of the people at both levels — Parliament and the State Assemblies — so the office belongs to the whole Union. Removal is deliberately difficult: a two-thirds vote of the total membership in both Houses.

### Section 2 — The powers
The President's powers look vast, but almost all are exercised on ministerial advice. In answers, name the category and give a concrete example: "Executive — appoints the Governors of States."

### Section 3 — Emergencies and discretion
Emergency powers turn the federation into a near-unitary state for a time; discretionary powers are the President's own judgement in political crises. Learn two effects of each emergency.

### Section 4 — The Vice-President
The Vice-President has two roles — Chairman of the Rajya Sabha and stand-in President. Questions ask for qualifications (note: qualified for the **Rajya Sabha**), election by all MPs, and the Chairman's functions.

## 4. Vocabulary (Key Terms)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nominal head | A head of State who has formal powers but acts on the advice of ministers. |
| Electoral College | The body of electors that elects the President (or the Vice-President). |
| Indirect election | Election by elected representatives rather than by the people directly. |
| Impeachment | A quasi-judicial process by which Parliament removes the President for violating the Constitution. |
| Ordinance | A temporary law issued by the President when Parliament is not in session. |
| Promulgate | To issue formally (an Ordinance). |
| Assent | Formal approval of a Bill, which makes it an Act. |
| Pardon | Complete forgiveness of a sentence. |
| Reprieve | Temporary postponement of a sentence, especially a death sentence. |
| Commutation | Changing a sentence to a lighter kind. |
| Remission | Reducing the period of a sentence without changing its kind. |
| Contingency Fund | A fund at the President's disposal for unforeseen expenditure. |
| Finance Commission | A body appointed every five years to recommend the sharing of taxes between the Union and the States. |
| National Emergency | An emergency due to war, external aggression or armed rebellion (Art. 352). |
| President's Rule | Central rule in a State whose constitutional machinery has failed (Art. 356). |
| Financial Emergency | An emergency due to a threat to India's financial stability (Art. 360). |
| Discretionary power | A power the President uses on personal judgement. |
| Hung Parliament | A Lok Sabha in which no party or alliance has a majority. |
| Ex officio | By virtue of office. |

## 5. Important Facts, Formulas and Results

### 5.1 Must-know facts

| Point | President | Vice-President |
|---|---|---|
| Age | 35 | 35 |
| Must be qualified for | Lok Sabha | Rajya Sabha |
| Elected by | Elected MPs + elected MLAs (incl. Delhi, Puducherry) | All MPs (elected and nominated) |
| Term | 5 years | 5 years |
| Resigns to | Vice-President | President |
| Removal | Impeachment (2/3 of total membership in each House) | Rajya Sabha (majority of all members) + Lok Sabha agreement |

- Ordinance: lapses **six weeks** after Parliament reassembles.
- Emergencies: Art. 352 (war, external aggression, armed rebellion; approval in 1 month); Art. 356 (State; approval in 2 months; max 3 years); Art. 360 (financial; never used).
- Vacancy in the President's office must be filled within **six months**.

### 5.2 Useful facts

- Dr Rajendra Prasad (1950–1962) is the only President elected twice.
- Pratibha Patil (2007) was the first woman President; Droupadi Murmu (2022) is the first President from a Scheduled Tribe.
- President's Rule was first imposed in Punjab in 1951.

### 5.3 Beyond the text (general knowledge, not needed for exam answers)

- Rashtrapati Bhavan in New Delhi, designed by Edwin Lutyens, is the President's official residence; the President also has retreats at Shimla and Hyderabad.
- The value of an MLA's vote in the presidential election depends on the population of the State according to the 1971 census.

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 Dr Rajendra Prasad (1884–1963)
A leader of the Champaran movement and President of the Constituent Assembly, he became the **first President of India** (1950–1962) and is the only one to have served **two full terms**.

### 6.2 Dr A. P. J. Abdul Kalam (1931–2015)
A missile scientist who led India's satellite-launch and missile programmes, he was the **11th President** (2002–2007). Known as the "People's President", he was especially loved by students.

### 6.3 Pratibha Patil (born 1934)
A lawyer and former Governor of Rajasthan, she was the **first woman President** of India (2007–2012).

### 6.4 Droupadi Murmu (born 1958)
A former teacher and Governor of Jharkhand from Odisha, she became the **15th President** in 2022 — the **first President from a Scheduled Tribe** and the second woman President.

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Power and responsibility go together** — the ministers who answer to Parliament hold the real power; the President, who answers to no House, acts on their advice.
- **Emergency is an exception** — the Constitution lets the Union take extraordinary powers, but only with Parliament's approval and for limited periods; the 44th Amendment added safeguards after 1975.
- **Discretion in crises** — in a hung House the President's judgement decides who gets the first chance to form a government.

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **Republic Day:** the President takes the salute at the parade and confers awards such as the Bharat Ratna and the Padma awards.
- **Mercy petitions:** news of a President deciding a mercy petition is the power of pardon at work.
- **Ordinances in the news:** when Parliament is not sitting, urgent laws are often made by Ordinance and later replaced by Acts.

### 8.1 Graded exercises — solutions

#### Exercise 3A — Election and removal

**Q1.** Who are excluded from the Electoral College that elects the President?
**Answer:** Nominated members of Parliament and of the Assemblies, and members of the State Legislative Councils.

**Q2.** To whom does the President address a letter of resignation?
**Answer:** The Vice-President.

**Q3.** On what ground can the President be impeached?
**Answer:** Violation of the Constitution.

#### Exercise 3B — Powers

**Q4.** Classify these powers: (a) appointing Governors (b) promulgating an Ordinance (c) pardoning a convict (d) making advances from the Contingency Fund.
**Answer:** (a) Executive (b) Legislative (c) Judicial (d) Financial.

**Q5.** When does an Ordinance cease to operate?
**Answer:** Six weeks after Parliament reassembles, unless approved earlier (or if withdrawn).

**Q6.** State two effects of President's Rule in a State.
**Answer:** The State government is dismissed and the Assembly suspended or dissolved; Parliament makes laws for the State.

#### Exercise 3C — The Vice-President

**Q7.** Who elects the Vice-President?
**Answer:** All the members (elected and nominated) of both Houses of Parliament.

**Q8.** How is the Vice-President removed?
**Answer:** By a resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by a majority of all its then members and agreed to by the Lok Sabha, after 14 days' notice.

**Q9.** For how long can the Vice-President act as President?
**Answer:** Until a new President is elected, which must be within six months of the vacancy.

### 8.3 More practice in the ICSE style

**Q1.** (3 marks) State three effects of a National Emergency.
**Answer:** Parliament can make laws on State List subjects and the Union can direct the States; the term of the Lok Sabha can be extended one year at a time; the Article 19 freedoms are suspended and the right to approach courts for other Fundamental Rights (except Articles 20 and 21) may be suspended.

**Q2.** (2 marks) Distinguish between a pardon and a commutation.
**Answer:** A pardon completely frees the convict from the sentence; a commutation changes the sentence to a lighter kind (for example, death to life imprisonment).

**Q3.** (4 marks) Compare the election of the President with that of the Vice-President.
**Answer:** The President is elected by the elected MPs and the elected MLAs; the Vice-President by all MPs, elected and nominated; both by proportional representation through the single transferable vote; MLAs vote only for the President; nominated MPs vote only for the Vice-President.

**Q4.** (2 marks) Why is the President called the nominal head?
**Answer:** All powers are exercised on the advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister (Article 74); the real power lies with the ministers, who are responsible to the Lok Sabha.

**Q5.** (3 marks) State two effects of a Financial Emergency and when it can be declared.
**Answer:** When the financial stability or credit of India is threatened; salaries of government servants, including judges, can be reduced; State Money Bills can be reserved for the President.

## 10. Short-Answer Questions (2 and 3 marks)

### Part I questions (2 marks each)

**Q1.** Why is the President called the nominal head of the State?
**Answer:** The President exercises powers on the advice of the Council of Ministers (Article 74).
Real power lies with the Prime Minister and the ministers responsible to the Lok Sabha.

**Q2.** State two reasons for the indirect election of the President.
**Answer:** To avoid a rival centre of power to the Prime Minister in a parliamentary system.
To make the President represent the whole nation including the States (and to save cost).

**Q3.** Who form the Electoral College for the presidential election?
**Answer:** The elected members of both Houses of Parliament.
The elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the States, Delhi and Puducherry.

**Q4.** What is an Ordinance? When does it cease to operate?
**Answer:** A temporary law issued by the President when Parliament is not in session.
Six weeks after Parliament reassembles, unless approved earlier.

**Q5.** Classify: (a) appointment of Cabinet ministers (b) approval of a Money Bill.
**Answer:** (a) Executive power.
(b) Financial power.

**Q6.** State two judicial powers of the President.
**Answer:** Power to pardon, reprieve, remit or commute sentences, including death sentences.
Appointment of judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts.

**Q7.** State two effects of President's Rule.
**Answer:** The State government is dismissed and the Assembly suspended or dissolved.
Parliament makes laws for the State and the Governor administers it for the President.

**Q8.** When can the President use discretion to appoint the Prime Minister?
**Answer:** When no party or alliance has a clear majority in the Lok Sabha.
When the Prime Minister dies suddenly and there is no agreed successor.

**Q9.** State two qualifications of the Vice-President.
**Answer:** A citizen of India, at least 35 years old.
Qualified for election to the Rajya Sabha; holds no office of profit.

**Q10.** How is the Vice-President elected?
**Answer:** By all members (elected and nominated) of both Houses of Parliament.
By proportional representation through the single transferable vote, by secret ballot.

**Q11.** State two functions of the Vice-President.
**Answer:** Acts as the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
Acts as President when the office falls vacant or the President is unable to act.

**Q12.** State two financial powers of the President.
**Answer:** Money Bills can be introduced only on the President's recommendation.
Advances can be made from the Contingency Fund of India (also: the Budget is laid on the President's behalf; appoints the Finance Commission).

### Part II parts (3 marks each)

**Q13.** Mention three qualifications for the office of President.
**Answer:** Must be a citizen of India.
Must have completed 35 years of age.
Must be qualified for election to the Lok Sabha and hold no office of profit.

**Q14.** How is the President elected?
**Answer:** Indirectly, by an Electoral College.
Of the elected MPs and the elected MLAs of the States, Delhi and Puducherry.
By proportional representation through the single transferable vote, with weighted votes.

**Q15.** Name and explain the three types of emergency.
**Answer:** National Emergency (Art. 352) — war, external aggression or armed rebellion.
President's Rule (Art. 356) — failure of constitutional machinery in a State.
Financial Emergency (Art. 360) — threat to India's financial stability.

**Q16.** State three executive powers of the President.
**Answer:** All executive action is taken in the President's name.
Appoints the Prime Minister and other ministers.
Appoints Governors, judges, the CAG, the Chief Election Commissioner and ambassadors.

**Q17.** State three legislative powers of the President.
**Answer:** Summons and prorogues Parliament and dissolves the Lok Sabha.
Gives assent to Bills or returns them once.
Promulgates Ordinances (also: nominates 12 Rajya Sabha members; addresses Parliament).

**Q18.** Explain three discretionary powers of the President.
**Answer:** Appointing a Prime Minister when no party has a majority.
Dismissing a ministry that has lost the confidence of the Lok Sabha.
Dissolving the Lok Sabha when no stable government is possible (also: returning a Bill; pocket veto).

**Q19.** State three powers of the Vice-President as Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
**Answer:** Presides over the House and maintains order.
Suspends unruly members and adjourns the House in grave disorder.
Decides the admissibility of questions; casting vote in a tie.

**Q20.** State three effects of a National Emergency.
**Answer:** Parliament can legislate on State subjects; the Union can direct the States.
The Lok Sabha's term can be extended one year at a time.
Some Fundamental Rights (Article 19) are suspended.

**Q21.** Explain the President's power of pardon.
**Answer:** The President can pardon (fully forgive) a convict.
Can reprieve (postpone), remit (reduce the period) or commute (change to a lighter kind) a sentence.
It extends to death sentences and to sentences by a court-martial.

**Q22.** How can the Vice-President be removed?
**Answer:** By a resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by a majority of all its then members.
Agreed to by the Lok Sabha.
After 14 days' notice; no ground need be stated.

## 11. Long-Answer Questions (4 marks each)

### Part II parts (4 marks each)

**Q1.** Describe the procedure for the impeachment of the President.
**Answer:** The charge (violation of the Constitution) is brought in either House by a resolution signed by one-quarter of its members, with 14 days' notice.
It must be passed by two-thirds of the total membership of that House.
The other House investigates; the President may appear and be represented.
If that House also passes it by two-thirds of its total membership, the President is removed.

**Q2.** Mention two legislative and two executive powers of the President.
**Answer:** Legislative: summons and prorogues Parliament; dissolves the Lok Sabha.
Legislative: assent to Bills; promulgates Ordinances.
Executive: appoints the Prime Minister and ministers.
Executive: appoints Governors, judges, the CAG and ambassadors.

**Q3.** Mention two financial and two judicial powers of the President.
**Answer:** Financial: recommends Money Bills; Budget laid on the President's behalf.
Financial: Contingency Fund; appoints the Finance Commission.
Judicial: pardon, reprieve, remission and commutation.
Judicial: appoints judges; seeks the Supreme Court's advice.

**Q4.** Give two reasons for the indirect election of the President and describe the Electoral College.
**Answer:** A directly elected President could rival the Prime Minister and weaken the parliamentary system.
The President represents the whole nation including the States; direct election would be costly.
The Electoral College has the elected members of both Houses of Parliament.
And the elected members of the State Assemblies and of Delhi and Puducherry.

**Q5.** Explain the three emergencies with one effect each.
**Answer:** National Emergency — Parliament can legislate on State subjects.
President's Rule — the State government is dismissed; Parliament legislates for the State.
Financial Emergency — salaries, including judges', can be reduced.
Each must be approved by Parliament (within one month for Art. 352; two months for Arts. 356 and 360).

**Q6.** Explain four discretionary powers of the President.
**Answer:** Appointing the Prime Minister in a hung Lok Sabha.
Dismissing a Council of Ministers that has lost its majority.
Dissolving the Lok Sabha or exploring an alternative government.
Returning a Bill once for reconsideration and delaying assent (pocket veto).

**Q7.** Describe the qualifications, election and term of the Vice-President.
**Answer:** Citizen; 35 years; qualified for the Rajya Sabha; no office of profit.
Elected by all members of both Houses of Parliament.
By proportional representation through the single transferable vote.
Term of five years; may be re-elected.

**Q8.** Explain the position of the President in the Indian Constitution.
**Answer:** A constitutional, nominal head — "reigns but does not rule".
Must act on the advice of the Council of Ministers (may ask for reconsideration once).
Has discretionary powers in political crises.
Guardian of the Constitution and symbol of the nation's unity.

## 12. Competency / Application Questions

**Q1. (The hung House)** After a general election no party has a majority. Two alliances each claim support. What should the President do?
**Model answer:** Use discretion: invite the leader who is most likely to command a majority, and ask that leader to prove it by a vote of confidence in the Lok Sabha within a fixed time.

**Q2. (An urgent law)** Parliament has gone into recess, and a sudden crisis needs a new law at once. How can the government act?
**Model answer:** Advise the President to promulgate an Ordinance; it must be approved within six weeks of Parliament's reassembly.

**Q3. (The mercy petition)** A prisoner sentenced to death appeals to the President. What options does the President have?
**Model answer:** Pardon, reprieve, respite, remission or commutation (for example, to life imprisonment) — acting on the advice of the Council of Ministers.

**Q4. (A State in crisis)** The Governor of a State reports that the government cannot be carried on according to the Constitution. What may follow?
**Model answer:** President's Rule under Article 356 — the State government is dismissed, the Assembly suspended or dissolved, and Parliament legislates for the State; it must be approved within two months.

**Q5. (Election day for the President)** Which of these persons will vote: a nominated Rajya Sabha member, an elected MLA of Delhi, an elected MP, an MLC of Karnataka?
**Model answer:** The elected MLA of Delhi and the elected MP only.

**Q6. (The empty chair)** The President dies in office. Who performs the President's duties, and for how long?
**Model answer:** The Vice-President, until a new President is elected — within six months.

## 15. Revision Notes

### Chapter at a glance
- President: head of the State; executive power vested (Art. 53); acts on ministers' advice (Art. 74); first citizen.
- Qualifications: citizen; 35; qualified for the Lok Sabha; no office of profit; 50 proposers + 50 seconders.
- Electoral College: elected MPs + elected MLAs (States, Delhi, Puducherry); PR–STV; weighted votes; disputes to the Supreme Court.
- Indirect election: no rival power centre; represents States; saves cost; nominal head.
- Term 5 years; resigns to VP; vacancy filled within 6 months.
- Impeachment: violation of the Constitution; either House; 1/4 sign; 14 days; 2/3 of total membership; other House investigates; 2/3 again.
- Powers: executive (appointments, Supreme Commander), legislative (summon, dissolve, assent, Ordinances — lapse 6 weeks after reassembly), financial (Money Bills, Budget, Contingency Fund, Finance Commission), judicial (pardon etc.), emergency (352, 356, 360), discretionary.
- Vice-President: citizen; 35; qualified for the Rajya Sabha; elected by all MPs; 5 years; removed by RS (majority of all members) + LS; Chairman of RS; acts as President.

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct idea |
|---|---|
| Nominated MPs vote for the President | Only elected MPs and MLAs vote; all MPs vote for the Vice-President. |
| The Vice-President must be qualified for the Lok Sabha | For the Rajya Sabha (the President — for the Lok Sabha). |
| The President prepares Ordinances | The Council of Ministers prepares them; the President promulgates them. |
| An Ordinance lasts six weeks from its issue | Six weeks from the reassembly of Parliament. |
| Impeachment is decided in a joint sitting | Each House votes separately; there is no joint sitting. |
| President's Rule is a "National Emergency" | It is a State (constitutional) emergency under Article 356. |
| The Vice-President is impeached | Removed by a Rajya Sabha resolution agreed to by the Lok Sabha. |
| Advances for unforeseen expenses come from the Consolidated Fund | From the Contingency Fund of India. |
