## 1. Complete Lesson

### Sessions of Parliament

> - A **session** is the period during which a House **meets** day after day to do its business, from its first sitting to its **prorogation**.
> - The **President summons** each House to meet. The gap between two sessions **must not be more than six months** (Article 85) — so Parliament must meet **at least twice a year**.
> - By practice Parliament holds **three sessions** a year:

| Session | Usual months | Main business |
|---|---|---|
| **Budget Session** | **February–May** | The Union Budget (presented on 1 February) and the President's Address at the start of the year |
| **Monsoon Session** | **July–August** (sometimes into September) | Legislation and discussion |
| **Winter Session** | **November–December** | Legislation and discussion |

**Ending a sitting, a session and a House**

| Term | Who does it | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Adjournment** | The presiding officer (Speaker / Chairman) | Ends a **sitting** for hours or days; the session continues. |
| **Adjournment *sine die*** | The presiding officer | Ends a sitting **without fixing a day** for the next meeting — usually the last day of a session. |
| **Prorogation** | The **President** (on the advice of the government) | **Ends the session**; Bills pending in the House do not lapse. |
| **Dissolution** | The **President** | **Ends the life of the Lok Sabha**; a general election follows. The Rajya Sabha is never dissolved. |

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2018, Q3(c))* What is meant by the term "Session"? Name the three sessions of the Union Parliament.
**Solution:** A session is the period during which a House meets regularly to conduct its business, between its summoning by the President and its prorogation. The three sessions are the **Budget Session (February–May)**, the **Monsoon Session (July–August)** and the **Winter Session (November–December)**.

### Quorum

> - **Quorum** is the **minimum number of members** who must be present for a House to **conduct business**.
> - It is **one-tenth** of the **total number of members** of the House (Article 100), including the presiding officer — about **55** in the Lok Sabha and **25** in the Rajya Sabha.
> - If there is **no quorum**, the presiding officer must **adjourn the House** or **suspend the sitting** until there is a quorum.

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2024, Q1(v))* The strength of the House is 550. On a particular day 50 members are present. The Speaker decides to adjourn the House. Identify the most likely reason for the adjournment.
**Solution:** **Lack of quorum** — one-tenth of 550 is 55, and only 50 are present.

### Question Hour and Zero Hour

> - **Question Hour** is the **first hour** of every sitting (**11 a.m. to 12 noon**), when members ask **questions** of ministers about the working of their ministries. Questions are given with notice (about **ten days**), and the presiding officer decides which are admissible.
> - It is the most important way of getting **information** and of making the government **answerable** — the "right of interpellation".

| Type of question | Mark | How it is answered | Supplementary questions? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starred** | marked with an asterisk (*) | **Oral** answer on the floor of the House | **Yes** — members may ask follow-up questions |
| **Unstarred** | no asterisk | **Written** answer, placed on the table | **No** |
| **Short notice** | — | **Oral** answer; asked on a matter of **urgent public importance** with **less than ten days'** notice | Yes |

> - **Zero Hour** begins at **12 noon**, immediately **after** Question Hour. Members raise **urgent matters without prior notice**. It is an **Indian innovation** (from the early 1960s) and is **not mentioned in the Rules** of either House.

**Example 3.** *(ICSE 2019, Q1(b))* State the meaning of the term Question Hour.
**Solution:** The **first hour** of every working day of the House (**11 a.m. to 12 noon**), in which **members ask questions** of the government on matters of public interest.

### Motions

A **motion** is a formal proposal placed before the House for its decision.

| Motion | Purpose | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Adjournment Motion** | To **set aside the normal business** and discuss a **definite matter of urgent public importance** (a disaster, a serious incident) | Allowed by the **Speaker**; needs the support of **50 members**; moved only in the **Lok Sabha**; carries an element of **censure** of the government |
| **No-Confidence Motion** | To show that the **Council of Ministers has lost the confidence** of the Lok Sabha | Moved only in the **Lok Sabha**, usually by the **Opposition**; needs **50 members'** support; no reason need be given; if **passed**, the **whole Council of Ministers must resign** |
| **Censure Motion** | To **criticise** a minister or the government for a particular policy or act | **Reasons must be stated**; if passed, the government usually seeks a vote of confidence |
| **Confidence Motion** | Moved by the **government** to prove its majority | If it fails, the government resigns |
| **Calling Attention Motion** | To draw a **minister's attention** to an urgent matter | The minister makes a statement |
| **Motion of Thanks** | Thanks the President for the **Address** at the start of the year | Its defeat would mean the defeat of the government |
| **Cut Motions** | To **reduce a demand for grants** in the Budget | Policy cut, economy cut and token cut |

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2023, Q1(iii))* A major natural calamity has taken place and the Opposition wants the House to lay aside all other business and take up this matter of urgent importance. Which motion should the House move?
**Solution:** An **Adjournment Motion**.

**Example 5.** *(ICSE 2019, Q1(f))* What happens when a motion of no-confidence is passed against a minister?
**Solution:** The **entire Council of Ministers resigns** (the government falls), because ministers are **collectively responsible** to the Lok Sabha.

### How a Bill becomes a law

**Kinds of Bills**

| Bill | Introduced by | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Government Bill** | A minister | Either House (Money Bills: Lok Sabha only) |
| **Private Member's Bill** | A member who is not a minister | Either House |
| **Ordinary Bill** | Deals with any matter other than money matters | Either House |
| **Money Bill** | Deals **only** with taxes, government borrowing, the Consolidated Fund and similar money matters | **Lok Sabha only** |
| **Constitution Amendment Bill** | Changes the Constitution | Either House |

**Passing of an Ordinary Bill**

1. **First Reading** — the Bill is **introduced**; its title and aims are read; there is no debate.
2. **Second Reading** — the House **discusses the principles** of the Bill. It may be sent to a **Select Committee** or a **Joint Committee** of both Houses, which examines it **clause by clause** and reports. The House then considers each clause and votes on **amendments**.
3. **Third Reading** — the House **passes or rejects** the Bill as a whole; only small changes of wording are allowed.
4. **The other House** — the Bill goes through the **same three stages**. The other House may **pass** it, **amend** it, **reject** it, or **not act** on it.
5. **Joint sitting** — if the Houses **disagree**, or six months pass, the **President** may summon a **joint sitting** (Article 108). The **Speaker presides**; the Bill is decided by a **majority** of the members of **both Houses present and voting**, so the bigger **Lok Sabha** usually prevails. (There have been only three joint sittings — in 1961, 1978 and 2002.)
6. **President's assent** — the President may **give assent**, **withhold assent**, or **return** the Bill **once** for reconsideration. If both Houses pass it again, the President **must** give assent. The Bill then becomes an **Act**.

**Passing of a Money Bill**

- It can be introduced **only in the Lok Sabha**, **only by a minister** and **only on the recommendation of the President**.
- The **Speaker certifies** that it is a Money Bill; the certificate is **final**.
- After the Lok Sabha passes it, the Rajya Sabha **cannot reject or amend** it. It must **return the Bill within 14 days**, with or without **recommendations**, which the Lok Sabha may **accept or reject**. If not returned in 14 days, the Bill is **deemed passed** by both Houses.
- There is **no joint sitting** on a Money Bill, and the President, who recommended it, normally gives **assent** (it cannot be returned for reconsideration).

**Example 6.** *(ICSE 2017, Q1(h))* How long can the Rajya Sabha retain a Money Bill sent by the Lok Sabha?
**Solution:** **Fourteen days.** After that the Bill is deemed to have been passed by both Houses.

### Powers and functions of Parliament

#### 1. Legislative powers

- Parliament makes laws on all subjects in the **Union List** (defence, foreign affairs, railways, currency, banking, atomic energy).
- It makes laws on the **Concurrent List** together with the States; if a State law conflicts with a Union law, the **Union law prevails**.
- It alone makes laws on **residuary** subjects (those in no list).
- It can make laws on subjects in the **State List** in **five** cases:
  1. When the **Rajya Sabha** declares, by a resolution supported by **two-thirds** of the members present and voting, that it is **necessary in the national interest** (Article 249).
  2. During a **National Emergency** (Article 250).
  3. When **two or more States request** it by resolutions of their legislatures (Article 252).
  4. To give effect to **international treaties and agreements** (Article 253).
  5. When a State is under **President's Rule** because its constitutional machinery has failed (Article 356).
- It **approves Ordinances** issued by the President; an Ordinance **lapses six weeks** after Parliament reassembles unless approved (Chapter 3).
- It can **create new States**, change the **boundaries or names** of States, and **reorganise** them (Articles 3 and 4) — as when Jammu and Kashmir was made into two **Union Territories** in 2019.

#### 2. Financial powers

- **No tax** can be imposed or collected **without the authority of Parliament** (Article 265).
- Parliament **passes the Budget** — the **Annual Financial Statement** of the estimated income and expenditure of the government — and the **Appropriation Act**, without which no money can be spent from the Consolidated Fund.
- It passes **supplementary grants** when the money granted is not enough.
- It passes a **vote on account** — permission to spend money for part of the year until the full Budget is passed.
- Its committees — the **Public Accounts Committee** and the **Estimates Committee** — examine government spending and the reports of the **Comptroller and Auditor-General (CAG)**.
- It **fixes the salaries and allowances** of its members and other officials.
- **Cut motions** allow members to criticise and reduce demands for grants.

#### 3. Judicial powers

- **Impeachment** of the **President** for violation of the Constitution.
- **Removal** of **judges** of the Supreme Court and the High Courts for proved misbehaviour or incapacity.
- **Removal of the Vice-President** (by a Rajya Sabha resolution agreed to by the Lok Sabha), and of officials such as the Chief Election Commissioner and the CAG.
- **Punishing** members or outsiders for **breach of privilege** or **contempt of the House**.

#### 4. Electoral powers

- The **elected members of both Houses**, together with the elected members of the State Legislative Assemblies, **elect the President**.
- **All members of both Houses** elect the **Vice-President**.
- The Lok Sabha elects its **Speaker** and **Deputy Speaker**; the Rajya Sabha elects its **Deputy Chairman**.

#### 5. Amendment of the Constitution

- Parliament can **amend** the Constitution (Article 368). Most amendments need a **special majority** — a **majority of the total membership** of each House **and two-thirds** of the members **present and voting** — in **each House separately** (there is no joint sitting).
- Amendments affecting the **federal structure** (for example, the representation of States in Parliament) must also be **ratified by at least half** of the State legislatures.
- A few changes (such as forming a new State) need only a **simple majority**.

#### 6. Control over the executive

India has a **parliamentary system**: the Council of Ministers is **collectively responsible** to the Lok Sabha and stays in office only as long as it has its **confidence**. Parliament controls the executive by:
1. **Questions** in Question Hour, and **Zero Hour** matters.
2. **Debates and discussions**, including the debate on the **Motion of Thanks** on the President's Address.
3. **Adjournment motions** on urgent issues.
4. **No-confidence motions** — the government must resign if one is passed.
5. **Censure motions** and **calling attention** motions.
6. **Budget control** — refusing money, passing **cut motions**, rejecting a **Money Bill**.
7. **Rejecting government Bills** or passing a **Private Member's Bill** against the government's wishes.
8. **Parliamentary committees** and the reports of the **CAG**.

**Example 7.** *(ICSE 2025, Q3(i))* Mention any three circumstances under which Parliament can make laws on subjects in the State List.
**Solution:** **(1)** During a **National Emergency**; **(2)** when the **Rajya Sabha** approves it by a **two-thirds majority** in the national interest; **(3)** when **two or more States** request it. *(Also: when a State is under President's Rule; to implement international agreements.)*

### Exclusive powers of the two Houses

**Exclusive powers of the Lok Sabha**

1. **Money Bills** can be introduced **only in the Lok Sabha**; the Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend them and can hold them for only **14 days**.
2. The **Council of Ministers** is **responsible only to the Lok Sabha**; a **no-confidence motion** can be moved and passed **only in the Lok Sabha**.
3. The **Budget** — the **demands for grants** — is voted on only by the Lok Sabha; it **controls the national purse**.
4. An **adjournment motion** can be moved only in the Lok Sabha.
5. In a **joint sitting**, the Lok Sabha's **larger numbers** usually decide the outcome.

**Exclusive powers of the Rajya Sabha**

1. It can declare, by a **two-thirds majority**, that **Parliament may make laws** on a subject in the **State List** in the national interest (Article 249).
2. It can authorise the creation of **new All-India Services** by a **two-thirds majority** (Article 312).
3. It is a **permanent House**: when the Lok Sabha is **dissolved**, it alone can, for example, **approve a proclamation of emergency** for the time being.
4. A resolution to **remove the Vice-President** can be moved only in the Rajya Sabha.

**Powers the two Houses share equally**

- Passing **Ordinary Bills** and **Constitution Amendment Bills**.
- **Electing** the President (elected members) and the Vice-President.
- **Impeaching** the President and **removing judges**.
- **Approving** Ordinances and **proclamations of emergency**.
- **Questioning** ministers and discussing government policy.

**Example 8.** *(ICSE 2024, Q3(iii))* Mention any two exclusive powers of the Lok Sabha and two powers exclusive to the Rajya Sabha.
**Solution:** **Lok Sabha:** Money Bills can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha; a no-confidence motion can be moved only in the Lok Sabha. **Rajya Sabha:** it can approve the creation of new All-India Services; it can declare by a two-thirds majority that Parliament may legislate on a State List subject.

### Summary of the chapter

- **Sessions:** summoned by the President; gap not more than six months; Budget (Feb–May), Monsoon (Jul–Aug), Winter (Nov–Dec). Adjournment (presiding officer), prorogation and dissolution (President).
- **Quorum:** one-tenth of the members; no quorum → adjournment.
- **Question Hour** 11–12 (starred: oral + supplementaries; unstarred: written; short notice: urgent, oral); **Zero Hour** from 12 noon, no notice.
- **Motions:** adjournment (urgent matter, 50 members, Speaker's consent); no-confidence (Lok Sabha only; if passed, ministry resigns); censure; cut motions.
- **Bills:** Ordinary — three readings in each House, joint sitting in a deadlock, President's assent; Money — Lok Sabha only, Speaker certifies, Rajya Sabha 14 days, no joint sitting.
- **Powers:** legislative (incl. five cases for State List), financial, judicial, electoral, amendment, control over the executive.
- **Exclusive powers:** Lok Sabha — money, no-confidence, budget; Rajya Sabha — Articles 249 and 312, permanence.

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — Procedures
Parliament works by fixed rules: it meets in sessions, needs a quorum, and uses Question Hour, Zero Hour and motions to discuss and decide. These terms are the favourite one-mark and two-mark items.

### Section 2 — Bills
An Ordinary Bill needs the consent of both Houses and the President; a Money Bill is dominated by the Lok Sabha. Know who introduces each kind, the role of the Speaker's certificate, the 14-day rule and the joint sitting.

### Section 3 — Powers
Parliament is the supreme law-maker of the Union. The examiner expects lists — the five State List cases, four financial powers, the impeachment and election powers, and four or more ways of controlling the executive.

### Section 4 — The two Houses compared
The Lok Sabha is more powerful because it controls money and the government; the Rajya Sabha has a few special powers because it represents the States and is permanent.

## 5. Important Facts, Formulas and Results

### 5.1 Must-know facts

- Gap between two sessions: **not more than six months**; three sessions by practice.
- Quorum: **one-tenth** of the total membership.
- Question Hour: **11 a.m.–12 noon**; Zero Hour: from **12 noon**.
- Adjournment and no-confidence motions: **Lok Sabha only**, **50 members'** support.
- Money Bill: **Lok Sabha only**; Speaker certifies; Rajya Sabha **14 days**; no joint sitting.
- Joint sitting: summoned by the **President**, presided over by the **Speaker**.
- State List laws by Parliament: **Rajya Sabha resolution (2/3)**, **emergency**, **request by two or more States**, **international agreements**, **President's Rule**.

### 5.2 Useful facts

- The Union Budget has been presented on **1 February** since 2017.
- Joint sittings were held for the Dowry Prohibition Bill (1961), the Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill (1978) and the Prevention of Terrorism Bill (2002).
- The first no-confidence motion in the Lok Sabha was moved in 1963.

### 5.3 Beyond the text (general knowledge, not needed for exam answers)

- Parliament's proceedings are telecast live on Sansad TV.
- "Productivity" figures in the news compare the time a House actually worked with the time scheduled.

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 Dr B. R. Ambedkar (1891–1956)
Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constitution. He explained that India chose the parliamentary system because it gives **more responsibility** — the government must answer to Parliament every day — even though a presidential system gives more stability.

### 6.2 Acharya J. B. Kripalani (1888–1982)
A veteran freedom fighter and former Congress President, he moved the **first no-confidence motion** in the Lok Sabha, in August 1963, against the government of Jawaharlal Nehru. It was defeated, but it established the Opposition's right to test the government's majority.

### 6.3 Atal Bihari Vajpayee (1924–2018)
His government lost a **confidence vote** in the Lok Sabha in April 1999 **by a single vote**, showing that a government survives only while it has the House's confidence. He was later Prime Minister from 1999 to 2004.

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Answerability every day** — Question Hour and motions make the government explain itself.
- **The purse is the power** — whoever controls money (the Lok Sabha) controls the government.
- **Checks between the Houses** — the Rajya Sabha can delay and revise, but the people's House has the last word on money and confidence.

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **Budget Day:** on 1 February the Finance Minister presents the Budget in the Lok Sabha — the start of the Budget Session.
- **News headlines** such as "Opposition's adjournment motion disallowed" or "Government wins confidence vote" are the procedures of this chapter at work.
- **Your State:** the State Legislative Assembly follows similar rules, with its own Speaker, sessions and Question Hour.

### 8.1 Graded exercises — solutions

#### Exercise 2A — Procedures

**Q1.** Who summons and prorogues the Houses of Parliament?
**Answer:** The President.

**Q2.** Distinguish between adjournment and prorogation.
**Answer:** Adjournment ends a sitting and is done by the presiding officer; prorogation ends a session and is done by the President.

**Q3.** What is the quorum of a House, and what happens if it is not present?
**Answer:** One-tenth of its total members; the presiding officer adjourns the House or suspends the sitting.

**Q4.** Distinguish between a starred and an unstarred question.
**Answer:** A starred question is answered orally and supplementary questions may be asked; an unstarred question gets a written answer and no supplementaries.

#### Exercise 2B — Bills

**Q5.** In which House can a Money Bill be introduced, and who certifies it?
**Answer:** Only in the Lok Sabha; the Speaker certifies it.

**Q6.** Who summons and who presides over a joint sitting?
**Answer:** The President summons it; the Speaker presides.

**Q7.** Name the three readings of a Bill.
**Answer:** First reading (introduction), second reading (discussion and committee stage), third reading (passing).

#### Exercise 2C — Powers

**Q8.** Name two financial powers of Parliament.
**Answer:** Passing the Budget; approving taxes (also: supplementary grants; vote on account).

**Q9.** Who elects the Vice-President?
**Answer:** All the members of both Houses of Parliament.

**Q10.** Name two exclusive powers of the Rajya Sabha.
**Answer:** Declaring by a two-thirds majority that Parliament may legislate on a State List subject; authorising new All-India Services.

### 8.3 More practice in the ICSE style

**Q1.** (3 marks) Explain the passing of a Money Bill.
**Answer:** It is introduced only in the Lok Sabha, by a minister, on the President's recommendation; the Speaker certifies it. After the Lok Sabha passes it, the Rajya Sabha must return it within 14 days with or without recommendations, which the Lok Sabha may accept or reject; it cannot reject or amend it. The President then gives assent.

**Q2.** (3 marks) How is a deadlock between the two Houses on an Ordinary Bill resolved?
**Answer:** The President summons a joint sitting of both Houses; the Speaker presides; the Bill is decided by a majority of the members of both Houses present and voting, so the larger Lok Sabha usually prevails.

**Q3.** (2 marks) Distinguish between an adjournment motion and a no-confidence motion.
**Answer:** An adjournment motion sets aside business to discuss an urgent matter; a no-confidence motion tests whether the government has the House's support — if passed, the ministry resigns.

**Q4.** (4 marks) How is the Constitution amended?
**Answer:** A Bill is introduced in either House; it must be passed in each House separately by a majority of its total membership and two-thirds of those present and voting; amendments affecting the federal structure also need ratification by at least half the State legislatures; the President must then give assent. There is no joint sitting.

**Q5.** (2 marks) What is Zero Hour?
**Answer:** The time from 12 noon, just after Question Hour, when members raise urgent matters without prior notice; it is an Indian innovation not mentioned in the Rules.

## 10. Short-Answer Questions (2 and 3 marks)

### Part I questions (2 marks each)

**Q1.** What is meant by a session? Who summons the Houses?
**Answer:** The period during which a House meets day after day to do its business.
The President summons them.

**Q2.** Distinguish between adjournment and prorogation.
**Answer:** Adjournment ends a sitting, by the presiding officer.
Prorogation ends a session, by the President.

**Q3.** What is quorum? What is the quorum of a House of Parliament?
**Answer:** The minimum number of members needed for the House to do business.
One-tenth of its total membership.

**Q4.** What is Question Hour?
**Answer:** The first hour of every sitting (11 a.m.–12 noon).
Members ask ministers questions on matters of public interest.

**Q5.** Distinguish between starred and unstarred questions.
**Answer:** Starred: answered orally; supplementary questions allowed.
Unstarred: answered in writing; no supplementaries.

**Q6.** What is Zero Hour?
**Answer:** The period starting at 12 noon, after Question Hour.
Members raise urgent matters without prior notice.

**Q7.** What is an adjournment motion? Who allows it?
**Answer:** A motion to set aside normal business to discuss a matter of urgent public importance.
The Speaker allows it (with the support of 50 members).

**Q8.** What is a no-confidence motion? What happens if it is passed?
**Answer:** A motion in the Lok Sabha expressing lack of confidence in the Council of Ministers.
The whole Council of Ministers must resign.

**Q9.** Name two Bills that cannot be decided at a joint sitting.
**Answer:** A Money Bill.
A Constitution Amendment Bill.

**Q10.** Give two financial powers of Parliament.
**Answer:** Passing the Budget.
Approving taxes (also: supplementary grants; vote on account).

**Q11.** Give two electoral powers of Parliament.
**Answer:** Its elected members take part in electing the President.
All its members elect the Vice-President.

**Q12.** Give two judicial powers of Parliament.
**Answer:** Impeachment of the President.
Removal of judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts.

### Part II parts (3 marks each)

**Q13.** Name and describe the three sessions of Parliament.
**Answer:** Budget Session, February–May — the Budget is passed.
Monsoon Session, July–August.
Winter Session, November–December.

**Q14.** Explain the three types of questions asked in Parliament.
**Answer:** Starred — oral answer, supplementaries allowed.
Unstarred — written answer, no supplementaries.
Short notice — urgent matters, less than ten days' notice, oral answer.

**Q15.** Mention three circumstances in which Parliament can make laws on the State List.
**Answer:** When the Rajya Sabha so resolves by a two-thirds majority in the national interest.
During a National Emergency.
When two or more States request it (also: President's Rule; international agreements).

**Q16.** Explain the passing of a Money Bill.
**Answer:** Introduced only in the Lok Sabha by a minister on the President's recommendation; certified by the Speaker.
The Rajya Sabha must return it in 14 days with or without recommendations; it cannot reject or amend it.
The Lok Sabha may accept or reject the recommendations; there is no joint sitting.

**Q17.** Explain how a deadlock on an Ordinary Bill is resolved.
**Answer:** The President summons a joint sitting of both Houses.
The Speaker presides.
The Bill is decided by a majority of members present and voting; the Lok Sabha's numbers usually prevail.

**Q18.** Mention three legislative powers of Parliament.
**Answer:** Laws on the Union List and residuary subjects.
Laws on the Concurrent List (Union law prevails in a conflict).
Laws on the State List in special cases; approval of Ordinances.

**Q19.** Mention three financial powers of Parliament.
**Answer:** Passes the Budget and the Appropriation Act.
No tax can be imposed without its approval.
Passes supplementary grants and votes on account.

**Q20.** Mention three exclusive powers of the Lok Sabha.
**Answer:** Money Bills originate only in it.
A no-confidence motion can be passed only in it.
It votes the demands for grants (controls the national purse).

**Q21.** Mention three exclusive powers of the Rajya Sabha.
**Answer:** Article 249 resolution on State List subjects.
Authorising new All-India Services.
Acting when the Lok Sabha is dissolved (as a permanent House); moving the removal of the Vice-President.

**Q22.** Mention three powers common to both Houses.
**Answer:** Passing Ordinary and Constitution Amendment Bills.
Electing the President and Vice-President.
Impeaching the President and removing judges.

## 11. Long-Answer Questions (4 marks each)

### Part II parts (4 marks each)

**Q1.** Mention four ways in which Parliament controls the executive.
**Answer:** Questions in Question Hour and Zero Hour.
Adjournment motions and debates.
No-confidence and censure motions.
Control of the Budget — cut motions, refusing money (also: committees and CAG reports).

**Q2.** Mention four financial powers of Parliament.
**Answer:** Passes the Budget.
No tax without its approval.
Passes supplementary grants and votes on account.
Fixes salaries of members; examines spending through the Public Accounts Committee and the CAG.

**Q3.** State two exclusive powers of the Lok Sabha and two of the Rajya Sabha.
**Answer:** Lok Sabha: Money Bills originate only there.
Lok Sabha: no-confidence motion only there.
Rajya Sabha: new All-India Services by a two-thirds majority.
Rajya Sabha: Article 249 resolution allowing Parliament to legislate on the State List.

**Q4.** Describe the passing of an Ordinary Bill.
**Answer:** First reading — introduction.
Second reading — discussion, committee stage, clause-by-clause consideration.
Third reading — passing; then the same in the other House.
Joint sitting in a deadlock; the President's assent.

**Q5.** Explain how the Constitution is amended.
**Answer:** A Bill may be introduced in either House.
It must be passed by a majority of the total membership and two-thirds of those present and voting in each House separately.
Federal provisions also need ratification by half the State legislatures.
The President must give assent; there is no joint sitting.

**Q6.** Mention two judicial and two electoral powers of Parliament.
**Answer:** Judicial: impeachment of the President.
Judicial: removal of judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts.
Electoral: election of the President (elected members, with MLAs).
Electoral: election of the Vice-President (all members).

**Q7.** Explain four powers that show the superiority of the Lok Sabha over the Rajya Sabha.
**Answer:** Money Bills originate in it; the Rajya Sabha can delay them only 14 days.
The Council of Ministers is responsible to it; no-confidence only there.
It votes the Budget.
It prevails in a joint sitting because of its larger numbers.

**Q8.** Explain the motions of Parliament: adjournment, no-confidence, censure and cut motions.
**Answer:** Adjournment — sets aside business for an urgent matter; Lok Sabha only.
No-confidence — tests the government's majority; if passed, the ministry resigns.
Censure — criticises a policy, with reasons stated.
Cut motions — reduce demands for grants (policy, economy and token cuts).

## 12. Competency / Application Questions

**Q1. (A flood in the north-east)** A flood has killed hundreds of people. Opposition members want an immediate discussion in the Lok Sabha instead of the scheduled debate. What should they do, and who decides?
**Model answer:** Give notice of an adjournment motion, supported by at least 50 members; the Speaker decides whether to admit it.

**Q2. (Bill in the balance)** An Ordinary Bill passed by the Lok Sabha is rejected by the Rajya Sabha. What can the government do?
**Model answer:** Ask the President to summon a joint sitting; the Speaker presides, and the Bill is decided by a majority of members of both Houses present and voting.

**Q3. (Budget delay)** A general election is due in May, and the full Budget cannot be passed before March. How will the government pay salaries in April?
**Model answer:** Parliament passes a vote on account, allowing spending for part of the year until the full Budget is passed.

**Q4. (A State's request)** Three States pass resolutions asking for one national law on groundwater, a State subject. Can Parliament make it?
**Model answer:** Yes — when two or more States request it, Parliament can legislate on a State subject; the law applies to those States and others that adopt it.

**Q5. (The written answer)** An MP receives a written reply to a question and cannot ask a follow-up. What kind of question was it?
**Model answer:** An unstarred question.

**Q6. (A government survives)** A no-confidence motion is defeated by 30 votes. What does this show?
**Model answer:** The Council of Ministers still enjoys the confidence of the majority of the Lok Sabha and continues in office.

## 15. Revision Notes

### Chapter at a glance
- Sessions: summoned by the President; gap ≤ 6 months; Budget (Feb–May), Monsoon (Jul–Aug), Winter (Nov–Dec).
- Adjournment (presiding officer) — sitting; prorogation (President) — session; dissolution (President) — Lok Sabha.
- Quorum: one-tenth; no quorum → adjourn.
- Question Hour 11–12: starred (oral, supplementaries), unstarred (written), short notice (urgent). Zero Hour 12 noon, no notice.
- Motions: adjournment (urgent, 50 members, Speaker, LS only); no-confidence (LS only, ministry resigns); censure (reasons); confidence; calling attention; motion of thanks; cut motions.
- Ordinary Bill: 3 readings × 2 Houses → joint sitting if deadlock (President summons, Speaker presides) → assent (may return once).
- Money Bill: LS only, minister, President's recommendation, Speaker certifies; RS 14 days, recommendations only; no joint sitting.
- Amendment: special majority in each House (+ half the States for federal matters).
- Powers: legislative (Union, Concurrent, residuary, State List in 5 cases, Ordinances, reorganising States), financial, judicial, electoral, amendment, control over executive.
- Exclusive — LS: money, no-confidence, budget, adjournment motion; RS: Art 249, Art 312, permanence, removal of VP.

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct idea |
|---|---|
| The Speaker prorogues the House | The President prorogues; the Speaker adjourns sittings. |
| Quorum is one-third | Quorum is one-tenth. |
| Zero Hour questions need notice | No notice is needed. |
| A no-confidence motion can be moved in the Rajya Sabha | Only in the Lok Sabha. |
| The Rajya Sabha can reject a Money Bill | It can only recommend within 14 days. |
| A joint sitting decides Money Bills | There is no joint sitting on Money Bills (or amendments). |
| Exclusive power of the Rajya Sabha: impeaching the President | Both Houses take part in impeachment. |
| "The Lok Sabha makes laws on the State List" | It is Parliament (both Houses), in five special cases. |
