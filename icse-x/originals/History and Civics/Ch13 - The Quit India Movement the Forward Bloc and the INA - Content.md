## 1. Complete Lesson

### PART A — THE QUIT INDIA MOVEMENT (1942)

#### Background: the Second World War

- When the **Second World War** began in **September 1939**, the Viceroy **Lord Linlithgow** declared India a party to the war **without consulting** Indian leaders.
- The **Congress ministries** in the provinces **resigned** in protest (October–November 1939).
- Gandhi launched the **Individual Satyagraha** (1940–41) — selected individuals (the first was **Vinoba Bhave**, the second Jawaharlal Nehru) made anti-war speeches and courted arrest.

#### Causes of the Quit India Movement

**1. The failure of the Cripps Mission (March–April 1942)**
- With Japan advancing in Asia, the British Government sent **Sir Stafford Cripps** to India to win Indian support for the war.
- His proposals offered **dominion status only after the war** and allowed provinces to stay out of a future Indian Union; they gave **no real power now**.
- Both the **Congress** and the **Muslim League** rejected them; Gandhi called them "**a post-dated cheque**". The failure showed that Britain was **not willing to part with power**.

**2. The Japanese threat**
- **Japan** had swept through South-East Asia — **Singapore** (February 1942), **Malaya** and **Burma** (Rangoon, March 1942) fell; Japanese planes bombed places on India's eastern coast.
- The British **retreated** from Malaya and Burma, leaving the local people to their fate. Indians feared the same would happen in India.
- Gandhi believed that the **presence of the British** was itself the reason Japan might attack India; if the British left, a **free India** could defend itself better.

**3. Worsening of the communal problem**
- The British were encouraging the **Muslim League**, whose demand for Pakistan (1940) was dividing the country. Congress leaders felt that only the end of British rule would allow a settlement.

**4. Economic hardship**
- The war caused **rising prices**, **shortages** of food and essential goods, and hardship for the poor.

**5. Treatment of Indian evacuees from Burma**
- Indian refugees fleeing from Burma and Malaya were **discriminated against** — separate, worse routes and facilities were kept for them.

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2024, Q2(iii))* Mention any two causes of the Quit India Movement.
**Solution:** **Failure of the Cripps Mission**; **threat of Japanese invasion**.

#### The Quit India Resolution (8 August 1942)

- The **Congress Working Committee** met at **Wardha** (14 July 1942) and accepted the idea of a mass movement.
- The **All India Congress Committee** passed the **Quit India Resolution** at the **Gowalia Tank Maidan, Bombay**, on **8 August 1942**.
- It demanded the **immediate end of British rule** in India; declared that a **free India** would resist aggression and join the Allies against fascism; proposed a **provisional government**; and sanctioned a **mass struggle on non-violent lines** under Gandhi's leadership.
- Gandhi gave the call "**Do or Die**" (*Karo ya Maro*): "We shall either free India or die in the attempt." Every Indian was asked to act as a leader if the leaders were arrested.

#### The course of the movement

- In the early hours of **9 August 1942** the government struck: **Gandhi** and all the top Congress leaders were **arrested** (Gandhi was held at the **Aga Khan Palace, Poona**); the Congress was declared **illegal**.
- With the leaders in jail, the people rose **spontaneously**: **hartals**, **strikes** and **demonstrations** everywhere; attacks on symbols of British authority — **police stations, post offices, railway stations**; **telegraph lines cut**, rail lines removed.
- **Parallel governments** were set up in **Ballia** (Uttar Pradesh), **Satara** (Maharashtra) and **Tamluk** (Midnapore, Bengal).
- **Underground leaders** kept the movement alive — **Jayaprakash Narayan**, **Ram Manohar Lohia**, **Aruna Asaf Ali** (who hoisted the flag at Gowalia Tank on 9 August) and **Usha Mehta** (who ran a secret **Congress Radio**).
- Heroines such as **Matangini Hazra** (Bengal) and **Kanaklata Barua** (Assam) were shot while carrying the national flag.
- The government used **brutal repression** — firing on crowds, mass arrests (over 60,000), collective fines and even aerial machine-gunning. By 1943–44 the movement was **suppressed**; Gandhi was released in May 1944.

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2026 Improvement, Q7(i))* Read the passage [on the "rebel cry" of August 1942, the resolution at the Gowalia Tank Maidan and the call of "do or die"]. Identify the above movement. Mention any two causes which led to the movement.
**Solution:** The **Quit India Movement** (1). Any two causes (2): **failure of the Cripps Mission**; the **Japanese threat**; the **worsening communal problem**; economic hardship during the war.

#### Significance (effects) of the Quit India Movement

1. It **demonstrated the depth of nationalist feeling** in India and the capacity of Indians for **struggle and sacrifice**.
2. It **undermined the strength of the British** — it warned them that they were **not wanted** in India and that their **days were numbered**.
3. It was once again a **mass movement** — the **last** great mass movement — uniting **young and old** and people of **all religions and regions**.
4. It made clear that Indians would accept **nothing short of independence**.
5. It marked the **climax** of the Indian revolution and brought new **political awakening**; it strengthened the **Congress Socialist** group.
6. It drew the **attention of the world**, especially the **USA**, to India's demand.
7. It was **symbolic** of the **new confidence** of the Indian people.
8. It **hastened the British decision to quit India** — after the war, Britain began negotiations for the transfer of power.

**Example 3.** *(ICSE 2018, Q7(b))* Give any three effects of the Quit India Movement launched by Gandhi in 1942 that was significant to the last phase of the National Movement of India.
**Solution:** Any three: it **demonstrated the depth of nationalist feelings** in India and the capacity of Indians for struggle and sacrifice; it **undermined the strength of the British** in India; it once again became a **mass movement**; the movement was **symbolic** of the new confidence of the people.

### PART B — SUBHAS CHANDRA BOSE AND THE FORWARD BLOC

#### Subhas Chandra Bose (1897–1945) — "Netaji"

- Born at **Cuttack** (1897); passed the **Indian Civil Service** examination (1920) but **resigned** (1921) to join the freedom struggle under **C. R. Das**, whom he regarded as his political guru.
- A leader of the **youth** and of the **left wing** of the Congress; he demanded **complete independence** and **socialist** economic policies.
- Elected **President of the Congress** at **Haripura (1938)** and again at **Tripuri (1939)**, defeating Pattabhi Sitaramayya, the candidate supported by Gandhi.
- Because of differences with Gandhi and the Congress leadership over methods and the coming war, he **resigned** the presidency in April 1939.

#### The Forward Bloc (1939)

- Bose founded the **Forward Bloc** on **3 May 1939**, at first as a group within the Congress, to **unite all radical and left-wing elements** in the struggle for freedom.

**Objectives of the Forward Bloc**
1. To **attain complete independence** (the liberation of India) with the support of **workers, peasants and youth** and radical organisations.
2. To **establish a socialist state / society** — through the **reorganisation of agriculture and industry on socialist lines**.
3. **Abolition of the zamindari system**.
4. **Introduction of a new monetary and credit system**.
5. To make the **right to work** a **fundamental right** of the citizens.
6. To provide **workers** with benefits such as **minimum wages** and **occupational safety**.
7. **Freedom of religious worship**; **linguistic and cultural autonomy** for all sections of society.
8. **Equality and social justice**; **social ownership and control** of both **production and distribution**.

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2025, Q2(iv))* Mention any two objectives of the Forward Bloc.
**Solution:** Any two: to **attain independence** with the support of **workers, peasants and youth**; to work towards establishing a **socialist state** through the **reorganisation of agriculture and industry on socialist lines**; **abolition of the zamindari system**; **introduction of a new monetary and credit system**; making the **right to work a fundamental right**; providing **workers** with benefits like **minimum wages** and **occupational safety**; **freedom of religious worship**; **linguistic and cultural autonomy**; **equality and social justice**; **social ownership and control** of production and distribution.

### PART C — THE INDIAN NATIONAL ARMY (INA)

#### Bose's escape and work abroad
- In **1940** Bose was placed under **house arrest** in Calcutta. In **January 1941** he **escaped** in disguise, travelled through Afghanistan (Kabul) and reached **Germany**.
- In **Berlin** he set up the **Free India Centre** and the **Azad Hind Radio**, and raised an **Indian Legion** from Indian prisoners of war.

#### The founding of the INA (1942)
- When Japan captured **Malaya and Singapore** (February 1942), thousands of **Indian soldiers** of the British army became **prisoners of war**.
- **Captain Mohan Singh**, an Indian officer, organised these soldiers into the **Indian National Army** (**Azad Hind Fauj**) with Japanese help — he is the **founder of the INA**.
- **Rash Behari Bose**, a veteran revolutionary living in Japan, set up the **Indian Independence League** (1942), under which the INA worked.
- Differences with the Japanese led to the break-up of the first INA at the end of 1942.

#### Bose takes command (1943)
- In **1943** Subhas Chandra Bose travelled by **submarine** from Germany to Japan and reached **Singapore** (July 1943). **Rash Behari Bose handed over** the leadership of the Indian Independence League and the INA to him (4 July 1943).
- Bose became the **Supreme Commander** of the INA (August 1943) and reorganised it into brigades named **Gandhi, Nehru, Azad** and **Subhas**, and a women's regiment — the **Rani of Jhansi Regiment**, led by **Captain Lakshmi Swaminathan**.
- On **21 October 1943** he set up the **Provisional Government of Free India** (Azad Hind) at Singapore; it was recognised by Japan, Germany, Italy and several other countries and **declared war** on Britain and the USA.
- Japan handed over the **Andaman and Nicobar Islands**, which Bose renamed **Shaheed** and **Swaraj** islands; he **hoisted the tricolour** at **Port Blair** (December 1943).
- His slogans: "**Delhi Chalo**" (On to Delhi), "**Jai Hind**", and "**Give me blood, and I will give you freedom**" (*Tum mujhe khoon do, main tumhe azadi dunga*).

#### The INA campaign in North-East India (1944)
- In **1944** the INA, with the Japanese army, advanced from Burma into **India's north-east**. INA soldiers **hoisted the tricolour at Moirang** in **Manipur** (April 1944).
- The campaign against **Imphal** and **Kohima** failed — the **monsoon**, lack of supplies and air cover, disease, and the defeat of Japan forced a retreat.
- After **Japan's surrender** (August 1945) the INA surrendered. Bose is believed to have died in an **air crash** at Taihoku (Taiwan) on **18 August 1945**.

#### Objectives of the INA
1. To organise an **armed revolution** and **fight the British army** with **modern arms**.
2. To use the **Indians living abroad** (in East Asia) for the struggle against the British.
3. To organise a **Provisional Government of Free India**.
4. **Total mobilisation** of Indian **manpower and money** for a **total war**.
5. To **fight until the last Britisher is thrown out** of India.
6. To **train people** for armed struggle inside and outside the country.
7. Its motto: "**Unity, Faith and Sacrifice**" (*Ittehad, Itmad aur Qurbani*).

**Example 5.** *(ICSE 2023, Q8(iii))* Who was the founder of the Indian National Army? Mention any three of its objectives.
**Solution:** **Mohan Singh** (1). Any three objectives (3): to **organise an armed rebellion** against the British with **modern arms**; to assign the task of rebellion to the **Indians living outside India**; to organise a **Provisional Government of Free India**; to **fight until the last Britisher is thrown out** of the country; **total mobilisation of Indian manpower and money** for a total war; the motto of the INA was "**Unity, Faith and Sacrifice**".

#### The INA trials (1945–46)
- In **November 1945** the British put three INA officers — **Shah Nawaz Khan** (a Muslim), **Prem Kumar Sahgal** (a Hindu) and **Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon** (a Sikh) — on trial at the **Red Fort**, Delhi.
- They were defended by **Bhulabhai Desai**, **Tej Bahadur Sapru** and **Jawaharlal Nehru**. The whole country, of every community, protested; the sentences were **remitted**.
- The trials were followed by the **Royal Indian Navy mutiny** (February 1946) at Bombay. The British realised that they could **no longer rely on the loyalty of the Indian armed forces**.

#### Contribution of Subhas Chandra Bose

1. **Founder of the Forward Bloc** (1939).
2. **Supreme Commander of the INA**; reorganised it into an effective fighting force.
3. Set up the **Provisional Government of Free India** (1943).
4. Gave the inspiring calls "**Give me blood and I will give you freedom**", "**Delhi Chalo**" and "**Jai Hind**".
5. **President of the Indian National Congress** (1938, 1939).
6. **Renamed the Andaman and Nicobar Islands** as Shaheed and Swaraj and **hoisted the tricolour** there.
7. Introduced the **women's regiment** (Rani of Jhansi Regiment).
8. Set up the **Azad Hind Radio** and the **Free India Centre** in **Berlin**.
9. Set an **inspiring example** of courage and sacrifice; the INA united **all communities** and awakened patriotism in the **armed forces**, which hastened the end of British rule.

**Example 6.** *(ICSE 2023, Q8(ii))* Mention any three contributions of Subash Chandra Bose.
**Solution:** Any three: was the **founder of the Forward Bloc**; was the **Supreme Commander of the INA**; set up the **Provisional Government of Free India**; gave the call "**Give me blood and I will give you freedom**"; gave the slogans "**Delhi Chalo**" and "**Jai Hind**"; **President of the Indian National Congress**; renamed the **Andaman and Nicobar islands** as **Shaheed and Swaraj**; introduced the **women's regiment**; **hoisted the tricolour flag**; set up the **Azad Hind Radio**; set up the **Free India Centre in Berlin**; set an **inspiring example**.

### Summary of the chapter

- **WW II** (1939): India dragged in; Congress ministries resign; Individual Satyagraha (1940–41).
- **QIM causes:** **Cripps Mission failure** (March 1942 — dominion status after the war; "post-dated cheque"); **Japanese threat** (Singapore, Burma fallen); communal problem; economic hardship; treatment of evacuees.
- **Resolution:** 8 August 1942, **Gowalia Tank, Bombay**; immediate end of British rule; **"Do or Die"**.
- **Course:** leaders arrested 9 August; spontaneous uprising; parallel governments (Ballia, Satara, Tamluk); underground leaders (JP, Lohia, Aruna Asaf Ali, Usha Mehta); brutal repression.
- **Significance:** depth of nationalism; British weakened; last mass movement; nothing short of independence; world attention; hastened British exit.
- **Forward Bloc:** Bose, 3 May 1939; independence with workers, peasants, youth; socialist state; abolition of zamindari; new credit system; right to work.
- **INA:** founder **Captain Mohan Singh** (1942); Rash Behari Bose's Indian Independence League; **Bose** Supreme Commander (1943); Provisional Government (21 Oct 1943); Rani of Jhansi Regiment; Andaman (Shaheed and Swaraj); Moirang (Manipur) 1944; Imphal–Kohima failed; motto **Unity, Faith and Sacrifice**; INA trials (1945) and RIN mutiny (1946).

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — Why "Quit India" in 1942?
Two causes win the marks every time: the Cripps Mission offered nothing now, and Japan was at India's door. Add the communal problem for a third.

### Section 2 — A leaderless movement
With Gandhi and the Congress leaders arrested on 9 August, the people carried on — sometimes violently. Learn the names of parallel governments and underground leaders.

### Section 3 — Bose's two organisations
The **Forward Bloc** (1939) was a **political party** with socialist aims; the **INA** was a **military organisation** abroad. Don't mix their objectives.

### Section 4 — Founder vs commander
**Mohan Singh founded** the INA (1942); **Bose led** it as Supreme Commander (1943). Exam answers often lose a mark here.

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 Subhas Chandra Bose (1897–1945)
"Netaji", President of the Congress (1938–39), founder of the Forward Bloc and Supreme Commander of the INA, who led the Provisional Government of Free India and inspired Indians with the call "Delhi Chalo".

### 6.2 Captain Mohan Singh (1909–1989)
An officer of the British Indian army captured in Malaya, who organised Indian prisoners of war into the first Indian National Army in 1942.

### 6.3 Rash Behari Bose (1886–1945)
A revolutionary who lived in Japan, founded the Indian Independence League and handed the INA over to Subhas Chandra Bose in 1943.

### 6.4 Sir Stafford Cripps (1889–1952)
A British minister whose mission of 1942 failed to win Indian support for the war.

### 6.5 Aruna Asaf Ali (1909–1996)
The "heroine of 1942", who hoisted the Congress flag at Gowalia Tank on 9 August 1942 and led the underground movement.

### 6.6 Captain Lakshmi Swaminathan (Sahgal) (1914–2012)
A doctor who commanded the INA's Rani of Jhansi Regiment.

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Leaderless but not aimless** — the people continued the Quit India struggle after every leader was jailed.
- **Different paths to one goal** — Gandhi's non-violent mass movement and Bose's armed struggle both aimed at freedom.
- **Loyalty of the army** — the INA trials and the naval mutiny showed Britain it could no longer rely on Indian soldiers.

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **North-East India in the freedom struggle:** Moirang (Manipur) and Kohima (Nagaland) were battlefields of 1944; the INA Memorial at Moirang marks the first hoisting of the tricolour on Indian soil.
- **National greetings:** "Jai Hind" is used today in the armed forces and public life.

### 8.3 More practice in the ICSE style

**Q1.** (3 marks) Why did the Cripps Mission fail?
**Answer:** It offered dominion status only after the war and no real power now; it allowed provinces to stay out of the Union, which threatened unity; both the Congress and the League rejected it — Gandhi called it a "post-dated cheque".

**Q2.** (3 marks) Describe the course of the Quit India Movement after 9 August 1942.
**Answer:** The leaders were arrested; the people rose spontaneously with hartals and attacks on police stations, railway stations and telegraph lines; parallel governments were set up at Ballia, Satara and Tamluk; the government crushed the movement with firing and mass arrests.

**Q3.** (4 marks) Explain the contribution of the INA to the freedom struggle.
**Answer:** It showed Indians could fight the British in arms; it united soldiers of all religions; the INA trials (1945) aroused nationwide protest; the British realised that the loyalty of the Indian armed forces could no longer be relied upon, which hastened independence.

**Q4.** (2 marks) Distinguish between the Forward Bloc and the INA.
**Answer:** The Forward Bloc (1939) was a political party in India with socialist objectives; the INA was a military organisation formed abroad (1942) to fight the British.

**Q5.** (2 marks) What were the INA trials?
**Answer:** The trial of three INA officers — Shah Nawaz Khan, P. K. Sahgal and G. S. Dhillon — at the Red Fort in 1945; nationwide protests forced the British to remit their sentences.

**Answer:** Indians must free India or die in the attempt.

Supreme Commander of the INA; Provisional Government of Free India.

Satara (Maharashtra) (also Tamluk, Bengal).

Nationwide protests forced the remission of sentences.

**Answer:** Campaign for the abolition of zamindari and the reorganisation of agriculture and industry on socialist lines.
Organise workers, peasants and youth; demand the right to work.

**Answer:** Jayaprakash Narayan.
