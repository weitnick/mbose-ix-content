## 1. Complete Lesson

### The First Phase of the national movement

- The history of the Indian National Congress before 1947 is usually divided into **three phases**:

| Phase | Years | Leaders | Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| First (Early Nationalists / Moderates) | **1885–1907** | Dadabhai Naoroji, Surendranath Banerjee, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta, W. C. Bonnerjee, M. G. Ranade | Constitutional — prayers, petitions, protests |
| Second (Assertive Nationalists / Radicals) | 1905–1916 | Lal–Bal–Pal (Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal), Aurobindo Ghosh | Swadeshi, boycott, passive resistance (Chapter 10) |
| Third (Gandhian era) | 1919–1947 | Mahatma Gandhi | Mass non-violent movements (Chapters 12–13) |

> - The leaders of the first phase were mostly **educated, middle-class** Indians — lawyers, teachers, journalists — who admired **British liberal traditions** and believed that the British would grant reforms if they were shown that Indian demands were **just**.
> - They are called **Early Nationalists** because they led the first phase, and **Moderates** because their demands and methods were moderate.

### Beliefs of the Early Nationalists

1. **Faith in the British sense of justice and fair play** — they believed that the British people were just and would listen to reasonable demands.
2. **British rule was a blessing in some ways** — it had brought modern education, railways, the telegraph, law and order, and unity to India.
3. **Gradual reform** — India was not yet ready for self-rule; it should progress **step by step** towards self-government **within the British Empire**.
4. **Loyalty to the Crown** — they were loyal to the British Crown but critical of the policies of the government in India.

### Objectives (demands) of the Early Nationalists

**A. Political / constitutional demands**
- **Expansion** of the Central and Provincial **Legislative Councils** and an increase in the number of **elected Indian members**.
- More **powers** for the Councils — discussion of the **budget**, the right to ask questions.
- Ultimately, **self-government** within the British Empire, like the self-governing colonies (Canada, Australia).

**B. Administrative demands**
- **Indianisation of the higher services** — more Indians in high posts.
- **Simultaneous Indian Civil Service examinations** in **India and England**, and **raising the age limit** for the examination.
- **Separation of the judiciary from the executive**.
- Expansion of **education**, especially primary, technical and higher education.
- Reduction of the burden of **military expenditure**.

**C. Economic demands**
- An end to the **drain of wealth** from India to Britain.
- **Reduction of land revenue** and protection of peasants from oppression.
- **Protection and development of Indian industries** through tariffs (duties on foreign goods).
- Development of **irrigation** and banking facilities for farmers.
- Reduction of salt tax and of **home charges**.

**D. Civil rights**
- Defence of the freedom of **speech, the press and association** against restrictions (they protested against the arrest of Tilak in 1897 and against the Vernacular Press Act).

### Methods of struggle of the Early Nationalists

The Early Nationalists believed in **constitutional agitation within the legal framework** and slow, orderly political progress. Their methods were:
1. **The three Ps — Prayers, Petitions and Protests** (sometimes mocked by later leaders as "political mendicancy", i.e. begging).
2. Holding **meetings** where **speeches** were made and **resolutions** for popular demands were passed.
3. Sending **memorandums** and **petitions** to government officials and to the British Parliament.
4. Using the **press**, newspapers and journals to **criticise government policies** and to educate public opinion.
5. **Distributing pamphlets and leaflets**; holding **discussions** and peaceful **processions**.
6. Sending **deputations and delegations** of Indian leaders to **Britain** to present India's case; they carried on active **propaganda** there.
7. A **British Committee of the Indian National Congress** was set up in **London in 1889**; it published a weekly journal, ***India***, to present India's case before the British public.

> **What they did not do:** they did not organise **boycotts**, **strikes** or mass movements, and they did not use **force**. These became the methods of the Assertive Nationalists and of Gandhi.

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2025, Q2(ii))* Imagine you are an Early Nationalist leader. Mention any two methods you would follow to fight against the British.
**Solution:** Any two: **prayers, petitions and protests** (the 3Ps); preparing **memorandums**; giving **speeches** and holding **meetings**; sending **deputations** to Britain; using the **press** to criticise government policies; passing **resolutions**; distributing **pamphlets and leaflets**; peaceful **propaganda**, discussions and processions.

### Achievements of the Early Nationalists

1. **Awakened national consciousness** — they created a feeling that Indians were **one nation** with common interests.
2. **Exposed the economic exploitation** of India — the **drain of wealth** theory showed that British rule was the cause of India's poverty.
3. **Trained Indians in political work** — in public meetings, debates, organisation and the use of the press.
4. **The Indian Councils Act, 1892** — the number of members in the Legislative Councils was increased, some members were **indirectly elected**, and they could **discuss the budget** and ask questions. It was their first constitutional gain.
5. **Simultaneous ICS examinations** — the British House of Commons passed a resolution (1893) for holding the ICS examination simultaneously in India and England (it was not carried out at once).
6. The **Welby Commission** (1895) was appointed to inquire into Indian expenditure; Dadabhai Naoroji was a member and Gokhale gave evidence.
7. **Built the foundation** of the national movement — the later leaders built on the platform they had created.

### Limitations

- Their movement had a **narrow social base** — mainly the educated middle class; the masses were not involved.
- Their **faith in British justice** was misplaced — the government gave few reforms.
- Their **methods** (petitions and prayers) were seen by younger leaders as **too mild**, leading to the rise of the Assertive Nationalists after 1905 (Chapter 10).

### The leaders and their contributions

#### Dadabhai Naoroji (1825–1917) — "the Grand Old Man of India"

1. **Drain of wealth theory** — he showed with figures that British rule was **draining India's wealth** to Britain (through salaries, pensions, home charges and profits), causing India's poverty. He set this out in ***Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*** (1901).
2. **Founded the East India Association** in London (**1866**) to present Indian views before the British public.
3. **First Indian member of the British House of Commons** — elected in **1892** from Central Finsbury (London), where he spoke for Indian demands.
4. **President of the Congress three times** — **1886** (Calcutta), **1893** (Lahore) and **1906** (Calcutta); at the 1906 session he declared **Swaraj** (self-government) to be the goal of the Congress.
5. **Member of the Welby Commission** (1895) on Indian expenditure.
6. A **journalist** — he founded the Gujarati paper *Rast Goftar* ("Truth Teller").

#### Surendranath Banerjee (1848–1925) — "Rashtraguru"

1. Passed the **Indian Civil Service** examination (1869) but was **dismissed** from service on a minor charge — a case that showed Indians the injustice of the system.
2. Founded the **Indian Association** (**1876**) in Calcutta — one of the forerunners of the Congress.
3. Organised an **all-India agitation** against the **reduction of the ICS age** (1877–78) — the first all-India political campaign.
4. Organised the **Indian National Conference** (**1883**), which merged with the Congress in 1886.
5. Edited the newspaper ***The Bengalee***; he was jailed (1883) for criticising a judge — the first Indian journalist to be imprisoned.
6. **President of the Congress** in **1895** (Poona) and **1902** (Ahmedabad); led the **anti-Partition agitation** in Bengal (1905).

#### Gopal Krishna Gokhale (1866–1915) — "the political guru of Gandhi"

1. **Mentor of Mahatma Gandhi** — Gandhi called him his **political guru**.
2. Founded the **Servants of India Society** (**1905**) to train **dedicated national workers** for the service of the country.
3. **President of the Congress** in **1905** (Banaras).
4. As a member of the **Imperial Legislative Council**, his **budget speeches** criticised the government's economic policies — high military expenditure, high land revenue and neglect of education.
5. Moved a Bill for **free and compulsory primary education** (1910–11).
6. Gave evidence before the **Welby Commission**; worked with the **Deccan Education Society** and taught at Fergusson College, Poona.

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2023, Q1(xi))* Identify the Early Nationalist leader. (a) Gopal Krishna Gokhale (b) Bipin Chandra Pal (c) Jyotiba Phule (d) Bal Gangadhar Tilak
**Solution:** **(a) Gopal Krishna Gokhale.** Pal and Tilak were Assertive Nationalists; Phule was a social reformer.

#### Other Early Nationalists
**Pherozeshah Mehta** (the "Lion of Bombay"), **W. C. Bonnerjee**, **Badruddin Tyabji**, **M. G. Ranade**, **K. T. Telang**, **Anand Mohan Bose**, **Romesh Chunder Dutt** (*Economic History of India*).

### How to write a 4-mark answer on this chapter

**Question:** Explain any four methods of struggle of the Early Nationalists. [4]

**Model answer (one mark per point, each point explained in one line):**
1. **Prayers, petitions and protests** — they sent petitions and appeals to the government through constitutional means.
2. **Meetings and resolutions** — they held public meetings where speeches were made and resolutions on popular demands were passed.
3. **Use of the press** — they wrote in newspapers and journals to criticise government policies and educate public opinion.
4. **Deputations to Britain** — they sent leaders to Britain and set up the British Committee of the INC (1889), which published *India*, to win British public opinion.

> **Examiner's tip:** write four separate, numbered points; do not repeat the same idea in different words ("petitions" and "memorandums" together count as one point unless each is explained).

### Summary of the chapter

- **First phase** 1885–1907; **Early Nationalists / Moderates**; educated middle class; faith in British justice; gradual reform within the Empire.
- **Demands:** expansion of councils and more elected Indians; Indianisation of services, simultaneous ICS exams; separation of judiciary and executive; reduce land revenue, military expenditure and the drain; protect Indian industries; civil liberties.
- **Methods:** 3Ps; meetings, speeches, resolutions; petitions and memorandums; press; pamphlets; deputations to Britain; British Committee of the INC (London, 1889) and the journal *India*.
- **Achievements:** national consciousness; drain theory; Indian Councils Act 1892; political training.
- **Leaders:** Naoroji (drain theory, *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*, House of Commons 1892, Swaraj 1906); Banerjee (Indian Association, ICS age agitation, *The Bengalee*, anti-Partition); Gokhale (Servants of India Society 1905, Gandhi's guru, budget speeches, primary education).

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 Dadabhai Naoroji (1825–1917)
A Parsi scholar and businessman from Bombay, the "Grand Old Man of India". He explained the drain of wealth, sat in the British House of Commons and, in 1906, declared Swaraj the goal of the Congress.

### 6.2 Surendranath Banerjee (1848–1925)
A Bengali leader, teacher and journalist known as "Rashtraguru" (teacher of the nation). He founded the Indian Association, organised the first all-India agitation and edited *The Bengalee*.

### 6.3 Gopal Krishna Gokhale (1866–1915)
A teacher from Maharashtra, founder of the Servants of India Society and a powerful critic of government finances in the Imperial Legislative Council. Gandhi regarded him as his political guru.

### 6.4 Pherozeshah Mehta (1845–1915)
A leading lawyer of Bombay, the "Lion of Bombay", who helped build the Bombay Municipal Corporation and founded the *Bombay Chronicle*.

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Constitutional struggle** — the Early Nationalists showed that a movement can begin with arguments, facts and petitions.
- **Economic nationalism** — the drain theory turned India's poverty into a political issue.
- **Foundations matter** — later movements stood on the organisation and awareness they built.

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **Petitions today:** citizens still write petitions, letters to newspapers and memorandums to authorities — the Early Nationalist way.
- **Public budget debates:** Gokhale's budget speeches resemble today's parliamentary debates on the Union Budget.

**Q1.** (3 marks) Mention three objectives of the Early Nationalists.
**Answer:** Expansion of the Legislative Councils with more elected Indians; Indianisation of the services and simultaneous ICS examinations; reduction of land revenue and protection of Indian industries (also: separation of judiciary from executive; end of the drain).

**Q2.** (4 marks) Give two contributions each of Dadabhai Naoroji and Gopal Krishna Gokhale.
**Answer:** Naoroji — explained the drain of wealth in *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*; first Indian in the British House of Commons (1892). Gokhale — founded the Servants of India Society (1905); his budget speeches criticised government finances (also: Gandhi's political guru).

**Q3.** (3 marks) Give three contributions of Surendranath Banerjee.
**Answer:** Founded the Indian Association (1876); organised the all-India agitation against the reduction of the ICS age; edited *The Bengalee* and led the anti-Partition movement (also: Indian National Conference 1883).

**Q4.** (3 marks) State three achievements of the Early Nationalists.
**Answer:** Awakened national consciousness; exposed economic exploitation through the drain theory; secured the Indian Councils Act, 1892.

**Q5.** (2 marks) Why were the methods of the Early Nationalists criticised by later leaders?
**Answer:** Prayers and petitions were seen as "political mendicancy" (begging); they produced few results and did not involve the masses.

**Q6.** (4 marks) Mention four demands of the Early Nationalists, one from each of the political, administrative, economic and civil-rights groups.
**Answer:** Political — expansion of the Legislative Councils with more elected Indians; administrative — simultaneous ICS examinations in India and England; economic — reduction of land revenue; civil rights — freedom of the press and of speech.

**Q7.** (3 marks) Name the President of the Congress at each of these sessions: (a) 1886, Calcutta (b) 1905, Banaras (c) 1895, Poona.
**Answer:** (a) Dadabhai Naoroji (b) Gopal Krishna Gokhale (c) Surendranath Banerjee.

**Q8.** (2 marks) What was the aim of the Servants of India Society?
**Answer:** To train dedicated national workers who would serve the country — in education, social reform and public life — without seeking personal gain.

**Q9.** (3 marks) Why is Dadabhai Naoroji's 1906 presidential address important?
**Answer:** At the Calcutta session he declared **Swaraj** (self-government) as the goal of the Congress; it held the Moderates and the Assertives together for a time; and it marked a shift from asking for reforms to asking for self-rule.

It published the journal *India* to present India's case to the British public.

**Q11.** Name two achievements of the Early Nationalists.
**Answer:** The Indian Councils Act, 1892.
Awakening of national consciousness and the drain theory.

**Q12.** Why was the method of the Early Nationalists called "political mendicancy"?
**Answer:** Because they relied on prayers and petitions — like begging.
Critics felt it produced few results.

### Part II parts (3 marks each)

**Q13.** Mention three objectives of the Early Nationalists.
**Answer:** Expansion of Legislative Councils with more elected Indians.
Indianisation of the services; simultaneous ICS examinations.
Reduction of land revenue and military expenditure; protection of industries.

**Q14.** Explain three methods of struggle of the Early Nationalists.
**Answer:** Prayers, petitions and protests through constitutional means.
Meetings, speeches and resolutions; the press to criticise policies.
Deputations to Britain; British Committee of the INC and the journal *India*.

**Q15.** Give three contributions of Dadabhai Naoroji.
**Answer:** Drain of wealth theory — *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*.
East India Association (1866); House of Commons (1892).
Declared Swaraj the goal of the Congress (1906).

**Q16.** Give three contributions of Surendranath Banerjee.
**Answer:** Indian Association (1876) and Indian National Conference (1883).
Agitation against the reduction of the ICS age.
Edited *The Bengalee*; led the anti-Partition movement.

**Q17.** Give three contributions of Gopal Krishna Gokhale.
**Answer:** Servants of India Society (1905).
Budget speeches in the Imperial Legislative Council.
Bill for free and compulsory primary education; mentor of Gandhi.

**Q18.** State three achievements of the Early Nationalists.
**Answer:** Awakened national consciousness.
Exposed economic exploitation — the drain theory.
Indian Councils Act, 1892; political training of Indians.

**Q19.** State three limitations of the Early Nationalists.
**Answer:** Narrow social base — mainly the educated middle class.
Misplaced faith in British justice.
Mild methods with few results.

**Q20.** Give three beliefs of the Early Nationalists.
**Answer:** Faith in British justice and fair play.
British rule had brought some benefits — education, railways, unity.
Gradual reform towards self-government within the Empire.

**Q21.** Explain the drain of wealth theory.
**Answer:** India's wealth flowed to Britain as salaries, pensions, home charges and profits.
Nothing returned to India.
This, Naoroji argued, was the cause of India's poverty.

**Q22.** What was the Indian Councils Act, 1892?
**Answer:** It enlarged the Legislative Councils.
Some members were indirectly elected.
Members could discuss the budget and ask questions.

## 12. Competency / Application Questions

**Q1. (The broken streetlight)** The streetlights in your colony have not worked for a month. As a follower of the Early Nationalists, list three things you would do.
**Model answer:** Write a petition to the municipal office; hold a residents' meeting and pass a resolution; write a letter to a newspaper highlighting the problem.

**Q2. (Moderate or not?)** Classify each as an Early Nationalist method or not: (a) a memorandum to the Viceroy; (b) a boycott of British cloth; (c) a deputation to London; (d) blocking a road.
**Model answer:** (a) and (c) are Early Nationalist methods; (b) and (d) are not.

**Q3. (A speech in Parliament)** Dadabhai Naoroji has five minutes in the House of Commons. What two points from his drain theory would he make?
**Model answer:** That India's wealth flows to Britain as salaries, pensions and home charges without return; and that this drain, not Indian laziness, causes India's poverty and famines.

**Q4. (A society for service)** Why would Gokhale want to train young people as full-time "servants of India"?
**Model answer:** To create dedicated workers who would spread education and political awareness and serve the country without seeking personal gain.

**Q5. (A fair verdict)** Were the Early Nationalists a failure? Give a balanced answer in three sentences.
**Model answer:** They won few reforms and did not involve the masses. But they awakened national consciousness, exposed economic exploitation and trained Indians in politics. The later movement was built on their foundation, so they were not a failure.

## 13. Assertion–Reason Questions

**Directions:** Read the Assertion (A) and the Reason (R). Choose the correct option:
- (a) A is true, but R is false.
- (b) A is false, but R is true.
- (c) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
- (d) Both A and R are true, but R is **not** the correct explanation of A.

**Q1.**
**A:** The Early Nationalists are called Moderates.
**R:** Their demands and methods were moderate.
**Answer:** (c)
**Explanation:** Gradual reform through constitutional means.

**Q2.**
**A:** The Early Nationalists used boycott as a method.
**R:** They believed in constitutional agitation.
**Answer:** (b)
**Explanation:** Boycott was an Assertive method; R is true.

**Q3.**
**A:** Dadabhai Naoroji wrote *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*.
**R:** He wanted to show that British rule drained India's wealth.
**Answer:** (c)
**Explanation:** The book set out the drain theory.

**Q4.**
**A:** Gokhale founded the Servants of India Society.
**R:** Surendranath Banerjee edited *The Bengalee*.
**Answer:** (d)
**Explanation:** Both true but unrelated.

**Q5.**
**A:** The Indian Councils Act, 1892 allowed members to vote on the budget.
**R:** The Act enlarged the Legislative Councils.
**Answer:** (b)
**Explanation:** Members could discuss, not vote on, the budget.

**Q6.**
**A:** Younger leaders criticised the Moderates' methods as political mendicancy.
**R:** Prayers and petitions produced few results.
**Answer:** (c)
**Explanation:** This led to the rise of the Assertive Nationalists.

**Q7.**
**A:** Surendranath Banerjee organised an all-India agitation in 1877–78.
**R:** Lord Lytton had reduced the maximum age for the ICS examination.
**Answer:** (c)
**Explanation:** The age had been cut from 21 to 19.

**Q8.**
**A:** The Early Nationalists had a mass base among the peasants.
**R:** Most of them belonged to the educated middle class.
**Answer:** (b)
**Explanation:** Their base was narrow; R is true.

## 14. Case-Based Questions

*Read each situation and answer the four questions, 1 mark each (4 × 1 = 4).*

### Case 1 — A meeting in 1890

> At a Congress session, delegates passed resolutions asking for more elected Indians in the Legislative Councils and for the ICS examination to be held in India too. They decided to send a memorandum to the Secretary of State and a deputation to London.

**(i)** Which group of leaders is this?
**Answer:** The Early Nationalists (Moderates).

**(ii)** Name one political demand mentioned.
**Answer:** More elected Indians in the Legislative Councils.

**(iii)** Name one administrative demand mentioned.
**Answer:** Simultaneous ICS examinations in India.

**(iv)** Name two methods used.
**Answer:** Resolutions and a memorandum (also: a deputation to London).

### Case 2 — Three leaders

> Leader X explained the drain of wealth and sat in the House of Commons. Leader Y founded the Indian Association and edited *The Bengalee*. Leader Z founded a society in 1905 and was called the political guru of Gandhi.

**(i)** Name X.
**Answer:** Dadabhai Naoroji.

**(ii)** Name Y.
**Answer:** Surendranath Banerjee.

**(iii)** Name Z.
**Answer:** Gopal Krishna Gokhale.

**(iv)** Name the society founded by Z.
**Answer:** The Servants of India Society.

### Case 3 — A neighbourhood problem

> Rohan's school bus route is blocked by garbage dumped on the road. His class has studied the Early Nationalists and wants to follow their methods.

**(i)** Should the class block the garbage trucks?
**Answer:** No — Early Nationalists used peaceful, legal methods.

**(ii)** Name one written method the class can use.
**Answer:** A petition to the municipal authorities.

**(iii)** Name one way to use the press.
**Answer:** A letter to a newspaper highlighting the problem.

**(iv)** Which method of the Assertive Nationalists would the class avoid?
**Answer:** Boycott (also: passive resistance).

## 15. Revision Notes

### Chapter at a glance
- First phase 1885–1907: Early Nationalists / Moderates; educated middle class.
- Beliefs: British justice; gradual reform within the Empire; loyalty to the Crown.
- Demands: councils (more elected Indians); Indianisation; simultaneous ICS exams; separation of judiciary; less land revenue and military spending; protect industries; end the drain; civil rights.
- Methods: 3Ps; meetings, speeches, resolutions; memorandums, petitions; press; pamphlets; deputations to Britain; British Committee of INC (London 1889) and *India*.
- Achievements: national consciousness; drain theory; Indian Councils Act 1892; political training.
- Limitations: narrow base; faith in British justice; few results.
- Naoroji: Grand Old Man; drain theory; *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*; East India Association 1866; House of Commons 1892; Swaraj 1906.
- Banerjee: Rashtraguru; Indian Association 1876; ICS age agitation; *The Bengalee*; anti-Partition.
- Gokhale: Servants of India Society 1905; Gandhi's guru; budget speeches; primary education Bill; President 1905.

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct idea |
|---|---|
| Early Nationalists used boycott | Boycott was an Assertive method. |
| Naoroji was the first President of the Congress | W. C. Bonnerjee was; Naoroji presided in 1886. |
| Surendranath Banerjee was an Assertive Nationalist | He was an Early Nationalist. |
| Tilak was Gandhi's political guru | Gokhale was. |
| The Moderates demanded complete independence | They wanted gradual self-government within the Empire. |
