## 1. Complete Lesson

### Background (1945–1946)

- The **Second World War** ended in **1945**. In Britain the **Labour Party** under **Clement Attlee** came to power (July 1945); it was more willing to grant India independence.
- Britain had been weakened by the war; the **INA trials** and the **Royal Indian Navy mutiny** (February 1946) showed that the loyalty of Indian forces could no longer be relied on.
- The **Simla Conference** (June 1945) under the Viceroy **Lord Wavell** failed because the Muslim League insisted that it alone could nominate Muslim members.
- In the elections of **1945–46**, the **Congress** won most of the **general seats** and the **Muslim League** won most of the **Muslim seats** — the League now spoke for a large section of Muslims and pressed for **Pakistan** (demanded since the **Lahore Resolution of 1940**).

### PART A — THE CABINET MISSION PLAN (1946)

- In **March 1946** the British Government sent a mission of three **Cabinet ministers** — **Lord Pethick-Lawrence** (Secretary of State for India), **Sir Stafford Cripps** and **A. V. Alexander** — to find a way of transferring power.
- After talks with the leaders, it announced its plan on **16 May 1946**. It **rejected the demand for a separate Pakistan** as impractical.

#### Clauses (proposals) of the Cabinet Mission Plan

1. **A Federal Union of India** — comprising the **British provinces** and the **Princely States**.
2. **Union subjects** — the Union Government would deal with **defence, foreign affairs and communications** (and could raise the money needed for these).
3. **Union executive and legislature** — the Union would have its own executive and legislature, made up of representatives of the provinces and the states.
4. **Constituent Assembly** — a Constituent Assembly of **389 members** would frame the new Constitution: **296** elected from the **British provinces** (by the provincial assemblies) and **93** from the **Princely States**.
5. **Grouping of provinces** — the British provinces would be divided into **three groups** on a communal basis:
   - **Group A** — the Hindu-majority provinces (Madras, Bombay, United Provinces, Bihar, Central Provinces, Orissa);
   - **Group B** — the Muslim-majority provinces of the north-west (Punjab, North-West Frontier Province, Sind);
   - **Group C** — Bengal and Assam.
   A province could **opt out** of its group by a majority vote of its legislature after the first general elections.
6. **Provincial autonomy** — the provinces would enjoy **full autonomy** in all subjects other than the Union subjects; **residuary powers** would rest with the provinces.
7. **Interim Government** — an **Interim Government** of **14 members**, with the support of the major parties, would be formed at the Centre until the new Constitution came into force.
8. **Commonwealth** — India would be **free to remain within the British Commonwealth or to leave it**.

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2019, Q8(c))* State any four clauses of the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946.
**Solution:** Any four: a **Federal Union** comprising the British provinces and the Princely States; the Union Government to deal with **defence, foreign affairs and communications**; the Union to have its own **executive and legislature** composed of members elected by all provinces; a **Constituent Assembly of 389 members** — **296** from the British provinces and **93** from the Princely States; the British provinces to be divided into **three groups — A, B and C** — on a communal basis, with the right to opt out by majority vote; **full provincial autonomy** in all subjects other than Union subjects; an **Interim Government** at the Centre with **14 members**; India **free to remain within the British Commonwealth or secede** from it.

#### Response of the Congress and the Muslim League

- **The Muslim League** first **accepted** the plan (June 1946), because the **grouping** of provinces (Groups B and C) gave it hope of a future Pakistan. But when **Jawaharlal Nehru** declared (July 1946) that the Congress would not be bound by the grouping, the League **withdrew its acceptance** (29 July 1946) and called for **"Direct Action"** to achieve Pakistan.
- **The Congress** **accepted** the plan for the **Constituent Assembly** (the long-term plan) but **objected to compulsory grouping** and at first refused the League-favoured scheme for the Interim Government; it later **joined the Interim Government** (September 1946).
- **Direct Action Day** (**16 August 1946**) led to terrible **communal riots** in **Calcutta**, followed by riots in **Noakhali** (Bengal), **Bihar** and elsewhere.

#### The Interim Government and the Constituent Assembly
- An **Interim Government** headed by **Jawaharlal Nehru** took office on **2 September 1946**. The **League joined** it in **October 1946** — but, as Congress leaders complained, it joined to **obstruct, not to cooperate** (its Finance Member, **Liaquat Ali Khan**, made the work of other ministers difficult).
- The **Constituent Assembly** met on **9 December 1946**; the **League boycotted** it.
- Deadlock and rioting convinced many leaders that a united India was no longer possible.

### PART B — THE MOUNTBATTEN PLAN (3 June 1947)

#### Attlee's announcement and Lord Mountbatten
- On **20 February 1947** Prime Minister **Attlee** announced that Britain would **transfer power** to Indian hands **by June 1948**.
- **Lord Louis Mountbatten** was sent as the **last Viceroy** of India (arrived March 1947).
- **Why he came:** to take the necessary steps for the **transfer of power** to Indians; to **restore peace** between the Congress and the League; and to **present a plan** (for partition) acceptable to both.

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2020, Q8(a))* (i) Name the last Viceroy of India. (ii) State any two reasons for him to come to India.
**Solution:** (i) **Lord Mountbatten.** (ii) Any two: to take the **necessary steps for the transfer of power** to the Indians; to **restore peace** between the two sections — the Congress and the League; to **present a plan for the partition**.

#### Clauses (proposals) of the Mountbatten Plan

1. **Partition into two dominions** — British India would be divided into two independent dominions, **India and Pakistan**.
2. **Bengal and Punjab** — the members of the **legislative assemblies** of Bengal and Punjab, meeting in two parts (Muslim-majority districts and the rest), would vote on whether the province should be **partitioned**.
3. **Sind** — the **legislative assembly** of Sind would decide which dominion to join.
4. **North-West Frontier Province** — a **referendum** (plebiscite) would decide whether it would join India or Pakistan.
5. **Sylhet** — a **referendum** would decide whether the Muslim-majority **district of Sylhet** (in Assam) would join East Bengal (Pakistan).
6. **Boundary Commission** — a **Boundary Commission** would fix the **boundaries** of the divided provinces (it was headed by **Sir Cyril Radcliffe**).
7. **Princely States** — British **paramountcy** would end; all **treaties** with the states would **lapse**; the states would be free to **join India or Pakistan** or to **remain independent**.
8. **Constituent Assemblies** — each dominion would have its **own Constituent Assembly** to frame its constitution.
9. **Commonwealth** — each dominion would decide its **relations** with the other and with the **British Commonwealth**.
10. **Transfer of power** — power would be transferred **before June 1948**; Mountbatten brought the date forward to **15 August 1947**.

**Example 3.** *(ICSE 2024, Q8(i))* Look at the picture given [the front page of The Times of India, 4 June 1947: "WAY OUT OF INDIAN DEADLOCK — Britain's plan for smooth transfer of power"]. Name the Viceroy who announced the plan for smooth transfer of power. Mention any two clauses of his plan with reference to the Princely States.
**Solution:** **Lord Mountbatten** (1). The Princely States could **join either India** or **Pakistan**, or **remain independent** (2).

#### Why did the Congress accept the Mountbatten Plan (Partition)?

1. **Communal riots** — partition seemed the only solution to the **large-scale communal riots** and the communal problem; further delay could plunge India into **civil war**.
2. **Experience of the Interim Government** — working with the League had convinced the Congress that a **joint administration was impossible**; the League had joined the government **to obstruct, not to cooperate**.
3. **A weak Centre was the only alternative** — the only alternative to partition was a **federation with a weak Centre** (as in the Cabinet Mission Plan); a **smaller India with a strong Centre** was better than a bigger one with a weak Centre.
4. **End of British rule** — **any further continuation of British rule** would be a **greater calamity**; the people and leaders wanted the British to go at any price.
5. **Separate electorates would go** — partition would **rid the Constitution of separate electorates** and other undemocratic procedures.
6. **Danger of many Pakistans** — the British were encouraging the **princes to remain independent**; refusing partition might result in the creation of **many Pakistans**.
7. **Direct Action Day** — the violence that followed it showed that the League would not accept a united India.

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2023, Q2(iv))* Give two reasons for the acceptance of the Mountbatten Plan by the Congress.
**Solution:** Any two: it was a solution to the **large-scale communal riots**; the League had joined the Interim Government **to obstruct and not cooperate**; the only alternative to partition was a **federation with a weak centre**; further continuity of British rule would mean a **greater calamity**; not agreeing to partition might result in the creation of **many Pakistans**; further delay could lead to a **civil war**; partition would help get rid of **separate electorates** and other undemocratic procedures; the result of **Direct Action Day**; a **small India with a strong centre** was better than a bigger state with a weak centre; the British were **encouraging the rulers of Indian states to remain independent**.

### PART C — THE INDIAN INDEPENDENCE ACT, 1947

The British Parliament passed the **Indian Independence Act** in **July 1947** (it received royal assent on **18 July 1947**) to give legal effect to the Mountbatten Plan.

#### Provisions (clauses) of the Indian Independence Act

1. **Two new dominions** — from **15 August 1947**, two independent dominions, **India and Pakistan**, would be set up.
2. **Provisions of partition** — **Bengal and Punjab** would be divided; the territories of each dominion were defined (with the NWFP and Sylhet decided by referendum).
3. **Governor-General for each dominion** — each dominion would have a **Governor-General**, appointed by the British King on the **advice of the dominion's Cabinet**.
4. **Constituent Assemblies as central legislatures** — the **Constituent Assembly** of each dominion would act as its **central legislature**, with **full powers to make laws**.
5. **End of the jurisdiction of the British Parliament** — the British Parliament's authority over India would **cease from 15 August 1947**; His Majesty's Government would cease to have any responsibility for the government of India.
6. **Title "Emperor of India" abolished** — the British monarch would no longer use the title.
7. **Government of India Act, 1935** — until new constitutions were framed, each dominion would be governed under the **Act of 1935** (which the Governor-General could adapt until 31 March 1948).
8. **Princely States** — British **paramountcy would lapse**; **all treaties and agreements** with the states would **end**; the states would be **free to join either dominion or remain independent**.
9. **Division of the army and assets** — the **Indian army** would be divided between the two dominions, and **assets and liabilities** would be shared.
10. **Office of the Secretary of State abolished** — the office of the **Secretary of State for India** would end; the interests of existing officers (ICS etc.) would be safeguarded.
11. **Bringing the Act into operation** — the **Governor-General** was given the necessary powers to bring independence into effect.

**Example 5.** *(ICSE 2019, Q2(e))* State any two provisions of the Indian Independence Act of 1947 that was to decide the fate of the Princely States.
**Solution:** Any two: the Princely States would become **independent from the British authority**; **all treaties and agreements** made by the British with the states would **lapse**; the states could **remain independent**; or **join either India or Pakistan**.

### Independence and after (outline, not tested)
- **Pakistan** became independent on **14 August 1947** with **Muhammad Ali Jinnah** as Governor-General; **India** on **15 August 1947**, with **Jawaharlal Nehru** as Prime Minister and **Lord Mountbatten** as the first Governor-General.
- The **Radcliffe Award** fixed the boundaries; partition caused the **migration** of about 1.5 crore people and **communal violence** in which lakhs died.
- **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** and **V. P. Menon** brought almost all the Princely States into India through the **Instrument of Accession**.

- **Background:** Labour government (Attlee); INA trials and RIN mutiny; Simla Conference failure; 1945–46 elections; Pakistan demand (1940).
- **Cabinet Mission** (March 1946; Pethick-Lawrence, Cripps, Alexander; plan 16 May 1946): **Federal Union**; Union — defence, foreign affairs, communications; Union executive and legislature; **Constituent Assembly of 389** (296 + 93); **Groups A, B, C**, opt-out; **provincial autonomy**; **Interim Government (14)**; freedom to leave the Commonwealth.
- **Responses:** League accepted, then withdrew — **Direct Action Day** (16 Aug 1946); Congress accepted the Constituent Assembly, opposed compulsory grouping; Interim Government (2 Sept 1946); League joined to obstruct.

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — One India, three groups
The Cabinet Mission tried to keep India united by giving it a **weak Centre** and **grouping** the provinces. Remember the numbers: **389 = 296 + 93**, **14** members of the Interim Government.

### Section 2 — From deadlock to partition
The League's Direct Action, the riots and the obstruction in the Interim Government made the Cabinet Mission Plan unworkable.

### Section 3 — The Mountbatten Plan
Learn the plan region by region: Bengal and Punjab — assemblies vote; Sind — assembly; NWFP and Sylhet — referendum; boundaries — commission; states — free to choose.

### Section 4 — The Act
The Act turned the plan into law. Its key words: **two dominions, Governor-General, Constituent Assembly, end of British Parliament's authority, princely states, division of army**.

|---|---|

| Instrument of Accession | The document by which a princely state joined India or Pakistan. |

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 Lord Louis Mountbatten (1900–1979)
The last Viceroy of British India (March–August 1947) and the first Governor-General of independent India (1947–48), who announced the plan of 3 June 1947.

### 6.2 Clement Attlee (1883–1967)
The Labour Prime Minister of Britain (1945–51) who sent the Cabinet Mission and announced the British decision to leave India.

### 6.3 Muhammad Ali Jinnah (1876–1948)
Leader of the Muslim League who led the demand for Pakistan and became its first Governor-General.

### 6.4 Jawaharlal Nehru (1889–1964)
Head of the Interim Government (1946) and first Prime Minister of independent India.

### 6.5 Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (1875–1950)
Deputy Prime Minister and Home Minister who integrated the Princely States into India.

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Unity vs. power** — the Cabinet Mission kept India united at the cost of a weak Centre; the Congress finally chose a smaller India with a strong Centre.
- **The cost of division** — partition brought freedom but also migration and violence on a vast scale.
- **Law follows politics** — the Indian Independence Act gave legal form to a political settlement.

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **Borders today:** the India–Bangladesh border through Sylhet and Bengal and the India–Pakistan border in Punjab follow the Radcliffe line.
- **Partition memory:** 14 August is now observed in India as Partition Horrors Remembrance Day.

### 8.3 More practice in the ICSE style

**Q1.** (3 marks) Explain the grouping of provinces under the Cabinet Mission Plan.
**Answer:** Group A — the Hindu-majority provinces (Madras, Bombay, UP, Bihar, CP, Orissa); Group B — Punjab, NWFP and Sind; Group C — Bengal and Assam; a province could leave its group by a majority vote after the first elections.

**Q2.** (3 marks) What happened on Direct Action Day and what were its effects?
**Answer:** On 16 August 1946 the League called for Direct Action for Pakistan; terrible riots broke out in Calcutta and spread to Noakhali and Bihar; the violence convinced many leaders that partition was unavoidable.

**Q3.** (4 marks) Distinguish between the Cabinet Mission Plan and the Mountbatten Plan (four points).
**Answer:** United India vs partition into two dominions; a weak Union with three groups vs two strong dominions; one Constituent Assembly vs two; Pakistan rejected vs Pakistan accepted.

**Q4.** (2 marks) Why did Mountbatten bring forward the date of independence?
**Answer:** To stop the growing communal violence and to transfer power quickly while the administration could still function.

**Q5.** (3 marks) State three provisions of the Indian Independence Act about government.
**Answer:** A Governor-General for each dominion; the Constituent Assemblies to act as central legislatures; until new constitutions, government under the Act of 1935.

## 15. Revision Notes

### Chapter at a glance
- Background: Attlee (Labour, 1945); INA trials; RIN mutiny (Feb 1946); Simla Conference failed (1945); 1945–46 elections; Pakistan demand (1940).
- Cabinet Mission (Mar 1946; Pethick-Lawrence, Cripps, Alexander; 16 May 1946): Federal Union; Union — defence, foreign affairs, communications; Union executive and legislature; Constituent Assembly 389 (296 + 93); Groups A, B, C with opt-out; provincial autonomy, residuary powers with provinces; Interim Government (14); Commonwealth choice.
- Responses: League accepted → withdrew (29 July 1946) → Direct Action (16 Aug 1946); Congress accepted the Constituent Assembly, opposed compulsory grouping; Interim Government (2 Sept 1946, Nehru); League joined to obstruct; Constituent Assembly met 9 Dec 1946 (League boycotted).
- Attlee: leave by June 1948 (20 Feb 1947). Mountbatten — last Viceroy; came to transfer power, restore peace, present a plan.
- Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947): two dominions; Bengal and Punjab assemblies vote; Sind assembly; NWFP and Sylhet referendums; Boundary Commission (Radcliffe); princely states free; separate Constituent Assemblies; Commonwealth; transfer on 15 Aug 1947.
- Congress acceptance: riots, civil war risk; League obstruction; weak Centre the only alternative; smaller India with strong Centre; British rule a calamity; end of separate electorates; many Pakistans; Direct Action.
- Indian Independence Act (18 July 1947): two dominions from 15 Aug 1947; Bengal and Punjab divided; Governor-General each; Constituent Assemblies as legislatures; British Parliament's authority ends; "Emperor of India" dropped; 1935 Act meanwhile; princely states free; army and assets divided; Secretary of State abolished.

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct idea |
|---|---|
| Cabinet Mission proposed partition | It proposed a united Federal Union and rejected Pakistan. |
| Writing Act provisions for the Mountbatten Plan (or the reverse) | Plan = proposals (June 1947); Act = law (July 1947) — overlap is allowed, but referendums and the Boundary Commission belong to the plan. |
| Last Viceroy — Lord Wavell | Lord Mountbatten. |
| Constituent Assembly of 389 under the Mountbatten Plan | 389 was the Cabinet Mission figure. |
| The Act was passed by Indians | It was passed by the British Parliament. |
| Princely States had to join India | They could join India or Pakistan or remain independent. |
