## 1. Complete Lesson

### India's federal set-up

> - In a **federation**, power is **divided** between a **central (Union) government** and **regional (State) governments**, and each works in its own field under a **written constitution**.
> - Article 1 of the Constitution says: **"India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States."** The word *Union* shows that the States did not come together by an agreement and **cannot leave** the Union.
> - India is therefore called a **federation with a strong centre** (or a "quasi-federal" state): it has the main features of a federation, together with some unitary features.

**Federal features of the Indian Constitution**

| Feature | What it means |
|---|---|
| Two sets of government | A Union Government for the whole country and a government in each State. |
| Division of powers | The Seventh Schedule divides subjects into three lists — the **Union List** (defence, foreign affairs, railways, currency), the **State List** (police, public health, agriculture, local government) and the **Concurrent List** (education, forests, marriage, criminal law), on which both may make laws. |
| Written and rigid constitution | The division of powers is written down and cannot be changed by one side alone; important amendments need a special majority and, for federal matters, the approval of half the States. |
| Supremacy of the Constitution | Both the Union and the States draw their powers from the Constitution and must work within it. |
| Independent judiciary | The Supreme Court settles disputes between the Union and the States and protects the Constitution. |
| Bicameral Union legislature | The **Rajya Sabha** represents the **States** in Parliament. |

> **The three lists:** when the Constitution began, the Union List had **97** subjects, the State List **66** and the Concurrent List **47**. Amendments have since moved some subjects (in 1976, education and forests went from the State List to the Concurrent List), so the present counts are about 100, 61 and 52. Subjects in **none** of the lists are **residuary** subjects — only **Parliament** can make laws on them (for example, laws on cyber crime).

**Unitary features (why the centre is strong)**

- **Single Constitution** for the Union and the States, and **single citizenship**.
- **Integrated judiciary** — one system of courts with the Supreme Court at the top.
- **All-India Services** (IAS, IPS) whose officers serve both the Union and the States.
- **Emergency provisions** — during an emergency the federation works like a unitary state.
- Parliament can make laws on State subjects in special cases (Chapter 2), and can **change the boundaries or names of States**.
- **Governors** of the States are appointed by the President.

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2017, Q1(a))* State any one federal feature of the Indian Constitution.
**Solution:** **Division of powers** between the Union and the States through the Union, State and Concurrent Lists. *(Also correct: a written and rigid constitution; supremacy of the Constitution; an independent judiciary; a bicameral Union legislature.)*

### The Union Parliament

> - **Article 79:** *"There shall be a Parliament for the Union which shall consist of the **President** and two Houses to be known respectively as the **Council of States** and the **House of the People**."*
> - The **House of the People (Lok Sabha)** is the **lower house**, directly elected by the people. The **Council of States (Rajya Sabha)** is the **upper house**, representing the States. The Hindi names were adopted in **1954**.
> - A legislature with two Houses is called **bicameral**. India chose two Houses so that the **people** (Lok Sabha) and the **States** (Rajya Sabha) are both represented, and so that hasty laws passed by one House can be **reconsidered** by the other.

**Why is the President a part of Parliament?** The President is not a member of either House and does not sit in Parliament, yet no Bill becomes law without the President's **assent**. The President also **summons** and **prorogues** the Houses, **dissolves** the Lok Sabha, **addresses** Parliament and can issue **Ordinances** when Parliament is not in session (Chapter 3).

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2020, Q1(a))* Name the two Houses of the Indian Parliament.
**Solution:** The **Lok Sabha** (House of the People) and the **Rajya Sabha** (Council of States).

### The Lok Sabha

**Composition**

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Maximum strength | **550** — not more than **530** members from the States and not more than **20** from the Union Territories (Article 81). |
| Present strength | **543** elected members (the 18th Lok Sabha, elected in 2024). |
| Nominated members | **None now.** Until January 2020 the President could nominate **two Anglo-Indians** if the community was not adequately represented; the **104th Amendment** ended this. |
| Reserved seats | Seats are reserved for **Scheduled Castes (84)** and **Scheduled Tribes (47)**. The **106th Amendment (2023)** reserves **one-third** of the seats for women from the first delimitation after the next census. |
| How seats are shared | Each State gets seats roughly in proportion to its population; Uttar Pradesh has the most (80). |

**Election**

- Members are **directly elected** by the people on the basis of **universal adult franchise** — every citizen aged **18** or more may vote (the 61st Amendment of 1988 lowered the voting age from 21).
- The country is divided into **single-member territorial constituencies**; the candidate who gets the **most votes** wins (the "first-past-the-post" system).
- Voting is by **secret ballot**, and elections are conducted by the **Election Commission of India**.

**Term**

- The normal term is **five years** from the date of its first meeting.
- The **President** can **dissolve** it earlier on the advice of the Prime Minister — for example, when the government loses its majority and no other government can be formed.
- During a **National Emergency**, Parliament can **extend** the term by law **one year at a time**, but not beyond **six months after the emergency ends**. (This was done only once — the Lok Sabha elected in 1971 sat until 1977.)

**Example 3.** *(ICSE 2019, Q1(a))* What is the normal term of office of the Lok Sabha?
**Solution:** **Five years** (from the date of its first meeting).

**Qualifications for membership** (Article 84 and the Representation of the People Act, 1951)

A candidate for the Lok Sabha must:
1. be a **citizen of India**;
2. be **not less than 25 years** of age;
3. be registered as a **voter** in some constituency in India;
4. make an **oath or affirmation** of allegiance to the Constitution before an authority appointed by the Election Commission;
5. have any other qualifications that Parliament lays down by law.

**Disqualifications** (Article 102) — a person cannot be, or remain, a member if he or she:
- holds an **office of profit** under the Union or a State government (other than offices exempted by law, such as that of a minister);
- is of **unsound mind** as declared by a court;
- is an **undischarged insolvent** (declared bankrupt and not yet cleared);
- is **not a citizen** of India or has voluntarily taken the **citizenship of another country**;
- is disqualified by a law of Parliament — for example, a person **convicted** of an offence and sentenced to **two years or more** of imprisonment;
- is disqualified for **defection** under the Tenth Schedule.

> A member's seat also falls vacant if the member is **absent** from all meetings for **60 days** without the permission of the House, or is elected to **both** Houses (one seat must be given up).

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2023, Q3(i))* Mr Raj wants to be a member of the Lok Sabha. Mention any three qualifications required to contest elections for the Lok Sabha.
**Solution:** He must be a **citizen of India**; must be at least **25 years** old; must have his name in the **electoral roll** in some part of the country. *(Also: must not be an insolvent; must not hold an office of profit; must not be of unsound mind; must not be a convicted criminal.)*

### The Anti-Defection Law

> - **Defection** means a legislator **leaving the party** on whose ticket he or she was elected, or **voting against the party's instructions (whip)**, usually for personal gain.
> - In the 1960s frequent defections brought down many State governments. The phrase **"Aaya Ram, Gaya Ram"** came from a Haryana legislator who changed parties three times in a fortnight in 1967.
> - The **52nd Amendment (1985)** added the **Tenth Schedule** — the Anti-Defection Law. The **91st Amendment (2003)** made it stricter.

**Provisions (grounds for disqualification)**

| Member | Disqualified if he or she… |
|---|---|
| Member of a political party | **voluntarily gives up** membership of the party; or **votes or abstains** in the House **against the party's direction** without its permission (unless the party condones it within 15 days). |
| Independent member | **joins a political party** after the election. |
| Nominated member | joins a political party **more than six months** after taking the seat. |

- **Exception — merger:** there is no disqualification if **at least two-thirds** of the members of a legislature party agree to **merge** with another party. (The old exception for a "split" by one-third of the members was removed in 2003.)
- **Who decides:** the **Speaker** (in the Lok Sabha) or the **Chairman** (in the Rajya Sabha). The Supreme Court held in 1992 (*Kihoto Hollohan* case) that this decision can be **reviewed by the courts**.
- **Purpose:** to give **stable governments**, stop **"horse-trading"** (buying and selling of legislators) and make members loyal to the party programme on which they were elected.
- **Criticism:** it limits a member's freedom to vote according to conscience, and decisions by the Speaker are sometimes delayed or seen as partisan.

### The Speaker of the Lok Sabha

**Election**

- The Lok Sabha **chooses two of its members** to be the **Speaker** and the **Deputy Speaker** (Article 93).
- The Speaker is elected by a **simple majority** of the members **present and voting**, usually from the ruling party or alliance; often the choice is unanimous.
- Before the Speaker is chosen, the President appoints a **Speaker *pro tem*** (usually a senior member), who administers the oath to newly elected members and presides over the election of the Speaker.

**Term and removal**

- The Speaker holds office until the new Lok Sabha meets — even after a **dissolution** the Speaker does not vacate office until **immediately before the first meeting** of the new House.
- The Speaker may **resign** by writing to the Deputy Speaker.
- The Speaker can be **removed** by a resolution of the Lok Sabha passed by a **majority of all the then members** of the House, after **14 days' notice** (Article 94).
- While such a resolution is being considered, the Speaker **cannot preside**, but may speak and **vote in the first instance** (not a casting vote).
- The Speaker also ceases to hold office on ceasing to be a member of the Lok Sabha.

**Example 5.** *(ICSE 2025 Improvement, Q3(ii))* How and by whom is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha elected?
**Solution:** The Speaker is elected **by the members of the Lok Sabha from among themselves**, by a **simple majority** of the members **present and voting**, at the first meeting of a newly elected House; a **Speaker *pro tem*** presides over the election.

**Powers and functions of the Speaker**

| Area | Functions |
|---|---|
| Presiding | **Presides** over the meetings of the Lok Sabha and conducts its business; decides who may speak and the **order of speeches**; asks a member to yield the floor. |
| Order and discipline | **Maintains order**; can **suspend** an unruly member or ask a member to withdraw; can **adjourn** the House or **suspend** the sitting in case of **grave disorder**; **expunges** (removes) unparliamentary words from the record. |
| Quorum | If there is **no quorum** (one-tenth of the members), adjourns the House or suspends the meeting until there is one. |
| Questions and motions | Decides which **questions, motions and resolutions** are **admissible**; allows or disallows **adjournment motions**. |
| Money Bills | **Certifies** whether a Bill is a **Money Bill** — the decision is **final**. |
| Voting | Puts questions to vote and **announces results**; does not vote in the first instance but has a **casting vote** when votes are **equal (a tie)**. |
| Joint sitting | **Presides** over a **joint sitting** of the two Houses. |
| Members | Decides questions of **disqualification for defection**; protects the **privileges** of members and decides whether there is a **breach of privilege** or **contempt of the House**. |
| Bills and records | **Signs (authenticates)** every Bill passed by the House; **communicates** the decisions of the House to the President and other authorities. |
| Committees | **Appoints** the chairpersons of committees and is the ex-officio chairperson of committees such as the Business Advisory Committee and the Rules Committee. |
| Administration | Controls the **Lok Sabha Secretariat**; regulates the admission of **visitors and the press**; **interprets** the rules of the House. |

> **An impartial office:** the Speaker's salary is charged on the **Consolidated Fund of India** (it is not voted on), the Speaker's conduct of the House cannot be questioned in a court, and the Speaker is expected to be impartial to all parties.

**Example 6.** *(ICSE 2017, Q1(e))* When can the Speaker of the Lok Sabha cast his vote?
**Solution:** Only when the votes are **equally divided (a tie)** — the Speaker then uses a **casting vote**.

### The Rajya Sabha

**Composition**

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Maximum strength | **250** — **238** representatives of the **States and Union Territories** and **12** members **nominated** by the President (Article 80). |
| Present strength | **245** — 233 elected and 12 nominated. |
| Nominated members | The President nominates 12 persons with **special knowledge or practical experience** in **literature, science, art and social service**. |
| How seats are shared | Seats are allotted to the States roughly according to population (the Fourth Schedule): Uttar Pradesh has 31; small States such as Meghalaya have 1. Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu and Kashmir also elect members. |

**Election**

- Members are **indirectly elected** by the **elected members of the State Legislative Assemblies**.
- The voting is by the system of **proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote** — a voter ranks the candidates 1, 2, 3 …, and surplus votes pass to the next preference, so that smaller parties also win seats.
- Since 2003 the voting is by **open ballot** (a member shows the vote to the party's agent) to prevent cross-voting for money.

**Qualifications:** a citizen of India, **not less than 30 years** of age, registered as a voter, with the same disqualifications as for the Lok Sabha.

**Term — a permanent House**

- The Rajya Sabha is a **permanent body** — it is **never dissolved as a whole** (Article 83).
- Each member is elected for **six years**.
- **One-third** of the members **retire every two years**, and new members are elected in their place (biennial elections), so the House always has members with experience.

**Presiding Officer**

- The **Vice-President of India** is the **ex-officio Chairman** of the Rajya Sabha. The Vice-President is **not a member** of the House.
- The Rajya Sabha elects a **Deputy Chairman** from among its members, who presides in the Chairman's absence.
- The Chairman's functions are like the Speaker's — presiding, maintaining order, adjourning the House, deciding on admissibility of questions, casting vote in a tie, and deciding disqualification for defection — but the Chairman **does not** certify Money Bills or preside over joint sittings.

**Example 7.** *(ICSE 2024, Q3(i))* The Rajya Sabha is called a Permanent House. Explain this statement.
**Solution:** The Rajya Sabha is **never dissolved as a whole**. Its members are elected for **six years**, and **one-third** of them **retire every two years**, new members being elected in their place. So the House continues without a break, unlike the Lok Sabha, which is dissolved every five years.

**Example 8.** *(ICSE 2025, Q1(ii))* Given below are details of a few Indian citizens. W: 25, a famous journalist; X: 45, an industrialist; Y: 35, has declared insolvency; Z: 50, has taken citizenship of another country. Select the one who is eligible to be a member of the Rajya Sabha.
**Solution:** **X.** W is under 30; Y is an insolvent; Z is no longer an Indian citizen.

### Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha compared

| Point | Lok Sabha | Rajya Sabha |
|---|---|---|
| Also called | House of the People; lower House | Council of States; upper House |
| Maximum strength | 550 | 250 (238 + 12 nominated) |
| Present strength | 543 | 245 |
| Election | Direct, by the people | Indirect, by elected members of State Assemblies (proportional representation, single transferable vote) |
| Nominated members | None (since 2020) | 12 |
| Minimum age | 25 years | 30 years |
| Term | 5 years; can be dissolved earlier | Permanent; members serve 6 years; one-third retire every 2 years |
| Presiding officer | Speaker (a member, elected by the House) | Chairman — the Vice-President (not a member) |
| Represents | The people | The States |

### Summary of the chapter

- India is a **Union of States** — a federation with a strong centre: two governments, three lists, a written constitution and an independent judiciary, with unitary features such as single citizenship and emergency powers.
- **Parliament = President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha.**
- **Lok Sabha:** maximum 550, now 543, directly elected; age 25; term 5 years, can be dissolved; extended during a National Emergency one year at a time.
- **Anti-Defection Law** (Tenth Schedule, 1985): a member who leaves the party, defies the whip, or (if independent) joins a party is disqualified; merger by two-thirds is allowed; the Speaker or Chairman decides.
- **Speaker:** elected by the Lok Sabha; removed by a majority of all members after 14 days' notice; presides, keeps order, certifies Money Bills, casting vote, presides over joint sittings.
- **Rajya Sabha:** 250 maximum (238 + 12), now 245; elected by MLAs by proportional representation; age 30; permanent House, 6-year terms, one-third retire every 2 years; the Vice-President is Chairman.

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — Federal set-up
The Union and the States each have their own field of power, written into three lists. India's federation is "indestructible": a State cannot leave it, and Parliament can even reorganise States. That is why it is described as a federation with a unitary bias.

### Section 2 — The Lok Sabha
The Lok Sabha is the House of the people, elected directly every five years. Its numbers and its five-year term are the most-asked facts; eligibility questions test the age of 25, citizenship and the disqualifications.

### Section 3 — The Anti-Defection Law
The Tenth Schedule disqualifies members who change sides, protecting stable government. Learn the three grounds, the two-thirds merger exception and the role of the Speaker or Chairman.

### Section 4 — The Speaker
The Speaker is the guardian of the Lok Sabha's procedure and dignity. Questions ask for the election, the casting vote and four functions — order, quorum, Money Bills, joint sittings and disqualification are the safest points.

### Section 5 — The Rajya Sabha
The Rajya Sabha represents the States, is indirectly elected and never dissolves. The numbers 250 = 238 + 12 and "one-third retire every two years" are asked almost every year.

## 4. Vocabulary (Key Terms)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Federation | A system in which power is divided between a central government and regional governments by a written constitution. |
| Union List | The list of subjects on which only Parliament can make laws (defence, foreign affairs, currency). |
| State List | The list of subjects on which normally only State legislatures make laws (police, agriculture, public health). |
| Concurrent List | Subjects on which both Parliament and State legislatures can make laws; a Union law prevails in a conflict. |
| Residuary powers | Power to make laws on subjects in none of the three lists; it belongs to Parliament. |
| Bicameral | Having two Houses. |
| Universal adult franchise | The right of every adult citizen (18 and above) to vote, without discrimination. |
| Constituency | An area that elects a representative. |
| Office of profit | A government post that carries a salary or other benefit; holding one disqualifies a person from being a member. |
| Insolvent | A person declared unable to pay debts (bankrupt). |
| Defection | Leaving one's party, or defying its whip, after being elected on its ticket. |
| Whip | A party's instruction to its members on how to vote. |
| Speaker *pro tem* | A temporary Speaker who swears in new members and presides until the Speaker is elected. |
| Casting vote | The deciding vote of the presiding officer when the votes are equal. |
| Proportional representation | An electoral system in which parties get seats in proportion to their votes. |
| Single transferable vote | A voter ranks the candidates; votes not needed by an elected candidate pass to the next preference. |
| Ex officio | By virtue of holding an office (the Vice-President is ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha). |
| Dissolution | The ending of the life of the Lok Sabha, followed by a general election. |
| Expunge | To remove words from the official record of the House. |

## 5. Important Facts, Formulas and Results

### 5.1 Must-know facts

| Item | Lok Sabha | Rajya Sabha |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum strength | 550 | 250 |
| Present strength | 543 | 245 |
| Nominated | 0 (since 2020) | 12 |
| Minimum age | 25 | 30 |
| Term | 5 years | 6 years; one-third retire every 2 years |
| Presiding officer | Speaker | Vice-President (Chairman) |

- Parliament = President + two Houses (Article 79).
- Speaker: elected by the Lok Sabha; removed by a majority of all the then members after 14 days' notice; casting vote only in a tie.
- Anti-Defection Law: Tenth Schedule, 52nd Amendment, 1985; merger allowed with two-thirds of the party.
- Term of the Lok Sabha can be extended during a National Emergency, one year at a time.

### 5.2 Useful facts

- The first Lok Sabha was elected in 1951–52 and first met on 13 May 1952; the 18th Lok Sabha was elected in 2024.
- Reserved seats in the Lok Sabha: 84 for Scheduled Castes, 47 for Scheduled Tribes.
- The voting age was lowered from 21 to 18 by the 61st Amendment (1988).
- Quorum = one-tenth of the total membership of a House.

### 5.3 Beyond the text (general knowledge, not needed for exam answers)

- The new Parliament building in New Delhi was inaugurated in May 2023; the Lok Sabha chamber seats 888 and the Rajya Sabha chamber 384, so that joint sittings can be held in the Lok Sabha chamber.
- The 106th Amendment (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, 2023) reserves one-third of Lok Sabha and Assembly seats for women after the next delimitation.

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 G. V. Mavalankar (1888–1956)
Ganesh Vasudev Mavalankar was the **first Speaker of the Lok Sabha** (1952–1956). A lawyer from Gujarat and earlier President of the Central Legislative Assembly, he laid down many of the rules and traditions of the House, and is called the "Father of the Lok Sabha".

### 6.2 Meira Kumar (born 1945)
Meira Kumar was the **first woman Speaker** of the Lok Sabha (2009–2014). A former diplomat, she presided over the 15th Lok Sabha.

### 6.3 Dr Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan (1888–1975)
The philosopher and teacher was the **first Vice-President** of India and so the **first Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** (1952–1962), later the second President. His birthday, 5 September, is celebrated as Teachers' Day.

### 6.4 Om Birla (born 1962)
A member from Kota, Rajasthan, Om Birla was elected Speaker of the 17th Lok Sabha in 2019 and again of the 18th Lok Sabha in 2024.

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Two Houses, two kinds of representation** — the Lok Sabha speaks for the people, the Rajya Sabha for the States.
- **Rules make debate possible** — the Speaker's powers exist so that 543 people with strong views can discuss and decide in an orderly way.
- **Stability versus freedom** — the Anti-Defection Law gives stable governments but limits a member's freedom to vote.

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **Your MP:** find the name of the Lok Sabha member for your constituency and the Rajya Sabha members from your State.
- **News:** reports such as "House adjourned amid uproar" show the Speaker or Chairman using the power to adjourn for grave disorder.
- **Nominated members:** famous nominated members of the Rajya Sabha have included the cricketer Sachin Tendulkar and the athlete P. T. Usha.

**2019 Q1(c).** The **Speaker**.

**2020 Q1(c).** The Speaker is **elected by the members of the Lok Sabha** from among themselves, by a **simple majority** of the members present and voting.

**2025 Improvement Q3(ii).** The Speaker is elected **by the members of the Lok Sabha** (1) **from among its own members** (1), by a **simple majority of the members present and voting**, at the first meeting of the newly elected House, the election being conducted by a **Speaker *pro tem*** appointed by the President (1). *(Worked answer.)*

**2020 Q3(c).** Any four: **regulates the proceedings** of the House and presides over its meetings; **maintains discipline** and preserves order; **certifies a Bill as a Money Bill**; **puts issues to vote** and announces the results; uses a **casting vote** in case of a tie; **presides over the joint session** of Parliament; recognises a member on the floor and decides the order of speeches; **expunges unparliamentary words**; decides which questions to admit; **can adjourn the House**; decides on **breach of privilege** or contempt of the House; regulates the press and visitors; appoints the chairmen of parliamentary committees; **disqualifies members under the anti-defection law**; signs all Bills; interprets the rules of the House.

**2024 specimen Q3(iii).** Any four: the Speaker **presides** over the House and decides who speaks and in what order; **maintains order and discipline** — can ask an unruly member to withdraw or **suspend** the member; can **adjourn** the House or **suspend** the sitting in case of **grave disorder**; **expunges** unparliamentary words from the record; adjourns the House if there is **no quorum**; decides the **admissibility of questions and motions**; decides whether there is a **breach of privilege**. *(Worked answer.)*

**2026 Q3(ii).** The members are elected by the **elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the States** (and of Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu and Kashmir), by proportional representation through the single transferable vote (1). Any two exclusive powers (1 + 1): it can declare by a **two-thirds majority** that **Parliament may make laws on a subject in the State List** in the national interest; it can authorise the creation of a **new All-India Service** by a two-thirds majority; as a permanent House it **continues to function** — for example, to approve an emergency proclamation — when the Lok Sabha is **dissolved**. *(Worked answer.)*

**2024 Q3(i).** It **cannot be dissolved as a whole**, because **one-third** of its members **retire every two years** and new members are elected in their place; each member has a term of six years, so the House is never empty.

**2024 Q3(ii).** A total of **250** members; **238** are elected by the members of the **State Legislative Assemblies**; **12** members are **nominated by the President**.

**2026/2027 specimen Q3(ii).** The session is adjourned **sine die** by the **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha — the Vice-President of India** (1). Composition (2): a total of **250** members — **238** elected from the States and Union Territories and **12 nominated by the President**.

**2024 specimen Q1(ii).** **(c) Y.** W is an undischarged insolvent (bankrupt); X is below 30; Z has been convicted and sentenced to two years' imprisonment, which disqualifies a person; belonging to a backward class is no bar. *(Worked answer.)*

**2018 Q3(b).** Any three (1 each): the person must be a **citizen of India**; must be **not less than 25 years of age**; must **not hold an office of profit** under the Government; must not be an **insolvent**, of **unsound mind** or a convicted **criminal**.

**2019 Q3(b).** The Rajya Sabha is **never dissolved as a whole** (it is a continuing body); **one-third of its members retire every two years** and are replaced by newly elected members, each member serving **six years** — so the House always continues.

### 8.3 More practice in the ICSE style

**Q1.** (3 marks) India is described as a "federation with a strong centre". Give three unitary features that justify this description.
**Answer:** Single constitution and single citizenship; an integrated judiciary with the Supreme Court at the top; emergency provisions under which the federation works like a unitary state *(also: All-India Services; Governors appointed by the President; Parliament can change the boundaries of States)*.

**Q2.** (3 marks) State three grounds on which a member of a party is disqualified under the Anti-Defection Law, and name the authority that decides.
**Answer:** Voluntarily giving up party membership; voting or abstaining against the party whip without permission; (for an independent) joining a party after election. The Speaker (or the Chairman) decides.

**Q3.** (4 marks) Distinguish between the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha on four points.
**Answer:** Election — direct v. indirect; age — 25 v. 30; term — 5 years and dissolvable v. permanent with 6-year terms; presiding officer — Speaker v. Vice-President (Chairman) *(also: strength 550 v. 250; nominated members 0 v. 12)*.

**Q4.** (2 marks) Why is the President considered a part of Parliament though not a member of either House?
**Answer:** No Bill becomes law without the President's assent; the President summons and prorogues the Houses and dissolves the Lok Sabha.

**Q5.** (3 marks) Explain the procedure for the removal of the Speaker.
**Answer:** A resolution for removal is moved after 14 days' notice; it must be passed by a majority of all the then members of the Lok Sabha; while it is under consideration the Speaker cannot preside, but may speak and vote in the first instance.

## 9. MCQs with Explanations

### Federal set-up and the Union Parliament (Q1–Q18)

**Q1.** Article 1 of the Constitution describes India as a
- (a) Union of States
- (b) Federation of States
- (c) Confederation of States
- (d) League of States

**Answer:** (a) Union of States
**Explanation:** "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States."

**Q2.** The subjects on which both Parliament and State legislatures can make laws are in the
- (a) Union List
- (b) State List
- (c) Concurrent List
- (d) Residuary List

**Answer:** (c) Concurrent List
**Explanation:** A Union law prevails in case of conflict.

**Q3.** Which subject is in the Union List?
- (a) Defence
- (b) Police
- (c) Agriculture
- (d) Public health

**Answer:** (a) Defence
**Explanation:** Defence, foreign affairs and currency are Union subjects.

**Q4.** Residuary powers belong to
- (a) the State legislatures
- (b) the Supreme Court
- (c) the President alone
- (d) Parliament

**Answer:** (d) Parliament
**Explanation:** Subjects in none of the lists are for Parliament.

**Q5.** Which of these is a federal feature of the Indian Constitution?
- (a) Single citizenship
- (b) Integrated judiciary
- (c) Division of powers between the Union and the States
- (d) All-India Services

**Answer:** (c) Division of powers between the Union and the States
**Explanation:** The other three are unitary features.

**Q6.** Which of these is a unitary feature of the Indian Constitution?
- (a) Written constitution
- (b) Single citizenship
- (c) Independent judiciary
- (d) Two sets of government

**Answer:** (b) Single citizenship
**Explanation:** Indians are citizens of India only, not of their States.

- (b) it has unitary features such as emergency powers and a single constitution

**Q15.** The Hindi names Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha were adopted in

## 10. Short-Answer Questions (2 and 3 marks)

### Part I questions (2 marks each)

**Q1.** State two federal features of the Indian Constitution.
**Answer:** Division of powers between the Union and the States through three lists.
An independent judiciary (also: a written constitution; a bicameral legislature).

**Q2.** State two unitary features of the Indian Constitution.
**Answer:** Single citizenship.
Emergency provisions (also: integrated judiciary; All-India Services).

**Q3.** What is meant by residuary powers? Who exercises them?
**Answer:** The power to make laws on subjects not in any of the three lists.
Parliament exercises them.

**Q4.** State the maximum and the present strength of the Lok Sabha.
**Answer:** Maximum 550.
At present 543.

**Q5.** Under what circumstances can the term of the Lok Sabha be extended, and for how long?
**Answer:** During a National Emergency.
By law, one year at a time, but not beyond six months after the emergency ends.

**Q6.** State two disqualifications for membership of Parliament.
**Answer:** Holding an office of profit under the government.
Being an undischarged insolvent (also: unsound mind; not a citizen; conviction).

**Q7.** What is defection? Name the Schedule that deals with it.
**Answer:** Leaving one's party, or defying its whip, after being elected on its ticket.
The Tenth Schedule.

**Q8.** State the exception to the Anti-Defection Law.
**Answer:** A merger of the party with another party.
At least two-thirds of the members of the legislature party must agree.

**Q9.** When does the Speaker use a casting vote? Who presides over a joint sitting?
**Answer:** When the votes are equal (a tie).
The Speaker of the Lok Sabha.

**Q10.** What is the composition of the Rajya Sabha?
**Answer:** A maximum of 250 members.
238 representatives of the States and Union Territories and 12 nominated by the President.

**Q11.** How are the twelve nominated members of the Rajya Sabha chosen?
**Answer:** They are nominated by the President.
From persons with special knowledge or practical experience of literature, science, art and social service.

**Q12.** Who is the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha? Who presides in the Chairman's absence?
**Answer:** The Vice-President of India (ex officio).
The Deputy Chairman.

### Part II parts (3 marks each)

**Q13.** Explain the meaning of the federal set-up in India.
**Answer:** Power is divided between the Union Government and the State Governments.
The division is made by a written constitution through the Union, State and Concurrent Lists.
Both work in their own fields; disputes are settled by an independent Supreme Court — though the centre is made strong by unitary features.

**Q14.** Mention three qualifications for membership of the Lok Sabha.
**Answer:** Must be a citizen of India.
Must be at least 25 years old.
Must be a registered voter in some constituency (and must not hold an office of profit or be an insolvent).

**Q15.** How are members of the Lok Sabha elected?
**Answer:** Directly by the people.
On the basis of universal adult franchise (all citizens aged 18 and above).
From single-member territorial constituencies, the candidate with the most votes winning, by secret ballot.

**Q16.** State three grounds of disqualification under the Anti-Defection Law.
**Answer:** A member voluntarily gives up membership of the party.
A member votes or abstains against the party's direction without permission.
An independent member joins a political party after the election.

**Q17.** How is the Speaker elected and removed?
**Answer:** Elected by the members of the Lok Sabha from among themselves by a simple majority.
Removed by a resolution passed by a majority of all the then members.
After 14 days' notice.

**Q18.** Explain three functions of the Speaker relating to order in the House.
**Answer:** Maintains order and can suspend an unruly member.
Can adjourn the House or suspend the sitting in case of grave disorder.
Expunges unparliamentary words from the record.

**Q19.** How are the members of the Rajya Sabha elected?
**Answer:** By the elected members of the State Legislative Assemblies.
The election is indirect.
By proportional representation through the single transferable vote (by open ballot).

**Q20.** Why is the Rajya Sabha called a permanent House?
**Answer:** It is never dissolved as a whole.
Its members are elected for six years.
One-third of them retire every two years and are replaced by newly elected members.

**Q21.** State three points of difference between the Speaker and the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
**Answer:** The Speaker is a member of the Lok Sabha; the Chairman (the Vice-President) is not a member of the Rajya Sabha.
The Speaker certifies Money Bills; the Chairman does not.
The Speaker presides over joint sittings; the Chairman does not.

**Q22.** Why did the Constitution makers provide for a second House?
**Answer:** To represent the States in the Union legislature.
To reconsider and revise hasty legislation passed by the Lok Sabha.
To give a place to experienced and eminent persons (through nomination and indirect election).

## 11. Long-Answer Questions (4 marks each)

### Part II parts (4 marks each)

**Q1.** List any four functions of the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
**Answer:** Presides over the meetings of the House and regulates its proceedings.
Maintains order and discipline; can adjourn the House in case of grave disorder.
Certifies whether a Bill is a Money Bill; the decision is final.
Uses a casting vote in a tie and presides over joint sittings (also: decides disqualification for defection; decides the admissibility of questions; expunges words).

**Q2.** Distinguish between the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha on four points.
**Answer:** Lok Sabha — direct election; Rajya Sabha — indirect election by MLAs.
Lok Sabha — minimum age 25; Rajya Sabha — 30.
Lok Sabha — five-year term, can be dissolved; Rajya Sabha — permanent, six-year terms, one-third retire every two years.
Lok Sabha — presided over by the Speaker; Rajya Sabha — by the Vice-President as Chairman.

**Q3.** Explain the Anti-Defection Law: its grounds, its exception and the deciding authority.
**Answer:** Grounds: voluntarily giving up party membership; voting or abstaining against the whip.
An independent member joining a party; a nominated member joining a party after six months.
Exception: merger supported by two-thirds of the legislature party.
The Speaker (Lok Sabha) or Chairman (Rajya Sabha) decides; the decision can be reviewed by courts.

**Q4.** Describe the composition of the Lok Sabha.
**Answer:** A maximum of 550 members — up to 530 from the States and up to 20 from the Union Territories.
At present 543 members, all directly elected.
Seats reserved for Scheduled Castes (84) and Scheduled Tribes (47); one-third will be reserved for women after the next delimitation.
No nominated members since the 104th Amendment (2020).

**Q5.** Mention four disqualifications for membership of either House.
**Answer:** Holding an office of profit under the Union or a State government.
Being of unsound mind, as declared by a court.
Being an undischarged insolvent; not being a citizen of India.
Being convicted and sentenced to two years or more; defection under the Tenth Schedule.

**Q6.** Explain four ways in which the Speaker ensures the smooth working of the Lok Sabha.
**Answer:** Decides who may speak and in what order.
Decides the admissibility of questions and motions.
Adjourns the House if there is no quorum.
Keeps order — suspends unruly members and expunges unparliamentary words.

**Q7.** Explain the composition, election and term of the Rajya Sabha.
**Answer:** Maximum 250 — 238 elected and 12 nominated (now 245).
Elected by the elected members of State Assemblies by proportional representation (single transferable vote).
Members serve six years.
One-third retire every two years; the House is never dissolved.

**Q8.** "India is a federation with a strong centre." Justify with two federal and two unitary features.
**Answer:** Federal: division of powers through three lists.
Federal: an independent judiciary to settle Union–State disputes.
Unitary: single citizenship and a single constitution.
Unitary: emergency provisions under which the centre controls the States.

## 12. Competency / Application Questions

**Q1. (Eligibility check)** A — 24, graduate; B — 32, holds a salaried post in a State government department; C — 31, a farmer; D — 45, declared insolvent last year. Who can contest the Rajya Sabha election?
**Model answer:** Only C. A is below 30; B holds an office of profit; D is an undischarged insolvent.

**Q2. (Uproar in the House)** Members rush into the well of the House and shout slogans; debate becomes impossible. What can the Speaker do?
**Model answer:** Ask the members to return to their seats; name and suspend the unruly members; adjourn the House or suspend the sitting because of grave disorder; expunge any unparliamentary words.

**Q3. (Party change)** An MP elected on Party X's ticket votes against the party whip on an important Bill without permission. What may happen to the MP, and who decides?
**Model answer:** The MP can be disqualified under the Anti-Defection Law (Tenth Schedule), unless Party X condones the act within 15 days; the Speaker decides.

**Q4. (A dissolved Lok Sabha)** The Lok Sabha has been dissolved and elections are due. Which House of Parliament continues to exist, and why?
**Model answer:** The Rajya Sabha, because it is a permanent House that is never dissolved; only one-third of its members retire every two years.

**Q5. (Your constituency)** Explain to a younger student why every adult in your constituency, rich or poor, has one vote in a Lok Sabha election.
**Model answer:** India follows universal adult franchise — every citizen aged 18 or above has an equal vote, so the Lok Sabha represents all the people equally.

**Q6. (Small and large States)** Why does Meghalaya have only one Rajya Sabha member while Uttar Pradesh has thirty-one?
**Model answer:** Rajya Sabha seats are given to the States roughly in proportion to their population (Fourth Schedule).

## 14. Case-Based Questions

*Read each situation and answer the four questions, 1 mark each (4 × 1 = 4).*

### Case 1 — A new Lok Sabha

> The results of the general election have been declared. The newly elected members gather for the first meeting of the House. A senior member, appointed by the President, administers the oath to them. Two days later the House elects its presiding officer by a simple majority.

**(i)** Name the senior member who administers the oath.
**Answer:** The Speaker *pro tem*.

**(ii)** Name the presiding officer elected two days later.
**Answer:** The Speaker.

**(iii)** For how long will this House normally last?
**Answer:** Five years from its first meeting.

**(iv)** Who can dissolve it earlier?
**Answer:** The President (on the advice of the Prime Minister).

### Case 2 — A disorderly day

> During a debate, some members shout slogans and refuse to return to their seats. Later in the afternoon, only 40 of the 543 members are present when a Bill is to be discussed.

**(i)** What can the presiding officer do about the members who refuse to obey?
**Answer:** Name and suspend them (or ask them to withdraw).

**(ii)** What is the minimum number needed for the House to transact business?
**Answer:** The quorum — one-tenth of the members, that is 55.

**(iii)** What will the presiding officer do in the afternoon?
**Answer:** Adjourn the House or suspend the sitting for lack of quorum.

**(iv)** Who removes unparliamentary words used in the debate from the record?
**Answer:** The Speaker (by expunging them).

### Case 3 — The Rajya Sabha elections

> Every two years elections are held for some seats of the Rajya Sabha. In one State, the members of the Legislative Assembly mark their ballots 1, 2, 3 in order of preference and show them to their party's agent before dropping them in the box.

**(i)** Why are elections held for only some seats every two years?
**Answer:** One-third of the members retire every two years.

**(ii)** Name the system of voting described.
**Answer:** Proportional representation by single transferable vote.

**(iii)** Why do the members show their ballots to the party agent?
**Answer:** Voting is by open ballot (since 2003), to prevent cross-voting.

**(iv)** For how long will the newly elected members hold office?
**Answer:** Six years.

## 15. Revision Notes

### Chapter at a glance
- India: "a Union of States"; federal features (two governments, three lists, written constitution, independent judiciary, bicameral legislature) and unitary features (single citizenship, integrated judiciary, emergency powers, All-India Services).
- Parliament = President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha.
- Lok Sabha: max 550 (530 + 20), now 543, direct election, age 25, 5 years, dissolvable; extended in emergency one year at a time; no nominated members since 2020.
- Disqualifications: office of profit, unsound mind, insolvency, foreign citizenship, conviction (2 years+), defection.
- Anti-Defection Law: Tenth Schedule (1985); grounds; merger by two-thirds; Speaker/Chairman decides.
- Speaker: elected by the House; removed by a majority of all members after 14 days' notice; presides, order, quorum, Money Bill certificate, casting vote, joint sitting.
- Rajya Sabha: max 250 (238 + 12), now 245; indirect election by MLAs (PR-STV, open ballot); age 30; permanent; 6 years; one-third retire every 2 years; Vice-President is Chairman.

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct idea |
|---|---|
| The Lok Sabha has two nominated members | Not since January 2020 (104th Amendment). |
| The Rajya Sabha can be dissolved by the President | It is never dissolved; only the Lok Sabha is. |
| The Speaker can dissolve the Lok Sabha | Only the President dissolves it. |
| The Speaker votes on every Bill | Only a casting vote in a tie. |
| Rajya Sabha members are elected by the people | Indirectly, by the elected members of State Assemblies. |
| The Vice-President is a member of the Rajya Sabha | The Vice-President presides as ex-officio Chairman but is not a member. |
| Age 25 for both Houses | 25 for the Lok Sabha; 30 for the Rajya Sabha. |
| The Chairman certifies Money Bills | Only the Speaker does. |
