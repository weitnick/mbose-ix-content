## 1. Complete Lesson

### What Question 3 asks

Question 3 has **two linked parts** about **one event** at your school:

| Year | (i) Notice | (ii) E-mail |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | an interschool debate competition at your school | to the principal of a neighbouring school, informing him/her of the competition and requesting a team |
| 2025 | an Art and Craft exhibition | to a local artist, inviting him/her to inaugurate the exhibition as Chief Guest |
| 2026 | an Inter House Poetry Writing Festival | to an eminent poet, inviting him/her to inaugurate the event |
| 2027 | a poster designing competition | to a well-known artist, inviting her/him to judge the event |

The pattern is clear: the **notice** informs **students**; the **e-mail** invites or informs **an outsider** — a guest, a judge or another school — about **the same event**.

### The CISCE notice format

CISCE's 2026 samples and 2027 scheme show a short, **title-led** notice:

| Line | What to write | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. A creative title | a catchy name for the event | *Canvas of Creativity* / *Words Worth!* |
| 2. The event | the full name of the event | Annual Inter-School Poster Designing Competition |
| 3. Date | a single date, about **ten days to a fortnight** after the notice | on 26th February 2027 |
| 4. Time | start and end | from 10.00 a.m. to 3.00 p.m. |
| 5. Venue | from the smaller place to the larger | at Carnation Hall, St. Helen's School |
| 6. Who may take part and how | who, with whom to register, by when (**two or three days** after the notice) | Pupils of Classes IX and X who wish to participate should give their names to the Vice Principal on or before 17th February 2027. |

> **Traditional format:** Many schools also teach a boxed notice with the school's name, the word NOTICE, the date of issue, a heading, the body and the signature and designation of the issuer. This is not wrong, but CISCE's own samples use the shorter, title-led format above — and that is what the marking scheme checks: **title, event, date, time, venue, participation**.

### The CISCE e-mail format

The 2027 scheme lists the parts of the e-mail:

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| E-mail ID (To) | artist.meera@gmail.com |
| Subject | To judge the Annual Inter-School Poster Designing Competition |
| Salutation | Dear Madam, / Dear Sir, |
| Opening sentence | the purpose — who you are and why you are writing |
| Body | **all the details of the notice** (event, date, time, venue) |
| Closing sentence | a polite request for confirmation / thanks |
| Subscription | Yours faithfully, / Thank you. + full name (and designation, e.g. Secretary, Art Club) |

> **Key rule (2027 scheme):** "All details of the notice are to be included in the e-mail." Never leave out the date, time or venue.

### How the two parts are marked

The 2027 scheme lists the required points (above). **Core9X guide:**

| Part | Marks | What earns them |
|---|---|---|
| **Notice** | 5 | creative title and event name (1); date, time and venue (2); who may take part, with whom and by when to register (1); clear, concise expression (1) |
| **E-mail** | 5 | e-mail ID, subject, salutation and subscription (2); purpose and **all** the notice details (2); polite, formal expression (1) |

### Writing a good notice

- **Short and factual** — about 50 words.
- **Catchy title** — a play on words connected with the event (*Poster Power*, *Debate-o-Mania*, *Brush Strokes*).
- **Dates that make sense:** the event about **10–14 days** away; registration closing **2–3 days** after the notice.
- **Venue from small to large:** the hall, then the school.
- **Clear instruction:** who may take part, with whom to register, by when.
- Use **third person** and **future** forms ("will be held", "should give their names").

### Writing a good e-mail

- **Subject line:** short and specific — not a whole sentence.
- **Opening:** who you are and the purpose ("On behalf of the Art Club of St. Helen's School, I cordially invite you…").
- **Details:** event, date, time, venue (and who is taking part).
- **Request:** clear and polite ("We would be honoured if you could judge the event.").
- **Closing:** request a confirmation ("A line in confirmation would be much appreciated."), thank them.
- **Tone:** formal and courteous — the reader is a guest or an official.

### Common mistakes

- Leaving out the **date, time or venue** in the e-mail.
- An **event date in the past**, or a registration deadline **after** the event.
- A notice that is a **letter** ("Dear students, …").
- Long, chatty notices with unnecessary details.
- A subject line that is a full sentence ("I am writing to invite you to…").
- Informal language in the e-mail ("Hey!", "Can u come?").
- Forgetting the **name and designation** at the end of the e-mail.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, students will be able to:

1. **Write** a concise notice in the CISCE format with a creative title and all essential details.
2. **Choose** sensible dates for the event and the registration deadline.
3. **Write** a formal e-mail with the correct parts: e-mail ID, subject, salutation, opening, details, closing and subscription.
4. **Include** every detail of the notice in the e-mail.
5. **Use** a polite, formal tone suited to guests, judges and other schools.
6. **Avoid** common errors of format, detail and tone.

## 4. Vocabulary

### 4.1 Useful words and phrases for notices

| Purpose | Phrases |
|---|---|
| announcing | will be held / is being organised / will be conducted |
| inviting participation | Pupils who wish to participate should… / Interested students may… |
| registering | give their names to… / register with… on or before… |
| reminding | Participants must bring… / Entries must be submitted by… |
| encouraging | Come one, come all! / Unleash your creativity! |

### 4.2 Useful words and phrases for e-mails

| Purpose | Phrases |
|---|---|
| opening | On behalf of…, I am writing to… / I have the pleasure of inviting you… |
| inviting | We would be honoured if you would… / We cordially invite you to… |
| giving details | The event will be held on… at… from… to… |
| requesting confirmation | A line in confirmation would be much appreciated. / Kindly confirm your availability. |
| closing | We look forward to your presence. / Thank you for your time. |

### 4.3 Vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| inaugurate | open an event formally |
| chief guest | the most important invited guest |
| adjudicate / judge | decide the winners of a competition |
| inter-school | between different schools |
| inter-house | between the houses of one school |
| venue | the place where an event is held |
| register | enrol, give one's name |
| cordially | warmly and politely |
| grace the occasion | honour the event by attending |
| confirmation | a statement that something is certain |

## 15. Revision Notes

### Notice (5)

**Title** · **event** · **date** (10–14 days ahead) · **time** · **venue** (small → large) · **who / register with whom / by when** (2–3 days after the notice). About 50 words. Third person. No "Dear students".

### E-mail (5)

**To** (e-mail ID) · **Subject** (short) · **Dear Sir/Madam,** · opening (who and why) · **all notice details** · request · closing sentence · **Yours faithfully,** · name and designation.

### Avoid

Missing date/time/venue in the e-mail · event in the past · deadline after the event · letter-style notices · sentence-length subject lines · informal language.
