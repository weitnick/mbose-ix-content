## 1. Complete Lesson

### Introduction

*The Elevator* is a short, chilling story by the American writer **William Sleator**. **Martin**, a thin and timid twelve-year-old, has just moved with his **father** into an **old apartment building**. They live on the **seventeenth floor**, so Martin has to use the building's **elevator** (lift) every day. It is tiny, dim, dirty and shaky, and Martin dreads it.

Then a strange, enormously **fat lady in a green coat** begins to appear in the elevator. She never speaks; she only **stares** at him. Each meeting is more frightening than the last. Martin's father, who thinks his son is a coward, refuses to take his fears seriously. In the end, Martin, on **crutches** after a fall, is left **alone** in the elevator with the lady. She smiles and says, "**Hello, Martin.**" The story stops there — we never learn who she is or what happens next.

The story works on two levels. On the surface it is a **horror story** about a mysterious, perhaps supernatural, figure. Underneath, it is a story about **fear, loneliness and a child who gets no support** from the one adult who should protect him.

### About the author

**William Sleator** (1945–2011) was an American writer of **science fiction and horror for young readers**. He studied English at **Harvard University** and worked for some years as a rehearsal **pianist for a ballet company** before becoming a full-time writer. His best-known novels include *House of Stairs*, *Interstellar Pig* and *Singularity*. His stories often place ordinary young people in strange, frightening situations, and often end without a neat explanation.

### The story, retold in our own words

#### 1. The old building and the tiny elevator

Martin and his father have just moved into an **old building**, to an apartment on the **seventeenth floor**. The elevator is **very small** — it can hold at most **three people** — and **poorly lit**, with **dirty walls**. Its door **slams shut** and the old machine **shudders and groans** as it moves between floors. Even with only two people inside, it feels crowded and suffocating.

Martin is always uneasy in elevators. He is afraid this one might **fall**, and inside it he feels **trapped** — the walls seem to close in on him, although he tells himself he is not **claustrophobic** (afraid of closed spaces).

#### 2. The stairs, and a father's scorn

One afternoon Martin avoids the elevator and climbs the **stairs** instead. The stairwell is worse: no windows, poor light, and his own footsteps **echo** so that it sounds as though **someone else** is climbing with him. He reaches the seventeenth floor gasping and trembling.

His **father** is not sympathetic. He has always been disappointed that Martin is **thin, weak and bad at sports**, and he thinks his son is **timid** — even a **coward**. He makes it clear that Martin must use the elevator like everyone else. Martin, ashamed, decides to **force himself** to ride it.

#### 3. The first meeting: the fourteenth floor

One morning, as Martin rides down to go to school, the elevator stops at the **fourteenth floor**. The door slides open and an **enormously fat woman** steps in. She wears a **shabby green coat** that billows around her, and Martin feels the little elevator **sink** under her weight. Her face is **piggish**, with **tiny, sharp blue eyes**, large **fleshy cheeks**, almost **no chin** — her face seems to melt into a mass of neck. She does not speak. She simply **stares** at Martin all the way down, until he gets out on the ground floor.

All day Martin wonders about her. Does she live in the building, or was she visiting? It was very early in the morning for a visit. And he feels **ashamed** of himself: why should he be afraid of an **old lady**?

#### 4. The second meeting: the third floor, going up

That afternoon Martin returns **after school**, feeling **nervous**. He presses the button, hoping the elevator will not stop on the way — but it **stops at the third floor**. The door slides open, and there she is. *(2027 specimen Q4 extract.)*

In a nervous, high voice Martin says the elevator is going up. She nods and squeezes in. He watches her plump hand move to the buttons — and she presses not fourteen, the floor where she got in that morning, but **eighteen, the top floor**. She stands in front of the buttons, staring at him. When the elevator reaches **seventeen**, she barely moves aside to let him out, and she **watches him** until the door closes. Martin realises with horror that **now she knows which floor he lives on**.

#### 5. The father refuses to help

At dinner Martin asks his father whether he has noticed a strange lady in the elevator. His father **dismisses** the question, refuses to ride with Martin to see her, calls the whole thing **ridiculous**, and goes back to ignoring him. Martin is left alone with his fear.

#### 6. The third meeting: the smile

The next morning the lady is there again. This time, as the door closes, she gives Martin a **strange, knowing smile** — the first expression she has ever shown. Martin **panics**. As soon as he can, he escapes from the elevator and **runs down the dark stairs**.

#### 7. The fall

In the darkness Martin **trips, falls down the cement steps and breaks his leg**. *(2025 specimen Q1(xv).)* His father takes him to the **hospital**, where his leg is put in a **cast** and he is given **crutches**. His father is **angry and disappointed** that his son has behaved like a fool and a coward, and **does not talk to him** on the way to the hospital. Martin comes to believe that the lady **smiled because she knew this would happen**.

#### 8. The ending: alone on crutches

On the way back up to the apartment, Martin and his father ride the elevator together, and for once Martin feels a little safer. But at the **ninth floor** his father **gets off** to visit a neighbour. Martin begs him to stay. His father mocks him — is he afraid to be in the elevator alone? — and tells him, "**Grow up, Martin.**" The door slams shut.

Martin is **alone**, on **crutches**, unable to move quickly. The elevator does not go straight up: it **stops at the tenth floor**. The door slides open. The fat lady in the green coat is waiting. She steps in quickly — Martin is far too slow on his crutches to get out — and the **door closes**. She reaches for the buttons and presses **STOP**, so the elevator halts. Then she **smiles** at him and says, "**Hello, Martin.**"

The story ends there.

### Conclusion: why the ending is so frightening

- Martin is **trapped**: on crutches, in a tiny box, with the door closed and the elevator stopped.
- He is **alone**: his father, the one person who could protect him, has just walked away.
- The lady **knows his name**, although they have never spoken.
- We are **never told** who she is or what she wants. Our own imagination supplies the horror. This kind of open ending is called a **cliff-hanger**.

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — The building and the elevator

- **What happens:** Martin and his father move to the seventeenth floor of an old building; the elevator frightens Martin.
- **Points to note:**
  - The elevator is **tiny** (three people at most), **poorly lit**, with **dirty walls**; the door **slams**; it **shudders** and **groans**.
  - Martin fears it will **fall** and feels **trapped** and **suffocated** — signs of **claustrophobia**, though he denies it.
  - The **setting** itself creates fear: a small, closed, mechanical space that the boy cannot control.
- **Exam focus:** 2027 specimen Q4(i) — why Martin was afraid of this elevator (any two: poorly lit, dirty walls, very small, felt claustrophobic/trapped, doors closed quickly, shuddered, afraid it would fall) and what this tells us about him (lacking in confidence / anxious / insecure).

### Section 2 — The stairs and the father

- **What happens:** Martin climbs the dark stairs; his father scorns him and insists he use the elevator.
- **Points to note:**
  - The stairwell is no refuge: dark, windowless, echoing footsteps that seem to follow him.
  - The father sees Martin as **thin, weak, bad at sports, timid** — a **coward**; he expects his son to "be a man".
  - Martin's shame comes from **his father's opinion** of him. *(2027 specimen Q4(iii))*

### Section 3 — The first meeting

- **What happens:** the elevator stops at the fourteenth floor; the fat lady in the green coat gets in and stares at Martin.
- **Points to note:**
  - **Description** (2027 specimen Q4(ii)): fat lady; old green coat; **piggish** face; **tiny, sharp blue eyes**; **large fleshy cheeks**; no chin; a **mass of neck**.
  - Her silence and **staring** are more frightening than words.
  - Martin feels **terrified / threatened / nervous**.

### Section 4 — The second meeting

- **What happens:** after school the elevator stops at the third floor; the lady gets in, presses eighteen, and watches Martin get out at seventeen.
- **Points to note:**
  - Martin hoped the elevator would not stop — his fear is already expecting her.
  - She presses **eighteen** (the top floor), not **fourteen** — her movements make no sense, adding mystery.
  - "Now she knows" his floor — the threat becomes **personal**.
  - Martin is **ashamed** to fear an **old lady** — his father's voice inside his head. *(2027 specimen Q4 extract and (iii))*

### Section 5 — The father dismisses Martin

- **What happens:** at dinner Martin asks about the lady; his father calls it ridiculous and will not ride with him.
- **Points to note:** the father's **lack of empathy** isolates Martin; the reader begins to fear for him because no adult will help.

### Section 6 — The smile and the fall

- **What happens:** the lady smiles; Martin panics, runs down the dark stairs, falls and breaks his leg; his father, angry, takes him to hospital in silence.
- **Points to note:**
  - The **smile** is the first change in her — a sign that something is about to happen.
  - **Cause and effect** (2025 specimen Q1(xv)): Martin's terror leads to the fall (statement 1), which leads to the father's angry silence (statement 2) — "(1) is the cause for (2)".
  - Martin believes she **knew** he would fall — is she supernatural, or is it his imagination?

### Section 7 — The ending

- **What happens:** the father gets off at the ninth floor, telling Martin to grow up; at the tenth floor the lady gets in; the door closes; she presses STOP, smiles and says, "Hello, Martin."
- **Points to note:**
  - Martin is on **crutches** — he **cannot escape**. *(2027 specimen Q4(v))*
  - The father's **"Grow up, Martin"** is his last failure to protect his son.
  - The lady **knows his name** though they have never met — the **sinister** climax. *(2027 specimen Q4(iv))*
  - The story ends abruptly: a **cliff-hanger**, **open-ended**, **horrifying**, **suspenseful**.

### 5.4 Beyond the text (general knowledge, not needed for exam answers)

- **Claustrophobia** is a real anxiety disorder — an intense fear of closed or narrow spaces, such as lifts, tunnels or crowded rooms. It can cause a racing heart, sweating and panic.
- Fear grows when a person feels **alone** and **not believed**. Talking to a trusted adult or friend, and being taken seriously, helps people manage fears.
- Writers of horror often use an **ordinary setting** (a lift, a staircase, a house) so that readers feel the danger could reach them too.

## 7. Themes / Messages

- **Fear and anxiety:** how fear grows in a lonely mind until it controls a person's life.
- **Parent and child:** a father's lack of understanding leaves his son unprotected; the story shows how much children need **empathy** and support.
- **Masculinity and shame:** Martin is made to feel ashamed for not being "manly"; shame stops him from asking for help.
- **The unknown:** the lady is never explained; the most frightening things are those we do not understand.
- **Isolation:** Martin is alone in a new building, alone with his fear, and finally alone in the elevator.

### 7.1 How Sleator creates suspense and horror

| Technique | Example |
|---|---|
| a claustrophobic setting | the tiny, dim, shaking elevator |
| escalation | each meeting is worse: stare → floor 18 → smile → "Hello, Martin" |
| mystery | why does she press eighteen? where does she live? |
| foreshadowing | the smile before the fall; the father's indifference before he leaves |
| point of view | told closely through Martin's feelings, so we share his fear |
| vulnerability | the hero is a child, then a child on crutches |
| open ending | the story stops at the moment of greatest danger |

### 7.2 Is the lady real? Three readings

| Reading | Support |
|---|---|
| a real but strange woman | she rides the elevator like any resident; nothing supernatural is shown directly |
| a supernatural figure | she seems to know he will fall; knows his name; appears wherever he goes |
| a projection of Martin's fear | the story is told through his anxious mind; his father sees nothing |

*Any reading can be argued in an exam, provided it is supported with details from the story.*

### 7.3 Significance of the title

The title names the place where every frightening encounter happens. The **elevator** is Martin's daily trap — a small, closed box from which he cannot escape. By the end, the ordinary machine has become a symbol of his **helplessness**.

### 7.4 Comparing *The Elevator* with other stories in the syllabus

| Point | *The Elevator* | *The Pedestrian* | *With the Photographer* |
|---|---|---|---|
| main character | Martin, a timid boy | Leonard Mead, a lonely walker | the narrator, a meek customer |
| source of pressure | his father's scorn; the mysterious lady | a society that punishes difference | an overbearing photographer |
| feeling created | fear and suspense | unease and sadness | laughter |
| setting | a tiny, closed elevator | empty city streets at night | a photo studio |
| ending | open; Martin trapped | Mead taken away | narrator leaves in tears |
| shared idea | an individual alone against forces that do not listen | | |

### 7.5 Fear in the story: real and imagined

Some of Martin's fears are about **real** things — the elevator really is old, small and dirty. Others grow in his **imagination** — the echoing footsteps on the stairs, the sense that the lady "knew" he would fall. Sleator never tells us where the line between them lies. That uncertainty is what makes the story frightening: if we cannot be sure what is real, we cannot be sure Martin is safe.

**(iii) 'He felt ashamed of himself.' Why did Martin feel this way? How was his response shaped by his father's opinion of him?** [3]

**(iv) How does the story end? Bring out the element of horror in the ending of the story.** [3]

**(v) Why was Martin nervous when his father got out of the lift on the ninth floor? Give two words to describe the ending of the story.** [4]
Martin had **broken his leg** and was **on crutches**, so he knew he **would not be able to escape** if the lady entered the elevator — and now his father would not be there to protect him. **Two words (personal response, any two):** **cliff-hanger**, **open-ended**, **horrifying**, **suspenseful**.

Any three: it was **poorly lit**; its walls were **dirty**; it was **very small (cramped)**, so he felt **trapped** in it; the door **closed quickly with a clang**; it **shuddered** each time it moved; it was **old**, and he feared it might **fall**.

**(iii) What led to Martin's fall down the stairs? What did it result in?** [3]
Next morning the elevator door opened and **the lady was waiting inside**, smiling at him. **Terrified, Martin turned and ran down the dark stairs**, fell, and **broke his leg**.

**(iv) How does the story end? Bring out the element of horror in the ending.** [3]
Coming home on crutches, Martin is left **alone in the elevator** when his father gets off at the ninth floor. At the tenth floor **the lady gets in**; the door closes, she **presses the STOP button**, smiles and says, "Hello, Martin." Martin cannot escape. The horror lies in the **cliff-hanger**: he is **trapped** with her, and the reader never learns what happens next.

**(v) What was Martin's father's opinion of him? What does this reveal to us about his father's character?** [4]
He thinks Martin is **weak and cowardly**, **poor at sports**, **timid** and **immature** — a boy who needs to "grow up". This shows that the father is **insensitive** and **emotionally distant**: he has **no sympathy** for his son's fears and does not try to understand them.

**2025 Improvement Q1(xiii).** **(d) Statement (2) is the cause of Statement (1).** Seeing the lady waiting inside terrified Martin, so he ran down the stairs and fell.

### 8.2 More practice in the ICSE extract style

**Extract A (paraphrased).** Martin rides down one morning. The elevator stops at the fourteenth floor and an enormously fat woman in a green coat squeezes in. She says nothing, but her small, sharp eyes stay fixed on him until he gets out on the ground floor.

**(i)** Where do Martin and his father live? Why does Martin dislike the elevator? [3]
**Answer:** On the **seventeenth floor** of an **old apartment building**. The elevator is **tiny** (three people at most), **dim** and **dirty**; its door slams and it **shudders**; he feels **trapped** and fears it may **fall**.

**(ii)** Describe the woman. [3]
**Answer:** She is **enormously fat** and wears a **shabby green coat**; her face is **piggish**, with **tiny, sharp blue eyes**, **fleshy cheeks** and almost **no chin**; the elevator seems to sink under her weight.

**(iii)** What questions trouble Martin about her afterwards? [3]
**Answer:** Whether she **lives in the building or is only visiting** — it seemed too early in the morning for a visit; and why he, a boy, should be so **afraid of an old lady** — which makes him **ashamed**.

**(iv)** What happens at their second meeting? [3]
**Answer:** After school the elevator **stops at the third floor** and she gets in; she presses **eighteen**, the top floor, not fourteen; she stands in front of the buttons, and when Martin gets out at **seventeen** she **watches him** — so now she **knows his floor**.

**(v)** How does Sleator make these meetings frightening even though the woman does nothing violent? [4]
**Answer:** Through **silence and staring**, which make her seem watchful and threatening; through her **overwhelming size** in a **tiny, closed space**; through **mystery** (pressing eighteen, appearing on different floors); and through **escalation** — each meeting tells her more about Martin, so we feel the danger growing.

**Extract B (paraphrased).** Coming home from the hospital with his leg in a cast, Martin begs his father not to get out at the ninth floor. His father mocks him for being afraid to ride alone and tells him to grow up. The door slams shut, and Martin is alone.

**(i)** Why is Martin's leg in a cast? [3]
**Answer:** Terrified after the lady **smiled** at him, he fled and **ran down the dark stairs**, **fell** and **broke his leg**; at the hospital his leg was put in a **cast** and he was given **crutches**.

**(ii)** How had his father behaved on the way to the hospital? Why? [3]
**Answer:** He was **angry and disappointed** and **did not talk to him**, because he felt his son had behaved like a **fool and a coward** (2025 specimen Q1(xv)).

**(iii)** Why does Martin beg his father to stay? [3]
**Answer:** He is **on crutches** and **cannot move quickly**; if the lady got in, he **could not escape**; he feels safe only with his father beside him.

**(iv)** What happens after the door slams? [3]
**Answer:** The elevator **stops at the tenth floor**; the **lady** is waiting and steps in quickly; the **door closes**; she presses **STOP**; she **smiles** and says, "**Hello, Martin.**"

**(v)** "Martin's father is as much to blame for his fate as the lady." Do you agree? Give reasons. [4]
**Answer:** *(Personal response.)* **Yes:** he never listened to Martin's fears, called them ridiculous, refused to ride with him, humiliated him as a coward — which drove Martin to flee down the dark stairs — and at the crucial moment he **left his injured son alone**. **Or partly:** the father could not know the lady was dangerous, and the lady's intentions are never shown; but his **lack of empathy** clearly left Martin isolated and helpless.

**Extract C (paraphrased).** At dinner Martin asks his father whether he has seen an odd woman in the elevator. His father barely looks up, says he has no intention of riding up and down to look for her, and tells Martin he is being ridiculous.

**(i)** Why does Martin ask this question? [3]
**Answer:** He has met the lady twice: at the **fourteenth floor**, where she stared at him, and at the **third floor**, where she pressed **eighteen** and watched him get out at **seventeen**. He is frightened and wants his father to **believe him** and help.

**(ii)** What does the father's reply show about him? [3]
**Answer:** He **lacks empathy**: he dismisses Martin's fear without listening, refuses to check, and **mocks** him as ridiculous — consistent with his view of Martin as **timid and cowardly**.

**(iii)** How might Martin feel after this conversation? [3]
**Answer:** **Alone, ashamed and more frightened**: the one person who could protect him will not help, so Martin must face the lady by himself — and he becomes less likely to ask for help again.

**(iv)** What happens the next morning? [3]
**Answer:** The lady is in the elevator again and gives Martin a **strange smile**; he **panics**, escapes, **runs down the dark stairs**, **falls** and **breaks his leg**.

**(v)** How would the story have been different if the father had listened? Give a reasoned answer. [4]
**Answer:** *(Personal response.)* If the father had **ridden with Martin**, he might have seen the lady, reassured his son or found out who she was; Martin would not have felt so **alone and ashamed**, might not have **fled down the stairs**, and so might not have been **on crutches** and **alone** at the end. The story suggests that **support and belief** from adults can protect children from fear — and its absence leaves them helpless.

### 8.3 Writing practice linked to the story (Paper 1 skills)

*ICSE Paper 1 Question 1 offers a choice of compositions (300–350 words), including a narrative and a descriptive topic. This story is a good model for both.*

**Task A (narrative).** Write a story that ends with the words: "… and then the lights went out."

**Plan:**
1. **Opening (setting and character):** a specific, ordinary place (a lift, a school corridor, a bus stop at night) and one character with a clear feeling — for example, a student staying late after a practice.
2. **Rising tension:** two or three small, strange events, each worse than the last (escalation, as in Sleator's encounters).
3. **Climax:** the character is trapped or alone; build the moment with short sentences and sense details (sound, light, touch).
4. **Ending:** stop at the given words — an open ending, like *The Elevator*.

**Model opening (our own writing):** The school corridor was empty by six o'clock, and every one of my footsteps came back to me twice. I told myself it was only the echo. Then, near the staff room, I heard a third step that was not mine.

**Task B (descriptive).** Describe a place that makes you feel uneasy.

**Tips:**
- Choose **three senses** and give each a strong detail (a flickering tube-light; the smell of damp walls; a metal handrail cold to the touch).
- Use **precise adjectives** (dim, narrow, stale) rather than general ones (bad, scary).
- Show the **feeling** through physical reactions (a tight chest, quick breathing) rather than only naming it.
- End with a line that leaves an image in the reader's mind.

## 10. Short-Answer Questions (3 and 4 marks)

### Extract parts (3 marks each)

**Q1.** Why was Martin afraid of using this particular elevator? What does this tell us about him? *(2027 specimen)*
**Answer:** It was tiny, poorly lit and dirty; it shuddered and its door closed quickly (any two).
He felt trapped and feared it would fall.
This shows he was anxious and lacking in confidence.

**Q2.** Describe the fat lady. How does she make Martin feel? *(2027 specimen)*
**Answer:** She is enormously fat and wears an old green coat.
She has a piggish face, tiny sharp blue eyes, fleshy cheeks and no chin.
She makes Martin feel terrified and threatened.

**Q3.** Why did Martin feel ashamed? How did his father's opinion shape this? *(2027 specimen)*
**Answer:** He was ashamed to be afraid of an old lady.
His father humiliated him and thought him timid and cowardly.
He expected Martin to behave like a "man", so Martin judged himself harshly.

**Q4.** What happens when Martin climbs the stairs instead of taking the elevator?
**Answer:** The stairwell is dark and windowless.
His footsteps echo, as if someone were following him; he arrives gasping.
His father scorns him as a coward and insists he use the elevator.

**Q5.** What happens at the second meeting with the lady?
**Answer:** After school, the elevator stops at the third floor and she gets in.
She presses eighteen, the top floor, not fourteen.
She watches Martin get out at seventeen — now she knows his floor.

**Q6.** How does Martin's father respond when Martin asks about the lady?
**Answer:** He dismisses the question.
He refuses to ride the elevator with Martin to see her.
He calls the whole thing ridiculous.

**Q7.** How does Martin break his leg?
**Answer:** The lady smiles at him and he panics.
He escapes and runs down the dark stairs.
He falls down the cement steps and breaks his leg.

**Q8.** How does Martin's father behave on the way to the hospital, and why?
**Answer:** He does not talk to Martin.
He is angry and disappointed.
He thinks his son has behaved like a fool and a coward.

**Q9.** Why was Martin nervous when his father got out on the ninth floor? *(2027 specimen)*
**Answer:** Martin had broken his leg and was on crutches.
He knew he could not escape if the lady entered.
His father, his only protection, was leaving him.

**Q10.** How does the story end? *(2027 specimen)*
**Answer:** The elevator stops at the tenth floor and the lady gets in.
Martin, on crutches, is too slow to get out; the door closes and she presses STOP.
She smiles and says, "Hello, Martin."

**Q11.** What makes the lady mysterious?
**Answer:** She never explains herself and speaks only at the very end.
She gets in at different floors and presses eighteen after getting in at fourteen.
She seems to know Martin will fall, and she knows his name.

**Q12.** What role does the setting play in the story?
**Answer:** The old building and tiny elevator create a closed, trapped atmosphere.
The dark stairwell offers no escape either.
The setting makes Martin — and the reader — feel helpless.

### Extract parts (4 marks each)

**Q13.** Bring out the element of horror in the ending. *(2027 specimen)*
**Answer:** Martin is trapped with the lady in a stopped elevator.
He is on crutches and cannot escape; his father is gone.
Her sinister smile adds menace.
She calls him by name though they have never met — and the story stops there.

**Q14.** Give two words to describe the ending and justify them. *(2027 specimen)*
**Answer:** Cliff-hanger: it stops at the moment of greatest danger.
We never learn what happens.
Suspenseful/horrifying: we are left fearing for Martin.
Our imagination supplies the worst.

**Q15.** Is Martin's father to blame for what happens? Give reasons.
**Answer:** (Personal response.) Yes, largely.
He ignored and mocked Martin's fears and refused to see the lady.
His scorn made Martin ashamed to ask for help.
At the crucial moment he left his injured son alone.

**Q16.** How does Sleator build suspense through the story?
**Answer:** A claustrophobic setting: the tiny, dim, shaking elevator.
Escalation: stare, floor eighteen, smile, "Hello, Martin".
Mystery about the lady, and foreshadowing (the smile before the fall).
An abrupt open ending at the point of greatest danger.

**Q17.** Is the lady real, supernatural, or imagined? Argue one view.
**Answer:** (Any view with support.) Supernatural: she seems to know Martin will fall.
She appears wherever he goes and knows his name.
Real: nothing impossible is shown; she rides the lift like a resident.
Imagined: the story is told through Martin's anxious mind, and his father sees nothing.

**Q18.** Write a character sketch of Martin.
**Answer:** A thin, nervous twelve-year-old, lacking confidence.
Sensitive and imaginative — he hears footsteps on the stairs.
Ashamed and insecure because of his father's scorn.
Lonely and finally vulnerable — alone on crutches, trapped.

**Q19.** What does the story suggest about how adults should respond to children's fears?
**Answer:** Fears should be listened to and taken seriously.
Mockery and shame make children hide their fears.
Support and company help a child face what frightens them.
Martin's father does the opposite, and Martin is left defenceless.

**Q20.** Discuss the significance of the title.
**Answer:** Every frightening encounter takes place in the elevator.
It is a small, closed box from which Martin cannot escape.
It is his daily trap, and finally the scene of the climax.
It becomes a symbol of his helplessness and fear.
