## 1. Complete Lesson

### What Question 4 asks

| Part | What you do | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| (i) | choose the meaning of **two words** as used in the passage (MCQ) | 1 + 1 |
| (ii) | choose the word in the passage that means the **opposite** of a given word (MCQ) | 1 |
| (iii) | answer **five questions** briefly **in your own words** | 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 2 |
| (iv) | write a **summary** of a specified part or aspect in **not more than 50 words** | 8 |

The specimen passages have all been **narrative extracts** from well-known stories — a tiger walking into a school (2024, R. K. Narayan), a boy who learns to swim with a buffalo-keeper (2025, Ruskin Bond), and a sick boy who waits all day to die because he has confused two kinds of thermometer (2027, adapted from Ernest Hemingway's *A Day's Wait*).

### Reading the passage (5 minutes)

1. **Read the questions first**, quickly — they tell you what to look for.
2. **Read the whole passage once** for the story: who, where, what happens, how it ends.
3. **Read it again**, marking the lines that answer each question.
4. Note **line numbers** given in the vocabulary questions, and read the whole sentence around the word.

### Word meanings in context (part i)

- The question asks for the meaning **"as used in the passage"**. Many words have several meanings; only one fits.
- **Substitute** each option into the sentence. The right one keeps the meaning and grammar.
- *2027 example:* "avoided" in "There was no danger if you avoided pneumonia" — the options included *steering clear away* and *stayed away from*; the scheme answer was **(d) stayed away from**, the option that fits the sentence exactly. "Following" in "he was not following me" meant **paying attention**, not "running after".

### Antonyms from the passage (part ii)

- You must find the **opposite** of a given word **in the passage**.
- Check the **part of speech**: the antonym must be the same kind of word (verb for verb).
- *2027 example:* the opposite of "concluded" was **"commenced"** (began). *2024 example:* the opposite of "liabilities" was **"assets"**.

### Short answers in your own words (part iii)

- **Answer exactly what is asked** — "why", "how", "which sentence", "what do you think".
- Use **your own words**; copy only short phrases when you must, and never long sentences.
- **Marks guide length:** a 1-mark answer is usually one fact or one sentence; a 2-mark answer has two points or a point with a reason.
- **"Which sentence tells us…?"** — here you **quote** the sentence exactly (it is the one time copying is required).
- **"Why do you think…?"** — give an inference based on the passage, with a reason.

### Writing the summary (part iv)

The 2027 scheme gives **6 marks for content points** (six points listed) and **2 marks for expression**.

**Method:**

1. **Read the question** carefully: what part or aspect must you summarise? ("how the narrator understood why Schatz was upset and how he got rid of his doubts")
2. **Find the relevant lines** and list the **key points** — usually **five or six**.
3. **Leave out** examples, dialogue, repetition and description.
4. **Write one paragraph** in your own words, in **connected sentences** (not a list), in **the past tense** for a narrative.
5. **Count the words** — no more than 50. Write the number at the end if your teacher asks.

**2027 scheme points for the summary (in brief):** (1) Schatz asked the narrator what time he would die; (2) the narrator then understood Schatz's confusion about temperature scales; (3) he explained the difference between Celsius and Fahrenheit; (4) he compared it to miles and kilometres; (5) this cleared Schatz's misunderstanding; (6) and removed his fear. *Expression: 2.*

### Common mistakes

- Choosing a dictionary meaning that does **not fit** the sentence.
- Giving an antonym that is **not in the passage**, or is the wrong part of speech.
- **Copying whole sentences** for "own words" answers.
- Writing a 1-line answer for a 2-mark question that has **two parts**.
- Summaries that **exceed 50 words**, include **dialogue**, or summarise the **wrong part**.
- Writing the summary as **bullet points**.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, students will be able to:

1. **Read** an unseen narrative passage efficiently, using the questions to guide them.
2. **Choose** the meaning of a word as used in a particular context.
3. **Find** antonyms of given words within a passage.
4. **Answer** short questions accurately and in their own words, matching the marks.
5. **Quote** exact sentences when asked, and **infer** feelings and motives with reasons.
6. **Write** a focused summary of not more than 50 words covering all the key points.

## 4. Vocabulary

### 4.1 Question words and what they want

| Question begins | What it wants |
|---|---|
| Why… | a reason (because…) |
| How… | a manner or method (by…) / a feeling (how did he feel) |
| What do you think… / Why do you think… | an inference supported by the passage |
| Which sentence/word… | an exact quotation from the passage |
| In not more than 50 words… | a summary of the specified part only |

**(iv)** Why did the aunt send the narrator to the market? [2]
**Answer:** She felt that at fourteen the narrator should learn the value of money.
She wanted the narrator to learn that money has to be earned.

**(v)** Why did the narrator sell nothing in the first hour? [2]
**Answer:** The narrator was too shy to call out to customers.
Customers walked past as if the narrator were invisible.

**(vi)** What advice did the old woman give, and how did it help? [2]
**Answer:** She advised the narrator to look at customers and smile.
When the narrator smiled and spoke to a schoolteacher, she bought a dozen oranges, and selling became easier.

**(vii)** Which sentence shows that not everything went well? [1]
**Answer:** "A man paid me with a torn note that my aunt later said nobody would accept, and a goat from the next lane ate three oranges before I noticed."

**(viii)** Why do you think those few coins felt so precious to the narrator? [2]
**Answer:** They were the narrator's first earnings, gained through a full day's effort.
They taught the narrator how hard money is to earn.

**(ix)** In not more than 50 words, describe what the narrator learnt from the day at the market. [8]
**Answer:** At first too shy to sell, the narrator learnt from an old vendor to smile and speak to customers. Through the day the narrator learnt to count change, sort bruised fruit and handle bargainers, faced some losses, and finally understood, through a small wage, how hard money is to earn. (50 words)

## 15. Revision Notes

### The pattern (20 marks)

(i) two word meanings in context — 2 · (ii) antonym from the passage — 1 · (iii) five short answers — 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 2 · (iv) summary in ≤ 50 words — 8 (6 content + 2 expression).

### Method

1. Read the questions → read the passage → re-read and mark answers.
2. Meanings: substitute each option into the sentence.
3. Antonym: same part of speech, found in the passage.
4. Short answers: own words; match the marks; quote only when asked "which sentence".
5. Summary: find 5–6 points from the specified part; one connected paragraph; past tense; ≤ 50 words.

### Avoid

Dictionary meanings that do not fit · antonyms not in the passage · copying whole sentences · one-point answers to 2-mark questions · summaries over 50 words, with dialogue or bullet points.
