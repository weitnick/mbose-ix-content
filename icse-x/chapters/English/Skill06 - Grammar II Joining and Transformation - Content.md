## 1. Complete Lesson

### What parts (iii) and (iv) ask

Since 2024 both parts are **multiple choice**: you choose the **one correct** version from four. The wrong options are carefully built from **common errors** — wrong correlatives (*no sooner … when*), wrong tense after *had*, wrong pronoun in reported speech, double negatives, or changed meaning. So you must know each pattern **exactly**.

### How to choose the right option

1. **Read the instruction** (*Begin with…*, *Use…*, *End with a question tag*).
2. Check **grammar**: correct pairs (*no sooner … than*, *hardly … when*, *neither … nor*), verb forms, agreement.
3. Check **meaning**: the new sentence must say exactly what the original said — no more, no less.
4. **Eliminate** options with obvious errors, then compare the rest.

### Joining sentences (without *and*, *but*, *so*)

| Pattern | Use | Example (original → joined) |
|---|---|---|
| **No sooner … than** | one action follows another at once | The Chief Guest arrived. The band started playing. → **No sooner did the Chief Guest arrive than** the school band started playing. (2027) |
| **Hardly / Scarcely … when** | the same | The music stopped. The audience left. → **Hardly had the music stopped when** the audience left. (2024) |
| **Neither … nor** | two negatives | Arjun does not know the rules. Arif does not know the rules. → **Neither Arjun nor Arif knows** the rules. (2027) — verb agrees with the nearer subject |
| **Either … or** | alternatives | |
| **Not only … but also** | addition with emphasis | |
| **because / since / as** | reason | The pupil was impolite. The teacher admonished her. → The teacher admonished the pupil **because** she was impolite. (2027) |
| **Besides + -ing** | addition | It was dark and windy. It was getting late. → **Besides being** dark and windy, it was getting late. (2027) |
| **Despite / In spite of + -ing / noun** | contrast | We placed our order late. We received the food on time. → **Despite placing** our order late, we received the food on time. (2024) |
| **Although / Though** | contrast | |
| **unless** (= if … not) | condition | You cannot enter the club. You do not have a membership. → You cannot enter the club **unless** you have a membership. (2024) |
| **too … to** | excess, so that something is impossible | She was very late. She could not catch the train. → She was **too late to** catch the train. (2024) |
| **to + verb** (infinitive of purpose) | purpose | Roy's mother rang up the school office. She wanted to enquire… → Roy's mother rang up the school office **to enquire** about… (2025) |
| **relative pronouns** (who, which, whose, where) | adding information about a noun | |
| **participle** (-ing / having + -ed) | one action leading to another | |
| **noun clause** (where, what, that) | one sentence becomes part of another | I left my keys somewhere. Do you know where they are? → Do you know **where I left** my keys? (2025) |

### Transformation patterns

| Instruction | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Begin with: No sooner…** | No sooner **had/did** + subject + verb … **than** | The burglar ran away the moment he saw the guard. → **No sooner had** the burglar seen the guard **than** he ran away. (2024) |
| **Begin with: Hardly…** | Hardly **had** + subject + past participle … **when** | Hardly had Ranjan lifted the box **when** he felt a pain. ↔ No sooner had Ranjan lifted the box **than** he felt a pain. (2027) |
| **Use: too … to** | too + adjective + to + verb (no negative) | It is so hot and humid that I cannot sleep. → It is **too hot and humid to sleep**. (2025) |
| **Use: so … that** | so + adjective + that + clause (with negative) | He is too shy to participate. → He is **so shy that he cannot** participate in the play. (2027) |
| **Use: unless** | unless = if … not; keep the meaning | I wouldn't eat that food if I was not really hungry. → I wouldn't eat that food **unless I was really hungry**. (2025) |
| **Begin with: If** (from unless) | if … not | Unless we leave early, we might get caught in traffic. → **If we do not leave early**, we will get caught in traffic. (2027) |
| **Begin with: In spite of** | In spite of + -ing / noun, + main clause | The puppy made several attempts but could not climb over. → **In spite of making several attempts**, the puppy could not climb over the fence. (2027) |
| **Active → passive** | object becomes subject; correct tense of *be* + past participle | The students were discussing the results. → The examination results **were being discussed** by the students. (2027) The parents have trained their children. → The children **have been trained** … (2024) |
| **Direct → reported speech** | *asked* + question word + statement order; tense back-shift; pronoun and time changes | "Sohini, why did you not reach the office before 8?" → Heena asked Sohini **why she had not reached** the office before 8. (2027) "Why were you absent yesterday?" → The teacher asked me why I had been absent **the day before**. (2025) |
| **Degrees of comparison** | *Very few … as … as X* → X is **better than most** / **one of the most …** | Very few pupils can paint as well as Abhilash. → Abhilash paints **better than most** other pupils. (2027) Very few dancers are as graceful as she is. → She is **one of the most graceful** dancers. (2025) |
| **Use: few** | the reverse | Nithin is more talented than most… → **Few** professional musicians I know **are as talented as** Nithin. (2024) |
| **Question tags** | positive statement → negative tag, and vice versa; same auxiliary | We often go out for dinner on weekends, **don't we?** (2027) Yesterday was so much fun, **wasn't it?** (2025) |
| **It has been … since** | time since a past event | My cousins returned home one month ago. → It has been one month **since** my cousins returned home. (2024) |
| **I would rather … than** | preference | I prefer going out with friends to staying alone. → I **would rather go out** with friends **than stay** alone at home. (2025) |
| **Noun / phrase change** | e.g. *surprised* → *took … by surprise* | The news surprised everyone. → The news **took** everyone in my family **by surprise**. (2025) |
| **Adverb ↔ adjective/verb** | thorough cleaning → cleaned thoroughly | The house needs a thorough cleaning. → The house **needs to be cleaned thoroughly** again. (2024) |

### Common traps

- **No sooner … when** (wrong) → **No sooner … than**. **Hardly … than** (wrong) → **Hardly … when**.
- After *No sooner had*, use the **past participle** (*No sooner had he **seen***, not *saw* or *finish*).
- After *No sooner did*, use the **base form** (*No sooner did he **arrive***).
- **too … to** takes **no** *not* (*too late to catch*, not *too late to not catch*).
- **Neither … nor** with a **singular** nearer subject takes a **singular** verb (*Neither Arjun nor Arif **knows***).
- **Reported questions** use **statement order** (*why she had not reached*, not *why had she not reached*), with **no** *that*.
- **Passive** must keep the **tense** (*were discussing* → *were being discussed*).
- **Question tag**: *was … wasn't it?* — not *isn't it*.
- **unless** already contains *not*: *unless I was really hungry*, not *unless I was not really hungry*.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, students will be able to:

1. **Join** two sentences into one without *and*, *but* or *so*, using correlatives, subordinators, participles, infinitives and relative pronouns.
2. **Transform** sentences using *no sooner/hardly*, *too … to / so … that*, *unless/if*, *in spite of*, *rather … than*, *it has been … since*.
3. **Change** active to passive and direct to reported speech accurately.
4. **Rewrite** comparisons using *very few*, *one of the most*, *better than most*.
5. **Add** correct question tags.
6. **Spot** the errors built into the wrong options and choose the correct one.

## 4. Vocabulary (Key Terms)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| synthesis | joining two or more sentences into one |
| transformation | changing the form of a sentence without changing its meaning |
| correlative conjunctions | pairs used together: neither … nor, either … or, no sooner … than, not only … but also |
| subordinating conjunction | a word introducing a dependent clause: because, although, unless, when |
| infinitive of purpose | to + verb showing why (to enquire) |
| participle phrase | a phrase beginning with -ing / having + past participle |
| reported (indirect) speech | reporting someone's words without quotation marks |
| active / passive voice | subject does the action / receives the action |
| question tag | a short question added to a statement (…, don't we?) |
| degrees of comparison | positive (as … as), comparative (better than), superlative (the best) |

## 9. MCQs with Explanations

### Joining sentences (Q1–Q30)

**Q1.** Join without *and*, *but* or *so*: "He saw the snake. He screamed."
- (a) No sooner did he see the snake when he screamed.
- (b) No sooner did he see the snake than he screamed.
- (c) No sooner he saw the snake than he screamed.
- (d) No sooner had he see the snake than he screamed.

**Answer:** (b) No sooner did he see the snake than he screamed.
**Explanation:** no sooner did + base form … than.

**Q2.** "The bell rang. The children rushed out."
- (a) Hardly had the bell rung than the children rushed out.
- (b) Hardly had the bell rang when the children rushed out.
- (c) Hardly the bell had rung than the children rushed out.
- (d) Hardly had the bell rung when the children rushed out.

**Answer:** (d) Hardly had the bell rung when the children rushed out.
**Explanation:** hardly had + past participle … when.

**Q3.** "Ravi did not attend the meeting. Sita did not attend the meeting."
- (a) Neither Ravi or Sita attended the meeting.
- (b) Neither Ravi nor Sita attended the meeting.
- (c) Either Ravi nor Sita attended the meeting.
- (d) Ravi and Sita neither attended the meeting.

**Answer:** (b) Neither Ravi nor Sita attended the meeting.
**Explanation:** neither … nor.

**Q4.** "It was raining heavily. The match continued."
- (a) Despite it was raining heavily, the match continued.
- (b) Although it was raining heavily, yet the match continued but.
- (c) Although it was raining heavily, the match continued.
- (d) In spite of it rained heavily, the match continued.

**Answer:** (c) Although it was raining heavily, the match continued.
**Explanation:** although + clause.

**Q5.** "It was raining heavily. The match continued." (Use *despite*)
- (a) Despite it rained heavily, the match continued.
- (b) Despite of the heavy rain, the match continued.
- (c) Despite the heavy rain, the match continued.
- (d) Despite raining heavily the match continue.

**Answer:** (c) Despite the heavy rain, the match continued.
**Explanation:** despite + noun phrase (no "of").

**Q6.** "She is intelligent. She is hard-working."
- (a) She is not only intelligent but also hard-working.
- (b) She is not only intelligent also hard-working.
- (c) Not only she is intelligent but hard-working.
- (d) She not only is intelligent but also is hard-working also.

**Answer:** (a) She is not only intelligent but also hard-working.
**Explanation:** not only … but also, in parallel positions.

**Q7.** "The boy was tired. He could not walk further."
- (a) The boy was too tired to walk further.
- (b) The boy was too tired to not walk further.
- (c) The boy was so tired to walk further.
- (d) The boy was very tired that he could not walk further.

**Answer:** (a) The boy was too tired to walk further.
**Explanation:** too … to (no "not").

**Q8.** "The boy was tired. He could not walk further." (Use *so … that*)
- (a) The boy was so tired that he could walk further.
- (b) The boy was so tired that he could not walk further.
- (c) The boy was too tired that he could not walk further.
- (d) The boy was so tired as he could not walk further.

**Answer:** (b) The boy was so tired that he could not walk further.
**Explanation:** so … that + negative.

**Q9.** "You must work hard. You will not pass otherwise."
- (a) Unless you do not work hard, you will not pass.
- (b) Unless you work hard, you will not pass.
- (c) If you work hard, you will not pass.
- (d) Unless you work hard, you will pass.

**Answer:** (b) Unless you work hard, you will not pass.
**Explanation:** unless = if not; meaning kept.

**Q10.** "This is the house. Shakespeare was born here."
- (a) This is the house who Shakespeare was born.
- (b) This is the house which Shakespeare was born.
- (c) This is the house where Shakespeare was born.
- (d) This is the house where Shakespeare was born here.

**Answer:** (c) This is the house where Shakespeare was born.
**Explanation:** "where" for place; no repeated "here".

**Q11.** "I met a girl. Her father is a pilot."
- (a) I met a girl whom father is a pilot.
- (b) I met a girl who father is a pilot.
- (c) I met a girl whose father is a pilot.
- (d) I met a girl which father is a pilot.

**Answer:** (c) I met a girl whose father is a pilot.
**Explanation:** "whose" shows possession.

**Q12.** "He finished his homework. He went out to play."
- (a) Having finished his homework, went out to play he.
- (b) Having finish his homework, he went out to play.
- (c) Finished his homework, he went out to play.
- (d) Having finished his homework, he went out to play.

**Answer:** (d) Having finished his homework, he went out to play.
**Explanation:** perfect participle.

**Q13.** "She went to the market. She wanted to buy vegetables."
- (a) She went to the market buying vegetables wanted.
- (b) She went to the market for buy vegetables.
- (c) She went to the market to buy vegetables.
- (d) She went to the market as she want to buy vegetables.

**Answer:** (c) She went to the market to buy vegetables.
**Explanation:** infinitive of purpose.

**Q14.** "He is poor. He is honest."
- (a) He is poor though honest he is.
- (b) Though he is poor, but he is honest.
- (c) Though poor, but honest he is.
- (d) Though he is poor, he is honest.

**Answer:** (d) Though he is poor, he is honest.
**Explanation:** no "but" after "though".

**Q15.** "You can take the bus. You can take the train."
- (a) You can take either the bus or the train.
- (b) You can take either the bus nor the train.
- (c) You can take neither the bus or the train.
- (d) You can either take the bus nor the train.

**Answer:** (a) You can take either the bus or the train.
**Explanation:** either … or.

**Q16.** "The teacher entered. The students stood up."
- (a) As soon as the teacher entered, then the students stood up.
- (b) As soon as the teacher entered, the students stood up.
- (c) As soon the teacher entered, the students stood up.
- (d) Soon as the teacher entered the students stood.

**Answer:** (b) As soon as the teacher entered, the students stood up.
**Explanation:** as soon as + clause.

**Q17.** "He is rich. He is not happy." (Use *in spite of*)
- (a) In spite of he is rich, he is not happy.
- (b) In spite of being rich, he is not happy.
- (c) In spite being rich, he is not happy.
- (d) In spite of rich, he is not happy.

**Answer:** (b) In spite of being rich, he is not happy.
**Explanation:** in spite of + -ing.

**Q18.** "She sings. She also dances." (Use *besides*)
- (a) Beside singing, she dances.
- (b) Besides singing, she dances.
- (c) Besides sing, she dances.
- (d) Besides she sings, she dances.

**Answer:** (b) Besides singing, she dances.
**Explanation:** "besides" (= in addition to) + -ing.

**Q19.** "He was ill. He did not go to school." (Use *being*)
- (a) Being ill, school was not attended by him.
- (b) Being ill, he did not go to school.
- (c) Ill being, he did not go to school.
- (d) Being ill, did not go to school he.

**Answer:** (b) Being ill, he did not go to school.
**Explanation:** the participle must refer to the subject "he".

**Q20.** "The man is a doctor. He lives next door."
- (a) The man who lives next door is a doctor.
- (b) The man which lives next door is a doctor.
- (c) The man whom lives next door is a doctor.
- (d) The man who lives next door he is a doctor.

**Answer:** (a) The man who lives next door is a doctor.
**Explanation:** "who" as subject; no extra "he".

**Q21.** "I do not know something. Where does she live?"
- (a) I do not know that where she lives.
- (b) I do not know where does she live.
- (c) I do not know where she does live?
- (d) I do not know where she lives.

**Answer:** (d) I do not know where she lives.
**Explanation:** noun clause in statement order.

**Q22.** "The train was late. We missed the meeting." (Use *because of*)
- (a) Because of the late train, we missed the meeting.
- (b) Because of the train was late, we missed the meeting.
- (c) Because the train late, we missed the meeting.
- (d) Because of late, the train, we missed the meeting.

**Answer:** (a) Because of the late train, we missed the meeting.
**Explanation:** because of + noun phrase.

**Q23.** "The thief saw the police. He ran away." (Use *on*)
- (a) On saw the police, the thief ran away.
- (b) On seeing the police, the thief ran away.
- (c) On seen the police the thief ran away.
- (d) On see the police the thief ran away.

**Answer:** (b) On seeing the police, the thief ran away.
**Explanation:** on + -ing.

**Q24.** "Wear a raincoat. You will get wet otherwise." (Use *or*)
- (a) Wear a raincoat or else you will get dry.
- (b) Wear a raincoat or you will not get wet.
- (c) Wear a raincoat or you will get wet.
- (d) Wear or a raincoat you will get wet.

**Answer:** (c) Wear a raincoat or you will get wet.
**Explanation:** "or" = otherwise.

**Q25.** "He spoke loudly. Everyone could hear him." (Use *so that*)
- (a) He spoke loudly so everyone heard him.
- (b) He spoke so loudly that everyone could not hear him.
- (c) He spoke loudly so that everyone could hear him.
- (d) He spoke loudly so that everyone can heard him.

**Answer:** (c) He spoke loudly so that everyone could hear him.

## 15. Revision Notes

### Fixed pairs

no sooner … **than** · hardly / scarcely … **when** · neither … **nor** · either … **or** · not only … **but also** · so … **that** · too … **to** · such … **that** · would rather … **than** · prefer … **to**

### Verb form after the trigger

| Trigger | Form |
|---|---|
| No sooner **had** / Hardly **had** | past participle |
| No sooner **did** | base form |
| in spite of / despite / besides / on | -ing (or a noun) |
| to (purpose), too … to, enough … to | base form |
| unless | positive verb (no *not*) |

### Reported speech

statement order · tense back-shift (is → was, will → would, did → had done) · pronoun change · now → then, today → that day, yesterday → the day before, tomorrow → the next/following day · questions: asked + wh-word / if · commands: told/asked + to + verb.

### Passive

object → subject · *be* in the original tense + past participle · *by* + agent (or omit).

### Question tags

positive → negative tag; negative → positive tag · same auxiliary · *I am → aren't I* · *Let's → shall we* · *nobody → did they*.
