## 1. Complete Lesson

### Introduction

*The Pedestrian* is a short, haunting story by the American writer **Ray Bradbury**. It is set in an American city in the year **A.D. 2053**. Every evening, **Leonard Mead** goes out for a **walk** through the silent streets. He never meets anyone. Everyone else is indoors, sitting in the dark in front of their **television screens** ("viewing screens"), like the dead in their tombs.

One night, the city's **only police car** — a car with **no driver**, controlled by machines — stops him. Its mechanical voice questions him. Mead is a **writer** (a profession no longer needed, since nobody reads), he is **unmarried**, he has **no viewing screen**, and he is walking **for no purpose** except to see and breathe the air. To the machine, this makes no sense. Mead is taken away to a **Psychiatric Center for Research on Regressive Tendencies** — arrested, in effect, for **walking**.

The story is a warning. Written in 1951, when television was spreading fast in American homes, it imagines a future in which technology has made people **isolated, passive and alike**, and in which anyone who is **different** is treated as mentally ill. *(2025 specimen Q1(xvi): dystopian; 2027 specimen Q1(xiv): isolation and alienation.)*

### About the author

**Ray Bradbury** (1920–2012) was one of the best-known American writers of **science fiction and fantasy**. His works include *The Martian Chronicles* (1950), *The Illustrated Man* (1951) and the novel ***Fahrenheit 451*** (1953), about a future society in which books are burned. *Fahrenheit 451* grew partly out of the ideas in *The Pedestrian*. Bradbury himself loved walking at night, and he said the story came from an experience in which a police car stopped him and asked what he was doing on foot. He used science fiction not mainly to predict machines, but to **warn** about what technology could do to human beings.

### The story, retold in our own words

#### 1. A November night in A.D. 2053

It is a misty evening in **November** in the year **2053**. **Leonard Mead** loves nothing more than to go out into the silence of the city, put his feet on the buckled concrete pavement, and **walk**. He has done this almost every night for **ten years**, covering thousands of miles — and in all that time he has **never met another person walking**. *(2025 specimen Q5(i): he was a writer; he loved to go out on long walks in the evening, in the silence, along the concrete pavements and grassy patches; the story is set in A.D. 2053.)*

#### 2. A city like a graveyard

He stands at a **street corner** and looks down the long, **moonlit avenues** in all four directions, choosing which way to go — though it makes no real difference, since he is completely **alone** in this world of 2053. *(2025 specimen Q5 extract, paraphrased.)*

The houses he passes are **dark**, except for the faint, flickering grey light of television screens moving on the walls inside. Walking past them, Mead feels it is **like walking through a graveyard**: the people inside sit silently like the dead in their tombs, with only ghostly light moving over their faces. He sometimes whispers at the windows, asking what is on the different **channels** tonight, what show, what comedy, what murder.

The streets themselves are empty. Mead pictures himself as **alone as a hawk's shadow** gliding over open country, or as alone as someone standing in the **middle of a desert plain in Arizona**, with **no house for a thousand miles** — only dry riverbeds and empty streets. *(2025 specimen Q5(ii).)* The great highways, crowded with cars by day, are silent at night, like dry stream-beds.

#### 3. Sneakers, not hard heels

Mead used to walk in **hard-heeled shoes**, but their sound on the pavement made **dogs bark** and **lights come on** in the houses, and people looked out to see who was there. Wanting to move **quietly and unobserved**, he changed to **sneakers** (soft rubber-soled shoes). *(2025 specimen Q5(iii).)* Even so, he walks with his hands in his pockets, breathing the cold air, enjoying the frost and the smell of the night.

#### 4. The police car

On this particular night, as Mead turns for home, a car comes round the corner and **catches him in the beam of its headlights**. He stands **motionless — spellbound**, like a night moth stunned by a lamp — and then is drawn towards it. *(2025 specimen Q5(iv).)*

It is a **police car** — the **only one** left in a city of three million people. Crime has fallen so low that the other police cars are no longer needed. A **metallic voice** orders Mead to stand still and not move.

#### 5. The interview

The voice fires questions at him, and his answers make less and less sense to it:

| Question | Mead's answer | How the machine reacts |
|---|---|---|
| Name? | Leonard Mead | — |
| Business or profession? | He supposes you could call him a **writer**. | It records "**no profession**" — magazines and books no longer sell; nobody reads. |
| What are you doing out? | **Walking** — just walking, for the air, and to see. | It cannot understand walking with no purpose. |
| Do you have an **air conditioner**? | Yes. | Then why go out for air? |
| Do you have a **viewing screen**? | **No.** | A long, crackling silence: this is very suspicious. |
| Are you married? | No; nobody wanted him. | It orders him not to speak unless spoken to. |

The voice orders him to get into the car. When Mead says he has done nothing wrong, the machine simply repeats the order.

#### 6. The empty car

Mead walks to the car and looks inside. **There is no one in it** — no driver, no police officer at all. The car is run entirely by **machines**. The back seat is like a small **black cell with bars**, smelling of hard steel and antiseptic — clean, cold and **inhuman**. Mead gets in.

When he asks where he is being taken, the voice tells him: to the **Psychiatric Center for Research on Regressive Tendencies**. In other words, Mead's love of walking is treated as a **mental illness** — a sign that he is going backwards ("regressive") instead of living as everyone else does.

#### 7. The lit house

As the car carries him away, they pass a single house in the dark street. **Every window is ablaze with bright, warm light** — the only lit house in the whole city. It is **Mead's own house**. "That's my house," he says. The car does not answer. It drives on down the empty, moonlit streets, and the city is left in silence again.

### Conclusion: what the story warns us about

- Technology (television, machines) can cut people off from **nature**, from **each other** and from **themselves**.
- A society that values **conformity** may treat **individuality** as a crime or a sickness.
- When machines replace human judgement, people can be treated **without understanding or mercy**.
- Reading, writing, conversation and simple pleasures like walking can disappear if people stop valuing them.

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — The setting and Mead's habit

- **What happens:** we meet Leonard Mead, who has walked the empty city every night for ten years.
- **Points to note:**
  - **Time:** a misty November evening in **A.D. 2053** — a future world.
  - **Mead:** a **writer**; loves long, silent evening walks on the concrete pavements and grassy patches. *(2025 specimen Q5(i))*
  - In ten years he has **never met another walker** — the first sign of a strange society.

### Section 2 — The city as a graveyard

- **What happens:** Mead passes dark houses where people sit before their television screens.
- **Points to note:**
  - **Graveyard image:** the houses are like **tombs**, the people like the dead; only grey, flickering TV light moves.
  - **Alone:** like the **shadow of a hawk** over open country, or a man in the **middle of an Arizona desert** with no house for a thousand miles. *(2025 specimen Q5(ii))*
  - **Irony:** the people indoors are "alive" but act like the dead; the solitary walker is the only living person.

### Section 3 — The sneakers

- **What happens:** Mead explains why he gave up hard-heeled shoes.
- **Points to note:** hard heels **attracted attention** — **dogs barked**, **lights came on**, people looked out; he wanted to move **quietly and unobserved**. *(2025 specimen Q5(iii))* Even walking quietly is a kind of hiding in this society.

### Section 4 — The police car arrives

- **What happens:** a lone car turns the corner and catches Mead in its headlights.
- **Points to note:**
  - Mead stands **motionless, spellbound** — like a **moth** caught in a light — and moves towards it. *(2025 specimen Q5(iv))*
  - The **only police car** in a city of **three million** — crime has almost disappeared because people never go out.

### Section 5 — The interview

- **What happens:** the car questions Mead about his name, profession, purpose, home and marriage.
- **Points to note:**
  - "Writer" counts as **no profession** — nobody reads books or magazines any more.
  - Walking "for air" makes no sense when every home has an **air conditioner**; walking "to see" makes no sense when everyone watches screens.
  - Having **no viewing screen** is the most suspicious fact of all.
  - The machine allows **no explanation**: it only orders.

### Section 6 — The empty car and the Psychiatric Center

- **What happens:** the car is empty; Mead is locked in its cell-like back seat and told he is being taken to the Psychiatric Center for Research on Regressive Tendencies.
- **Points to note:**
  - **No driver:** the state is run by **machines**; there is no human to appeal to.
  - The back seat is a small **prison** — cold, clean, metallic.
  - **"Regressive tendencies"** — walking without a purpose is treated as **mental illness**. *(2025 specimen Q5(v): he is taken away for walking without a purpose.)*

### Section 7 — The ending

- **What happens:** they pass Mead's house, the only one brightly lit; the car drives on.
- **Points to note:**
  - **Contrast:** Mead's house glows with warm yellow light; all the others are dark with grey TV glow — his house is full of **life and individuality**.
  - The car's silence at "That's my house" shows a world with **no compassion**.
  - The ending is quiet but chilling: an ordinary man disappears for being **different**.

### 5.4 Beyond the text (general knowledge, not needed for exam answers)

- When Bradbury wrote the story (1951), television sets were rapidly entering American homes. Many writers worried that people would stop reading, talking and going out.
- A **dystopia** is the opposite of a **utopia** (an ideal society). Famous dystopian novels include George Orwell's *Nineteen Eighty-Four* and Aldous Huxley's *Brave New World*, as well as Bradbury's *Fahrenheit 451*.
- Today, discussions about "screen time", social media and **self-driving cars** make the story feel surprisingly modern.

## 7. Themes / Messages

- **Technology and isolation:** screens keep people indoors and apart; nobody walks, talks or reads.
- **Conformity versus individuality:** anyone who behaves differently is treated as sick.
- **Dehumanisation:** machines replace people — even the police car has no human in it.
- **Loss of nature and simple joys:** walking, fresh air and silence are no longer valued.
- **Loss of literature and thought:** writing is "no profession"; nobody reads.
- **Control by the state:** a citizen can be taken away without crime, trial or appeal.

### 7.1 Why the story is dystopian

| Feature of a dystopia | In *The Pedestrian* |
|---|---|
| a future society that seems orderly | crime has almost disappeared |
| hidden control | one automated police car controls the city |
| loss of freedom | a man cannot walk the streets at night |
| conformity | everyone watches screens indoors |
| punishment of difference | Mead is sent to a psychiatric centre |
| a warning to the reader | about technology and passivity |

*(2025 specimen Q1(xvi): the story is best described as dystopian.)*

### 7.2 Science fiction or horror?

| Science fiction | Horror |
|---|---|
| set in the future (A.D. 2053) | a man is taken away for no crime |
| a driverless, talking police car | the city is like a graveyard; people like the dead |
| "viewing screens" in every home | the cold, cell-like back seat |
| imagines the effect of technology on society | the chilling, helpless ending |

*(2025 specimen Q5(v): accept either, with two plausible reasons.)*

### 7.3 Imagery and comparisons

| Image | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| walking through a graveyard | the people indoors are as good as dead |
| the shadow of a hawk over open country | Mead's solitude and freedom |
| the middle of an Arizona desert, no house for a thousand miles | total emptiness and isolation |
| a moth caught in a lamp | Mead stunned and helpless in the car's lights |
| the back seat like a small black cell | the inhuman cruelty of the system |
| the brightly lit house | warmth, life and individuality |

### 7.4 Then and now

| In the story (written 1951) | Today |
|---|---|
| "viewing screens" in every room | televisions, phones, tablets, streaming |
| nobody walks at night | people spend long hours online indoors |
| a driverless police car | self-driving cars, surveillance cameras, automated systems |
| reading has died out | concern about falling reading habits |
| isolation and alienation | loneliness despite constant connection |

*(2027 specimen Q1(xiv): the aspect of our world that most resembles Mead's is isolation and alienation.)*

### 7.5 Significance of the title

A **pedestrian** is simply a person who walks. In Mead's world, this ordinary word has become strange: he is the **only** pedestrian in the city, and being one is enough to get him arrested. The plain title is therefore **ironic** — it names the "crime" that makes Mead an outsider.

**Solutions — MCQs**

**2025 specimen Q1(xvi).** **(b) dystopian.** It shows a future society where technology isolates people and difference is punished.
**2027 specimen Q1(xiv).** **(a) isolation and alienation.** Like Mead's neighbours, many people today spend long hours alone with screens.

**2025 specimen Q5.**

**(i) What was Leonard Mead's occupation? What did he love to do? When is the story set?** [3]
He was a **writer**. He loved to go out on **long walks in the evening**, in the **silence**, walking on the **concrete pavement (sidewalk)** and on **grassy patches**. The story is set in **A.D. 2053**.

**(ii) To what does Mead compare his walk through the empty streets? Mention TWO reasons he gives for making this comparison.** [3]
He compares his walks to the **shadow of a hawk in mid-country**. Reasons: the roads were as **deserted as the centre of a plain in the Arizona desert, with no house in a thousand miles**; and he was **as alone in the city as a hawk in mid-country**, with no habitation or people around.

**(iii) Why had Mead decided to change his footwear from hard-heeled shoes to sneakers?** [3]
His **hard-heeled shoes attracted unwanted attention**: **dogs on the streets barked**, and **lights came on** in the houses as people looked out. He wanted to **move quietly and unobserved**, so he wore sneakers.

**(iv) What happened quite suddenly as he was making his way home? What was Mead's immediate reaction?** [3]
**A lone car turned the corner** and **caught him in its headlights**. He **stood motionless — spellbound, fascinated** — and then **moved towards it**.

**(v) Why was Mead taken away by the police car? Would you call this a horror story or a piece of science fiction? Give reasons for your answer.** [4]
He was taken away **for walking without a purpose** — in his society this was seen as abnormal, a "regressive tendency". *(Personal response — accept either, with two plausible reasons.)* **Science fiction:** it is set in the **future (2053)** and imagines technology such as **driverless, talking police cars** and **viewing screens** in every home, and their effect on society. **Horror:** an innocent man is **seized by a machine** and taken to a psychiatric centre for **no crime**; the city is like a **graveyard** of living people, and the ending leaves us chilled.

**2026 Q1(x).** **(a) Watching television** — sitting in their dark houses before the viewing screens.

**2026 Q5.**

**(i) In which year is the story set? What did Mead 'most dearly love to do'?** [3]
In **2053 A.D.**, on a misty evening in November. He most loved **to go out into the silent city at night and walk** — along the buckled concrete pavements, through the empty streets, for hours.

**(ii) What is the walk compared to? Two reasons why the comparison suits.** [3]
To **walking through a graveyard**. It suits because: the houses are **dark, silent and lifeless** like tombs; the people inside sit **motionless before their screens, as if dead**; only **faint flickers of light** show behind the windows, like will-o'-the-wisps over graves; and **no living person walks** the streets. (Any two.)

**(iii) Which three responses from Mead are viewed with suspicion?** [3]
That he is **a writer** (recorded as "no profession"); that he is **walking just to walk — "for air" and "to see"**; that he has **no viewing screen** in his house (also that he is **not married**). (Any three.)

**(iv) Where is Mead taken? What does the author observe about his house?** [3]
To the **Psychiatric Center for Research on Regressive Tendencies**. **(v) Two dangers the author warns against; an alternative title with a reason.** [4]

**2026 Improvement Q1(ix).** **(a) Addiction to phones.** Written in 1951, the story shows people glued to television (viewing screens), not phones.

**2025 Q1(xiv).** **(c) 3, 1, 4, 2.** Mead's love of night walks (3), the police car stops him (1), the metallic voice questions him (4), and he is taken away (2).

**2025 Improvement Q1(xv).** **(b) a writer** — which the police car records as "No profession", since nobody reads any more.

**2025 Improvement Q4.**

**(i) Why had Mead changed to sneakers for his night walks?** [3]
Because **hard heels made a noise** on the pavement that set **dogs barking**, made **lights click on** and **faces appear** at the windows; the whole street was startled by a lone figure passing in the night. Sneakers let him walk **silently, without drawing attention**.

**(ii) What did Mead enjoy doing every evening? What does this tell you about him?** [3]
He loved **walking alone through the silent city at night**, often for hours and miles, and he had done so for **ten years**. It shows that he is a **lover of solitude, nature and fresh air**, **thoughtful and imaginative**, and **different (a non-conformist)** in a society where everyone else stays indoors watching screens.

**(iii) Why is the walk compared to 'walking through a graveyard'? Two other words that create this atmosphere.** [3]
Because the houses are **dark and silent**, and the people inside sit **motionless before their television screens**, as if **dead**; only faint flickers of light show at the windows, and **no one else is ever out** walking. **(iv) Mention any three questions the police car asked him.** [3]

**(v) Where was the car taking Mead? Why? What are your feelings for him at the end?** [4]
To the **Psychiatric Center for Research on Regressive Tendencies**. ### 8.2 More practice in the ICSE extract style

**Extract A (paraphrased).** Passing the dark houses, Mead feels as if he were walking through a graveyard. Only faint grey lights flicker behind the windows, and he sometimes whispers to the houses, asking what is on the channels tonight.

**(i)** Why are the houses dark? What are the people doing? [3]
**Answer:** The people are **indoors, watching television** ("viewing screens") in the dark; the only light is the **flickering grey glow** of the screens.

**(ii)** Why does Mead compare his walk to walking through a graveyard? [3]
**Answer:** The houses are like **tombs** and the silent, motionless people inside are like the **dead** — they have no real life, conversation or movement.

**(iii)** What does Mead's whispering at the windows show about him? [3]
**Answer:** His **humour and loneliness** — he has no one to talk to — and his **gentle mockery** of a society that lives only through its screens.

**(iv)** Why does Mead wear sneakers? [3]
**Answer:** His **hard-heeled shoes** made **dogs bark** and **lights come on**, drawing attention; he wanted to walk **quietly and unobserved**.

**(v)** What does this passage suggest about the society of 2053? How is it similar to our world today? [4]
**Answer:** It is a society of **isolated, passive people** controlled by technology, who have given up walking, talking and reading. Today, too, many people spend long hours alone with **phones and screens**, and may feel **isolated and alienated** even when "connected" — the similarity named in the 2027 specimen.

**Extract B (paraphrased).** The voice from the car asks Mead whether he has a viewing screen. When he says he does not, there is a long, crackling silence.

**(i)** Why has the car stopped Mead? [3]
**Answer:** He is the only person **outdoors at night**; in a society where everyone stays home, a man **walking** is suspicious.

**(ii)** What other questions has the car asked, and how did Mead answer? [3]
**Answer:** His **name** (Leonard Mead); his **profession** (a writer — recorded as "no profession"); what he is doing (**walking**, for air and to see); whether he has an **air conditioner** (yes); and later, whether he is **married** (no).

**(iii)** Why is having no viewing screen so suspicious? [3]
**Answer:** **Everyone** in the city watches screens; not having one marks Mead as **different** — a nonconformist who does not live like others.

**(iv)** What does Mead find when he looks into the car? [3]
**Answer:** **No one** — the car has **no driver**; it is run by machines. The back seat is like a small **black cell with bars**, cold and metallic.

**(v)** Where is Mead taken, and what does the name of the place suggest? [4]
**Answer:** To the **Psychiatric Center for Research on Regressive Tendencies**. It suggests that his **individuality** — walking, writing, not watching screens — is treated as a **mental illness**, a step "backwards"; the society does not tolerate difference.

## 10. Short-Answer Questions (3 and 4 marks)

### Extract parts (3 marks each)

**Q1.** What was Leonard Mead's occupation? What did he love to do? When is the story set? *(2025 specimen)*
**Answer:** He was a writer.
He loved long, silent evening walks on the concrete pavements and grassy patches.
The story is set in A.D. 2053.

**Q2.** To what does Mead compare his walk, and why? *(2025 specimen)*
**Answer:** To the shadow of a hawk in mid-country.
The roads are as deserted as the centre of an Arizona desert, with no house in a thousand miles.
He is as alone in the city as a hawk over empty country.

**Q3.** Why did Mead change from hard-heeled shoes to sneakers? *(2025 specimen)*
**Answer:** Hard heels attracted unwanted attention.
Dogs barked and lights came on in the houses.
He wanted to move quietly and unobserved.

**Q4.** What happened suddenly as Mead was walking home? How did he react? *(2025 specimen)*
**Answer:** A lone car turned the corner.
It caught him in its headlights.
He stood motionless, spellbound, and then moved towards it.

**Q5.** Why does Mead feel he is walking through a graveyard?
**Answer:** The houses are dark, lit only by flickering grey TV screens.
The people inside sit silent and still, like the dead.
The houses seem like tombs.

**Q6.** Why is there only one police car in the city?
**Answer:** Crime has fallen very low.
People stay indoors watching screens.
Other police cars are no longer needed.

**Q7.** Why does the car record Mead's profession as "no profession"?
**Answer:** Mead says he is a writer.
But people no longer read books or magazines.
So writing has no value in this society.

**Q8.** Why does Mead's answer about the viewing screen cause a long silence?
**Answer:** Everyone in the city watches viewing screens.
Mead has none.
This marks him as strange and suspicious.

**Q9.** What does Mead find when he looks into the car?
**Answer:** No one is inside — there is no driver.
The car is run by machines.
The back seat is like a small black cell with bars.

**Q10.** Where is Mead taken, and why? *(2025 specimen)*
**Answer:** To the Psychiatric Center for Research on Regressive Tendencies.
For walking without a purpose.
His individuality is treated as mental illness.

**Q11.** Describe the ending of the story.
**Answer:** The car passes Mead's house, the only one brightly lit.
Mead says it is his house.
The car does not answer and drives on through the empty streets.

**Q12.** What makes Mead different from the people of his city?
**Answer:** He walks outdoors at night; they stay indoors.
He has no viewing screen; they all watch screens.
He is a writer in a world where no one reads.

### Extract parts (4 marks each)

**Q13.** Would you call this a horror story or science fiction? Give reasons. *(2025 specimen)*
**Answer:** (Either, with two reasons.) Science fiction: set in A.D. 2053.
It imagines driverless talking police cars and viewing screens in every home.
Horror: an innocent man is seized by a machine for no crime.
The city is a graveyard of living people; the ending is chilling.

**Q14.** Why is the story called dystopian? *(2025 specimen)*
**Answer:** It shows a future society that seems orderly — almost no crime.
But people are isolated, passive and controlled by technology.
Freedom is lost: a man cannot walk at night.
Anyone different is punished as mentally ill.

**Q15.** What aspect of our world is most similar to Mead's? Explain. *(2027 specimen)*
**Answer:** Isolation and alienation.
Many people spend long hours alone with phones and screens.
Face-to-face conversation and outdoor activity decline.
People can feel lonely even when constantly "connected".

**Q16.** Write a character sketch of Leonard Mead.
**Answer:** An individualist who walks every night and has no viewing screen.
A lover of nature, silence and fresh air.
Lonely — unmarried, he has never met another walker.
Gentle and harmless, yet defenceless against a machine-run state.

**Q17.** What does the police car symbolise?
**Answer:** A state controlled by technology, not people.
Its metallic voice allows no explanation.
It treats difference as illness.
It shows no compassion, even at "That's my house".

**Q18.** Explain the contrast at the end of the story.
**Answer:** Mead's house is the only one with every window brightly lit.
The other houses are dark, with only grey TV light.
His house stands for warmth, life and individuality.
Yet it is he who is taken away — the society punishes life itself.

**Q19.** What warning does Bradbury give through this story?
**Answer:** Technology can cut people off from nature and each other.
People may stop reading, thinking and talking.
A conformist society may treat individuals as sick.
We must keep human contact and freedom alive.

**Q20.** Discuss the significance of the title.
**Answer:** A pedestrian is simply a person who walks.
Mead is the only pedestrian in the city.
Being one is enough to get him arrested.
The plain title is ironic: it names the "crime" that makes him an outsider.
