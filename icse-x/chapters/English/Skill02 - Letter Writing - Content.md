## 1. Complete Lesson

### What Question 2 asks

Question 2 gives **two** tasks — one **informal (friendly)** letter and one **formal** letter — and you write **one** of them.

| Year | Informal task | Formal task |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | to a friend about a book that impacted you deeply, and two ways you will change your behaviour | to the Principal seeking permission to start a gardening club that meets after school |
| 2025 | to your uncle, thanking him for a gift of ₹1,000, saying how and why you will spend it | to the Municipal Commissioner about overflowing garbage bins, with suggestions |
| 2026 | to your parents abroad about being selected School Captain, your feelings and plans | to the Municipal Commissioner about garbage not cleared for several days, with suggestions |
| 2027 | to your grandfather, who encouraged outdoor games, about a game you enjoyed and what you learnt | to the Director of the Museum about the poor condition of exhibits, requesting action |

### The CISCE formats

In 2026 CISCE published sample letters in **four accepted layouts** (fully blocked, or with indented addresses, date and paragraphs) and said: "**Any one format may be followed, all are acceptable.**" What matters is that every part is present and in the right order.

**Informal (friendly) letter — parts in order**

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| 1. Sender's address (with PIN code) | 26, Hill View Road, Laitumkhrah, Shillong – 793 003. |
| 2. Date | 16th September 2026 (or 16 September 2026) |
| 3. Salutation | My dear Mother and Father, / Dear Grandfather, / Dear Aditi, |
| 4. Body | 3–4 paragraphs: warm opening; main content; closing remarks |
| 5. Closing | With love, / Yours affectionately, / Yours lovingly, |
| 6. Name | your first name only |

**Formal letter — parts in order**

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| 1. Sender's address | 14, Police Bazar, Shillong – 793 001. |
| 2. Date | 16th September 2026 |
| 3. Receiver's designation and address | The Municipal Commissioner, Shillong Municipal Board, Shillong – 793 001. |
| 4. Salutation | Dear Sir, / Dear Madam, / Dear Sir/Madam, |
| 5. Body | 3–4 paragraphs: purpose; details; request or suggestions; polite close |
| 6. Closing | Thanking you, / Yours faithfully, |
| 7. Name | full name (in a formal letter, CISCE's samples sign with the full name) |

> **Subject line:** CISCE's 2026 and 2027 sample formal letters go straight from the receiver's address to "Dear Sir,". Some schools teach a subject line; it is not wrong, but it is **not required** by the CISCE samples. If you use one, keep it short.

### How it is marked

The 2027 scheme says: "**Format, Content, Expression are important.**" For content, it lists what each letter must include (for example, for the museum letter: the name of the museum, a description of the condition of the artefacts, and the steps to be taken to restore them).

**Core9X marking guide (10 marks):**

| What | Marks | What earns it |
|---|---|---|
| Format | 2 | all parts present and in order; correct salutation and closing for the type |
| Content | 5 | every point in the question covered, with relevant details |
| Expression | 3 | suitable tone; clear, accurate, well-paragraphed language |

### The informal letter

- **Tone:** warm, natural and personal — but still grammatical. Avoid slang and text-message spellings.
- **Opening paragraph:** a greeting and the reason for writing ("I have some wonderful news…", "Thank you so much for…").
- **Middle paragraphs:** the main content — **answer every part** of the question (e.g. *how* you will spend the money **and** *why*).
- **Closing paragraph:** good wishes, a question about the reader, a hope to meet.
- **Closing:** *With love,* / *Yours affectionately,* / *Yours lovingly,* + first name.

### The formal letter

- **Tone:** polite, clear and to the point; no emotional exaggeration, no contractions.
- **Paragraph 1:** who you are and why you are writing.
- **Paragraph 2:** the details — what, where, since when, the effects.
- **Paragraph 3:** your request or suggestions (specific and practical).
- **Paragraph 4:** a polite close ("I look forward to your cooperation in this matter.").
- **Closing:** *Thanking you,* (optional) + *Yours faithfully,* + full name.

### Useful phrases

| Purpose | Informal | Formal |
|---|---|---|
| opening | I hope you are keeping well. / It was lovely to hear from you. | I am writing to bring to your notice… / I wish to draw your attention to… |
| giving news / details | Guess what happened last week! | The situation has persisted for over a week. |
| thanking | Thank you so much for… | I am grateful for… / I would appreciate it if… |
| requesting | Do write back soon. | I request you to kindly… / I would be obliged if you could… |
| suggesting | Why don't we…? | I would like to suggest that… / It may be helpful to… |
| closing | Give my love to everyone at home. | I look forward to your cooperation. / Thanking you in anticipation. |

### Common mistakes

- Writing *Yours faithfully* in a letter to a relative, or *Yours affectionately* to an official.
- Leaving out the **date** or the **sender's address**.
- In a formal letter, forgetting the **receiver's designation and address**.
- Missing a **required point** — for example, not saying *why* you chose to spend the money a certain way.
- Using **slang** or **text language** ("u", "pls", "gonna").
- Writing **one long paragraph**.
- Over-length: a long letter does not earn more marks; covering every point clearly does.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, students will be able to:

1. **Recognise** the difference between informal and formal letters in purpose, tone and format.
2. **Lay out** both kinds of letter in a format accepted by CISCE.
3. **Plan** the content so that every point in the question is covered.
4. **Write** warm, natural informal letters and polite, concise formal letters.
5. **Use** suitable salutations, closings and phrases.
6. **Avoid** common errors of format, tone and language.

### 8.2 Planning a letter in three minutes

| Step | Informal example (2025 (i)) | Formal example (2027 (ii)) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Underline every point | thank · how · why | the museum · the poor condition · request action |
| 2. One paragraph per point | P1 thanks; P2 how; P3 why; P4 closing | P1 visit and purpose; P2 damage seen; P3 steps requested; P4 close |
| 3. Choose the tone | warm and grateful | polite and concerned |
| 4. Check the frame | address, date, *Dear Uncle,* … *Yours affectionately,* | address, date, receiver, *Dear Sir,* … *Yours faithfully,* full name |

**2026 Q2(i).**

**Type:** informal letter.

**Format:** your address, date, *Dear Kabir,*, body in paragraphs, *Your friend,*, first name.

**Paragraph plan:** (1) The invitation — a small study group of four before the examinations. (2) **How you plan to revise** — meet three evenings a week at one another's homes; each member leads a subject; solve past papers under time; quiz one another; keep phones away. (3) **Why it helps** — doubts cleared quickly, strengths shared, regular practice, less stress, motivation. (4) Ask for a reply; close warmly.

**2026 Q2(ii).**

**Type:** formal letter (complaint).

**Format:** sender's address, date, *The Municipal Commissioner, …*, *Sir/Madam,*, *Yours faithfully,*, full name.

**Paragraph plan:** (1) Purpose — broken, choked drains in your locality. (2) **Problems** — overflowing dirty water on the roads, foul smell, mosquitoes and diseases such as dengue, waterlogging in the rains, damage to roads and homes, danger to children and the elderly. (3) **Solutions** — clean and repair the drains at once, cover open drains, regular inspection, ban dumping of garbage in drains, a complaint helpline. (4) Request for prompt action; thanks.

**Q1.** Question 2 of ICSE Paper 1 carries

## 11. Long-Answer Questions (10 marks each)

### Informal letters (10 marks each)

**Q1.** Write a letter to your grandfather who has always encouraged you to play outdoor games. Tell him about a game you enjoyed playing with your friends. Share how the experience made you feel and what you learned from it. *(ICSE 2027 specimen)*
**Model answer:** 7, Lady Hydari Road,\
Shillong – 793 001.\
12th March 2027.

Dear Grandfather,

I hope you are keeping well and enjoying your morning walks. I am writing to thank you, because without your encouragement I would never have discovered how much I love playing outdoors.

Last Saturday, my friends and I played a long game of football at the ground behind our colony. We were eight a side, with schoolbags for goalposts. The ground was muddy after the rain, and within minutes we were all covered in mud from head to toe. My team was losing two–nil at half-time, but I remembered what you always say — "Keep the ball moving and talk to your team" — and we started passing instead of running alone. We scored three goals in the second half, and I set up the winning one.

I cannot describe how happy I felt. My legs ached and I was soaked, but I felt energetic, proud and more alive than I had in weeks of sitting with my phone. Even our opponents laughed and shook our hands at the end.

The game taught me that teamwork matters more than individual skill, and that a match is never lost until the final whistle. It also showed me that time spent outdoors with friends makes me calmer and happier when I sit down to study.

Thank you for always pushing me out of the door, Grandfather. I will tell you all about our next match when I visit in the holidays.

Yours lovingly,\
Aman

**Marking:**
- Format: address, date, salutation, closing, name — 2 marks
- Content: thanks for his encouragement; the game; feelings; lessons learnt (CISCE's points) — 5 marks
- Expression: warm, natural, well-paragraphed — 3 marks

## 15. Revision Notes

### Formats

| Informal | Formal |
|---|---|
| sender's address | sender's address |
| date | date |
| — | receiver's designation and address |
| *Dear Grandfather,* | *Dear Sir,* / *Dear Madam,* |
| body (3–4 paragraphs) | body (3–4 paragraphs) |
| *With love,* / *Yours affectionately,* / *Yours lovingly,* | *Thanking you,* (optional) *Yours faithfully,* |
| first name | full name |

### Remember

- CISCE accepts **any one** of its sample layouts (blocked or indented).
- CISCE's samples have **no subject line**.
- **Cover every point** in the question; one paragraph per point.
- Marks: **format, content, expression** (Core9X guide: 2 · 5 · 3).
- Spend no more than **20 minutes**.

### Avoid

Wrong closing for the type · missing date or address · slang and text language · one long paragraph · leaving out a required point.
