## 1. Complete Lesson

### Mineral-based industries

**Mineral-based industries** use **minerals** as raw materials — **iron and steel, aluminium, copper smelting, cement, petrochemicals** (from petroleum), and **electronics** (which uses metals, silicon and plastics). They are mostly **large-scale, heavy and capital-intensive**.

### 1. The iron and steel industry

**Why is iron and steel a basic (key) industry?** Its products — **steel and machinery** — are the **raw materials and tools for almost all other industries**: **railways, roads, bridges, buildings, automobiles, ships, machines, defence, infrastructure**. A country's **industrial strength** is measured by its steel output. That is why its **importance will never decline**.

**Raw materials** — **iron ore**, **coking coal**, **limestone** (and **dolomite**) as a flux, **manganese** (to toughen steel); plus large amounts of **water** and **power**. Iron ore and coal are **bulky**, so plants are located **near the coalfields and iron-ore mines** — mostly in the **Chhota Nagpur plateau** and its neighbourhood — or at **ports** where raw materials can be imported and steel exported.

**Integrated and mini steel plants**
- An **integrated steel plant** carries out **all the processes of steel-making in one unit** — from **smelting iron ore** in a **blast furnace** to **making and rolling steel** into finished products.
- A **mini steel plant** uses **scrap steel and sponge iron** in **electric arc furnaces**; it makes mild and alloy steel. **Mini steel plants help conserve coal**, because they **operate on electricity and do not use coal for smelting**.

**Other steel plants** — **Durgapur** (West Bengal, British help), **Bokaro** (Jharkhand, Soviet help), **Burnpur** (West Bengal), **Salem** (Tamil Nadu), **Bhadravati** (Karnataka), and private plants such as **Vijayanagar** (Karnataka).

**Problems of the iron and steel industry** — **shortage of good coking coal** (much is imported); **high cost of production**; **power shortages**; some **outdated technology**; **low labour productivity** in older plants; **pollution**.

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2027 specimen, Q10(i))* (a) In which state is the Visakhapatnam Steel Plant located? (b) Why is it called a "shore-based" plant?
**Solution:** (a) **Andhra Pradesh**. (b) Visakhapatnam has a **coastal location**; hence it is called a shore-based plant.

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2025, Q10(iii))* (a) Name the iron and steel industry located in Chhattisgarh. (b) From where does it get its supply of 1. iron ore 2. coal?
**Solution:** (a) **Bhilai Steel Plant**. (b) 1. Iron ore — **Dalli–Rajhara**, Bastar / Durg. 2. Coal — **Korba, Raniganj, Jharia, Kargali, Bokaro**.

### 2. The petrochemical industry

**Petrochemicals** are chemicals obtained from **petroleum (crude oil) and natural gas**. At a refinery, crude oil gives **naphtha** and gases such as **ethylene and propylene**, which petrochemical plants turn into many products.

![Fig. 14.3 The petrochemical chain](figures/g_ch14_fig03_petrochemicals.png)

**Raw materials** — **crude oil (naphtha)**, **natural gas**.
**Products** — **plastics** (buckets, mugs, pipes, PVC), **synthetic fibres** (nylon, polyester, acrylic), **synthetic rubber**, **detergents**, **paints, dyes**, **fertilisers**, **insecticides**, medicines, cosmetics.

**Why are petrochemical plants located close to oil refineries?** The **raw materials** are **by-products of the petroleum-refining process**; locating near refineries makes them **easily available** and **reduces transport costs**.

**Why are petrochemical products popular and in great demand?** They are **cheap / cost-effective**, **durable**, **light**, **attractive**, **rust-proof**, can be **manufactured quickly in large quantities**, and do **not depend on agriculture** (so supply is **stable**); they replace wood, metal, cotton and natural rubber in countless uses, and the industry **creates employment**.

**Major centres** — **Mumbai (Trombay)**, **Vadodara (Koyali)**, **Jamnagar**, **Hazira** (Surat), **Ankleshwar**, **Panipat** (Haryana), **Haldia** (West Bengal), **Visakhapatnam**, **Bongaigaon** (Assam).

**Example 3.** *(ICSE 2026 specimen, Q10(iii))* With reference to the petrochemical industry: (a) State a reason for its growing popularity. (b) Why is it located close to oil refineries? (c) Name two petrochemical products.
**Solution:** (a) Any one: **cheaper**; **durable**; can be **manufactured in a short span of time**; **attractive**. (b) It **facilitates easy availability of raw materials** / **reduces transport costs**. (c) Any two: **plastics, synthetic fibre, synthetic rubber, PVC pipes, detergents**.

### 3. The electronics industry

The **electronics industry** makes **televisions, radios, mobile phones, computers and laptops, semiconductors, telecommunication equipment, medical instruments, defence and space electronics** and household appliances; it is closely linked with the **software (IT) industry**.

**Major centres** — **Bengaluru** (the "**Electronics Capital of India**" or "Silicon Valley of India"), **Hyderabad**, **Pune**, **Mumbai**, **Chennai**, **Delhi–Noida–Gurugram**, **Kolkata**, **Thiruvananthapuram**, **Kochi**, Mohali.

![Fig. 14.6 Petrochemical centres and electronics centres](figures/g_ch14_fig06_petro_electronics_map.png)

![Fig. 14.4 How the electronics industry shapes modern life](figures/g_ch14_fig04_electronics.png)

**Relevance in the modern world — why the electronics industry is important and growing fast**
1. **Communication** — mobile phones, internet, satellites; the whole world is connected.
2. **Education** — computers, interactive boards, laptops, e-books and e-content, online classes.
3. **Health care** — scanners, monitors, diagnostic machines.
4. **Defence, research, space exploration, weather forecasting**.
5. **Entertainment** — TV, music, games.
6. **Offices, banking, postal services** — automation and digital payments.
7. **Smart devices** at home **reduce our workload**.
8. **Employment** for skilled youth and **exports** of hardware and software.
9. New technologies — **robots, artificial intelligence**, digital gadgets — keep the industry **growing at a fast rate**.

The electronics industry has **revolutionised the lifestyle** of the Indian masses.

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2024, Q10(iii))* Give one reason each: (a) Mini-steel plants help in conservation of coal. (b) Location of the sugar industry is shifting to the southern part of India. (c) The electronic industry is growing at a fast rate.
**Solution:** (a) Mini-steel plants **conserve coal** as they **operate on electricity** and **do not use coal for smelting**. (b) Any one: **favourable climate** (temperature, rainfall, frost-free days) ensures a steady supply of cane; **fertile black soil**; **large holdings**; **scientific farming and improved machinery**; **mills near cane fields**; **long crushing season**; **cooperative farming**. (c) Any one: demand for **digital devices, computers, robots, artificial intelligence**; their use in **education, defence, research, entertainment, offices, medicine, space, meteorology, postal services**.

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — Steel follows coal and ore
Most steel plants sit in a ring around the **Chhota Nagpur plateau**, where coal and iron ore lie close together; **Visakhapatnam** broke the pattern by using the **sea**.

### Section 2 — Partners from abroad
India built its public-sector steel plants in the 1950s with help from the **USSR (Bhilai, Bokaro)**, **West Germany (Rourkela)** and **Britain (Durgapur)**.

### Section 3 — From oil to everything
Refinery by-products become plastics, fibres and rubber — so petrochemical complexes grow **next to refineries**.

### Section 4 — The knowledge industry
Electronics depends less on heavy raw materials and more on **skilled people, research and markets** — so it clusters in cities like **Bengaluru and Hyderabad**.

## 4. Vocabulary (Key Terms)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Basic (key) industry | An industry whose products are used by other industries. |
| Integrated steel plant | A plant doing all steel-making processes in one unit. |
| Mini steel plant | A small plant making steel from scrap and sponge iron in electric furnaces. |
| Blast furnace | A furnace in which iron ore is smelted with coke and limestone. |
| Pig iron | Crude iron from a blast furnace. |
| Coking coal | Bituminous coal used to make coke for blast furnaces. |
| Flux | A substance (limestone) that removes impurities while smelting. |
| Sponge iron | Iron made directly from ore without melting, used in mini plants. |
| Shore-based plant | A plant located on the coast with port facilities. |
| SAIL | Steel Authority of India Ltd., which runs public-sector plants. |
| Petrochemicals | Chemicals made from petroleum and natural gas. |
| Naphtha | A refinery product used as feedstock for petrochemicals. |
| Synthetic fibre | A man-made fibre such as nylon or polyester. |
| Electronics | Industry making devices that use electric circuits and chips. |
| Semiconductor | A material (silicon) used to make computer chips. |
| Electronics Capital of India | Bengaluru. |

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 Jamsetji Tata and Jamshedpur
Jamsetji Nusserwanji Tata dreamt of an Indian steel plant; after his death (1904) his sons founded the **Tata Iron and Steel Company** at Sakchi in **1907**. The town was renamed **Jamshedpur** in his honour.

### 6.2 Sir M. Visvesvaraya (1861–1962)
The engineer-statesman of Mysore who started the **Mysore Iron Works** at **Bhadravati** (now Visvesvaraya Iron and Steel Plant). His birthday, 15 September, is celebrated as **Engineers' Day** in India.

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Steel is the skeleton of development** — no railways, bridges or machines without it.
- **Raw materials pull heavy industry; skills pull high-tech industry.**
- **Petrochemicals bring convenience and pollution** — plastics must be used and disposed of wisely (Chapter 16).

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **North-East India:** the **Numaligarh** and **Bongaigaon** refineries in Assam support petrochemical units.
- **Your school:** steel benches, plastic chairs, a smart board and computers — all three industries in one classroom.
