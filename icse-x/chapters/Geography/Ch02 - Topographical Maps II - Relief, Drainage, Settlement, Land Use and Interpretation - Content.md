## 1. Complete Lesson

### Reading relief from contours

**Relief** means the **ups and downs of the land** — hills, valleys and slopes. On a toposheet it is shown by **contours** (brown lines of equal height), helped by **spot heights**, **triangulated heights**, **relative heights** and symbols such as **broken ground** and **sand dunes**.

**Two rules decide almost every answer:**
1. **Spacing** — contours **close together** mean a **steep slope**; contours **far apart** mean a **gentle slope**.
2. **Values** — the land rises towards the **higher contour values**, so check which way the values increase.

![Fig. 2.1 Landforms from contours](figures/g_ch02_fig01_contour_landforms.png)

| Landform | How the contours look |
|---|---|
| **Hill** | **Closed** contours, one inside another, the **highest value at the centre**; often a spot or triangulated height on top |
| **Conical hill** | Roughly **circular** closed contours at **equal, close spacing** |
| **Ridge** | **Long, narrow** closed contours — an elongated hill |
| **Valley** | **V-shaped** contours with the **V pointing upstream** (towards **higher** land); a stream runs along the V's |
| **Spur** | **V-shaped** contours with the **V pointing downhill** (towards **lower** land) — a tongue of high land |
| **Steep / gentle slope** | Contours **crowded** / **widely spaced** |
| **Plateau** | **Crowded edges** with a **wide, flat top** inside (few or no contours in the middle) |
| **Saddle (col)** | A **low gap** between **two hills** |
| **Escarpment / cliff** | Contours **bunch up almost into one line** on one side |

**Other relief clues**
- **Broken ground** — rough, uneven, gullied land shown by a black symbol (common near streams in dry areas).
- **Sand dunes** — brown stipple or ridges, often with a **relative height** (e.g. **.10r**).
- **Watershed (divide)** — the high ground separating streams that flow in different directions.
- **General slope of the land** — the direction in which the **spot heights and contour values fall** and in which the **streams flow**.

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2024, Q1(iii))* (a) Identify the landform marked by the contours in the grid square 3896. (b) What is the pattern of drainage seen in the grid square 3697?
**Solution:** (a) **Hill** (the marking scheme also accepted valley, ridge, escarpment, steep slope, conical hill, watershed or saddle). (b) **Radial**.

### Drainage

**Drainage** means the **rivers, streams and water bodies** of an area.

**Perennial and seasonal streams**
- **Perennial** rivers and streams carry water **all the year**; they are shown in **blue**.
- **Seasonal (intermittent) streams** flow **only in the rainy season**; on these sheets they are drawn as **dry streams** in **black**. A wide river may be shown as **dry with a water channel** — a sandy bed (stippled) with a thin blue channel.
- The sheets used by ICSE (a semi-arid region) have **mostly seasonal streams**; their notes say that **tanks shown dry usually contain water only from July to February**.

**Drainage patterns**

![Fig. 2.2 Drainage patterns](figures/g_ch02_fig02_drainage_patterns.png)

| Pattern | Appearance | Where it forms |
|---|---|---|
| **Dendritic** | **Tree-like**; tributaries join the main stream at acute angles like branches | Areas of **uniform rock**; the commonest pattern |
| **Radial** | Streams **flow outwards in all directions** from a central high point | Around a **hill** or dome |
| **Trellis** | Tributaries join the main stream at **right angles** | Areas of **parallel ridges and valleys** |
| **Parallel** | Streams run **side by side** | Down a **uniform, steep slope** |

A **disappearing stream** fades out in sandy or porous ground — a sign of a dry area.

**Banks of a river.** Stand in the river **facing downstream** (the direction of flow): the bank on your **left hand** is the **left bank**, the one on your **right hand** is the **right bank**. Find the **direction of flow** first — from the **arrow**, or from higher to lower spot heights, or from the way tributaries join.

![Fig. 2.4 River banks](figures/g_ch02_fig04_river_banks.png)

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2025, Q1(ii))* (a) On which bank of the Sukli nadi is the settlement of Pamera situated? (b) Identify the settlement pattern seen in the grid square 6732.
**Solution:** (a) **Right bank**. (b) **Scattered / dispersed**.

### Sources of water and irrigation

| Source | Symbol / clue | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Wells** | **Lined** well — solid blue dot; **unlined** — open blue circle; **tube-well** | The **main source** of water and **irrigation** on these sheets; "**30r**" against a well = its depth |
| **Tanks** | Blue (perennial) or dotted outline (dry, seasonal) | Store rain-water; dry tanks hold water only part of the year |
| **Reservoir with an embankment / dam** | A large blue water body behind an **embankment** (with relative height **r**) or a **sluice** | **Artificial (man-made)**; stores water of seasonal streams for **irrigation** and drinking — this is why water is present when the streams are dry |
| **Perennial river or water channel** | Blue line | Few on these sheets |
| **Canals** | Blue straight lines | Irrigation from a reservoir |

**Brackish wells.** In a dry region some wells give **brackish (salty, saline)** water that is **not fit for drinking**.

**Example 3.** *(ICSE 2019, Q1(g))* Give a reason for each of the following: (i) The water in some of the wells in the north-west quarter of the map is not fit for drinking. (ii) The region near Anadra and Gulabganj has many causeways.
**Solution:** (i) The water in some of the wells is **brackish / salty / saline**. (ii) There are **many seasonal streams** in the region, and **causeways** have to be built where **metalled roads** cross them, so that the roads can cross the streams.

### Settlements

A **settlement** is a place where people live — from a few **huts** to a large village or town. Huts are shown as small **squares**: **permanent huts** (built of brick or stone, filled squares) and **temporary huts** (of mud, grass or thatch, used seasonally, outline squares).

**Settlement patterns**

![Fig. 2.3 Settlement patterns](figures/g_ch02_fig03_settlement_patterns.png)

| Pattern | Appearance | Usual reasons |
|---|---|---|
| **Nucleated (compact, clustered)** | Huts **packed closely** round a centre | A **reliable water source** (well, tank), **fertile flat land**, **security**, a **road junction** |
| **Scattered (dispersed)** | Huts **spread far apart** | **Scarce water and poor land**, rugged or hilly terrain; each family lives beside its fields |
| **Linear** | Huts **in a line** | Along a **road, railway, river bank or canal** |

**Why settlements grow where they do** — near **water** (wells, tanks, rivers), on **flat, cultivable land**, along **roads**, and **above flood level**. Areas with **few settlements** are usually **hilly, rocky or forested** (often **Reserved Forest**), or **sandy and dry**.

**The most important settlement** is the one with the **most services**: a **post office (PO)**, **police station / chowki (PS)**, **dispensary or hospital**, **veterinary hospital**, **dak bungalow**, **school**, a **metalled road** passing through it, the **largest cluster of huts**.

**Fairs.** A settlement where a **fair (mela)** is held is where people **meet socially and for trade at least once a year**; the month is printed beside it.

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2019, Q1(i))* Which according to you is the most important settlement? Give a reason to support your answer.
**Solution:** **Anadra** — it has a **metalled road** passing near it, a **dispensary**, a **dak bungalow**, a **post and telegraph office** and a **police chowki**.

### Land use and natural vegetation

| Land use | How it is shown | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cultivated land** | **Yellow** | Main use of land; irrigated by wells and tanks |
| **Forest** | **Green**, with names such as **Reserved Forest** or **Protected Forest** | Types: **dense mixed jungle**, **fairly dense mixed jungle**, **open scrub** |
| **Open scrub / grass** | Green symbols on white | Grazing |
| **Barren / uncultivated land** | **White** | Includes **sand dunes** and **broken ground** |

**Reserved Forest.** Forests protected by law: **cutting trees, grazing and collecting wood** are **not allowed** without permission, so people of nearby areas **cannot use them for commercial or household purposes**.

**Fire lines** are **straight, cleared strips** cut through a forest. They **prevent forest fires from spreading** and help in the **protection of forest reserves**.

**Example 5.** *(ICSE 2026 specimen, Q1(iv))* (a) How do the local people benefit from the causeways seen in this region? (b) Explain why fire lines are significant from the point of view of the environment.
**Solution:** (a) **Causeways are raised roads** that help people to **cross minor seasonal streams when they have water during the monsoons**. (b) **Fire lines prevent forest fires from spreading** and help in the **protection of forest reserves**.

### Transport and communication

| Means | On the map | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Metalled road / National Highway** | Red double line; distance stones | All-weather; links important settlements |
| **Unmetalled road** | Red broken double line | Usable in the dry season |
| **Cart-track** | Thin red line (OSM sheets) | **Bullock carts**; the **commonest** means in villages |
| **Pack-track** | Black broken line | Pack animals; hilly ground |
| **Foot-path** | Dotted line | On foot |
| **Railway** | Black line with cross-ticks; **RS** = railway station | Long distance, bulky goods |
| **Causeway** | Road dipping across a stream bed | Crossing **seasonal streams** |
| **Communication** | **PO** (post office), **PTO / P & T** (post and telegraph office), **power lines** | Power lines show the village has **electricity** |

**Example 6.** *(ICSE 2024, Q1(ii))* (a) Mention two man-made features seen in the grid square 3794. (b) Mention two means of transport used by the people of Nani Bhatamal in the grid square 3691.
**Solution:** (a) Any two: **permanent hut, chatri, lined perennial well, well, cart track, pack track, metalled road, tehsil or taluk boundary, cultivated land**. (b) Any two: **cart track, pack track, metalled road**.

### Interpreting the human geography of the extract

**Occupation of the people**
- **Agriculture (cultivation)** — shown by **yellow (cultivated) land**, **wells** and **tanks** for irrigation.
- **Animal rearing** — **open scrub and grassland**, a **veterinary hospital**.
- **Others** — a **lime kiln** or brick kiln (lime or brick making), a **fair** (trade).

**Evidence of scanty or seasonal rainfall**
- **Seasonal (dry) streams** and a **dry river with a water channel**.
- **Dry tanks** (holding water only from July to February).
- **Many wells** — people depend on **groundwater**.
- **Tanks and reservoirs with embankments** built to **store monsoon water**.
- **Sand dunes**, **open scrub**, **broken ground** and **disappearing streams**.

**Natural and man-made features**

| Natural features | Man-made features |
|---|---|
| Hill, valley, spur, ridge, slope | Huts and settlements, temple, chhatri |
| Seasonal stream, river, river bed | Well, tank with embankment, dam, canal |
| Sand dune, broken ground, barren land | Metalled road, cart-track, pack-track, railway |
| Forest, scrub, grass | Post office, police station, dispensary, power line |
| Plain | Cultivated land, boundary, lime kiln, causeway |

**A temple** is socially significant: it is a **place of worship** where people **gather for festivals, fairs and social meetings**.

**Example 7.** *(ICSE 2018, Q1(e))* Give two evidences which suggest that the rainfall received in the region shown on the map extract is seasonal.
**Solution:** Any two: **seasonal streams**, **seasonal tanks**, **dry tanks**, **broken ground**, a **seasonal river with a water channel**, **dry streams**.

### Summary of the chapter

- **Relief** — contours: close = steep, far = gentle; hill (closed, highest in centre), conical hill, ridge, valley (V **upstream**), spur (V **downhill**), plateau, saddle, escarpment; broken ground, sand dunes; general slope = direction heights fall and streams flow.
- **Drainage** — perennial (blue) vs seasonal (dry, black); patterns **dendritic** (tree-like), **radial** (from a hill), **trellis** (right angles), **parallel**; **left/right bank** facing downstream.
- **Water** — wells (main source; lined/unlined), tanks (perennial/dry), **reservoir behind an embankment** (artificial); brackish wells.
- **Settlements** — **nucleated, scattered, linear**; permanent vs temporary huts; most important = most services; **fair** = social meeting and trade.
- **Land use** — yellow cultivated; green forest (**Reserved Forest** — no use by people); open scrub; white barren; **fire lines** stop fires.
- **Transport** — metalled road, cart-track (commonest), pack-track, foot-path, railway; **causeways** cross seasonal streams; **power lines** = electricity.
- **Interpretation** — occupation **agriculture**; **scanty, seasonal rainfall** (dry streams, dry tanks, many wells, dunes, scrub); natural vs man-made features.

## 5. Important Facts, Formulas and Results

### 5.1 Must-know facts

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Contours close together | Steep slope |
| V of a valley points | Upstream (to higher land) |
| V of a spur points | Downhill (to lower land) |
| Streams flowing out from a hill | Radial pattern |
| Tree-like streams | Dendritic pattern |
| Huts close together / far apart / in a line | Nucleated / scattered / linear |
| Why causeways | Many seasonal streams cross the roads |
| Why fire lines | To prevent the spread of forest fires |
| Why water in a reservoir when streams are dry | An embankment / dam stores the water |
| Main source of irrigation on ICSE sheets | Wells (lined perennial wells) |
| Commonest means of transport in villages | Cart-track |
| Power line in a village | It has electricity |
| Main occupation shown | Agriculture (cultivation) |
| Blue dot in a settlement | A well — man-made |
| Evidence of seasonal rainfall | Seasonal streams, dry tanks, wells, sand dunes, broken ground |

### 5.2 Memory aids

- **"Valleys point Up, Spurs point Down"** (V up-stream, spur down-hill).
- **"Tree, Star, Ladder"** — dendritic, radial, trellis.
- **"Clustered, Scattered, Lined up"** — nucleated, dispersed, linear.
- **"Dry land clues: SWADS"** — **S**easonal streams, **W**ells, **A**rtificial tanks, **D**unes, **S**crub.

### 5.3 Beyond the text (general knowledge, not needed for exam answers)

- Satellite images now let the Survey of India update toposheets far faster than ground surveys alone.
- The contour idea is used underwater too: **isobaths** join points of equal depth on sea charts.
- Many hill villages in North-East India are strung along ridge-top roads — a **linear** pattern shaped by the relief.

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 Nain Singh Rawat (1830–1882)
A schoolteacher from Kumaon who, trained by the Survey of India, secretly surveyed Tibet in the 1860s, counting his paces with prayer beads and measuring heights with boiling-point thermometers. His route surveys fixed the position and height of Lhasa and mapped long stretches of the Tsangpo (Brahmaputra).

### 6.2 Alexander von Humboldt (1769–1859)
A German naturalist who showed how relief, climate and vegetation change together with height, and who helped popularise the use of lines joining equal values (such as isotherms) on maps — the same idea as contours.

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Landforms decide life** — flat, watered land draws settlements and farms; hills and forests stay empty.
- **Water shapes everything in a dry land** — wells, tanks, embankments and causeways all respond to seasonal rain.
- **Evidence over opinion** — every interpretation must point to symbols on the map.

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **Meghalaya:** a toposheet of the Shillong Plateau shows **closely packed contours** on its southern edge (steep slopes towards the plains of Bangladesh) and **dendritic** streams on its rolling top.
- **Your district:** look at where the main roads, markets and police stations are — the "most important settlement" logic of the map works in real life.
- **Planning:** engineers use contour maps to choose routes for roads and sites for dams and tanks.

**Q1.** How can you tell a steep slope from a gentle slope on a toposheet?
**Answer:** Contours close together show a steep slope.
Contours far apart show a gentle slope.

**Q2.** Distinguish between a valley and a spur as shown by contours.
**Answer:** In a valley the V-shaped contours point upstream, towards higher land.
In a spur they point downhill, towards lower land.

**Q3.** Distinguish between dendritic and radial drainage.
**Answer:** Dendritic — tree-like, tributaries joining like branches, on uniform rock.
Radial — streams flowing outwards in all directions from a hill.

**Q4.** Why are there many causeways on the ICSE sheets?
**Answer:** Many seasonal streams cross the roads.
Causeways let traffic cross them when they carry water in the monsoon.

**Answer:** To prevent forest fires from spreading.
To protect the forest reserves.

**Q6.** Why can people not use a Reserved Forest for household purposes?
**Answer:** It is protected by law.
Cutting trees, grazing and collecting wood are not allowed.

**Q7.** Distinguish between nucleated and scattered settlements.
**Answer:** Nucleated — huts packed closely round a water source or road junction.
Scattered — huts spread far apart, where water and good land are scarce.

## 15. Revision Notes

### Chapter at a glance
- Relief — close contours steep, far gentle; hill (closed, highest centre), conical hill, ridge, valley (V upstream), spur (V downhill), plateau, saddle, escarpment; broken ground, sand dunes; general slope = where heights fall and streams flow.
- Drainage — perennial (blue) vs seasonal (dry, black); dendritic, radial, trellis, parallel; disappearing streams; left/right bank facing downstream.
- Water — wells (main source; lined dot, unlined circle; brackish in places), tanks (perennial / dry), reservoirs behind embankments (artificial).
- Settlements — nucleated, scattered, linear; permanent vs temporary huts; most important = most services; fair = social meeting and trade.
- Land use — yellow cultivated; green forest (Reserved — no use), dense mixed jungle, open scrub; white barren; fire lines stop fires.
- Transport — metalled road, unmetalled road, cart-track (commonest), pack-track, foot-path, railway (RS); causeways cross seasonal streams; power lines = electricity.
- Interpretation — agriculture; seasonal / scanty rainfall (dry streams, dry tanks, wells, dunes, scrub, broken ground); natural vs man-made.

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct idea |
|---|---|
| "The area is dry" with no evidence | Name seasonal streams, dry tanks, wells, dunes. |
| Valley V's pointing downhill | They point upstream. |
| Calling a well or tank a natural feature | Wells and tanks are man-made. |
| Writing "village" for a settlement pattern | Use nucleated, scattered or linear. |
| Facing upstream to decide banks | Always face downstream. |
| Explaining causeways by "heavy traffic" | Seasonal streams cross the roads. |
| Fire lines "to mark the forest boundary" | They stop the spread of forest fires. |
