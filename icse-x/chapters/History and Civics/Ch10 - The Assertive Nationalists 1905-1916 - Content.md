## 1. Complete Lesson

### Why Assertive Nationalism arose

By 1905 a younger group of leaders had lost patience with the methods of the Early Nationalists (Chapter 9). The main reasons were:
1. **Failure of the Moderates' methods** — twenty years of prayers and petitions had brought very few reforms; the Indian Councils Act of 1892 was disappointing.
2. **Economic distress** — terrible **famines** (1896–1900) and **plague** (1896–97) killed lakhs of people; the government's relief was poor and its plague measures in Poona were harsh. People saw the drain of wealth at work.
3. **Lord Curzon's repressive policies** (Viceroy 1899–1905):
   - the **Calcutta Corporation Act** (1899) reduced the number of elected Indian members;
   - the **Indian Universities Act** (1904) placed the universities under **official control**;
   - the **Official Secrets Act** (1904) restricted the freedom of the press;
   - a lavish **Delhi Durbar** (1903) was held while famine continued;
   - and finally the **Partition of Bengal** (1905).
4. **International events** — the defeat of Italy by **Abyssinia** (Ethiopia) in 1896 and of Russia by **Japan** in **1905** showed that European powers were not invincible and that an Asian nation could defeat them.
5. **Growth of self-confidence and pride** — the teachings of Vivekananda, Dayananda and Bankim Chandra made Indians proud of their culture and ready for sacrifice.

### The Partition of Bengal (1905)

**What happened**
- The Bengal Presidency (Bengal, Bihar and Orissa) had a population of about **78 million**.
- **Lord Curzon** divided it into two provinces:
  - **Bengal** (western Bengal with Bihar and Orissa) — with a Hindu majority;
  - **Eastern Bengal and Assam**, with its capital at **Dacca** — with a Muslim majority.
- The Partition was announced in **July 1905** and came into effect on **16 October 1905**.

**The official (British) reason**
- Bengal was **too large** to be administered efficiently by one Lieutenant-Governor; the partition would give **better administration** and develop the neglected eastern districts. Curzon called it "a mere adjustment of boundaries for better administration".

**The Nationalists' perspective — the real motive**
1. **Divide and rule** — to **divide the Hindus and the Muslims** by creating a Muslim-majority province.
2. To **stop the rising tide of nationalism** — **Bengal was the nerve centre of Indian nationalism**; dividing it would weaken the national movement.
3. To **reduce the Bengalis to a minority** — in the new Bengal they would be outnumbered by Hindi- and Oriya-speakers, and in the eastern province by Assamese and Muslims.
4. To **demonstrate the strength of the British Raj** and its contempt for Indian opinion.

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2024, Q7(i))* Who partitioned Bengal? Mention any two points to explain the perspective of the Nationalists regarding the actual motive behind the decision of Partition.
**Solution:** **Lord Curzon.** Any two: to **divide Hindus and Muslims**; to **stop the rising tide of nationalism**, because Bengal was the nerve centre of Indian nationalism; to **demonstrate the strength of the British Raj**.

**16 October 1905 — a day of mourning**
- The day the Partition came into effect was observed as a **day of national mourning** throughout Bengal.
- People **fasted**; no food was cooked; there was a **hartal** (shops closed) in Calcutta.
- People bathed in the Ganga and walked the streets singing ***Vande Mataram*** and Rabindranath Tagore's ***Amar Sonar Bangla***.
- Hindus and Muslims tied **rakhis** on one another's wrists as a symbol of **unity** (Raksha Bandhan).

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2020, Q2(c))* Why is October 16, 1905 regarded as an important day in the history of the Indian National Movement?
**Solution:** On this day the **Partition of Bengal** came into effect. It led to communal disharmony, and the Congress felt it was a scheme of the **divide and rule** policy; the day was observed as a day of mourning and the **Swadeshi and Boycott Movement** gained strength.

### The Swadeshi and Boycott Movement (1905–1911)

The protest against the Partition became the **Swadeshi Movement** — the first mass movement of the national struggle.
- **Boycott** — a resolution to boycott British goods was passed at the **Calcutta Town Hall on 7 August 1905**. People **burnt foreign cloth**, **picketed** shops selling British goods and refused to buy British salt and sugar.
- **Swadeshi** ("of one's own country") — Indians were urged to use **Indian-made goods**. Swadeshi **textile mills**, **match and soap factories**, **swadeshi stores** and banks were set up; Acharya **P. C. Ray's Bengal Chemicals** grew; **V. O. Chidambaram Pillai** started the **Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company** (1906) at Tuticorin.
- **National education** — the **Bengal National College** (1906, with **Aurobindo Ghosh** as principal) and the **National Council of Education** (1906) were set up as alternatives to government-controlled institutions.
- **Participation** — **students**, **women** and, for the first time, many ordinary people joined; the movement spread to **Maharashtra** (Tilak), **Punjab** (Lajpat Rai, Ajit Singh), **Madras** (Chidambaram Pillai) and **Delhi**.
- **The 1906 Calcutta session** under **Dadabhai Naoroji** declared **Swaraj** the goal and passed resolutions on **Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott and National Education**.

**Result:** the British **annulled the Partition** in **1911** (announced at King George V's Delhi Durbar); at the same time the capital was shifted from **Calcutta to Delhi**.

### Objectives of the Assertive Nationalists

1. **Swaraj** — complete **self-rule / Purna Swaraj** (complete independence), not merely reforms within the Empire. Tilak declared: "**Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it.**"
2. To **end British economic exploitation** and develop Indian industry.
3. To **restore India's self-respect** and pride in its culture.
4. To make the national movement a **mass movement**.

### Beliefs of the Assertive Nationalists

1. They had **no faith in the British sense of justice**; British rule was **not a blessing** but a system of exploitation.
2. **Freedom must be won, not begged** — they rejected "political mendicancy".
3. **Self-reliance and sacrifice** — Indians must depend on their own strength and be ready to suffer for freedom.
4. **Faith in the masses** — the common people must be brought into the movement.
5. **Pride in India's past** and culture.

### Methods of struggle of the Assertive Nationalists

1. **Swadeshi** — use of Indian-made goods; promotion of Indian industries.
2. **Boycott** — of British **goods**, and also of government **schools, colleges, courts, services, titles and councils**.
3. **National Education** — setting up national schools and colleges that taught patriotism and Indian values.
4. **Passive Resistance** — peaceful refusal to cooperate with or obey unjust laws of the government.
5. **Revivalism** — using **festivals** (Ganapati, Shivaji) and religious and cultural traditions to rouse national feeling.
6. **Personal sacrifices** — readiness to go to jail and suffer.
7. **Mass movement** — involving students, women, workers and peasants.
8. **Aggressive or assertive methods** — strong speeches and writing; **akharas** and **lathi clubs** to train the youth.

**Example 3.** *(ICSE 2019, Q6(c))* Explain briefly any three differences in the methods adopted between the Early Nationalists and Radicals, in the National Movement.
**Solution:**

| Early Nationalists | Radicals (Assertive Nationalists) |
|---|---|
| **Constitutional agitation** within the legal framework; slow, orderly political progress | **Aggressive / assertive** methods; **mass movement** |
| **Prayers, petitions and protests**; memorandums; meetings and resolutions | **Swadeshi** and **Boycott** |
| Used the **press** to criticise policies; **deputations** to Britain; British Committee of the INC and the journal *India* | **National Education**; **Passive Resistance**; **Revivalism**; **personal sacrifices** |

### Differences between the Early and the Assertive Nationalists

| Point | Early Nationalists (Moderates) | Assertive Nationalists (Radicals) |
|---|---|---|
| Period | 1885–1905 | 1905–1916 |
| Objective | Reforms and gradual **self-government within the British Empire** | **Swaraj** — complete self-rule / independence |
| Faith in the British | Believed in **British justice**; rule was a blessing in some ways | **No faith**; British rule was exploitation |
| Methods | **Constitutional** — prayers, petitions, protests | **Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education, Passive Resistance** |
| Social base | **Educated middle class** | **Masses** — students, women, workers, lower middle class |
| Inspiration | Western liberal thought | India's **culture and history** |
| Leaders | Naoroji, Banerjee, Gokhale, Mehta | Tilak, Lajpat Rai, Pal, Aurobindo |

### The Surat Split (1907)

At the **Surat session** of the Congress in **December 1907**, the Moderates and the Assertives quarrelled openly; the session ended in **chaos** and the Congress **split**.

**Reasons for the split**
1. **Difference in objectives** — the Moderates wanted **self-government within the Empire**, gradually; the Assertives wanted **Swaraj** at once.
2. **Difference in methods** — the Moderates wanted to use only constitutional methods and to **confine the boycott** to Bengal and to British goods; the Assertives wanted to **extend the Swadeshi and Boycott movement to the whole country** and to the boycott of government schools, courts, services and councils (**passive resistance**).
3. **The 1906 Calcutta resolutions** — the Assertives wanted the resolutions on **Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott and National Education** to be reaffirmed; they feared the Moderates would **dilute or drop** them at Surat.
4. **The choice of President** — the Assertives wanted **Lala Lajpat Rai** (or Tilak) as President; the Moderates elected **Rash Behari Ghosh**. The venue had also been moved from **Nagpur**, a stronghold of the Assertives, to **Surat**.

**Result of the split**
- The Congress **split into two groups**; the **Assertive Nationalists were removed** (expelled) from the Congress, which came under **Moderate control**.
- The government took advantage — it **repressed the Assertives** (Tilak was sentenced to **six years in Mandalay** in 1908; Aurobindo withdrew to Pondicherry; Lajpat Rai had already been deported to Mandalay in 1907) and tried to **win over the Moderates** with the **Morley–Minto Reforms** (1909), which also introduced **separate electorates** for Muslims.
- The national movement was **weakened** until the two groups **reunited at Lucknow in 1916** (Chapter 11).

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2024, Q2(iv))* In which year did the Surat Split happen? What was the result of this split?
**Solution:** **1907.** The **Congress party split** — the **Assertive Nationalists were removed** from the party.

### The leaders and their contributions

#### Bal Gangadhar Tilak (1856–1920) — "Lokmanya"

1. Gave the slogan "**Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it.**"
2. Started two newspapers — ***Kesari*** (Marathi) and ***Mahratta*** (English) — to spread nationalism.
3. **Revived the Ganapati (1893) and Shivaji (1895) festivals** to bring people together and rouse national feeling.
4. Organised **akharas** and **lathi clubs** to make the youth strong and fearless.
5. Led a **no-rent campaign** during the Maharashtra famine (1896–97) and stayed in close contact with the **masses**.
6. **Opposed the Partition of Bengal**; advocated **Swadeshi, Boycott and National Education**.
7. Launched the **Home Rule League** (**April 1916**, Belgaum).
8. Wrote ***Gita Rahasya*** (in Mandalay jail), ***The Orion*** and ***The Arctic Home in the Vedas***.
9. Regarded as the "**forerunner of Gandhi**" — he used Swadeshi and Boycott and believed in **mass movements** before Gandhi; the British called him "the Father of Indian Unrest".

#### Bipin Chandra Pal (1858–1932) — "the Father of Revolutionary Thought in India"

1. A powerful **journalist** — he worked on *The Tribune*, started the English weekly ***New India*** and the daily ***Bande Mataram*** (later edited by Aurobindo Ghosh) to propagate nationalism.
2. Wrote ***The New Economic Menace to India*** and ***The Spirit of Indian Nationalism***.
3. Advocated **Swadeshi, Boycott** and **National Education**; toured Madras (1907) spreading the Swadeshi message.
4. Played an active role in the **anti-Partition movement**.
5. A **social reformer** — opposed the caste system, supported **widow remarriage** and women's education.

#### Lala Lajpat Rai (1865–1928) — "Punjab Kesari" (the Lion of Punjab)

1. A **writer and journalist** — founded the ***Punjabee***, the Urdu daily ***Vande Mataram***, the English weekly ***The People***, and in America the monthly ***Young India***; he inspired the youth through his writing.
2. Helped found the **D.A.V. College, Lahore**, and opened **orphanages, hospitals and schools**.
3. Led the **anti-Partition** and Swadeshi movement in Punjab; was **deported to Mandalay** (1907) for leading the peasants' agitation with Ajit Singh.
4. In the USA he founded the **Indian Home Rule League of America** (1917) to win support for India's freedom.
5. **President** of the Congress special session at **Calcutta (1920)** and the **first President of the All India Trade Union Congress** (1920).
6. Founded the **Servants of the People Society** (1921) for the welfare of the downtrodden.
7. Led the protest against the **Simon Commission** at Lahore (1928); he died of injuries from a **lathi charge**.

#### Aurobindo Ghosh (1872–1950)
Principal of the **Bengal National College**, editor of ***Bande Mataram***, a leading theorist of **passive resistance**; after the Alipore Bomb Case (1908) he withdrew to **Pondicherry** (1910) and became a spiritual teacher.

### The Home Rule Movement (1916)

- With the Congress divided and the leaders in jail or abroad, two **Home Rule Leagues** were set up in **1916**: by **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** (April 1916, Belgaum, for Maharashtra and the Central Provinces) and by **Annie Besant** (September 1916, Madras, for the rest of India).
- Aim: **Home Rule** — self-government for India within the British Empire, like Ireland's demand.
- It revived the national movement, spread political awareness to new areas and prepared the way for Gandhi's mass movements.

### Summary of the chapter

- **Rise:** failure of Moderate methods; famines and plague; **Curzon** (Calcutta Corporation Act, Universities Act, Official Secrets Act, Delhi Durbar 1903, Partition); Japan's victory 1905; pride in culture.
- **Partition of Bengal** — Curzon; effective **16 October 1905**; Eastern Bengal and Assam (capital Dacca); official reason — administration; nationalist view — **divide Hindus and Muslims**, **weaken Bengal** (nerve centre), reduce Bengalis to a minority, show British strength; annulled **1911**.
- **Swadeshi and Boycott** — Calcutta Town Hall, 7 Aug 1905; swadeshi industries; national education; mass participation; 1906 resolutions.
- **Objective:** **Swaraj / Purna Swaraj**. **Methods:** Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education, Passive Resistance, Revivalism, sacrifice, mass movement.
- **Surat Split, 1907:** objectives, methods, 1906 resolutions, President (Lajpat Rai vs Rash Behari Ghosh); Assertives removed; reunion 1916.
- **Leaders:** Tilak (Kesari, Mahratta, festivals, akharas, Home Rule League 1916, Gita Rahasya); Pal (New India, Bande Mataram, books, national education); Lajpat Rai (Punjabee, D.A.V. College, Home Rule League of America, AITUC, Simon Commission).

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — From petitions to protest
The Assertives did not appear suddenly: famines, plague, Curzon's arrogance and Japan's victory all pushed young Indians to demand more.

### Section 2 — The Partition
Always give both sides — the British said "administration", the Nationalists saw "divide and rule". The 2024 board answer needs Curzon plus two motives.

### Section 3 — Methods
Swadeshi (build) + Boycott (refuse) + National Education (teach) + Passive Resistance (disobey peacefully). Application MCQs describe a modern situation; if someone buys local goods and refuses foreign ones, it is "Swadeshi and Boycott".

Learn four reasons for the split and its result. For each of Lal–Bal–Pal, learn a title, two newspapers or books, and one organisation.

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 Bal Gangadhar Tilak (1856–1920)
A teacher and journalist from Maharashtra, called "Lokmanya" (accepted by the people). He gave the slogan of Swaraj as a birthright, used festivals to unite the people, and was jailed several times.

### 6.2 Bipin Chandra Pal (1858–1932)
A Bengali orator and journalist, "the Father of Revolutionary Thought in India", who spread the Swadeshi message across India and wrote on the economic exploitation of India.

### 6.3 Lala Lajpat Rai (1865–1928)
The "Lion of Punjab", a writer, educationist and leader who worked for the poor, the workers and the cause of freedom, and who died after the lathi charge on the Simon Commission protest.

### 6.4 Lord Curzon (1859–1925)
Viceroy of India 1899–1905, an able but arrogant administrator whose Partition of Bengal provoked the Swadeshi Movement.

### 6.5 Annie Besant (1847–1933)
An Irish-born Theosophist leader who founded the Home Rule League (1916) and became the first woman President of the Congress (1917).

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Self-reliance** — Swadeshi showed that building one's own economy is a form of freedom.
- **Mass politics** — the movement moved from council halls to streets, schools and homes.
- **Unity in protest** — Hindus and Muslims tied rakhis on 16 October 1905 to defeat "divide and rule".

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **"Vocal for local" and "Make in India"** echo the Swadeshi idea of supporting Indian-made goods.
- **Consumer boycotts** are still used worldwide as a peaceful form of protest.

## 10. Short-Answer Questions (2 and 3 marks)

### Part I questions (2 marks each)

**Q1.** Who partitioned Bengal, and in which year?
**Answer:** Lord Curzon.
In 1905 (effective 16 October 1905).

**Q2.** Why is 16 October 1905 important?
**Answer:** The Partition of Bengal came into effect.
It was observed as a day of mourning and strengthened the Swadeshi Movement.

**Q3.** What was the objective of the Assertive Nationalists?
**Answer:** Swaraj — Purna Swaraj (complete independence).
Not mere reforms within the Empire.

**Q4.** Mention two methods of the Assertive Nationalists.
**Answer:** Swadeshi.
Boycott (also: national education; passive resistance).

**Q5.** In which year did the Surat Split take place? What was its result?
**Answer:** 1907.
The Congress split and the Assertive Nationalists were removed.

**Q6.** Give two contributions of Bal Gangadhar Tilak.
**Answer:** Started *Kesari* and *Mahratta*.
Revived the Ganapati and Shivaji festivals (also: Home Rule League; "Swaraj is my birthright").

**Q7.** Give two contributions of Bipin Chandra Pal.
**Answer:** Journalist — *New India*, *Bande Mataram*.
Advocated national education and Swadeshi; wrote *The New Economic Menace to India*.

**Q8.** Give two contributions of Lala Lajpat Rai.
**Answer:** Founded the *Punjabee* and helped found D.A.V. College, Lahore.
First President of the AITUC; led the Simon Commission protest.

**Q9.** What was the official reason given for the Partition of Bengal?
**Answer:** Bengal was too large to be administered efficiently.
Partition would improve administration and develop the east.

**Q10.** Name two leaders of the Home Rule Movement.
**Answer:** Bal Gangadhar Tilak.
Annie Besant.

**Q11.** What is meant by Swadeshi and Boycott?
**Answer:** Swadeshi — using Indian-made goods.
Boycott — refusing British goods and institutions.

**Q12.** Mention two repressive measures of Lord Curzon.
**Answer:** The Indian Universities Act (1904).
The Partition of Bengal (1905) (also: Calcutta Corporation Act; Official Secrets Act).

### Part II parts (3 marks each)

**Q13.** Who partitioned Bengal? Give two points of the Nationalists' perspective on its real motive.
**Answer:** Lord Curzon.
To divide Hindus and Muslims.
To weaken Bengal, the nerve centre of nationalism.

**Q14.** Explain three reasons for the Surat Split.
**Answer:** Different objectives — self-government vs Swaraj.
Different methods — extend Swadeshi and Boycott or not.
Dispute over the 1906 resolutions and the choice of President.

**Q15.** What was the objective of the Assertive Nationalists? Give two contributions of Tilak.
**Answer:** Purna Swaraj.
*Kesari* and *Mahratta*.
Ganapati and Shivaji festivals.

**Q16.** Explain three methods of the Assertive Nationalists.
**Answer:** Swadeshi — Indian goods and industries.
Boycott — British goods, schools, courts.
National Education and Passive Resistance.

**Q17.** Give three beliefs of the Assertive Nationalists.
**Answer:** No faith in British justice.
Freedom must be won, not begged.
Faith in the masses; self-reliance and sacrifice.

**Q18.** Give three contributions of Lala Lajpat Rai.
**Answer:** Writer — *Punjabee*, *The People*, *Young India*.
D.A.V. College, Lahore; Servants of the People Society.
AITUC President; Simon Commission protest.

**Q19.** Give three contributions of Bipin Chandra Pal.
**Answer:** Journalist — *New India* and *Bande Mataram*.
Books on Indian nationalism and economic exploitation.
Advocated Swadeshi, Boycott and national education; social reform.

**Q20.** Describe how 16 October 1905 was observed.
**Answer:** A day of mourning — fasting and hartal.
*Vande Mataram* and *Amar Sonar Bangla* were sung.
Hindus and Muslims tied rakhis to show unity.

**Q21.** State three results of the Surat Split.
**Answer:** The Assertives were expelled; the Moderates controlled the Congress.
The government repressed the Assertives (Tilak to Mandalay).
The movement was weakened until the 1916 reunion.

**Q22.** State three effects of the Swadeshi Movement.
**Answer:** Growth of Indian industries.
National education institutions.
Mass participation; annulment of the Partition in 1911.

## 11. Long-Answer Questions (4 marks each)

### Part II parts (4 marks each)

**Q1.** State four methods advocated by the Assertive Nationalists.
**Answer:** Swadeshi.
Boycott.
National Education.
Passive Resistance (also: revivalism; personal sacrifice; mass movement).

**Q2.** Explain three differences in the methods of the Early Nationalists and the Radicals.
**Answer:** Constitutional agitation vs aggressive, mass methods.
Prayers, petitions and protests vs Swadeshi and Boycott.
Press and deputations to Britain vs National Education and Passive Resistance.
Faith in British justice vs self-reliance and sacrifice.

**Q3.** Explain four causes of the rise of Assertive Nationalism.
**Answer:** Failure of the Moderates' methods.
Famines and plague; economic distress.
Curzon's repressive policies, especially the Partition.
Japan's victory over Russia (1905); pride in Indian culture.

**Q4.** Describe the Partition of Bengal — its official reason and the Nationalists' view.
**Answer:** Curzon divided Bengal in 1905 into Bengal and Eastern Bengal and Assam.
Official reason — better administration of a large province.
Nationalist view — divide Hindus and Muslims.
Weaken Bengal, the nerve centre of nationalism; reduce Bengalis to a minority.

**Q5.** Explain the Surat Split of 1907.
**Answer:** Differences in objectives and methods.
Assertives wanted to reaffirm the 1906 resolutions.
Dispute over the President — Rash Behari Ghosh vs Lajpat Rai.
Session broke up; Assertives expelled.

**Q6.** Give four contributions of Bal Gangadhar Tilak.
**Answer:** "Swaraj is my birthright"; demand for Swaraj.
*Kesari* and *Mahratta*.
Ganapati and Shivaji festivals; akharas and lathi clubs.
Home Rule League (1916); *Gita Rahasya*.

**Q7.** Describe the Swadeshi and Boycott Movement.
**Answer:** Boycott resolution, 7 August 1905; burning of foreign cloth.
Swadeshi industries and stores.
National education — Bengal National College.
Spread across India; Partition annulled in 1911.

**Q8.** Give two contributions each of Lala Lajpat Rai and Bipin Chandra Pal.
**Answer:** Lajpat Rai — *Punjabee*, *Young India*; D.A.V. College.
Lajpat Rai — AITUC; Simon Commission protest.
Pal — *New India*, *Bande Mataram*.
Pal — *The New Economic Menace to India*; national education.

## 12. Competency / Application Questions

**Q1. (The local market)** Your town's shops sell only imported toys. How would an Assertive Nationalist respond?
**Model answer:** Urge people to buy toys made by local craftspeople (Swadeshi), refuse imported toys (Boycott) and help set up a local workshop.

**Q2. (Two newspapers, 1905)** Write one headline each that a British-owned paper and *Kesari* might have printed on 16 October 1905.
**Model answer:** British paper: "Bengal reorganised for better administration". *Kesari*: "Bengal cut in two — the nation mourns and resolves on Swadeshi".

**Q3. (Choose the method)** Classify: (a) a petition to the Viceroy; (b) burning foreign cloth; (c) a national school; (d) a deputation to London.
**Model answer:** (a) and (d) — Early Nationalist; (b) and (c) — Assertive Nationalist.

**Q4. (A school festival)** Why did Tilak turn the Ganapati festival into a public celebration?
**Model answer:** To bring people of all classes together, spread nationalist ideas through songs and speeches, and get around bans on political meetings.

**Q5. (Divide and rule)** How did Hindus and Muslims answer "divide and rule" on 16 October 1905?
**Model answer:** By tying rakhis on each other, mourning together and joining the Swadeshi Movement — showing their unity.

## 14. Case-Based Questions

*Read each situation and answer the four questions, 1 mark each (4 × 1 = 4).*

### Case 1 — A province divided

> In 1905 a Viceroy divided a large province into two, creating a new province with Dacca as its capital. He said it was for better administration; the people saw it differently and observed the day it took effect as a day of mourning.

**(i)** Name the Viceroy.
**Answer:** Lord Curzon.

**(ii)** Name the new province.
**Answer:** Eastern Bengal and Assam.

**(iii)** On which date did the partition take effect?
**Answer:** 16 October 1905.

**(iv)** Give one reason the people saw it "differently".
**Answer:** To divide Hindus and Muslims (also: to weaken Bengal, the nerve centre of nationalism).

### Case 2 — Surat, December 1907

> Two groups in a political organisation could not agree on their goal, on how far to carry a boycott, and on who should preside. The session broke up in confusion.

**(i)** Name the organisation.
**Answer:** The Indian National Congress.

**(ii)** Name the two groups.
**Answer:** The Moderates (Early Nationalists) and the Assertive Nationalists.

**(iii)** Who was chosen as President by the Moderates?
**Answer:** Rash Behari Ghosh.

**(iv)** What was the result?
**Answer:** The Congress split; the Assertives were removed.

### Case 3 — Lal, Bal and Pal

> Leader P edited *Kesari*. Leader Q was called the Lion of Punjab. Leader R wrote *The New Economic Menace to India*.

**(i)** Name P.
**Answer:** Bal Gangadhar Tilak.

**(ii)** Name Q.
**Answer:** Lala Lajpat Rai.

**(iii)** Name R.
**Answer:** Bipin Chandra Pal.

**(iv)** Which section of the Congress did all three represent?
**Answer:** The Assertive Nationalists.
