## 1. Complete Lesson

### Background: a world divided into two blocs

- After the Second World War the world was divided into two rival **power blocs** — the **Western (capitalist) bloc** led by the **USA** and the **Eastern (communist) bloc** led by the **USSR** — locked in the **Cold War** (Chapter 17).
- Each bloc built **military alliances**: **NATO** (1949), **SEATO** (1954) and the **Baghdad Pact / CENTO** (1955) on the Western side; the **Warsaw Pact** (1955) on the Eastern side.
- At the same time, dozens of countries in **Asia and Africa** were winning **independence** from colonial rule — India (1947), Indonesia (1949), Egypt (1952), Ghana (1957) and many more. Both superpowers tried to draw them into their camps.

### The meaning of non-alignment

- **Non-alignment** is the policy of a **sovereign state** according to which it **does not align itself with any of the power blocs**, but at the same time **actively participates in world affairs** and **decides each issue on its own merits**. It **chooses its own policy** and way of life.
- It is **not neutrality** or isolation: a non-aligned country speaks and acts on world problems — for peace, against colonialism and racism — but does not join military alliances or take sides automatically.
- The **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM)** is the group of countries that follow this policy and work together for their common aims.

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2025, Q2(vi))* What is meant by the term Non-Alignment?
**Solution:** Non-alignment is a **policy of a sovereign state** according to which it **will not align itself to any of the power blocs**, but at the same time **actively participates in world affairs** and **decides each issue on its own merits**; it **chooses its own policy** or way of life.

### Factors that led to the formation of NAM

1. **The Cold War and military blocs** — newly independent countries did not want to be **drawn into the rivalry** of the superpowers or into their **military alliances**, which could drag them into wars that were not theirs.
2. **Desire to protect hard-won independence** — after long struggles against colonial rule, they feared that joining a bloc would mean a **new form of dependence (neo-colonialism)**; they wanted a **free domestic and foreign policy**.
3. **Common experience of colonialism and racism** — they shared a determination to **end imperialism and colonialism** and to fight **racial discrimination** (such as **apartheid** in South Africa).
4. **Need for economic development** — poor countries needed **aid, trade and technology** from **both** blocs; remaining non-aligned allowed them to accept help from all sides without strings.
5. **Fear of nuclear war** — the **arms race** and nuclear weapons threatened all mankind; the new nations wanted **peace and disarmament**.
6. **Afro-Asian solidarity** — the **Asian Relations Conference** (New Delhi, 1947) and the **Bandung Conference** (Indonesia, **April 1955**) of 29 Asian and African countries brought them together; the principles of **Panchsheel** (1954) gave them a common basis.
7. **Leadership** — far-sighted leaders such as **Nehru, Tito, Nasser and Sukarno** gave the idea shape.

### The founding of NAM

- **Nehru, Nasser and Tito** met at **Brioni** (Yugoslavia) in **1956** and agreed on a common approach.
- The **first summit of the Non-Aligned Movement** was held at **Belgrade** (Yugoslavia) in **September 1961**, attended by **25 countries**.
- The movement grew steadily; today it has about **120 member countries** — the largest grouping of states after the UN. Summits are held regularly; the **7th summit** was held at **New Delhi** in **1983**.

### Objectives of the Non-Aligned Movement

1. To **preserve the freedom of the newly independent countries** of Asia and Africa from **colonial influence**, and to help colonies win independence — to **abolish imperialism and colonialism**.
2. To **condemn racial discrimination** (including **apartheid**).
3. To **keep away from military blocs** and to save the new nations from the **supremacy of the superpowers**; to pursue a **free domestic and foreign policy**.
4. To advocate the **sovereign equality of all states**.
5. To advocate the **peaceful settlement of international disputes** and encourage **friendly relations** among countries; **peaceful coexistence** based on **Panchsheel**.
6. To work for **disarmament** — to **eliminate nuclear weapons** and all causes that could lead to war.
7. To **strengthen the United Nations**.
8. To build a **New International Economic Order** based on **equity, equality and justice**, and promote **cooperation among developing countries**.
9. To **protect human rights and the environment**, and to maintain **international peace, harmony and cooperation**.

### Panchsheel

**Meaning**
- **Panchsheel** (*panch* = five, *sheel* = virtues / principles) means the **five principles of peaceful coexistence** between nations.
- They were first written into the **agreement between India and China** on **29 April 1954**, signed during the time of **Jawaharlal Nehru** and the Chinese Premier **Zhou Enlai**. They were adopted in a wider form at the **Bandung Conference** (1955) and became a **foundation of the Non-Aligned Movement**.

**The five principles** *(for understanding)*
1. **Mutual respect** for each other's **territorial integrity and sovereignty**.
2. **Mutual non-aggression**.
3. **Mutual non-interference** in each other's **internal affairs**.
4. **Equality and mutual benefit**.
5. **Peaceful coexistence**.

**The principles explained** *(for understanding)*

| Principle | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| Respect for territorial integrity and sovereignty | No country should claim or occupy another's territory or question its right to govern itself. |
| Mutual non-aggression | Countries promise not to attack each other. |
| Non-interference in internal affairs | No country should meddle in another's politics, elections or government. |
| Equality and mutual benefit | Relations — trade, aid, treaties — should be fair and help both sides, big or small. |
| Peaceful coexistence | Countries with different political and economic systems can live side by side in peace. |

> Panchsheel did not prevent the India–China war of 1962, but its principles remain part of international law and of the UN Charter's spirit.

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2024, Q1(xv))* Identify the principle of Panchsheel. (a) Mutual non-aggression (b) Regulate armaments (c) Take action against aggressor (d) Recommend admission of members
**Solution:** **(a) Mutual non-aggression.** The other three are functions of the UN Security Council.

### The role of Jawaharlal Nehru

1. **Chief architect of non-alignment** — even before independence (1946) Nehru declared that India would **keep away from power blocs**; non-alignment became the foundation of **India's foreign policy** from 1947.
2. **Afro-Asian solidarity** — he organised the **Asian Relations Conference** at **New Delhi** (1947) and played a leading part at the **Bandung Conference** (1955) with Sukarno.
3. **Panchsheel** — he gave the world the **five principles of peaceful coexistence** (1954), which became the basis of NAM.
4. **Founding of NAM** — with **Tito and Nasser** at **Brioni** (1956) and at the **Belgrade summit** (1961), he shaped the movement.
5. **Peace-maker in the Cold War** — India helped to settle the **Korean War** (chairing the commission for the exchange of prisoners), supported the **Geneva settlement in Indo-China** (1954) and opposed the attack on Egypt in the **Suez crisis** (1956).
6. **Champion of disarmament** — he called for an end to **nuclear tests** (1954) and for general disarmament.
7. **Voice of the colonised** — he supported the **freedom struggles** of Asian and African peoples and opposed **racial discrimination and apartheid**.
8. **Faith in the UN** — he worked to **strengthen the United Nations** as the forum for peace.

> Nehru often explained that non-alignment did not mean sitting on the fence: India would judge every issue on its merits and would stand for **peace, freedom and justice**.

### Achievements and relevance of NAM

- It gave **newly independent countries a voice** and protected their **freedom of action** during the Cold War.
- It supported **decolonisation** and the struggle against **apartheid** in South Africa.
- It helped to **reduce Cold War tension** and promoted **disarmament** and peaceful settlement of disputes.
- It made the **developing world** a strong group in the **UN**, pressing for a fairer world economic order.
- After the Cold War, NAM continues to work for **South–South cooperation**, development, and a more democratic world order.

### Non-alignment and military alliances compared

| Point | Military alliances (NATO, Warsaw Pact) | Non-Aligned Movement |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Military pacts led by a superpower | A movement of independent states, not a military pact |
| Aim | Collective defence against the rival bloc | Peace, freedom from blocs, end of colonialism |
| Foreign policy | Members follow the bloc leader's line | Each member decides issues on their merits |
| Arms | Build up forces and weapons | Work for disarmament and an end to nuclear weapons |
| Members | Mostly developed countries of Europe and North America | Mostly developing countries of Asia, Africa and Latin America |

### India's non-alignment in practice

- India accepted **economic help from both blocs** — food and technical aid from the **USA**, and help from the **USSR** in building steel plants such as **Bhilai** — while joining **no military pact**.
- India **criticised wrongs on both sides** — for example, colonial wars by Western powers and Soviet intervention where it affected the freedom of nations — and voted at the UN on the merits of each issue.
- India sent **peacekeeping forces** under the UN (Korea, Congo, Gaza) — showing active participation in world affairs.

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — Not neutral, but independent
A neutral country stays out of disputes; a non-aligned country takes part but chooses its own position on each issue.

### Section 2 — Why NAM?
Think of what the new nations wanted: freedom (no new masters), development (help from all), peace (no nuclear war) and dignity (no racism).

### Section 3 — The founders
Four names, four countries — learn them as pairs: Nehru–India, Tito–Yugoslavia, Nasser–Egypt, Sukarno–Indonesia.

### Section 4 — Panchsheel
The 2027 syllabus asks only its meaning — "five principles of peaceful coexistence". Know the principles to understand MCQs like "Identify the principle of Panchsheel".

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 Jawaharlal Nehru (1889–1964)
India's first Prime Minister and the chief architect of non-alignment, who gave the world Panchsheel and helped found NAM.

### 6.2 Josip Broz Tito (1892–1980)
President of Yugoslavia, a communist country that refused to join the Soviet bloc; he hosted the first NAM summit at Belgrade (1961).

### 6.3 Gamal Abdel Nasser (1918–1970)
President of Egypt, who nationalised the Suez Canal (1956) and was a leading voice of Arab and African independence and of NAM.

### 6.4 Sukarno (1901–1970)
First President of Indonesia, who hosted the Bandung Conference (1955) and helped found NAM.

### 6.5 Kwame Nkrumah (1909–1972)
First President of Ghana, the first sub-Saharan African colony to win independence (1957), and a founder-member of NAM.

### 6.6 Zhou Enlai (1898–1976)
Premier of the People's Republic of China, who with Nehru set out the five principles of Panchsheel in 1954 and attended the Bandung Conference.

### 6.7 V. K. Krishna Menon (1896–1974)
Indian diplomat and close associate of Nehru, who represented India at the UN and is credited with popularising the term "non-alignment".

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Independence in foreign policy** — small and new nations can refuse to be pawns of great powers.
- **Solidarity of the weak** — together, developing nations gained a strong voice in world affairs.
- **Peaceful coexistence** — countries with different systems can live side by side.

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **India's foreign policy today** still values **strategic autonomy** — friendly relations with many countries without joining military alliances.
- **South–South cooperation** — developing countries share technology, trade and experience with one another.
- **India at the UN** — India continues to speak for developing countries on climate change, trade and the reform of the Security Council, in the spirit of NAM.
- **Peacekeeping** — India is among the largest contributors of UN peacekeepers, continuing Nehru's tradition of active work for peace.

### 8.3 More practice in the ICSE style

**Q1.** (2 marks) Distinguish between non-alignment and neutrality.
**Answer:** A neutral country stays out of conflicts altogether; a non-aligned country takes an active part in world affairs and judges each issue on its merits, while not joining any power bloc.

**Q2.** (3 marks) Explain the role of Jawaharlal Nehru in the Non-Aligned Movement.
**Answer:** He made non-alignment the basis of India's foreign policy; he gave the world Panchsheel (1954) and led at Bandung (1955); with Tito and Nasser he founded NAM at Belgrade (1961) and worked for peace, disarmament and decolonisation.

**Q3.** (2 marks) What was the Bandung Conference?
**Answer:** A conference of 29 Asian and African countries at Bandung, Indonesia, in April 1955, which promoted Afro-Asian solidarity, opposed colonialism and paved the way for NAM.

**Q4.** (4 marks) Mention four achievements of NAM.
**Answer:** It gave new nations a voice and freedom of action; it supported decolonisation and opposed apartheid; it helped reduce Cold War tension and promoted disarmament; it made developing countries a strong group in the UN, pressing for a fairer economic order.

**Q5.** (3 marks) What is the meaning of Panchsheel, and how did it influence NAM?
**Answer:** Panchsheel means the five principles of peaceful coexistence, first agreed between India and China in 1954; adopted at Bandung (1955), they became the foundation of NAM's commitment to peaceful coexistence, non-interference and sovereign equality.

**Q6.** (3 marks) Why did the newly independent countries of Asia and Africa find non-alignment attractive?
**Answer:** It protected their hard-won freedom from new forms of domination; it let them accept aid and trade from both blocs for development; and it kept them out of wars between the superpowers.

**Q7.** (2 marks) Name the leader of Indonesia and the leader of Egypt who helped found NAM.
**Answer:** Sukarno (Indonesia) and Gamal Abdel Nasser (Egypt).

**Q8.** (4 marks) "Non-alignment is not neutrality." Explain with two arguments and one example.
**Answer:** Neutral states stay out of conflicts, but non-aligned states take an active part in world affairs; they judge each issue on its merits rather than staying silent. Example: India joined no bloc but worked actively for peace in Korea and Indo-China and opposed colonialism at the UN.

**Q9.** (2 marks) What is the New International Economic Order?
**Answer:** A demand by NAM and other developing countries for a fairer world system of trade, finance and technology based on equity, equality and justice.

**Q10.** (3 marks) How did the Cold War military pacts push countries towards non-alignment?
**Answer:** NATO, SEATO, CENTO and the Warsaw Pact divided the world into armed camps; the superpowers pressed new nations to join; joining would have meant foreign bases, involvement in others' wars and loss of an independent foreign policy — so many new nations chose non-alignment.

**Q11.** (4 marks) Explain four ways in which NAM worked for world peace.
**Answer:** It kept new nations out of military blocs, reducing the area of conflict; it called for disarmament and an end to nuclear tests; it promoted the peaceful settlement of disputes and peaceful coexistence; it strengthened the UN as a forum for dialogue.

**Q12.** (2 marks) Why is Yugoslavia's membership of NAM interesting?
**Answer:** Yugoslavia was a communist country, yet under Tito it refused to join the Soviet bloc — showing that non-alignment was about independence from blocs, not about ideology.

**Q13.** (3 marks) Give three examples of Nehru's efforts for peace during the Cold War.
**Answer:** India chaired the commission for the exchange of prisoners after the Korean War; Nehru supported the Geneva settlement in Indo-China (1954); he opposed the attack on Egypt in the Suez crisis (1956) and called for an end to nuclear tests.

## 12. Competency / Application Questions

**Q1. (A new country)** A newly independent country is invited to join a military alliance by one superpower. What would a NAM member advise, and why?
**Model answer:** Not to join, because it could be drawn into the superpowers' wars, lose its freedom of action and lose aid from the other side; it should keep friendly relations with all.

**Q2. (Spot the policy)** Classify: (a) a country buys arms from both the USA and Russia and joins no pact; (b) a country joins NATO.
**Model answer:** (a) non-alignment; (b) alignment with the Western bloc.

**Q3. (Panchsheel in daily life)** How can the principle of "non-interference in internal affairs" apply between neighbouring housing societies?
**Model answer:** Each society manages its own rules without interfering in the other's affairs, while cooperating on common issues like roads and water.

**Q4. (A headline)** Write a headline for 1 September 1961.
**Model answer:** "Belgrade: 25 nations unite — neither East nor West, but for peace and freedom".

**Q5. (Relevance)** Give one reason why non-alignment still matters after the Cold War.
**Model answer:** Developing countries still need an independent voice and cooperation among themselves on trade, climate and development.

**Q6. (Debate)** "A small country is safer inside a military alliance than outside it." Give one argument for and one against.
**Model answer:** For — a strong ally may deter an attack. Against — the country may be dragged into wars that are not its own and lose its freedom to decide its policies.

**Q7. (Class project)** You are making a poster on NAM. List four items you would include.
**Model answer:** A world map marking Belgrade and Bandung; photographs of Nehru, Tito, Nasser and Sukarno; the five principles of Panchsheel; three objectives of NAM.
