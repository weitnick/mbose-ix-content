## 1. Complete Lesson

### An integrated judiciary

> - India has a **single, integrated system of courts**. The **Supreme Court** is at the top; below it are the **High Courts** of the States, and below them the **subordinate courts** of the districts.
> - The same courts decide cases under both Union and State laws, and the law declared by the Supreme Court is **binding on all courts** in India (Article 141).
> - The Supreme Court was inaugurated on **28 January 1950** and sits in **New Delhi**.

### Composition

- The Supreme Court consists of the **Chief Justice of India** and **not more than 33 other judges** — **34 in all** (Article 124, as raised by Parliament in 2019).
- **Parliament** can change the number of judges by law.
- **Ad hoc** judges (from the High Courts) and **retired** judges may be asked to sit when needed.

### Appointment and qualifications

- The **Chief Justice** and the other judges are **appointed by the President**.
- In appointing them, the President consults the Chief Justice and the senior judges of the Supreme Court (the **collegium** of the CJI and the four senior-most judges recommends names). By convention, the **senior-most judge** is appointed Chief Justice.

**Qualifications** — a person must be a **citizen of India** and:
1. have been a **judge of a High Court** for at least **five years**; or
2. have been an **advocate of a High Court** for at least **ten years**; or
3. be, in the opinion of the President, a **distinguished jurist**.

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2026 specimen, Q5(i))* Who appoints the judges of the Supreme Court? What is the composition of the Supreme Court?
**Solution:** The **President** appoints them. The Court consists of the **Chief Justice of India and 33 other judges**.

### Term, removal and salary

- A judge holds office until the age of **65 years**, but may **resign** earlier by writing to the President.
- A judge can be **removed** only by an **order of the President** after an **address** by **each House** of Parliament, supported by a **majority of its total membership** and **two-thirds** of the members present and voting, on the ground of **proved misbehaviour or incapacity** — a process like impeachment. (No judge has yet been removed.)
- The salaries and allowances of the judges are **charged on the Consolidated Fund of India** and **cannot be reduced** to their disadvantage during their term, except during a **Financial Emergency**.

### Independence of the judiciary

The judiciary must be **free from the control of the executive and the legislature**, so that judges can decide cases "without fear or favour". The Constitution ensures this in many ways:

1. **Appointment** — judges are appointed by the President **in consultation with the judiciary**, on grounds of merit, not political favour.
2. **Security of tenure** — judges serve until **65**; they can be removed **only** by the difficult special-majority process for **proved misbehaviour or incapacity**.
3. **Salaries** — charged on the **Consolidated Fund of India**, **not voted on** by Parliament, and **cannot be reduced** during their term (except in a Financial Emergency).
4. **Conduct not discussed** — the conduct of a judge in performing duties **cannot be discussed** in Parliament or a State legislature, except during a motion for removal.
5. **Power to punish for contempt** — the Court can punish anyone who shows **contempt of court** or disobeys its orders.
6. **No practice after retirement** — a retired Supreme Court judge **cannot practise** as a lawyer in any court in India, so judges do not seek favours for a later career.
7. **Control over its own establishment** — the Court appoints its staff and makes its own rules of procedure.
8. **Separation of the judiciary from the executive** — a Directive Principle the States have followed.

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2018, Q5(b))* State any three ways by which the Constitution ensures the independence of the judiciary.
**Solution:** **(1) Appointment** — neither political bias nor personal favouritism plays a part. **(2) Security of tenure** — a judge remains in office till 65 and can be removed only on the ground of proved misbehaviour or incapacity after a thorough inquiry. **(3) Salaries** are charged on the **Consolidated Fund of India** and cannot be varied to the judges' disadvantage during their term.

### Jurisdiction of the Supreme Court

**Jurisdiction** means the **authority of a court to hear and decide cases**.

#### 1. Original jurisdiction

Cases that come **directly** to the Supreme Court — heard **in the first instance** (Article 131):
- disputes between the **Government of India and one or more States**;
- disputes between the **Government of India and one or more States on one side, and one or more other States on the other**;
- disputes **between two or more States**.

The Court also hears in the first instance:
- petitions for the **enforcement of Fundamental Rights** (Article 32 — "writ jurisdiction");
- disputes about the **election of the President and the Vice-President**;
- cases it **transfers to itself** from High Courts when they involve the same important question of law.

> In Union–State and inter-State disputes, the original jurisdiction of the Supreme Court is **exclusive** — no other court can hear them.

**Example 3.** *(ICSE 2024, Q5(iii))* What is meant by the term Original Jurisdiction? Mention any three cases which come under this jurisdiction.
**Solution:** Original jurisdiction refers to the cases that can be **brought directly** to the Supreme Court — cases heard **in the first instance**. Cases (any three): **Centre–State** disputes; **inter-State** disputes; the Union and a State on one side and other States on the other; **protection of Fundamental Rights**; **transfer of cases** from lower courts; **interpretation** of the Constitution.

#### 2. Appellate jurisdiction

The Supreme Court is the **highest court of appeal**. It hears **appeals against the judgements of the High Courts** in three kinds of cases:

| Kind of case | When an appeal lies |
|---|---|
| **Constitutional cases** | When the High Court certifies that the case involves a **substantial question of law about the interpretation of the Constitution** |
| **Civil cases** | When the High Court certifies that the case involves a **substantial question of law of general importance** that needs to be decided by the Supreme Court |
| **Criminal cases** | When the High Court has **reversed an acquittal** and **sentenced** the accused **to death**; or has withdrawn a case from a lower court and sentenced the accused to death; or certifies that the case is fit for appeal |

> **Special leave to appeal** (Article 136): the Supreme Court may, in its discretion, allow an appeal against the judgement of **any court or tribunal** in India (except military tribunals), even without a certificate.

#### 3. Advisory jurisdiction

- Under **Article 143**, the **President** may ask the Supreme Court for its **opinion** on any **question of law or fact of public importance**.
- The Court **may or may not** give its opinion.
- The opinion is **only advisory** — it is **not binding** on the President, because it is not a judgement in a case.

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2027 specimen, Q2(i))* "In India, the Supreme Court can exercise advisory jurisdiction under Article 143 of the Constitution. With regard to the President, these opinions are purely advisory." Mention any two points about the Supreme Court that can be inferred.
**Solution:** The **President can ask** the Supreme Court for its opinion on any question of **law or public importance**; the Court's opinion is **merely advisory and not binding** on the President. *(Also: the Court may decline to give an opinion, since it is not an executable judgement.)*

#### 4. Revisory jurisdiction

- The Supreme Court can **review (revise) its own judgements** to correct an **error** that may have crept in (Article 137).
- Because its judgements are binding like law, it cannot allow a mistake to stand; a **review petition** asks it to reconsider a decision.

**Example 5.** *(ICSE 2023, Q1(iv))* When the Supreme Court reviews any judgement made by it to remove an error, it falls under __________ jurisdiction.
**Solution:** **Revisory** jurisdiction.

### The Supreme Court as a Court of Record

Under **Article 129**, the Supreme Court is a **Court of Record**. This means:
1. Its **judgements and orders are recorded** and preserved for **evidence and future reference**.
2. These records **cannot be questioned** when produced in any court; they serve as **precedents** that lower courts must follow.
3. It has the **power to punish for contempt of itself** — anyone who disobeys or insults the Court can be punished.

**Example 6.** *(ICSE 2026 specimen, Q5(ii))* Under Article 129, the Supreme Court of India is called a Court of Record. Justify this statement with any three points.
**Solution:** Its **judgements and orders are preserved as a record**; they can be **produced in any court as precedents** for future reference; if a person commits **contempt of court**, it has the authority to **punish** him or her. *(Also: it acts as the guardian of the Constitution.)*

### Judicial review

> - **Judicial review** is the power of the Supreme Court (and the High Courts) to **examine the laws** passed by Parliament and the State legislatures, and the **orders of the executive**, to see whether they agree with the Constitution.
> - If a law or order **violates the Constitution** or any of its provisions — for example, by taking away a Fundamental Right — the Court can declare it **ultra vires** (beyond the powers) and **null and void**.
> - Through judicial review the Supreme Court is the **final interpreter** and the **guardian of the Constitution**. It has held that even **constitutional amendments** cannot destroy the **"basic structure"** of the Constitution (*Kesavananda Bharati* case, 1973).

**Example 7.** *(ICSE 2025, Q1(iii))* Advisory Jurisdiction : Advise the President :: Judicial Review : __________
**Solution:** **Review the laws passed by the Parliament.**

### Guardian of Fundamental Rights — the writs

- **Article 32** gives every citizen the **right to move the Supreme Court** directly for the enforcement of **Fundamental Rights**. Dr B. R. Ambedkar called it the **"heart and soul"** of the Constitution.
- The Supreme Court (under Article 32) and the **High Courts** (under Article 226) enforce these rights by issuing **writs** — written orders commanding a person or authority to do or not to do something.

| Writ | Meaning of the name | When it is issued |
|---|---|---|
| **Habeas Corpus** | "To have the body" | Orders a person who has **detained** someone to **produce the detained person** before the court, so the court can decide whether the **detention is lawful**; protects personal liberty against **illegal arrest or detention** |
| **Mandamus** | "We command" | Commands a **public official, body or lower court** to **perform a public duty** that it has failed or refused to perform |
| **Prohibition** | "To forbid" | Issued by a higher court to a **lower court or tribunal** to **stop proceeding** with a case that is **beyond its jurisdiction** — **before** a decision |
| **Certiorari** | "To be certified" | Orders a lower court or tribunal to **send up the records** of a case, and **quashes** an order passed **without jurisdiction** or against natural justice — usually **after** a decision |
| **Quo Warranto** | "By what authority" | Asks a person **holding a public office** to show **by what right** he or she holds it; if not entitled, the person is removed |

> The Court has also widened access through **Public Interest Litigation (PIL)** — any public-spirited person may approach it on behalf of people who cannot, sometimes even by a simple letter.

**Example 8.** *(ICSE 2027 specimen, Q1(vi))* Ravi was taken into police custody without being told the reasons, his family was not told where he was held, and he was not produced before a magistrate within 24 hours. His parents asked the Supreme Court to have the police produce him. Which writ will the Court issue?
**Solution:** **Habeas Corpus.**

### Summary of the chapter

- **Integrated judiciary:** Supreme Court → High Courts → subordinate courts; SC law binds all courts.
- **Composition:** Chief Justice + 33 judges (34); appointed by the President in consultation with the judiciary.
- **Qualifications:** citizen + 5 years a High Court judge, or 10 years a High Court advocate, or a distinguished jurist.
- **Tenure:** till 65; removal by the President after a special-majority address by both Houses for proved misbehaviour or incapacity.
- **Independence:** appointment, tenure, salaries on the Consolidated Fund, conduct not discussed, contempt power, no practice after retirement, own establishment.
- **Jurisdiction:** original (Union–State, inter-State, Fundamental Rights), appellate (constitutional, civil, criminal; special leave), advisory (Art. 143, not binding), revisory (reviews own judgements).
- **Court of Record** (Art. 129), **judicial review**, **writs** (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto).

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — The Court and its judges
A small, highly qualified bench of 34 judges sits at the top of a single court system. The appointment process gives the judiciary itself a voice, protecting it from political pressure.

### Section 2 — Independence
Independence is protected at every stage: entry (appointment), stay (tenure and salary), conduct (no discussion, contempt power) and exit (no practice after retirement). Learn at least five points.

### Section 3 — Jurisdictions
The four jurisdictions are the most-asked topic. Original — first instance; appellate — appeals; advisory — the President's questions; revisory — its own judgements. Give an example for each.

### Section 4 — Guardian of the Constitution
Judicial review and the writs make the Court the protector of the Constitution and of citizens' rights. In case-based MCQs, match the situation to the writ.

## 5. Important Facts, Formulas and Results

### 5.1 Must-know facts

- Composition: **CJI + 33** other judges (34); appointed by the **President**.
- Qualifications: citizen + **5 years** HC judge / **10 years** HC advocate / distinguished jurist.
- Retirement age: **65** (High Courts: 62).
- Removal: address by both Houses (majority of total membership + 2/3 present and voting); **proved misbehaviour or incapacity**.
- Salaries: **Consolidated Fund of India**; cannot be reduced except in a **Financial Emergency**.
- Articles: **124** (establishment), **129** (Court of Record), **131** (original), **136** (special leave), **137** (review), **141** (binding law), **143** (advisory), **32** (writs).

### 5.2 Useful facts

- The first Chief Justice of India was **H. J. Kania** (1950–1951).
- The first woman judge of the Supreme Court was **Justice M. Fathima Beevi** (1989).
- In *Kesavananda Bharati* (1973) the Court laid down the basic structure doctrine.

### 5.3 Beyond the text (general knowledge, not needed for exam answers)

- The Supreme Court's emblem carries the motto "Yato Dharmastato Jayah" ("where there is righteousness, there is victory").
- Many hearings are now live-streamed and judgements are available online and in Indian languages.

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 H. J. Kania (1890–1951)
Harilal Jekisundas Kania was the **first Chief Justice of India**, presiding when the Supreme Court was inaugurated on 28 January 1950.

### 6.2 Justice M. Fathima Beevi (1927–2023)
From Kerala, she became the **first woman judge of the Supreme Court** of India in 1989 — and the first Muslim woman to reach the higher judiciary — and later served as Governor of Tamil Nadu.

### 6.3 Justice P. N. Bhagwati (1921–2017)
Chief Justice in 1985–86, he is remembered as a pioneer of **Public Interest Litigation**, allowing the Court to act on letters and petitions on behalf of the poor and the voiceless.

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Rule of law** — no person or government is above the Constitution, and an independent court keeps it so.
- **Checks and balances** — judicial review checks the legislature and executive; Parliament checks the judiciary through the removal process and by amending the law.
- **Rights need remedies** — Fundamental Rights are real only because citizens can go to court and get writs.

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **News headlines** such as "Supreme Court strikes down law as unconstitutional" are judicial review in action.
- **Habeas corpus petitions** are filed when people are detained illegally.
- **Inter-State river disputes** reach the Supreme Court under its original jurisdiction.

### 8.3 More practice in the ICSE style

**Q1.** (3 marks) Explain the procedure for removing a Supreme Court judge.
**Answer:** A motion is moved in either House; the address must be supported by a majority of the total membership and two-thirds of those present and voting in each House; the charge (proved misbehaviour or incapacity) is investigated, and the President orders the removal.

**Q2.** (4 marks) Distinguish between the writs of Prohibition and Certiorari.
**Answer:** Both are issued to lower courts or tribunals; Prohibition stops a lower court from proceeding with a case beyond its jurisdiction while the case is pending; Certiorari quashes an order already passed without jurisdiction, after calling for the records.

**Q3.** (2 marks) Why can a retired Supreme Court judge not practise as a lawyer in India?
**Answer:** So that judges are not tempted to favour anyone for the sake of a later career, which protects their independence.

**Q4.** (3 marks) Explain the appellate jurisdiction of the Supreme Court in criminal cases.
**Answer:** An appeal lies when the High Court has reversed an acquittal and sentenced the accused to death; when it has withdrawn a case from a lower court and sentenced the accused to death; or when it certifies that the case is fit for appeal.

## 10. Short-Answer Questions (2 and 3 marks)

### Part I questions (2 marks each)

**Q1.** What is the composition of the Supreme Court?
**Answer:** The Chief Justice of India.
And 33 other judges (34 in all).

**Q2.** State two qualifications for a Supreme Court judge.
**Answer:** A citizen of India.
Five years a High Court judge, or ten years a High Court advocate, or a distinguished jurist.

**Q3.** On what grounds and how can a Supreme Court judge be removed?
**Answer:** Proved misbehaviour or incapacity.
By the President after an address by each House passed by a special majority.

**Q4.** State two ways in which the independence of the judiciary is ensured.
**Answer:** Security of tenure till 65 with a difficult removal process.
Salaries charged on the Consolidated Fund and not reducible.

**Q5.** What is original jurisdiction? Give one example.
**Answer:** Power to hear a case in the first instance.
A dispute between two States.

**Q6.** What is appellate jurisdiction?
**Answer:** Power to hear appeals against the judgements of lower courts.
The Supreme Court hears appeals from the High Courts in constitutional, civil and criminal cases.

**Q7.** What is advisory jurisdiction?
**Answer:** The President may seek the Court's opinion on a question of law or fact (Art. 143).
The opinion is not binding.

**Q8.** What is revisory jurisdiction?
**Answer:** The power of the Supreme Court to review its own judgements.
To correct any error that may have crept in.

**Q9.** Why is the Supreme Court called a Court of Record?
**Answer:** Its judgements are preserved as records and precedents.
It can punish for contempt of itself.

**Q10.** What is judicial review?
**Answer:** The power to examine whether laws and executive orders agree with the Constitution.
To declare those that do not as null and void.

**Q11.** Name two writs and state the purpose of one.
**Answer:** Habeas Corpus and Mandamus.
Habeas Corpus orders the production of a detained person to test the legality of the detention.

**Q12.** Distinguish between Prohibition and Certiorari.
**Answer:** Prohibition stops a lower court from proceeding beyond its jurisdiction (before a decision).
Certiorari quashes an order passed without jurisdiction (after a decision).

### Part II parts (3 marks each)

**Q13.** Who appoints Supreme Court judges? Mention two qualifications.
**Answer:** The President, in consultation with the judiciary.
A citizen of India.
Five years a High Court judge, ten years a High Court advocate, or a distinguished jurist.

**Q14.** Mention three ways in which the Constitution ensures the independence of the judiciary.
**Answer:** Security of tenure — till 65; removal only by a special majority of both Houses.
Salaries charged on the Consolidated Fund; cannot be reduced.
Conduct cannot be discussed in Parliament; power to punish for contempt; no practice after retirement.

**Q15.** Mention three cases under the original jurisdiction of the Supreme Court.
**Answer:** Disputes between the Union and one or more States.
Disputes between two or more States.
Enforcement of Fundamental Rights (also: election disputes of the President and Vice-President).

**Q16.** Explain the appellate jurisdiction of the Supreme Court in three kinds of cases.
**Answer:** Constitutional — on a substantial question of interpreting the Constitution.
Civil — on a substantial question of law of general importance.
Criminal — e.g. where the High Court has reversed an acquittal and sentenced the accused to death.

**Q17.** Explain the power of judicial review.
**Answer:** The Supreme Court is the interpreter of the Constitution.
It reviews laws of the Union and the States and executive orders.
It declares those violating the Constitution ultra vires, null and void.

**Q18.** Explain the Supreme Court as a Court of Record.
**Answer:** Its judgements and orders are preserved as records.
They are used as precedents and cannot be questioned when produced in any court.
It can punish for contempt of court.

**Q19.** Explain the advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court.
**Answer:** Under Article 143 the President may refer a question of law or fact of public importance.
The Court may give or decline to give its opinion.
The opinion is not binding on the President.

**Q20.** Name three writs and state when each is issued.
**Answer:** Habeas Corpus — illegal detention.
Mandamus — a public authority failing in its duty.
Quo Warranto — a person holding a public office without right.

**Q21.** Explain the term, salary and removal of Supreme Court judges.
**Answer:** Term — till 65 years.
Salary — charged on the Consolidated Fund; cannot be reduced except in a Financial Emergency.
Removal — address by both Houses by a special majority for proved misbehaviour or incapacity.

**Q22.** Why is the independence of the judiciary important?
**Answer:** So that judges can decide without fear or favour.
To protect citizens' rights against the government.
To act as guardian of the Constitution and uphold the rule of law.

## 11. Long-Answer Questions (4 marks each)

### Part II parts (4 marks each)

**Q1.** Explain the advisory and revisory jurisdictions of the Supreme Court.
**Answer:** Advisory: the President may seek the Court's opinion on a question of law or fact of public importance (Art. 143).
Advisory: the Court may decline; the opinion is not binding.
Revisory: the Court can review its own judgements (Art. 137).
Revisory: to correct errors, since its judgements are binding like law.

**Q2.** What is original jurisdiction? Mention three cases under it.
**Answer:** Cases heard in the first instance, directly by the Supreme Court.
Union–State disputes.
Inter-State disputes.
Enforcement of Fundamental Rights (also: Union and a State v. another State).

**Q3.** What is the composition of the Supreme Court? Why is it called a Court of Record?
**Answer:** The Chief Justice and 33 other judges.
Appointed by the President.
Its judgements are preserved as records and precedents.
It can punish for contempt of court.

**Q4.** Name the five writs and explain any two.
**Answer:** Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto.
Habeas Corpus: a detained person must be produced before the court to test the detention.
Mandamus: commands a public official or body to perform a public duty.
(Two writs explained, 1 mark each, plus the list.)

**Q5.** Explain four safeguards of the independence of the judiciary.
**Answer:** Appointment in consultation with the judiciary.
Security of tenure till 65.
Salaries on the Consolidated Fund; not reducible.
No practice after retirement; contempt power; conduct not discussed.

**Q6.** Explain judicial review with an example.
**Answer:** The power to examine the constitutionality of laws and executive orders.
Unconstitutional laws are declared ultra vires and void.
It makes the Court the guardian of the Constitution and of Fundamental Rights.
Example: the Kesavananda Bharati case — even amendments cannot alter the basic structure.

**Q7.** Explain the appellate jurisdiction of the Supreme Court.
**Answer:** It is the highest court of appeal.
Constitutional appeals — substantial question of interpreting the Constitution.
Civil appeals — substantial question of law of general importance; criminal appeals — e.g. death sentences after reversal of acquittal.
Special leave to appeal against any court's judgement (Art. 136).

**Q8.** Explain the original and advisory jurisdictions of the Supreme Court.
**Answer:** Original: cases in the first instance.
Original: Union–State and inter-State disputes; Fundamental Rights.
Advisory: the President's references under Article 143.
Advisory: the opinion is not binding.

## 12. Competency / Application Questions

**Q1. (Detained without reason)** A student activist is arrested and kept in a police station for three days without being produced before a magistrate. What remedy does the family have?
**Model answer:** Petition the High Court or the Supreme Court for a writ of Habeas Corpus; the police must produce the student and justify the detention.

**Q2. (A water dispute)** Two States quarrel over the sharing of a river's water. Which court will hear the case and under which jurisdiction?
**Model answer:** The Supreme Court, under its original jurisdiction (inter-State dispute).

**Q3. (The unconstitutional law)** A new law bans all newspapers from criticising the government. How can it be challenged?
**Model answer:** In the Supreme Court or a High Court, which can use judicial review to declare it void as violating the freedom of speech and expression.

**Q4. (The President's doubt)** The President is unsure whether a proposed law would be constitutional. What can the President do?
**Model answer:** Refer the question to the Supreme Court for its opinion under Article 143 (advisory jurisdiction); the opinion is not binding.

**Q5. (An unqualified officer)** A person is appointed to a public post without the qualifications required by law. Which writ can remove the person?
**Model answer:** Quo Warranto.

**Q6. (A wrong in the judgement)** After a judgement, the Supreme Court finds an important fact was overlooked. What can it do?
**Model answer:** Review its own judgement under its revisory jurisdiction (on a review petition) and correct the error.

## 15. Revision Notes

### Chapter at a glance
- Integrated judiciary: SC → HCs → subordinate courts; SC law binds all (Art. 141).
- SC: CJI + 33 (34); appointed by the President (collegium); citizen + 5 yrs HC judge / 10 yrs HC advocate / jurist; till 65; removal for proved misbehaviour or incapacity by special-majority address of both Houses.
- Independence: appointment, tenure, salaries (Consolidated Fund), conduct not discussed, contempt, no practice after retirement, own establishment.
- Original (Art. 131): Union–State, inter-State; Fundamental Rights (Art. 32); election disputes of P and VP; transfer of cases.
- Appellate: constitutional, civil, criminal; special leave (Art. 136).
- Advisory (Art. 143): not binding. Revisory (Art. 137): reviews own judgements.
- Court of Record (Art. 129): records, precedents, contempt. Judicial review: ultra vires, guardian of the Constitution.
- Writs: Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto.

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct idea |
|---|---|
| Revisory = hearing appeals from lower courts | Revisory = reviewing its own judgements; appeals are appellate. |
| The advisory opinion binds the President | It is only advisory. |
| SC judges retire at 62 | At 65 (HC judges at 62). |
| Judges are removed by the President on the PM's advice | Only after a special-majority address of both Houses. |
| Only the Supreme Court issues writs | The High Courts also issue writs. |
| Prohibition and Certiorari are the same | Prohibition before a decision; Certiorari after. |
| Composition "25 judges" | CJI + 33 other judges. |
