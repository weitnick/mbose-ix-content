## 1. Complete Lesson

### Introduction

- The **First World War** (1914–1918) was the first war fought on a **world scale** — in Europe, Asia, Africa and on the oceans — between two groups of powers:
  - the **Allied Powers** — **Britain, France, Russia**, later joined by **Italy** (1915), the **USA** (1917), **Japan** and others; and
  - the **Central Powers** — **Germany, Austria-Hungary**, **Turkey (the Ottoman Empire)** and **Bulgaria**.
- About **one crore (ten million) soldiers** died and many more civilians; whole regions were devastated. It was called "the Great War".

### Causes of the First World War

#### 1. Aggressive Nationalism

- **Nationalism**, love of one's nation, became **aggressive** in the late nineteenth century: military strength came to be seen as **national prestige**, and every country tried to increase its power at the expense of others; each nation thought only of its own interest.
- **France** wanted to take revenge on Germany and **recover Alsace-Lorraine**, which it had lost in the **Franco-Prussian War** (1870–71).
- **Germany**, newly united (1871), wanted to be the **leader of the world**.
- In the **Balkans**, the **Serbs** wanted to unite all Serbian people into a single state — including those in **Bosnia**, which **Austria-Hungary** had annexed (1908). **Russia** supported the Slav peoples (Pan-Slavism).
- **Italy** wanted the Italian-speaking areas still under Austria.

#### 2. Imperialism

- **Imperialism** is a system by which a **powerful nation establishes its control over another country** — by **political pressure**, **infiltration and annexation**, or **outright war** — to set up **colonies**.
- Industrial nations needed **raw materials** and **markets**; there was a **mad scramble** for colonies in **Asia and Africa**.
- **Britain and France** had built huge colonial empires. **Germany**, a late-comer, demanded its "**place in the sun**" and clashed with them — for example over **Morocco** (1905 and 1911).
- Colonial rivalry created **jealousy and hostility** among the European powers.

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2020, Q2(g))* What was meant by the term "Imperialism", as a cause for World War I?
**Solution:** A system by which a **powerful nation** used to **establish its control over another country** — by political pressure, infiltration and annexation, or by outright wars — to establish **colonies**; there was a **mad scramble** among European nations for new territories in Asia and Africa (e.g. the **Morocco crisis**).

#### 3. The Armament Race

- Fear and suspicion led every power to build up **huge armies and navies**.
- **Germany** under **Kaiser William II** expanded its army and built a powerful **navy**, challenging **Britain's naval supremacy**; Britain replied with bigger battleships (the **Dreadnought**, 1906).
- **Compulsory military service** (conscription) was introduced in most countries; military spending rose sharply; generals drew up war plans in advance.
- A large army ready for war made the governments **quick to fight** when a crisis came.

#### 4. Division of Europe into two hostile camps

- Through **secret treaties** Europe was divided into two armed camps:

| Triple Alliance (1882) | Triple Entente (1907) |
|---|---|
| **Germany** | **Britain (England)** |
| **Austria-Hungary** | **France** |
| **Italy** | **Russia** |

- The Triple Entente grew out of the **Franco-Russian alliance** (1894), the **Anglo-French Entente** (1904) and the **Anglo-Russian agreement** (1907).
- Each side **suspected** the other; a quarrel between any two powers could drag all the others into war. (**Italy** later left the Triple Alliance and joined the Allies in 1915.)

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2019, Q2(h))* Name the signatory countries of the Triple Alliance.
**Solution:** **Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy.**

#### 5. The immediate cause — the Sarajevo Crisis (28 June 1914)

1. On **28 June 1914**, **Archduke Franz (Francis) Ferdinand**, the **heir to the throne of Austria-Hungary**, and his wife were **assassinated** at **Sarajevo**, the capital of **Bosnia**, by **Gavrilo Princip**, a young **Serbian (Bosnian Serb) nationalist**.
2. The murder was planned by a secret society, the **"Union or Death"** (the **Black Hand**), formed by extremist Serbian nationalists whose aim was to **unite all Serbians into a single state**.
3. **Austria** blamed Serbia and served an **ultimatum** with a list of harsh demands. **Serbia accepted most of them**, except those that would have meant the **loss of its sovereignty**.
4. On **28 July 1914** **Austria declared war on Serbia** — the **beginning of the First World War**.
5. **Russia** mobilised to support Serbia; **Germany** declared war on **Russia** (1 August) and **France** (3 August) and invaded **Belgium**; **Britain** declared war on Germany (4 August). Within a week **all Europe was in arms**.

**Example 3.** *(ICSE 2026 specimen, Q9(i))* Look at the picture given [the front page of The Evening Herald headlined "ASSASSINATION MAY LEAD TO WAR"]. How did the assassination mentioned above become a cause for the First World War?
**Solution:** **Archduke Francis Ferdinand**, the heir to the throne of Austria, was **assassinated at Sarajevo**, the capital of Bosnia, on **28 June 1914**; it was carried out by a secret society called "**Union of Death**", formed by extremist **Serbian nationalists** whose aim was to unite all Serbians into a single state; **Austria served an ultimatum** to Serbia making several demands; Serbia accepted most of them except those that would have led to the **loss of sovereignty**; on **28 July 1914 Austria declared war on Serbia** — the beginning of the First World War. *(Any three points of explanation.)*

### The course of the war (outline)
- **1914–1917:** trench warfare on the **Western Front** (France and Belgium); fighting on the Eastern Front, in the Middle East and at sea; new weapons — machine guns, tanks, poison gas, aircraft, submarines.
- **1917:** the **USA** joined the Allies (after German submarine attacks); **Russia** left the war after its revolution.
- **11 November 1918:** Germany signed the **armistice**; the war ended.

### Results of the First World War

#### 1. The Paris Peace Conference and the Treaty of Versailles

- The victors met at **Paris** in **1919**. The "**Big Four**" were **Woodrow Wilson** (USA), **Lloyd George** (Britain), **Georges Clemenceau** (France) and **Vittorio Orlando** (Italy). Wilson's **Fourteen Points** proposed a just peace and a League of Nations.
- The **Treaty of Versailles** with **Germany** was signed on **28 June 1919** in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles; it came into force on **10 January 1920**. Germany was **not allowed to negotiate** — it was a "**dictated peace**".

**Clauses (terms) of the Treaty of Versailles**

*War guilt and reparations*
1. Germany was **declared guilty of aggression** (the "war guilt" clause).
2. Germany had to pay a **war reparation of 33 billion dollars** (6.6 billion pounds).
3. Germany had to **supply huge quantities of coal** to France (and to Italy and Belgium) for ten years; all **German merchant ships** were distributed among the Allies.

*Military restrictions*
4. The **German army** was restricted to **1 lakh (100,000) soldiers**.
5. The **navy** was limited to **15,000 men** and **24 ships**.
6. The **air force and submarines were banned**; Germany could not make or buy **tanks and armoured cars**.
7. The **Rhine valley** was **demilitarised**, and German territory **west of the Rhine** was to be **occupied by Allied troops for 15 years**.

*Territorial losses*
8. **Alsace-Lorraine** was returned to **France**.
9. The **Saar coal mines** were given to **France** (the Saar was placed under the League for 15 years).
10. **Eupen and Malmedy** went to **Belgium**; **North Schleswig** to **Denmark**.
11. **Danzig** became a **free port** in Polish territory (the "Polish Corridor" gave Poland access to the sea).
12. Germany **lost all her colonies** to the Allies; German colonies in the **Pacific** and her areas in **China** were given to **Japan**.

*Other*
13. **Complete independence** was granted to **Belgium, Poland, Czechoslovakia and Yugoslavia**.
14. The **Covenant of the League of Nations** was made part of the treaty.

> **Why the Germans called it harsh and humiliating:** it was imposed without negotiation; it blamed Germany alone; the reparations ruined its economy; it took away territory, colonies and its armed forces. This bitterness later helped **Hitler** rise to power (Chapter 16).

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2023, Q2(vi))* Mention any two clauses of the Treaty of Versailles.
**Solution:** Any two: Germany was **declared guilty of aggression**; had to pay a **war reparation of 33 billion dollars**; had to **supply coal** to France, Italy and Belgium for 10 years; the **Rhine valley was demilitarised** and occupied by the Allies for 15 years; Germany lost **Alsace-Lorraine to France**, **Eupen-et-Malmedy to Belgium**, **Schleswig to Denmark**; **Danzig** became a free port in Polish territory; the **Saar coal mines** were ceded to France; Germany lost **all her colonies**; German colonies in the Pacific were given to **Japan**; the **German army** was restricted to one lakh soldiers, the **navy** to 15,000 men and 24 ships, and the **air force and submarines were banned**; the treaty gave complete independence to **Belgium, Poland, Czechoslovakia and Yugoslavia**; the **Covenant of the League of Nations** was added to the treaty; all **German merchant ships** were given to the Allies.

#### 2. Territorial rearrangement of Europe

1. **Three ruling dynasties were destroyed** — the **Romanovs** in **Russia**, the **Hohenzollerns** in **Germany** and the **Habsburgs** in **Austria-Hungary**.
2. The rule of the **Ottomans** (Turkey) came to an end; its Arab lands were placed under Britain and France.
3. **Austria and Hungary** became **separate independent states**.
4. New independent states emerged — **Poland, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia**, **Finland**, **Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania**.
5. **Alsace-Lorraine** was restored to **France**; **Schleswig** went to **Denmark**; **Eupen and Malmedy** to **Belgium**.
6. **Monarchy collapsed** in many countries and **democracy** spread (though dictatorships soon followed).
7. It was the **end of European supremacy**; the **USA** emerged as a **world power**.
8. The bitterness of the defeated and the dissatisfied led to the **rise of dictatorships**.

> Separate treaties were signed with Austria (Saint-Germain), Hungary (Trianon), Bulgaria (Neuilly) and Turkey (Sèvres).

#### 3. The League of Nations (1920)

- To prevent another war, the **League of Nations** was set up on **10 January 1920**, with its headquarters at **Geneva** (Switzerland). It was the idea of US President **Woodrow Wilson**, though the **USA never joined** it.
- Its main organs were the **Assembly**, the **Council**, the **Secretariat** and the **Permanent Court of International Justice** (at The Hague); the **International Labour Organisation** was linked to it.

**Objectives of the League of Nations**

1. To **maintain international peace and security** and **avoid future wars**.
2. Member states had to **refer their mutual disputes** to the League for a **peaceful settlement**.
3. States were **prohibited from entering into secret treaties** and alliances.
4. Member states were **not to maintain huge armies**, warships and destructive armaments (**disarmament**).
5. All states had to **respect each other's independence** and territorial integrity.
6. Member states had to **take necessary action, as directed by the League**, against any country that **disturbed world peace** (collective security).
7. To **promote cultural, social and economic cooperation** among member states and to maintain **just and honourable relations** between nations.

**Example 5.** *(ICSE 2024, Q9(ii))* Name the organisation which was established in 1920 to maintain peace after this war. Mention any two of its objectives.
**Solution:** The **League of Nations** (1). Any two (2): all disputes must be **referred to the League** for a peaceful settlement; all states were **prohibited from entering into secret treaties**; member states were **not to maintain huge armies**, warships and destructive arms; all states should **respect each other's independence**; member states had to **take action, as directed by the League**, against the country which disturbs world peace; the League would **promote cultural, social and economic cooperation** among member states.

### Summary of the chapter

- **War:** 1914–1918; Allies (Britain, France, Russia, Italy 1915, USA 1917, Japan) vs Central Powers (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Turkey, Bulgaria).
- **Causes:** **Aggressive nationalism** (France–Alsace-Lorraine; Serbia; Germany); **Imperialism** (scramble for colonies; Morocco); **Armament race** (Kaiser, navy, Dreadnought, conscription); **Division of Europe** — **Triple Alliance 1882** (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy) vs **Triple Entente 1907** (Britain, France, Russia); **Sarajevo crisis** (28 June 1914, Archduke Franz Ferdinand, Gavrilo Princip, "Union or Death"; ultimatum; Austria declares war 28 July 1914).
- **Treaty of Versailles** (28 June 1919): war guilt; **$33 billion**; coal to France; army **1 lakh**, navy **15,000 men / 24 ships**, no air force, submarines, tanks; **Rhine** demilitarised, occupied 15 years; **Alsace-Lorraine** and **Saar** mines to France; Eupen-Malmedy to Belgium; Schleswig to Denmark; **Danzig** free port; all colonies lost; independence of Belgium, Poland, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia; League Covenant.
- **Territorial changes:** Romanov, Hohenzollern, Habsburg and Ottoman rule ended; Austria and Hungary separate; new states; USA a world power; end of European supremacy; rise of dictatorships.
- **League of Nations** (10 January 1920, Geneva): peace and security; disputes to the League; no secret treaties; disarmament; respect independence; collective action; cooperation.

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — Long-term causes
The four long-term causes made Europe a "powder keg"; the Sarajevo murder was the spark. Always separate **long-term** causes from the **immediate** cause.

### Section 2 — The alliances
Learn the two lists as pairs: **Alliance — Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy; Entente — Britain, France, Russia.** Japan was an Ally, not part of the Entente.

### Section 3 — Versailles
Group the clauses: guilt and money; army; territory; colonies; new states. Military clauses (1 lakh, 15,000, 24 ships, no air force) are asked very often.

### Section 4 — The League
The objectives are about peace by agreement: settle disputes, no secret treaties, disarm, respect independence, act together against aggressors, cooperate.

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 Archduke Franz Ferdinand (1863–1914)
Heir to the throne of Austria-Hungary, whose assassination at Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 became the immediate cause of the war.

### 6.2 Gavrilo Princip (1894–1918)
A young Bosnian Serb nationalist, linked with the secret society "Union or Death", who shot the Archduke.

### 6.3 Kaiser William II (1859–1941)
Emperor of Germany, whose ambition for colonies and a great navy increased tension before the war.

### 6.4 Woodrow Wilson (1856–1924)
President of the USA, author of the Fourteen Points and founder of the idea of the League of Nations.

### 6.5 Georges Clemenceau (1841–1929)
Prime Minister of France, who insisted on harsh terms for Germany at Versailles.

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Small spark, big fire** — a single murder led to a world war because of alliances, armies and rivalries already in place.
- **A harsh peace breeds new war** — Versailles humiliated Germany and prepared the ground for the Second World War.
- **International cooperation** — the League was the first attempt at a world organisation for peace; the United Nations learned from its failures.

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **Arms races today:** nations still compete in weapons; arms-control treaties try to limit them.
- **International disputes:** today the UN and the International Court of Justice play the role the League tried to play.

### 8.3 More practice in the ICSE style

**Q1.** (3 marks) Explain the armament race as a cause of the First World War.
**Answer:** Fear and suspicion made every power build huge armies and navies; Germany's naval expansion challenged Britain, which built Dreadnoughts; conscription and war plans made governments quick to fight when a crisis came.

**Q2.** (3 marks) How did the division of Europe into two camps lead to a world war?
**Answer:** The Triple Alliance and the Triple Entente were bound by treaties; a quarrel between Austria and Serbia drew in Russia, which drew in Germany, which drew in France and Britain — a local war became a world war.

**Q3.** (2 marks) Why was the USA not a member of the League of Nations?
**Answer:** The US Senate refused to ratify the Treaty of Versailles and the Covenant, preferring to stay out of European affairs.

**Q4.** (4 marks) Why did the Germans regard the Treaty of Versailles as a "dictated peace"?
**Answer:** Germany was not allowed to negotiate; it was forced to accept war guilt; it had to pay huge reparations; and it lost territory, colonies and most of its armed forces.

**Q5.** (3 marks) Name the Big Four of the Paris Peace Conference.
**Answer:** Woodrow Wilson (USA), Lloyd George (Britain), Georges Clemenceau (France), Vittorio Orlando (Italy).

**Answer:** The USA.

**Answer:** Geneva.

## 15. Revision Notes

### Chapter at a glance
- War 1914–18: Allies vs Central Powers.
- Causes: aggressive nationalism (France–Alsace-Lorraine; Serbs; Germany); imperialism (colonies; Morocco 1905/1911); armament race (Kaiser; navy; Dreadnought; conscription); division of Europe — Triple Alliance 1882 (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy) vs Triple Entente 1907 (Britain, France, Russia).
- Immediate: Sarajevo, 28 June 1914 — Archduke Franz Ferdinand; Gavrilo Princip; "Union or Death"; ultimatum; Austria declares war 28 July 1914; Russia, Germany, France, Britain join.
- Armistice 11 Nov 1918; Paris 1919; Big Four; Fourteen Points.
- Versailles (28 June 1919): war guilt; $33 billion; coal; army 1 lakh; navy 15,000/24 ships; no air force, submarines, tanks; Rhineland demilitarised, occupied 15 years; Alsace-Lorraine, Saar mines to France; Eupen-Malmedy to Belgium; Schleswig to Denmark; Danzig free port; colonies lost (Pacific to Japan); independence of Belgium, Poland, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia; League Covenant.
- Results: Romanov, Hohenzollern, Habsburg, Ottoman ended; Austria and Hungary separate; new states; democracy; end of European supremacy; USA world power; dictatorships later.
- League (10 Jan 1920, Geneva): peace; disputes to League; no secret treaties; disarmament; respect independence; collective security; cooperation; USA never joined.

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct idea |
|---|---|
| Italy in the Triple Entente | Italy was in the Triple Alliance (left in 1915). |
| Japan in the Triple Entente | Entente = Britain, France, Russia only. |
| Sarajevo is in Serbia | It is in Bosnia. |
| Versailles signed in 1918 | Signed 28 June 1919; in force 10 January 1920. |
| League headquarters in New York | Geneva (UN is in New York). |
| Mixing long-term and immediate causes | Sarajevo is the immediate cause; the other four are long-term. |
