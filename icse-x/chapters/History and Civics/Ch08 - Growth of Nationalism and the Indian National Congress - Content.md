## 1. Complete Lesson

### What is nationalism?

> - **Nationalism** is the feeling of **oneness** among people who see themselves as **one nation**, with a common history, common interests and a common future, and who wish to govern themselves.
> - In the second half of the nineteenth century this feeling grew rapidly in India. People of different regions, languages and religions began to see that they all suffered under **British rule** and that only **united action** could change it.

### Factors leading to the growth of nationalism

#### 1. Economic exploitation

British rule was organised for the benefit of Britain, not India, and every class of Indians felt its effects:
- **Peasants** — the government took a **large share of their produce** as land revenue; they fell into the hands of **moneylenders**; **famines** killed millions.
- **Artisans** — Indian handicrafts were ruined by cheap British **machine-made goods** that entered India almost duty-free, while **heavy duties** were charged on Indian goods in Britain.
- **Indian traders and industrialists** — British economic policies were **against Indian trade and industry**.
- **Workers** — factory workers were **exploited** by long hours and low wages.
- **Educated Indians** — they were **denied promotions and high posts**, and many were unemployed.
- **The drain of wealth** — a large part of India's wealth flowed to Britain without return; India was reduced to a supplier of raw materials and a market for British goods.

When Indians understood that their **poverty was caused by British policy** (as Dadabhai Naoroji showed — Chapter 9), anti-British feeling became a national feeling.

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2018, Q2(a))* Mention any two economic factors responsible for the growth of nationalism in India.
**Solution:** Any two: **peasants** — the government took away a large part of their produce; British economic policies were **against the interests of Indian trade and industry**; the **working class** was exploited by factory owners; **educated Indians** were denied promotions and jobs; **heavy duties** on Indian goods; India was reduced to an **agricultural colony** of Britain.

#### 2. Repressive colonial policies — Lord Lytton (Viceroy, 1876–1880)

Lord Lytton's policies showed an attitude of **racial superiority** and contempt for Indian opinion. They angered educated Indians and **became a catalyst** for nationalism:

| Policy | What it did |
|---|---|
| **Vernacular Press Act, 1878** (the "Gagging Act") | Placed severe **restrictions on newspapers in Indian languages**, which could be punished for criticising the government; **English newspapers were exempted**. |
| **Indian Arms Act, 1878** | Made it a crime for **Indians** to keep or carry arms **without a licence** — **Europeans and Anglo-Indians were exempted**. |
| **Grand Delhi Durbar, 1877** | A lavish ceremony to proclaim **Queen Victoria Empress of India** ("Kaisar-i-Hind"), held while a terrible **famine** was killing millions in South India. |
| **Reduction of the ICS age** | The **maximum age** for the Indian Civil Service examination (held only in London) was **cut from 21 to 19**, making it even harder for Indians to compete. |
| **Removal of import duties** | Import duties on **British cotton textiles** were removed (1879), harming Indian cloth industry. |

> **The Ilbert Bill controversy (1883):** under Lord **Ripon**, the **Ilbert Bill** proposed to allow **Indian judges** to try **Europeans** in criminal cases — to introduce **equality** between British and Indian judges. The Europeans in India organised a fierce **agitation** and forced the government to weaken the Bill. Educated Indians learnt two lessons: the British would not treat them as equals, and **organised agitation** works.

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2025, Q7(i))* The repressive policies of Lord Lytton became a catalyst to the growth of nationalism. Mention any three of his repressive policies to justify this statement.
**Solution:** Any three: he **removed import duties on cotton textiles**; he **reduced the age** for appearing in the Indian Civil Service examination **from 21 to 19**; the **Indian Arms Act**; the **Vernacular Press Act**; he organised the **Grand Delhi Durbar** when India was facing **famine**.

#### 3. Socio-religious reform movements

The reformers attacked evils such as caste discrimination, sati and child marriage, spread education and made Indians **proud of their culture** — preparing the ground for national awakening.

**Raja Rammohan Roy (1772–1833)** — "the Father of the Indian Renaissance"
- Founded the **Brahmo Samaj** (1828), which preached the worship of **one God** and opposed idol worship and meaningless rituals.
- Led the campaign against **sati**; it was banned by Lord William Bentinck in **1829**.
- Fought for **women's rights** — inheritance, education, and against polygamy and child marriage.
- Supported **English and scientific education**; helped found the Hindu College (1817) and schools in Calcutta.
- A pioneer of **Indian journalism** — he published *Sambad Kaumudi* (Bengali) and *Mirat-ul-Akbar* (Persian), defended the **freedom of the press**.

**Jyotiba Phule (1827–1890)**
- Founded the **Satyashodhak Samaj** (Society of Seekers of Truth, **1873**) to fight **caste discrimination** and the domination of the upper castes.
- Worked for the rights of the **depressed classes (Dalits)** and the **peasants**; wrote ***Gulamgiri*** (Slavery, 1873).
- With his wife **Savitribai Phule**, opened the **first school for girls** in Pune (**1848**) and schools for children of the lower castes.
- Supported **widow remarriage** and set up a home for widows' children.

**Swami Dayananda Saraswati (1824–1883)**
- Founded the **Arya Samaj** (**1875**) with the call **"Go back to the Vedas"**; wrote ***Satyarth Prakash***.
- Opposed **idol worship**, **untouchability**, caste by birth and **child marriage**; supported **widow remarriage** and the education of women.
- Started the **shuddhi** movement to bring converts back to Hinduism; his followers founded **D.A.V. (Dayanand Anglo-Vedic) schools and colleges**.
- Gave the idea of **swadeshi** and "**India for the Indians**", building national self-respect.

**Swami Vivekananda (1863–1902)**
- A disciple of **Ramakrishna Paramahamsa**; founded the **Ramakrishna Mission** (**1897**) for **social service** — schools, hospitals, famine relief.
- At the **Parliament of Religions in Chicago (1893)** he presented the greatness of Indian culture to the world, making Indians **proud of their heritage**.
- Preached **service to the poor** as service to God ("daridra narayan"), condemned caste and untouchability.
- Inspired the **youth** with self-confidence: "**Arise, awake, and stop not till the goal is reached.**"

**Example 3.** *(ICSE 2019, Q2(c))* Name each of the organisations founded by Jyotiba Phule and Raja Rammohan Roy.
**Solution:** **Satyashodhak Samaj** — Jyotiba Phule; **Brahmo Samaj** — Raja Rammohan Roy.

#### 4. The role of the press

From the 1860s, Indian newspapers — such as the *Amrita Bazar Patrika*, *The Hindu*, *Kesari*, *The Bengalee*, *The Indian Mirror* and *Voice of India* — spread rapidly. The press:
1. spread the message of **patriotism** and the modern ideas of **liberty, equality, freedom, home rule and independence**;
2. carried on **daily criticism** of unjust British policies and **exposed** the true nature of British rule;
3. helped people in different parts of the country **exchange views** and kept them informed of **political developments**;
4. made Indians aware of what was happening in the **world** — freedom struggles elsewhere;
5. **organised public opinion** and helped in organising political movements, bringing leaders and workers on a common platform.

> Nationalist **literature** — novels, poems, plays and essays such as Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay's *Anandamath* (which contains *Vande Mataram*) — also built national feeling.

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2019, Q6(b))* State any three ways in which the press played an important role in developing nationalism among Indians.
**Solution:** Any three: the press **spread the message of patriotism**; spread the ideals of **liberty, freedom and equality**; popularised the ideas of **home rule and independence**; carried on **daily criticism of British policies**; **exposed the true nature** of British rule; helped in the **exchange of views** among people of different parts of the country; made Indians **aware of world events**; **aroused public opinion**.

#### 5. Other factors

- **Western education and the English language** gave educated Indians from different regions a **common language** and introduced them to ideas of **liberty, democracy and nationalism** (the thinkers of Europe and the American and French Revolutions).
- **Modern means of transport and communication** — the **railways**, **telegraph** and **postal system** linked distant parts of India, allowing people and ideas to move quickly and leaders to meet and organise all-India movements.
- **Political and administrative unification** of India under one government, one law and one currency.
- **Rediscovery of India's past** — scholars revealed India's ancient achievements, restoring self-confidence.
- **Racial discrimination** — Indians were insulted and treated as inferior.

### The forerunners of the Congress

Before 1885 several **regional political associations** had been formed:

| Association | Year and place | Leaders |
|---|---|---|
| British Indian Association | 1851, Calcutta | Landlords of Bengal |
| East India Association | 1866, London | **Dadabhai Naoroji** |
| Poona Sarvajanik Sabha | 1870, Poona | M. G. Ranade |
| **Indian Association** | **1876, Calcutta** | **Surendranath Banerjee**, Anand Mohan Bose |
| **Indian National Conference** | **1883, Calcutta** | Organised by the Indian Association; merged with the Congress in 1886 |
| Madras Mahajan Sabha | 1884, Madras | |
| Bombay Presidency Association | 1885, Bombay | Pherozeshah Mehta, K. T. Telang, Badruddin Tyabji |

> The **Indian Association** and the **Indian National Conference** are usually named as the two main forerunners of the Congress.

### Foundation of the Indian National Congress (1885)

- The **Indian National Congress** was founded in **December 1885** at **Bombay** (at the Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College).
- Its **founder** was **Allan Octavian Hume**, a retired British civil servant, who worked with Indian leaders to bring them together on one all-India platform.
- The **first session** (28–30 December 1885, Bombay) was attended by **72 delegates** and presided over by **W. C. (Womesh Chunder) Bonnerjee**.
- The **second session** (1886, **Calcutta**) was presided over by **Dadabhai Naoroji**; the Indian National Conference merged with the Congress.
- (The third session, 1887, Madras, was presided over by Badruddin Tyabji.)

**Immediate objectives of the Congress**

1. To **promote friendly relations** between nationalist political workers from **different parts** of the country, so that they would know one another personally.
2. To **develop and consolidate** the feeling of **national unity**, irrespective of **caste, religion or province**, and to end racial, religious and provincial prejudices.
3. To **formulate popular demands** and **present them to the government** through petitions and resolutions.
4. To **train and organise public opinion** in the country.
5. To decide the **political tasks** to be undertaken during the coming year.

> At its beginning the Congress did **not** demand independence; it sought **reforms within British rule**. *Purna Swaraj* (complete independence) became its goal only in **1929** (Chapter 12).

**Example 5.** *(ICSE 2025, Q7(iii))* Who presided over the second session of the Indian National Congress? Mention any three objectives of the Congress.
**Solution:** **Dadabhai Naoroji.** Any three: to enable national workers from all parts of India to become **personally known** to each other; to **end racial, religious and provincial prejudices**; to **train and organise public opinion**; to promote **friendly relations** between nationalist political workers; to develop the feeling of **national unity**; to **formulate popular demands** and present them to the government; to decide the **political tasks** for the year.

### Summary of the chapter

- **Nationalism** — the feeling of oneness as one nation; it grew rapidly after 1857.
- **Factors:** economic exploitation (all classes); repressive policies of **Lytton** (Vernacular Press Act, Arms Act, Delhi Durbar during famine, ICS age 21→19, removal of cotton duties) and the **Ilbert Bill** (1883); reformers — **Rammohan Roy** (Brahmo Samaj, sati, women, education, press), **Jyotiba Phule** (Satyashodhak Samaj, girls' school 1848, Dalits), **Dayananda** (Arya Samaj, Vedas, swadeshi), **Vivekananda** (Ramakrishna Mission, Chicago 1893, service, youth); the **press**; Western education; transport and communication.
- **Forerunners:** Indian Association (1876), Indian National Conference (1883), East India Association, Poona Sarvajanik Sabha, Madras Mahajan Sabha, Bombay Presidency Association.
- **Congress:** 1885, Bombay; founder **A. O. Hume**; 1st session — **W. C. Bonnerjee**; 2nd (1886, Calcutta) — **Dadabhai Naoroji**; objectives — unity, friendly relations, demands to government, public opinion.

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 Raja Rammohan Roy (1772–1833)
Scholar of many languages, founder of the **Brahmo Samaj** (1828), campaigner against **sati** and for women's rights and modern education; often called the **"Father of the Indian Renaissance"** or "the Maker of Modern India".

### 6.2 Jyotiba Phule (1827–1890)
Social reformer of Maharashtra who founded the **Satyashodhak Samaj** (1873), opened the first girls' school in Pune with Savitribai Phule (1848) and fought for the rights of the lower castes and peasants. He wrote *Gulamgiri*.

### 6.3 Swami Dayananda Saraswati (1824–1883)
Founder of the **Arya Samaj** (1875), author of *Satyarth Prakash*; he called on Indians to "go back to the Vedas", attacked untouchability and child marriage and promoted swadeshi.

### 6.4 Swami Vivekananda (1863–1902)
Disciple of Ramakrishna, founder of the **Ramakrishna Mission** (1897); his speech at Chicago (1893) made Indians proud of their heritage, and his call to serve the poor inspired generations of young nationalists.

### 6.5 Allan Octavian Hume (1829–1912)
A retired British officer of the ICS who helped found the **Indian National Congress** in 1885 and served as its General Secretary for many years.

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Shared suffering, shared identity** — Indians of many regions discovered common interests under one foreign ruler.
- **Reform and nationalism together** — social reform made Indians confident and united; nationalism gave reform a wider purpose.
- **Ideas need channels** — newspapers, railways and the post carried nationalist ideas across India.

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **Freedom of the press today:** Article 19 protects free expression; compare it with the Vernacular Press Act of 1878.
- **Schools named after reformers:** Savitribai Phule Pune University, D.A.V. schools and Ramakrishna Mission schools continue their work.

### 8.1 Graded exercises — solutions

#### Exercise 8A — Causes

**Q1.** Name the Act called the "Gagging Act".
**Answer:** The Vernacular Press Act, 1878.

**Q2.** Why did the Indian Arms Act anger Indians?
**Answer:** Indians needed a licence to keep arms, while Europeans and Anglo-Indians were exempted — it was racial discrimination.

**Q3.** What was the aim of the Ilbert Bill?
**Answer:** To allow Indian judges to try Europeans in criminal cases — equality between British and Indian judges.

#### Exercise 8B — Reformers and the press

**Q4.** Who founded the Arya Samaj, and what was its slogan?
**Answer:** Swami Dayananda Saraswati; "Go back to the Vedas".

**Q5.** Give two contributions of Swami Vivekananda.
**Answer:** Founded the Ramakrishna Mission (1897) for social service; his Chicago speech (1893) made Indians proud of their culture.

**Q6.** Name two nationalist newspapers of the nineteenth century.
**Answer:** *Amrita Bazar Patrika* and *Kesari* (also *The Hindu*, *The Bengalee*).

#### Exercise 8C — The Congress

**Q7.** When and where was the Indian National Congress founded?
**Answer:** In December 1885, at Bombay.

**Q8.** Name the Presidents of the first two sessions.
**Answer:** W. C. Bonnerjee (1885) and Dadabhai Naoroji (1886).

**Q9.** Was complete independence an aim of the Congress in 1885?
**Answer:** No; it sought reforms within British rule.

### 8.3 More practice in the ICSE style

**Q1.** (3 marks) Explain the Ilbert Bill controversy and its effect on Indian nationalism.
**Answer:** In 1883 Lord Ripon's government proposed that Indian judges could try Europeans; the Europeans' agitation forced it to be weakened; Indians learnt that they were not treated as equals and that organised agitation could influence the government.

**Q2.** (4 marks) Give two contributions each of Swami Dayananda Saraswati and Swami Vivekananda.
**Answer:** Dayananda — founded the Arya Samaj (1875) with the call "Go back to the Vedas"; opposed untouchability and child marriage and promoted swadeshi. Vivekananda — founded the Ramakrishna Mission (1897); his Chicago speech (1893) made Indians proud of their culture and he inspired the youth to serve the poor.

**Q3.** (2 marks) Why is the Grand Delhi Durbar of 1877 called a cruel display?
**Answer:** Huge sums were spent on a ceremony to proclaim Queen Victoria Empress of India while a famine was killing millions in South India.

**Q4.** (3 marks) How did economic exploitation make every class of Indians anti-British?
**Answer:** Peasants lost their produce to high revenue; artisans were ruined by British goods; educated Indians were denied jobs and promotions — all saw British rule as the cause of their suffering.

## 15. Revision Notes

### Chapter at a glance
- Nationalism: feeling of oneness as one nation.
- Economic exploitation: peasants, artisans, traders, workers, the educated; drain of wealth.
- Lytton (1876–80): Vernacular Press Act 1878 (Gagging Act), Arms Act 1878, Delhi Durbar 1877 (famine), ICS age 21→19, cotton duties removed. Ilbert Bill 1883 (Ripon) — equality of judges; European agitation.
- Reformers: Rammohan Roy (Brahmo Samaj 1828, sati, women, education, press); Phule (Satyashodhak Samaj 1873, girls' school 1848, Dalits, *Gulamgiri*); Dayananda (Arya Samaj 1875, Vedas, *Satyarth Prakash*, swadeshi); Vivekananda (Ramakrishna Mission 1897, Chicago 1893, service, youth).
- Press, Western education, transport and communication, unification.
- Forerunners: Indian Association 1876 (S. N. Banerjee), Indian National Conference 1883, East India Association 1866 (Naoroji), Poona Sarvajanik Sabha 1870, Madras Mahajan Sabha 1884, Bombay Presidency Association 1885.
- INC: Dec 1885, Bombay, Hume; 72 delegates; W. C. Bonnerjee; 1886 Calcutta — Naoroji; objectives: friendly relations, unity, demands, public opinion.
