## 1. Complete Lesson

### The real executive

> - India has a **parliamentary system** of government. The **President** is the **nominal** head; the **real executive** is the **Council of Ministers** headed by the **Prime Minister** (Articles 74 and 75).
> - The ministers are members of **Parliament**, sit in it, and stay in office only while they have the **confidence of the Lok Sabha**. This close link between the legislature and the executive is the heart of the parliamentary system.

### Appointment of the Prime Minister

- The **President appoints the Prime Minister** (Article 75).
- By convention the President invites the **leader of the party or alliance that has a majority in the Lok Sabha**.
- If **no party has a clear majority**, the President uses **discretion** — inviting the leader most likely to win a vote of confidence, who is asked to prove the majority on the floor of the House.
- The Prime Minister is usually a member of the **Lok Sabha**, but may be a member of the **Rajya Sabha** (for example, Dr Manmohan Singh, 2004–2014).

### Formation of the Council of Ministers

- The **other ministers** are appointed by the **President on the advice of the Prime Minister**.
- The **President administers the oath** of office and secrecy to the Prime Minister and the other ministers.
- The **Prime Minister allocates portfolios** (departments) and can **reshuffle** them at any time.
- A minister must be a **member of either House of Parliament**. A **non-member** can be appointed, but must **become a member within six months**, or cease to be a minister.
- The total number of ministers, **including the Prime Minister**, cannot exceed **15 per cent** of the strength of the Lok Sabha (91st Amendment, 2003).
- The **salaries and allowances** of ministers are decided by **Parliament** by law.

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2025 Improvement, Q1(iii))* How can Mr Amit, a non-member of Parliament, be appointed to the Union Cabinet?
**Solution:** **He has to become a member of either House within six months of appointment** — otherwise he ceases to be a minister.

### The three categories of ministers

| Category | Rank and role |
|---|---|
| **Cabinet Ministers** | The **senior-most** ministers, heading the **most important** ministries — Home, Defence, Finance, External Affairs, Railways. They form the **Cabinet**, the **inner core** that decides policy, and meet regularly under the Prime Minister. |
| **Ministers of State** | Second rank. Some hold **independent charge** of a ministry; others **assist a Cabinet Minister**. They attend Cabinet meetings **only when invited**. |
| **Deputy Ministers** | The **junior-most** ministers; they **assist** Cabinet Ministers or Ministers of State, answer questions in Parliament on their behalf and help in administration. They do not attend Cabinet meetings. |

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2027 specimen, Q1(iii))* Cabinet : inner core :: __________ : junior-most ministers.
**Solution:** **Deputy Ministers.**

### Tenure

- Ministers hold office **"during the pleasure of the President"**, which in practice means **as long as they have the confidence of the Lok Sabha** — normally until the next general election (five years).
- The Council of Ministers ends if the **Prime Minister resigns or dies**, or if a **no-confidence motion** is passed against it.
- An individual minister can be **dropped** at any time: the President dismisses a minister **on the Prime Minister's advice**.

### The Council of Ministers and the Cabinet

| Point | Council of Ministers | Cabinet |
|---|---|---|
| Members | **All three categories** of ministers (often 60–80) | Only the **Cabinet Ministers** — senior ministers (about 20–30) |
| Size | A **larger** body | A **smaller** body inside the Council |
| Meetings | **Rarely meets as a whole** | Meets **regularly**, usually every week |
| Consultation | The Prime Minister **may or may not** consult them | The Prime Minister **always** consults them |
| Portfolios | May or may not hold important portfolios | Hold the **most important** portfolios |
| Advice to the President | Does not advise as a body | **Advises the President** through the Prime Minister |
| Constitution | Mentioned in the Constitution (Article 74) | Mentioned only since the 44th Amendment (1978); grew by convention |

**Example 3.** *(ICSE 2019, Q4(b))* Distinguish between the Council of Ministers and the Cabinet.
**Solution:** The Council of Ministers consists of **all three categories** of ministers; the Cabinet is a **group of senior ministers** holding important portfolios. The PM **may or may not consult** the Council; the PM **always consults** the Cabinet. The Council **rarely meets as a whole**; the Cabinet **meets frequently**. *(Also: the Council is the larger group; the Cabinet advises the President through the PM.)*

### Functions of the Cabinet (Council of Ministers)

**1. Policy making**
- Decides the **domestic and foreign policies** of the government — on defence, the economy, education, relations with other countries.
- Decisions are taken in **Cabinet meetings** presided over by the Prime Minister and are binding on all ministers.

**2. Administrative functions**
- **Implements** its policies through the ministries and departments; each ministry carries out the Cabinet's directions.
- **Coordinates** the work of different ministries and settles disputes between them.
- **Advises the President** on all **major appointments** — Governors, judges, the Attorney-General, the Chief Election Commissioner, ambassadors.
- Controls the vast **civil service** that runs the government day to day.

**3. Legislative functions**
- Prepares and **introduces Bills** in Parliament; almost all important laws are **Government Bills**.
- Advises the President to **summon and prorogue** Parliament and to **dissolve** the Lok Sabha.
- **Prepares the President's Address** to Parliament.
- Advises the President to issue **Ordinances**.
- Moves **amendments to the Constitution**.
- Acts as the **source of information** to Parliament, answering questions and debates.

**4. Financial functions**
- **Prepares the Budget** (Annual Financial Statement), presented by the Finance Minister.
- Proposes **taxes** and decides how money will be **spent**; introduces **Money Bills**.
- Controls the **expenditure** of the government and plans the economy.

**5. Emergency functions**
- The President can proclaim a **National Emergency** only on the **written advice of the Cabinet** (44th Amendment, 1978); the Cabinet also advises **President's Rule** and a **Financial Emergency**.

**6. Foreign relations**
- Decides relations with other countries, **negotiates treaties** and agreements, and makes decisions on **war and peace** (subject to Parliament).

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2023, Q2(ii))* State any two legislative powers of the Cabinet.
**Solution:** The Cabinet **introduces Bills** in Parliament; it **advises the President to issue Ordinances**. *(Also: moves amendments to the Constitution; advises the President to summon the Houses; prepares the President's special address; acts as a source of information.)*

### Collective responsibility

> **Article 75(3):** *"The Council of Ministers shall be collectively responsible to the House of the People."*

- All ministers are **jointly responsible to the Lok Sabha** for every decision and policy of the government — they **"swim and sink together"**.
- A **Cabinet decision binds every minister**, even one who disagreed in the meeting; in public, all ministers **support** it. A minister who cannot accept a decision must **resign**.
- If the Lok Sabha passes a **no-confidence motion** against **one** minister or against the government, the **whole Council of Ministers must resign**.
- Cabinet discussions are **secret**, so that ministers can speak freely and the government presents a united face.
- Collective responsibility makes the ministers **work as a team**.

**Example 5.** *(ICSE 2025, Q1(i))* The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the __________.
**Solution:** **Lok Sabha.**

### Individual responsibility

- Each minister is **individually responsible to the President**: ministers hold office **during the pleasure of the President**, who can **dismiss** a minister — but only **on the advice of the Prime Minister**.
- Each minister is also responsible to the **Prime Minister**, who can ask for a minister's **resignation**.
- Each minister is responsible for the **working of his or her own department**; serious failure or wrongdoing in the department may force the minister to **resign**. *(In 1956 Lal Bahadur Shastri resigned as Railway Minister, accepting moral responsibility for a train accident.)*

**Example 6.** *(ICSE 2027 specimen, Q1(v))* Replace the underlined word: "The Council of Ministers is individually responsible to the **Lok Sabha**."
**Solution:** **President.** Ministers are *collectively* responsible to the Lok Sabha, and *individually* responsible to the President.

### Position and powers of the Prime Minister

The Prime Minister is the **real head** of the government. The Constitution calls the PM only the head of the Council of Ministers, but the office has become the most powerful in the country — "the keystone of the Cabinet arch", "the sun around which the planets revolve". Once called *primus inter pares* ("first among equals"), the PM is now far more than first among equals.

**1. With reference to the Cabinet (Council of Ministers)**
- **Forms** the Council of Ministers — **selects** the ministers, who are appointed by the President on the PM's advice.
- **Allocates portfolios** and **reshuffles** them.
- **Can ask a minister to resign**, or advise the President to **dismiss** a minister.
- **Presides** over Cabinet meetings and **decides the agenda**.
- **Coordinates** and **directs** the work of all ministries.
- The PM's **resignation or death** ends the whole Council of Ministers.

**2. With reference to the President**
- The PM is the **link between the President and the Cabinet** (Article 78): communicates all **decisions** of the Council of Ministers to the President and gives any **information** the President asks for.
- **Advises** the President on the appointment of **ministers** and of **high officials** — Governors, judges, the Attorney-General, the CAG, the Chief Election Commissioner, ambassadors.
- **Advises** the President to **summon and prorogue** Parliament and to **dissolve** the Lok Sabha.
- **Advises** the President on proclaiming an **emergency** (through the Cabinet).

**3. With reference to Parliament**
- Is the **Leader of the House** (the Lok Sabha, if a member), deciding the government's business with the Speaker.
- **Announces** important **government policies** in Parliament and is its **chief spokesperson**.
- **Defends** the government, **answers questions** and **intervenes** in important debates.
- Advises the President on **summoning, prorogation and dissolution** — a dissolution means a fresh election for the members.

**4. As leader of the nation and the party**
- The **leader of the majority party** and of the nation; addresses the nation in crises and on important occasions.
- **Chairs** bodies such as **NITI Aayog** and the **Atomic Energy Commission**.
- **Shapes foreign policy** and represents India at international meetings.

**Example 7.** *(ICSE 2025, Q2(i))* Mention any two powers of the Prime Minister with reference to the Cabinet.
**Solution:** **Allocates portfolios** and reshuffles the Council of Ministers; **presides** over Cabinet meetings and decides their agenda. *(Also: selects and drops ministers; coordinates policies; the PM's resignation means the resignation of the ministry.)*

### Summary of the chapter

- **Real executive:** the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister; the President is the nominal head.
- **PM:** appointed by the President — leader of the majority in the Lok Sabha; discretion in a hung House.
- **Ministers:** appointed by the President on the PM's advice; oath by the President; must be MPs (or become MPs in six months); total ≤ 15% of the Lok Sabha; salaries fixed by Parliament.
- **Ranks:** Cabinet Ministers (inner core) → Ministers of State → Deputy Ministers (junior-most).
- **Cabinet v. Council:** smaller, senior, meets often, always consulted, advises the President.
- **Functions:** policy making, administrative, legislative, financial, emergency, foreign relations.
- **Collective responsibility** to the Lok Sabha; **individual responsibility** to the President (and the PM).
- **PM's powers:** over the Cabinet, with the President, in Parliament, as national leader.

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — Appointment and formation
The President's choice of Prime Minister is guided by the Lok Sabha majority; all other ministers are really the Prime Minister's choice. The six-month rule allows a capable non-member to be brought in.

### Section 2 — Ranks and the Cabinet
The Council of Ministers is a large body in three ranks; the Cabinet — its senior ranks — is the real decision-making body. Questions often ask for the ranks "in order" and for three differences.

### Section 3 — Functions
The Cabinet runs the government: it makes policy, carries it out, writes the laws, prepares the Budget and advises on emergencies. Answer with the category and an example.

### Section 4 — Responsibility and the Prime Minister
Collective responsibility binds the ministers to each other and to the Lok Sabha; individual responsibility binds each to the President, the PM and the department. The PM's powers are best learnt in three groups — Cabinet, President, Parliament.

## 4. Vocabulary (Key Terms)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Real executive | Those who actually exercise executive power — the PM and the Council of Ministers. |
| Nominal executive | The formal head in whose name power is exercised — the President. |
| Council of Ministers | All the ministers of the three ranks, headed by the Prime Minister. |
| Cabinet | The inner group of senior ministers that decides policy. |
| Portfolio | The department or ministry given to a minister. |
| Reshuffle | Changing the portfolios of ministers. |
| Minister of State | A minister of the second rank, with independent charge or assisting a Cabinet Minister. |
| Deputy Minister | The junior-most rank of minister, assisting senior ministers. |
| Collective responsibility | The joint responsibility of all ministers to the Lok Sabha for every government decision. |
| Individual responsibility | The responsibility of each minister to the President, the PM and for his or her department. |
| During the pleasure | For as long as the appointing authority wishes. |
| Coalition government | A government formed by an alliance of parties. |
| *Primus inter pares* | "First among equals." |
| Government Bill | A Bill introduced by a minister. |

## 5. Important Facts, Formulas and Results

### 5.1 Must-know facts

- PM appointed by the **President**; ministers on the **PM's advice**; oath by the **President**.
- A non-member minister must become an MP within **six months**.
- Maximum size: **15%** of the Lok Sabha, including the PM (91st Amendment).
- Ranks: **Cabinet Ministers → Ministers of State → Deputy Ministers**.
- **Collectively** responsible to the **Lok Sabha**; **individually** responsible to the **President**.
- Ministers' salaries: fixed by **Parliament**.
- National Emergency only on the Cabinet's **written** advice.

### 5.2 Useful facts

- Jawaharlal Nehru was the first and longest-serving Prime Minister (1947–1964).
- Indira Gandhi was the first woman Prime Minister (1966–1977, 1980–1984).
- Dr Manmohan Singh (2004–2014) was a member of the Rajya Sabha while Prime Minister.

### 5.3 Beyond the text (general knowledge, not needed for exam answers)

- The Prime Minister's Office (PMO) in New Delhi assists the PM; the Cabinet Secretary is the senior-most civil servant and secretary to the Cabinet.
- NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission in 2015; the PM is its chairperson.

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 Jawaharlal Nehru (1889–1964)
The **first Prime Minister** of India, from 15 August 1947 until his death in 1964 — the longest unbroken term. He built the traditions of Cabinet government, planned economic development and was an architect of non-alignment.

### 6.2 Lal Bahadur Shastri (1904–1966)
As Railway Minister he **resigned in 1956**, accepting moral responsibility for a serious train accident — a classic example of **individual responsibility**. He was Prime Minister from 1964 to 1966 and gave the slogan "Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan".

### 6.3 Indira Gandhi (1917–1984)
The **first woman Prime Minister** of India (1966–1977 and 1980–1984). Her government saw the Green Revolution, the 1971 war and the Emergency of 1975–77.

### 6.4 Dr Manmohan Singh (1932–2024)
An economist who, as Finance Minister in 1991, began India's economic reforms; he was Prime Minister from 2004 to 2014 while a **member of the Rajya Sabha**.

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Responsibility gives power its legitimacy** — ministers rule only as long as the people's House supports them.
- **Teamwork in government** — collective responsibility means one government, one policy, one voice.
- **Leadership matters** — the PM's personality, party strength and coalition partners decide how powerful the office is.

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **Swearing-in ceremony:** after an election, the President administers the oath to the PM and ministers at Rashtrapati Bhavan.
- **Cabinet decisions in the news:** "Cabinet approves new education policy" — policy making by the Cabinet.
- **Reshuffles:** news of ministers changing portfolios shows the PM's power over the Council.

### 8.1 Graded exercises — solutions

#### Exercise 4A — Appointment and ranks

**Q1.** Who appoints the ministers other than the Prime Minister?
**Answer:** The President, on the advice of the Prime Minister.

**Q2.** Name the three categories of ministers in order of rank.
**Answer:** Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State, Deputy Ministers.

**Q3.** What is the maximum size of the Council of Ministers?
**Answer:** 15 per cent of the strength of the Lok Sabha, including the Prime Minister.

#### Exercise 4B — Functions and responsibility

**Q4.** State one administrative function of the Cabinet.
**Answer:** It implements its policies through the ministries and coordinates their work (also: advises the President on major appointments).

**Q5.** To whom is the Council of Ministers collectively responsible?
**Answer:** The Lok Sabha.

**Q6.** What happens if a no-confidence motion is passed against one minister?
**Answer:** The whole Council of Ministers resigns, because of collective responsibility.

#### Exercise 4C — The Prime Minister

**Q7.** Why is the PM called the link between the President and the Cabinet?
**Answer:** The PM communicates the Cabinet's decisions to the President and gives the President information on administration and legislation.

**Q8.** What happens to the Council of Ministers if the PM resigns?
**Answer:** The whole Council of Ministers stands dissolved.

### 8.3 More practice in the ICSE style

**Q1.** (3 marks) How is the Prime Minister appointed? What happens if no party has a majority?
**Answer:** The President appoints the leader of the majority party or alliance in the Lok Sabha. If no party has a majority, the President uses discretion, inviting the leader most likely to command a majority and asking that leader to prove it by a vote of confidence.

**Q2.** (2 marks) Why do all ministers resign if a no-confidence motion is passed against one minister?
**Answer:** Because of collective responsibility — the Council of Ministers is responsible to the Lok Sabha as a team.

**Q3.** (4 marks) "The Prime Minister is more than first among equals." Justify with four points.
**Answer:** The PM selects and drops ministers; allocates portfolios; presides over the Cabinet and decides its agenda; the PM's resignation ends the whole ministry.

**Q4.** (3 marks) State three policy-making or emergency functions of the Cabinet.
**Answer:** Decides domestic policies; decides foreign policy and treaties; gives the written advice needed for a National Emergency.

## 10. Short-Answer Questions (2 and 3 marks)

### Part I questions (2 marks each)

**Q1.** Who appoints the Prime Minister and the other ministers?
**Answer:** The President appoints the Prime Minister.
The other ministers are appointed by the President on the PM's advice.

**Q2.** Under what condition can a non-member of Parliament be made a minister?
**Answer:** The person may be appointed.
But must become a member of either House within six months.

**Q3.** Name the three categories of ministers.
**Answer:** Cabinet Ministers and Ministers of State.
Deputy Ministers (the junior-most).

**Q4.** State two legislative powers of the Cabinet.
**Answer:** Introduces Bills in Parliament.
Advises the President to issue Ordinances (also: prepares the President's Address; summons Parliament through the President).

**Q5.** State two financial powers of the Cabinet.
**Answer:** Prepares the Budget.
Proposes taxes and controls expenditure.

**Q6.** What is collective responsibility?
**Answer:** All ministers are jointly responsible to the Lok Sabha for every decision.
If a no-confidence motion is passed, the whole ministry resigns.

**Q7.** What is individual responsibility?
**Answer:** Each minister holds office during the President's pleasure and can be dismissed on the PM's advice.
Each minister answers for the working of his or her own department.

**Q8.** State two powers of the PM with reference to the Cabinet.
**Answer:** Allocates portfolios and reshuffles them.
Presides over Cabinet meetings and decides the agenda.

**Q9.** State two powers of the PM with reference to the President.
**Answer:** Communicates the decisions of the Cabinet to the President.
Advises the President on the appointment of ministers and high officials.

**Q10.** State two functions of the PM in Parliament.
**Answer:** Leader of the House; announces government policies.
Defends the government in debates and answers questions.

**Q11.** State two differences between the Cabinet and the Council of Ministers.
**Answer:** The Cabinet is smaller, with senior ministers only; the Council includes all three ranks.
The Cabinet meets regularly; the Council rarely meets as a whole.

**Q12.** Why is the PM called the real head of the government?
**Answer:** The PM leads the majority in the Lok Sabha and heads the Council of Ministers.
The President acts on the advice given by the PM and the Cabinet.

### Part II parts (3 marks each)

**Q13.** Mention the three categories of ministers in order of rank, with their roles.
**Answer:** Cabinet Ministers — senior, head important ministries, decide policy.
Ministers of State — independent charge or assist Cabinet Ministers.
Deputy Ministers — junior-most, assist senior ministers.

**Q14.** Distinguish between the Council of Ministers and the Cabinet on three points.
**Answer:** All three ranks v. senior ministers only.
Rarely meets as a whole v. meets regularly.
PM may or may not consult v. PM always consults.

**Q15.** Explain collective responsibility.
**Answer:** All ministers are responsible to the Lok Sabha as a team.
Cabinet decisions bind all; a minister who disagrees must resign.
A no-confidence vote removes the whole Council of Ministers.

**Q16.** To whom are ministers individually responsible? Explain.
**Answer:** To the President — ministers hold office during the President's pleasure.
The President can dismiss a minister on the PM's advice.
Each minister answers for his or her own department.

**Q17.** State three administrative functions of the Cabinet.
**Answer:** Implements policies through the ministries.
Coordinates the work of different ministries.
Advises the President on major appointments.

**Q18.** State three legislative functions of the Cabinet.
**Answer:** Prepares and introduces Bills.
Advises summoning, prorogation and dissolution.
Prepares the President's Address and advises Ordinances.

**Q19.** How is the Council of Ministers formed?
**Answer:** The President appoints the leader of the majority as PM.
The other ministers are appointed on the PM's advice and take the oath before the President.
The PM allocates portfolios; non-members must become MPs within six months.

**Q20.** State three powers of the PM as leader of the nation.
**Answer:** Addresses the nation in crises and on important occasions.
Chairs NITI Aayog and the Atomic Energy Commission.
Shapes foreign policy and represents India abroad.

**Q21.** State three powers of the PM with reference to the President.
**Answer:** Link between the President and the Cabinet; communicates decisions.
Advises appointments of ministers and high officials.
Advises summoning, prorogation and dissolution.

**Q22.** State the policy-making, financial and emergency functions of the Cabinet, one each.
**Answer:** Policy making: decides domestic and foreign policy.
Financial: prepares the Budget.
Emergency: gives the written advice for a National Emergency.

## 11. Long-Answer Questions (4 marks each)

### Part II parts (4 marks each)

**Q1.** Write any four functions of the Cabinet.
**Answer:** Formulates and implements policies.
Coordinates the work of ministries.
Introduces Bills and advises Ordinances.
Prepares the Budget; recommends emergencies; advises major appointments.

**Q2.** Mention four powers of the PM with reference to the Cabinet.
**Answer:** Selects ministers and forms the Council.
Allocates and reshuffles portfolios.
Presides over the Cabinet and decides its agenda; coordinates ministries.
Can ask a minister to resign; the PM's resignation ends the Council.

**Q3.** Mention four functions of the PM with reference to the President.
**Answer:** Communicates all decisions of the Council of Ministers.
Furnishes information on administration and legislation.
Advises appointments of ministers and high officials.
Advises summoning, prorogation, dissolution and emergencies.

**Q4.** Mention four functions of the PM inside Parliament.
**Answer:** Leader of the House.
Announces government policies.
Defends the government and answers questions.
Intervenes in important debates and plans the House's business.

**Q5.** Explain collective and individual responsibility, two points each.
**Answer:** Collective: all ministers are responsible to the Lok Sabha as a team.
Collective: a no-confidence vote removes the whole ministry; Cabinet decisions bind all.
Individual: each minister holds office during the President's pleasure; can be dismissed on the PM's advice.
Individual: each answers for his or her own department.

**Q6.** Describe the appointment and tenure of the Council of Ministers.
**Answer:** The President appoints the PM — the leader of the majority in the Lok Sabha.
Other ministers are appointed on the PM's advice; oath by the President.
Non-members must become MPs within six months; size limited to 15% of the Lok Sabha.
They hold office while they have the Lok Sabha's confidence — normally five years.

**Q7.** Distinguish between the Council of Ministers and the Cabinet on four points.
**Answer:** Composition: all three ranks v. Cabinet Ministers only.
Size: large v. small.
Meetings: rarely as a whole v. regularly.
Consultation and advice: PM may or may not consult v. always consults; advises the President.

**Q8.** "The Prime Minister is the real head of the government." Justify with four points.
**Answer:** Leader of the majority in the Lok Sabha.
Forms the Council of Ministers and controls it.
The President acts on the PM's advice.
Leader of the House and of the nation.

## 12. Competency / Application Questions

**Q1. (The expert minister)** The PM wants a famous economist, who is not an MP, as Finance Minister. Is it possible?
**Model answer:** Yes. The economist can be appointed but must become a member of either House (usually the Rajya Sabha) within six months.

**Q2. (Disagreement in the Cabinet)** A Cabinet Minister disagrees strongly with a Cabinet decision. What should the minister do?
**Model answer:** Either support the decision in public or resign — collective responsibility does not allow a minister to oppose it openly while in office.

**Q3. (A railway accident)** After a major accident caused by the railways' negligence, the Opposition demands the Railway Minister's resignation. Which principle is involved?
**Model answer:** Individual responsibility — a minister answers for the working of his or her department; the PM may ask the minister to resign.

**Q4. (Reshuffle day)** The PM moves three ministers to new ministries and drops one. Which powers are being used?
**Model answer:** The PM's powers to allocate and reshuffle portfolios and to ask a minister to resign (dismissal by the President on the PM's advice).

**Q5. (The size limit)** The Lok Sabha has 543 members. What is the largest possible Council of Ministers?
**Model answer:** 15% of 543 ≈ 81 ministers, including the PM.

**Q6. (Teamwork)** Explain to a classmate why the government "speaks with one voice".
**Model answer:** Because of collective responsibility: all ministers share responsibility to the Lok Sabha, and Cabinet decisions bind every minister.

## 14. Case-Based Questions

*Read each situation and answer the four questions, 1 mark each (4 × 1 = 4).*

### Case 1 — After the election

> The results show that one alliance has won 290 of the 543 seats. Its leader is invited to Rashtrapati Bhavan, sworn in, and then gives the President a list of names; some will head ministries, some will assist them.

**(i)** Who invites and appoints the leader?
**Answer:** The President.

**(ii)** Who administers the oath?
**Answer:** The President.

**(iii)** On whose advice are the other ministers appointed?
**Answer:** The Prime Minister's.

**(iv)** Name the junior-most rank among those "who will assist".
**Answer:** Deputy Ministers.

### Case 2 — A week in the Cabinet

> On Wednesday the senior ministers meet under the Prime Minister and approve a new education policy and the draft Budget. One minister disagrees but later defends the policy in Parliament.

**(i)** What is this meeting of senior ministers called?
**Answer:** A Cabinet meeting.

**(ii)** Which function of the Cabinet is approving the education policy?
**Answer:** Policy making.

**(iii)** Which function is approving the draft Budget?
**Answer:** Financial.

**(iv)** Which principle makes the minister defend the policy?
**Answer:** Collective responsibility.

### Case 3 — Trouble in a ministry

> Reports reveal serious mismanagement in one ministry. The PM asks the minister to resign; the minister refuses and is dismissed by the President. Months later, the Opposition's no-confidence motion is defeated.

**(i)** Which principle made the minister answerable for the ministry?
**Answer:** Individual responsibility.

**(ii)** On whose advice did the President dismiss the minister?
**Answer:** The Prime Minister's.

**(iii)** In which House was the no-confidence motion moved?
**Answer:** The Lok Sabha.

**(iv)** What would have happened if it had been passed?
**Answer:** The whole Council of Ministers would have resigned.

## 15. Revision Notes

### Chapter at a glance
- Parliamentary system: President nominal; PM and Council of Ministers real (Arts. 74–75).
- PM: appointed by the President — majority leader in the Lok Sabha; discretion in a hung House; may be from either House.
- Ministers: appointed on the PM's advice; oath by the President; MPs (or within 6 months); ≤ 15% of the Lok Sabha; salaries by Parliament; portfolios by the PM.
- Ranks: Cabinet Ministers → Ministers of State → Deputy Ministers.
- Cabinet v. Council: senior/all; small/large; meets often/rarely; always/may consult; advises the President.
- Functions: policy, administrative, legislative, financial, emergency (written advice), foreign.
- Collective responsibility to the Lok Sabha; individual responsibility to the President (and PM; own department).
- PM: over the Cabinet; with the President (Art. 78 link); in Parliament; leader of the nation.

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct idea |
|---|---|
| Ministers are collectively responsible to the President | To the Lok Sabha. |
| Ministers are individually responsible to the Lok Sabha | To the President (and the PM). |
| Ministers of State are the junior-most | Deputy Ministers are. |
| The Cabinet and the Council of Ministers are the same | The Cabinet is the inner core of senior ministers. |
| The President chooses ministers freely | On the PM's advice. |
| A non-member can never be a minister | Yes, for up to six months. |
| Ministers' salaries are fixed by the Finance Minister | By Parliament. |
