## 1. Complete Lesson

### The formation of the United Nations

- The horrors of the two World Wars and the failure of the League of Nations convinced the world that a **stronger international organisation** was needed.
- The **Charter of the United Nations** was signed at **San Francisco** on **26 June 1945** by 50 countries; the UN came into existence on **24 October 1945**, observed every year as **United Nations Day**.
- Its **headquarters** is in **New York**. **India** was a founding member. Today almost every country of the world — **193 member states** — belongs to it.
- The **UN emblem** shows a **map of the world** (seen from above the North Pole) **encircled by olive branches**, the symbol of **peace**.

**The principal organs of the UN**
1. The **General Assembly**
2. The **Security Council**
3. The **Economic and Social Council** (ECOSOC)
4. The **Trusteeship Council** (its work has been completed)
5. The **International Court of Justice**
6. The **Secretariat**, headed by the **Secretary-General**

### Objectives of the United Nations

1. To **maintain international peace and security** and to **save succeeding generations from the scourge of war**.
2. To **develop friendly relations among nations**, based on **equal rights and self-determination** of peoples.
3. To **achieve international cooperation** in solving problems of an **economic, social, cultural and humanitarian** character.
4. To **promote respect for human rights** and fundamental freedoms for all, without distinction of race, sex, language or religion.
5. To be a **centre for harmonising the actions of nations** in attaining these common ends.
6. To establish conditions in which **international law and treaties** are respected.
7. In short — to **disarm**, to **decolonise** and to **develop** (the "Ds" of the UN).

**Example 1.** *(ICSE 2020, Q9(a))* Name the organisation associated with the above emblem [a map of the world encircled by olive branches]. Mention any two of its objectives.
**Solution:** The **United Nations** (1). Any two (2): **maintain international peace and security**; **develop friendly relations** among nations; **achieve international cooperation**; solve problems of an **economic, social, cultural and humanitarian** character; be a **centre for harmonising the actions** of nations; **disarm**; **decolonise**; **develop**; create **faith in human rights**; establish conditions to maintain **international law and treaties**; **save mankind from the scourge of war**.

### The General Assembly

**Composition**
1. **All members** of the United Nations are members of the General Assembly.
2. Each member state may send **up to five representatives**, but each state has **only one vote**.
3. It is a kind of **"Parliament of Nations"** — the world's main forum for discussion.
4. At the start of each session the Assembly **elects a new President** and **21 Vice-Presidents**, and the **chairmen of its six main committees**; the Presidency **rotates each year among five groups of states**.
5. It meets in **regular session once a year** (from September); **special sessions** can be called.
6. Decisions on **important questions** need a **two-thirds majority**; other decisions need a **simple majority**.

**Functions of the General Assembly**
1. To **discuss any question** relating to **international peace and security** and make **recommendations** for its maintenance.
2. To **make recommendations for the peaceful settlement of disputes**.
3. To make recommendations to **promote international political, social and economic cooperation** and **friendly relations** between nations; to **initiate studies**.
4. To **receive and consider reports** from the **Security Council** and the other organs.
5. To **consider and approve the budget** of the UN and **apportion the contributions** among member states.
6. **Elective functions** — to **elect the non-permanent members of the Security Council**, the members of **ECOSOC** and the **Trusteeship Council**, and (with the Security Council) the **judges of the ICJ**.
7. To **appoint the Secretary-General** on the recommendation of the Security Council.
8. To **admit new members** (on the recommendation of the Security Council) and to **amend the Charter**.
9. Under the **"Uniting for Peace" resolution** (1950), to act on threats to peace if the Security Council fails to act because of a veto.

> The General Assembly is called the **deliberative organ** of the UN because its main work is **discussion and recommendation**; its resolutions are **not binding** on members (except on internal matters like the budget).

**Example 2.** *(ICSE 2025, Q10(i))* What is the composition of the General Assembly?
**Solution:** Any three: **all members** of the United Nations are members of the General Assembly; each state has **five representatives**; each state has **only one vote**; it is a **Parliament of Nations**; the Assembly **elects a new President**, **21 Vice-Presidents** and the **chairmen of six main committees**; the **Presidency rotates each year among five groups** of states; decisions are taken by a **two-thirds majority** (on important questions); the members **meet once a year**; **special sessions** can be called.

### The Security Council

**Composition**
1. The Security Council consists of **15 members**.
2. **Five permanent members** — **China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom (Britain) and the United States of America** (the "P5").
3. **Ten non-permanent members**, **elected by the General Assembly** by a **two-thirds majority** for a term of **two years**; a retiring member is **not eligible for immediate re-election**.
4. The non-permanent seats are shared by region: **Afro-Asian countries — 5; Latin American — 2; West European and others — 2; East European — 1**.
5. The **Presidency** of the Council **rotates every month**, in the **English alphabetical order** of the members' names.
6. The Council is in **continuous session** so that it can meet at any time.

**The veto power**
- Decisions on important (substantive) matters need the **affirmative votes of nine members**, **including all five permanent members**.
- A **negative vote by any one permanent member** blocks the decision — this is the **veto** ("I forbid"). The **Council is powerless to act** if any of the five uses its veto.

**Example 3.** *(ICSE 2019, Q2(i))* What is meant by the term "Veto" power?
**Solution:** The **permanent members** of the Security Council have the veto power — a **negative vote** that any one of the **five permanent members** can cast to **block** a decision; the Council is **powerless to act** if any of the five uses its veto.

**Functions of the Security Council**
1. To **maintain international peace and security** — its primary responsibility.
2. To **investigate any dispute** or situation that might lead to international friction.
3. To **recommend methods of adjusting disputes** and the terms of settlement.
4. To **formulate plans to regulate armaments**.
5. To call on members to apply **economic sanctions** (and other measures short of war) against an aggressor.
6. To **take military action against an aggressor** — today usually through **peacekeeping forces**.
7. To **recommend the admission of new members**, and to **recommend the appointment of the Secretary-General**; with the General Assembly, to **elect the judges of the ICJ**.

**Example 4.** *(ICSE 2024, Q10(i))* Mention any three functions of the Security Council.
**Solution:** Any three: **maintain international peace and security**; **investigate disputes**; **take military action against an aggressor**; **recommend methods of adjusting disputes** and terms of settlement; **regulate armaments**; take **economic sanctions** against an aggressor; **recommend the admission of new members**; recommend to the General Assembly the **appointment of the Secretary-General** and the **judges of the International Court of Justice**.

### The International Court of Justice (ICJ)

- The **principal judicial organ** of the UN; it sits at **The Hague** (Netherlands).

**Composition**
1. It consists of **15 judges**.
2. They are **elected** for a term of **nine years** by the **General Assembly and the Security Council** (voting separately).
3. **Each judge is from a different country** — no two judges may be nationals of the same state.
4. **One-third** of the judges retire every three years; a **retiring judge may be re-elected**.
5. The Court **elects its President and Vice-President** for **three years**, and appoints a **Registrar**.
6. Its official languages are **English and French**.

**Functions**
1. To **decide disputes between member states** that are **referred** to it (with their consent).
2. To give **advisory opinions** on legal questions to the **organs and specialised agencies** of the UN.
3. To decide disputes concerning the **interpretation of international law** and of **treaties** signed between countries.
4. To decide the **compensation** to be paid for the **breach of an international obligation** (treaty or agreement).
5. To help in the **codification** (systematic writing down) of **international law**.
6. To **suggest methods for the peaceful settlement** of disputes, deciding cases according to international law.

**Example 5.** *(ICSE 2025, Q10(ii))* Mention any three functions of the International Court of Justice.
**Solution:** Any three: **advisory opinion** on legal matters to the **organs and special agencies** of the UN; **decides disputes between member states** when referred; disputes concerning the **interpretation of international law**; decides the **compensation** to be paid in case of **breach of contract**; **codification of international law**; disputes regarding **treaties** signed between countries; **peaceful settlement of disputes**; decides cases based on **international law**.

### The specialised agencies

#### UNICEF — United Nations International Children's Emergency Fund (now the United Nations Children's Fund)
- **Set up:** 11 December 1946, first to help children in war-torn Europe. **Headquarters: New York.** It won the **Nobel Peace Prize** in 1965.

**Functions of UNICEF**
1. Works for the **survival, protection, health and well-being of children**; it is the agency that **speaks on behalf of children**.
2. Provides **protective food** (milk and nutritious food) for children and cares for the needs of **women and pregnant mothers** — **maternal and child health**, **nutrition** and **sanitation**.
3. Works for **universal child immunisation** against preventable diseases, and to prevent **TB, malaria**, eye and skin diseases; works to reduce **infant deaths from diarrhoea** and to prevent **iodine deficiency**.
4. Provides **funds for training** health and sanitation workers, **teachers** and **nutritionists**.
5. Provides **technical supplies and equipment** — medicines for health clinics, **pipes and pumps** to bring **clean water** to villages, and **paper for textbooks**.
6. Gives **instant help to children and mothers** in **emergencies** — earthquakes, floods, epidemics.
7. Helps governments plan **community-based services**; ensures children's access to **education**, **clean water and toilets**; works for the **rights of girls and women** and to **reduce social inequalities**.

**Example 6.** *(ICSE 2025, Q10(iii))* Give the full form of UNICEF. Mention any three of its functions.
**Solution:** **United Nations International Children's Emergency Fund** (or **United Nations Children's Fund**) (1). Any three (3): works for the **protection, survival, health and well-being of children**; provides **funds for training** health and sanitation workers, teachers and nutritionists; **universal child immunisation** against preventable diseases; provides **technical supplies, equipment and other aids** — paper for textbooks, medicines for health clinics, **pipes and pumps to bring clean water** to villages; assists governments to plan **community-based services** — maternal and child health, nutrition, sanitation; helps **children and mothers in emergencies** arising from natural calamities and epidemics; tries to **prevent diseases** like TB, malaria, eye and skin diseases; is the **sole agency which speaks on behalf of children**; ensures children have access to **education, clean water and toilets**; **reduces infant mortality** due to diarrhoea; works to prevent **iodine deficiency**; works for the **rights of women**; **reduces social inequalities** based on gender, caste, ethnicity and region.

#### WHO — World Health Organisation
- **Set up:** 7 April 1948 (celebrated as **World Health Day**). **Headquarters: Geneva** (Switzerland).

**Functions of WHO**
1. To **direct and coordinate international health work**.
2. To fight **communicable diseases** and to work on other problems such as **mental health, cancer, heart disease, diabetes** and **blindness**.
3. **Eradication of diseases** — **smallpox was eradicated** by a global WHO campaign (1980); it runs **malaria** and **polio eradication** programmes.
4. **Vaccination / immunisation** against six major diseases — **measles, diphtheria, tetanus, TB, polio and whooping cough**.
5. To **set international standards** for **food, medicines, drugs, vaccines** and biological products.
6. To promote **maternal and child health**, **safe drinking water**, better **nutrition, housing, sanitation, working conditions** and environmental **hygiene**.
7. To **promote research** to cure and prevent diseases; to organise **conferences and seminars**; to **publish health journals and bulletins**; to train health workers of different countries.
8. Has built a **medical sciences library** in Geneva; its efforts have helped **reduce infant mortality**.

**Example 7.** *(ICSE 2024, Q10(ii))* Mention any three functions of the World Health Organisation.
**Solution:** Any three: **direct and coordinate health work** on an international scale; works in the fields of **communicable diseases** and other issues such as **mental health, cancer, heart disease, diabetes, blindness**; maintains **maternal and child health**; **prevents accidents** and provides **rehabilitation**; **eradication of smallpox** by a global campaign; promotes **good health and living conditions** / improves health systems; sets **international standards for food and medicines**; provides **safe drinking water**; **vaccination / immunisation** against six major diseases; **promotes research**; organises **conferences and seminars**; **publishes health journals**; sets standards for **drugs, medicines, vaccines**; improves **nutrition, housing, sanitation** and waste disposal; improves **work conditions** and **environmental hygiene**; built a **medical sciences library in Geneva**; helped **reduce infant mortality**; organised **malaria and polio eradication** programmes; **trains health-care personnel**; combats **diarrhoeal diseases**.

#### UNESCO — United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organisation
- **Set up:** 16 November 1945. **Headquarters: Paris** (France). Its aim is to build peace "in the minds of men" through **education, science and culture**.

**Functions of UNESCO**

*(a) Education*
1. **Removal of illiteracy**; encouragement of **adult education**, **distance education** and the **open school** system.
2. **Financial assistance** for the education of **disabled children**; **grants and fellowships** to teachers and scholars.
3. Organisation of **library systems**, **book fairs** and festivals; promotion of **international understanding through education**.

*(b) Science*
4. Encourages **science education** through regional training centres; organises **seminars and conferences of scientists**.
5. Promotes **basic research** in geology, mathematics, physics and oceanography; helps correct the **imbalance in scientific and technical manpower**; encourages the **social sciences**.

*(c) Culture — preservation of cultural heritage*
6. Provides **technical advice, assistance, equipment and funds** for the **preservation of monuments** and other works of art.
7. Has prepared a **World Heritage List** to identify the **monuments and sites to be protected**; also lists the **Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity** (living traditions — festivals, music, crafts).
8. Protects the world's inheritance of **books, works of art and rare manuscripts**; encourages the **translation** of rare manuscripts.
9. Encourages **artistic creation** in literature and fine arts and **cultural development through films**; sends **cultural missions** to different countries.
10. Improves the quality of the **press, films and video**; spreads knowledge about **human rights**.

> **India and UNESCO:** World Heritage Sites in India include the **Taj Mahal, Qutub Minar, Red Fort, the Sanchi Stupa, Hampi, Humayun's Tomb, Fatehpur Sikri**, and — in the North-East — **Kaziranga** and **Manas** National Parks and the **Moidams of Charaideo** (Assam). **Durga Puja in Kolkata** was added to the Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2021.

**Example 8.** *(ICSE 2019, Q10(a))* Write any three functions of UNESCO that preserve our "Cultural Heritage".
**Solution:** Any three: UNESCO provides **technical advice and assistance, equipment and funds for the preservation of monuments** and other works of art; it has prepared a **World Heritage List** to identify the monuments and sites which are to be protected; it aims to **protect the world inheritance of books, works of art and rare manuscripts**; it gives **encouragement to artistic creations** in literature and fine arts; it pays attention to **cultural development through films**; it sends **cultural missions** to different countries to develop contacts that promote peace and prosperity; it **helps member states in the preservation** of their cultural heritage; it encourages the **translation of rare manuscripts**; it plays a vital role in **distributing knowledge about human rights**.

### The Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR)

- The **Universal Declaration of Human Rights** was adopted by the **UN General Assembly** on **10 December 1948** — observed every year as **Human Rights Day**.
- **Meaning:** it is a declaration of the **basic rights and freedoms** to which **every human being** is entitled — irrespective of **race, colour, sex, language, religion, political opinion, nationality or birth**. It sets a **common standard** for all peoples and nations: the right to **life, liberty and security**; **equality before the law**; freedom from **slavery and torture**; freedom of **thought, religion, expression and assembly**; and **economic, social and cultural rights** such as the right to **work, education** and an adequate **standard of living**.
- It is not a law in itself, but it has inspired the constitutions of many countries (including India's **Fundamental Rights**) and many international treaties. India's **Hansa Mehta**, a member of the drafting body, ensured that the declaration speaks of "**all human beings**" rather than "all men".

### Summary of the chapter

- **UN:** Charter 26 June 1945 (San Francisco); founded **24 October 1945**; HQ **New York**; 193 members; emblem — world map in olive branches; six organs.
- **Objectives:** peace and security; friendly relations; cooperation (economic, social, cultural, humanitarian); human rights; centre for harmonising actions; international law; disarm, decolonise, develop.
- **General Assembly:** all members; 5 representatives, 1 vote; President, 21 Vice-Presidents, 6 committees; annual session; two-thirds majority on important questions. Functions: discuss and recommend; peaceful settlement; cooperation; reports; **budget**; **elections** (non-permanent SC members, ECOSOC, ICJ judges); **appoints Secretary-General**; admits members; amends Charter; Uniting for Peace.
- **Security Council:** **15** — **5 permanent** (China, France, Russia, UK, USA) + **10 non-permanent** (GA, two-thirds, **2 years**, no immediate re-election; 5 Afro-Asian, 2 Latin American, 2 West European, 1 East European); monthly presidency; **veto**. Functions: peace and security; investigate; recommend settlement; regulate armaments; sanctions; military action; recommend admission, Secretary-General; elect ICJ judges with GA.
- **ICJ:** The Hague; **15 judges**, **9 years**, elected by GA and SC; different countries; President and Vice-President for 3 years; Registrar. Functions: disputes between states; advisory opinions; interpret international law and treaties; compensation; codification.
- **UNICEF** (1946, New York) — children and mothers: food, immunisation, training, supplies, emergencies, water.
- **WHO** (1948, Geneva) — coordinate health; diseases (smallpox eradicated; polio, malaria); immunisation; standards; research.
- **UNESCO** (1945, Paris) — education (literacy), science (research), culture (World Heritage List; manuscripts; arts).
- **UDHR** (10 December 1948) — basic rights and freedoms of every human being.

## 3. Detailed Section Explanations

### Section 1 — Why the UN?
The UN tried to correct the League's weaknesses: all great powers are members, the Security Council can act, and specialised agencies tackle the causes of conflict — poverty, disease, ignorance.

### Section 2 — Three organs: compare them
General Assembly — **everyone discusses** (deliberative). Security Council — **a few decide** (executive, with the veto). ICJ — **judges settle legal disputes** (judicial).

### Section 3 — Composition answers
Composition answers need **numbers**: 5 representatives/1 vote (GA); 15 = 5 + 10, 2 years (SC); 15 judges, 9 years, GA + SC (ICJ).

### Section 4 — Agencies
For each agency, learn the full form, headquarters, year and four functions. Picture MCQs (monuments → UNESCO; polio vaccine → WHO; children → UNICEF) are common.

## 4. Vocabulary (Key Terms)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Charter | The founding document of the UN, signed in 1945. |
| General Assembly | The UN organ in which all members are represented; its deliberative body. |
| Security Council | The UN organ chiefly responsible for international peace and security. |
| Permanent members (P5) | China, France, Russia, the UK and the USA. |
| Veto | The power of a permanent member to block a Security Council decision by a negative vote. |
| Non-permanent members | Ten members elected for two years. |
| International Court of Justice | The UN's principal judicial organ, at The Hague. |
| Advisory opinion | Legal advice given by the ICJ to UN organs and agencies. |
| Codification | Writing laws down systematically. |
| Specialised agency | An international body linked to the UN for a particular field. |
| World Heritage Site | A place listed by UNESCO as being of outstanding value to humanity. |
| Intangible cultural heritage | Living traditions — festivals, music, crafts — recognised by UNESCO. |
| Immunisation | Protection against disease by vaccination. |
| Human rights | Basic rights and freedoms belonging to every human being. |
| Sanctions | Penalties, such as trade restrictions, imposed on a country. |

## 6. Person Profiles

### 6.1 Franklin D. Roosevelt (1882–1945)
The US President who coined the term "United Nations" during the Second World War.

### 6.2 Eleanor Roosevelt (1884–1962)
Chaired the commission that drafted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

### 6.3 Hansa Mehta (1897–1995)
Indian educationist and member of the UN Human Rights Commission, who ensured that the UDHR says "all human beings are born free and equal".

### 6.4 Vijaya Lakshmi Pandit (1900–1990)
India's representative at the UN and the first woman President of the General Assembly (1953).

## 7. Key Ideas and Real-Life Connections

### 7.1 Big ideas
- **Collective security with great-power responsibility** — the veto gives the P5 special power, but also allows any one of them to block action.
- **Peace through welfare** — UNICEF, WHO and UNESCO tackle hunger, disease and ignorance, which breed conflict.
- **Universal rights** — the UDHR made human dignity a world standard.

### 7.2 Real-life connections
- **Pulse Polio** in India was supported by WHO and UNICEF; India was declared polio-free in 2014.
- **World Heritage in the North-East:** Kaziranga and Manas (natural sites) and the Moidams of Charaideo (cultural site) in Assam are on UNESCO's list.

### 8.3 More practice in the ICSE style

**Q1.** (3 marks) Distinguish between the General Assembly and the Security Council (three points).
**Answer:** All members vs 15 members; one vote each, no veto vs veto for five permanent members; mainly discusses and recommends vs takes binding decisions on peace and security.

**Q2.** (2 marks) What is the Universal Declaration of Human Rights?
**Answer:** A declaration adopted by the UN General Assembly on 10 December 1948, setting out the basic rights and freedoms of every human being — such as life, liberty, equality and education — without discrimination.

**Q3.** (2 marks) Name the headquarters of WHO, UNESCO, UNICEF and the ICJ.
**Answer:** WHO — Geneva; UNESCO — Paris; UNICEF — New York; ICJ — The Hague.

**Q4.** (4 marks) Explain the veto power and give one argument for and one against it.
**Answer:** Any one of the five permanent members can block a Security Council decision by a negative vote. For — it keeps the great powers inside the UN and makes them responsible for peace. Against — a single country can stop action even when the world agrees.

**Q5.** (3 marks) State three functions of UNESCO in the field of science.
**Answer:** Encourages science education through regional centres; organises conferences of scientists; promotes basic research in geology, mathematics, physics and oceanography.

### Case 2 — A court at The Hague

> Two countries agree to send their dispute over a river to a world court of fifteen judges, each from a different country, elected for nine years.

**(i)** Name the court.
**Answer:** The International Court of Justice.

**(ii)** Who elects its judges?
**Answer:** The General Assembly and the Security Council.

**(iii)** For how long are its President and Vice-President elected?
**Answer:** Three years.

**(iv)** Name one other function of the court.
**Answer:** Giving advisory opinions to UN organs and agencies.

### Case 3 — Three agencies at work

> Agency X runs a polio campaign; Agency Y repairs an ancient stupa; Agency Z sends water pumps and textbooks to flood-hit villages for children.

**(i)** Name X and its headquarters.
**Answer:** WHO — Geneva.

**(ii)** Name Y and its headquarters.
**Answer:** UNESCO — Paris.

**(iii)** Name Z.
**Answer:** UNICEF.

**(iv)** Give the full form of Y.
**Answer:** United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organisation.

## 15. Revision Notes

### Chapter at a glance
- UN: Charter 26 June 1945, San Francisco; founded 24 Oct 1945; New York; 193 members; emblem — world map with olive branches; six organs.
- Objectives: peace and security; friendly relations; cooperation; human rights; harmonising centre; international law; disarm, decolonise, develop.
- GA: all members; 5 representatives, 1 vote; President, 21 VPs, 6 committees; annual; two-thirds on important matters. Functions: discuss/recommend; settlement; cooperation; reports; budget; elections; Secretary-General; admit members; amend Charter; Uniting for Peace.
- SC: 15 = 5 P (China, France, Russia, UK, USA) + 10 NP (2 yrs, GA two-thirds, no immediate re-election; 5 Afro-Asian, 2 Latin American, 2 West European, 1 East European); monthly presidency; veto (9 votes incl. P5). Functions: peace; investigate; recommend; armaments; sanctions; military action; admission; Secretary-General; ICJ judges.
- ICJ: The Hague; 15 judges, 9 yrs, GA + SC, one per country, one-third retire every 3 yrs; President/VP 3 yrs; Registrar. Functions: disputes; advisory opinions; international law; treaties; compensation; codification.
- UNICEF (1946, New York): children — immunisation, nutrition, training, supplies, water, emergencies.
- WHO (7 April 1948, Geneva): coordinate health; smallpox eradicated; polio, malaria; immunisation (6 diseases); standards; research.
- UNESCO (1945, Paris): literacy; science; World Heritage List; manuscripts; arts; intangible heritage (Durga Puja).
- UDHR: 10 Dec 1948; basic rights and freedoms of every human being.

### Common mistakes

| Mistake | Correct idea |
|---|---|
| The General Assembly has the veto | Only the five permanent members of the Security Council do. |
| Security Council term — 5 years | Non-permanent members serve 2 years. |
| ICJ at Geneva / New York | At The Hague. |
| UNESCO at Geneva | UNESCO — Paris; WHO — Geneva. |
| WHO = World Heritage Organisation | World Health Organisation. |
| Japan or India is a permanent member | P5 = China, France, Russia, UK, USA. |
